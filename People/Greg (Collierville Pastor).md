# Greg (Collierville Pastor)

**Full Name:** Greg (surname not stated in the source)
**Era:** Contemporary (2020s)
**Tradition:** Evangelical Protestant — Reformed / compatibilist
**Role:** Pastor; media ministry; critic of Latter-day Saint theology

## Bio

Greg is an evangelical pastor in Collierville, Tennessee (roughly five years pastoring as of the recording). He describes a dramatic conversion in 2013: a former Los Angeles actor, self-described as verbally abusive and narcissistic, he came to a place of despair and suicidal ideation before a born-again experience centered on Christ's substitutionary death and resurrection — "it's no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me" (Galatians 2:20).[^1] He works as an executive with the evangelical media ministry Anchored North, filming conversion testimonies.[^2]

Theologically he is **Reformed** and self-identifies as a **compatibilist** rather than a "hyper-Calvinist," affirming both God's exhaustive sovereignty ("no atom is out of place," citing [[R.C. Sproul]]) and genuine human moral responsibility.[^3] He is the evangelical debater opposite [[Hayden Carroll]] in [[Lets Talk w Hayden Carroll - Most Intense Religious Debate|"The Most Intense Religious Debate You've Ever Seen"]].

## Topics

### Salvation by Faith Alone
Greg presents the classic evangelical gospel of "God, man, Christ, response" and *sola fide* — salvation received by grace through faith apart from any meritorious work, contrasted with Carroll's [[Faith Is Allegiance to Christ Not Mere Mental Assent|faith-as-allegiance]] model.[^1]

### Compatibilism and the Problem of Evil
Greg grounds his response to suffering in God's transcendent sovereignty, using his family's seven-year infertility, a rape-conceived child's testimony, and Joseph in Egypt as cases where "God has ordained the suffering for good." His central illustration is a **Tolkien "transcendent author"** analogy — God authors the story from a categorically higher level, so tragedy within the story does not make the author a "monster." Carroll presses this hard in [[The Regeneration Rejoinder - Calvinist Evangelism Is Self-Defeating]].[^4]

### Nature of God
Greg defends classical theism: God is immaterial spirit (John 4:24), light with "no darkness at all" (1 John 1:5), morally perfect and unchanging "from everlasting to everlasting" (Malachi), and *cannot* sin because sin is contrary to His nature — a perfection, not a limitation. See [[God Can Sin But Freely Chooses Not To (LDS Free-Will Theism)]].[^5]

### "Sola Words of the Covenant" and the Canon
His signature constructive argument is a coined phrase, **"sola words of the covenant"**: God redeems and then gives covenant-writings that stand as a *witness against* His people (Exodus 20; Deuteronomy 31; Josiah's reform), so scripture "tells a story" from Genesis to Revelation and closes there — and "living oracles" (Acts 7:38) refers to the *writings*, not a living prophet. See [[Sola Words of the Covenant - Redemption Then Covenant Writings]].[^6]

### Critique of the Book of Mormon
Greg argues the Nephite temple and priesthood — built by descendants of Joseph, not Levi, and outside Zion — contradict the Mosaic covenant order (2 Chronicles 6:6). See [[Nephite Temple and Priesthood Outside the Levitical Line]].[^7]
