# Carl Sagan

**Full name:** Carl Edward Sagan (1934–1996)
**Role:** Astronomer, planetary scientist, and science communicator (Cornell University)
**Tradition:** Secular skepticism / scientific naturalism

## Bio
Carl Sagan was a leading American astronomer and the era's most recognizable popularizer of science, best known for the television series *Cosmos* and for books including *The Demon-Haunted World: Science as a Candle in the Dark* (1995). A prominent skeptic, he devoted much of his later work to distinguishing science from pseudoscience and to methods for evaluating extraordinary claims.

## The Dragon in the Garage
In *The Demon-Haunted World*, Sagan offers the parable of "the dragon in my garage": each time the skeptic proposes a test (look at it, feel its heat, spread flour to catch its footprints), the claimant adds a caveat (it's invisible, it's incorporeal, it floats) so that no possible observation could ever detect it. Sagan's point is that a claim insulated from every test — "unverifiable, unfalsifiable" — is empirically empty.

Dan McClellan and Dan Beecher of *Data Over Dogma* invoke the parable against the apologetic move that withholds evidence for God until the inquirer "chooses to believe," arguing it has the same structure as Sagan's invisible dragon.
