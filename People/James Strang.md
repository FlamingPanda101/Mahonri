# James Strang

James Jesse Strang (1813-1856) was a convert to the Latter Day Saint movement in February 1844, months before [[Joseph Smith]]'s assassination, who became the principal rival to [[Brigham Young]] in the succession crisis. He produced a "Letter of Appointment" purportedly from Joseph Smith naming him the next prophet, claimed to discover and translate buried plates (the Voree plates), and produced new scripture — the Book of the Law of the Lord from the "plates of Laban." He founded a colony on Beaver Island in Lake Michigan, had himself crowned "king of the kingdom of God on earth," secretly entered plural marriage (concealing wife Elvira Field as his "nephew" in male dress), and was assassinated in 1856 by disaffected followers with the involvement of the U.S. Navy. He is the subject of [[Kyle Beshears]]'s PhD dissertation. [^1]

## In Beshears's account
Strang is Beshears's favorite case study for the breadth of the restoration beyond Utah, and a striking parallel to Joseph Smith's own plates-and-translation pattern. See [[James Strang and the Strangite Movement]], [[Mormonism Is More Than the Utah Church]].

## The witness-comparison argument
Critics cite Strang's plates — the Voree plates and later the "plates of Laban," each with signed witnesses — as a parallel that neutralizes the Book of Mormon witnesses. The *All Those In Favor* hosts answer that Strang's witnesses attest only to physical metal plates, whereas Joseph Smith's decisive Three Witnesses attest to an angel, so the two cases are not evidentially equal; and that invoking the parallel actually forces a critic to concede Joseph's better-documented gold plates existed too. [^2] See [[The Strang Plates Have Witnesses Just Like the Book of Mormon]], [[Strang's Witnesses Saw Plates Not an Angel]], [[The Strangite Plates Argument Concedes Joseph Smith's Plates Existed]], and (for the one Book of Mormon witness who briefly joined him) [[Martin Harris Among the Strangites]].

Publicly crowned "king of God's church" before some 300 followers on Beaver Island with a crown of tin, a wooden scepter, and a robe — the coronation officiated by former Shakespearean actor George J. Adams. [^3] The surviving Strangite community today numbers roughly 130, without a living prophet, awaiting Christ's return. [^4]
