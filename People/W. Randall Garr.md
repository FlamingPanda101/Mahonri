# W. Randall Garr

**Full name:** W. Randall Garr
**Role:** Scholar of Northwest Semitic languages and biblical Hebrew
**Era:** Contemporary (active 1980s–present)
**Tradition:** Academic

## Bio

W. Randall Garr is a scholar of Northwest Semitic linguistics and the Hebrew Bible. His monograph *In His Own Image and Likeness: Humanity, Divinity, and Monotheism* (Brill, 2003) is a book-length study of the vocabulary and theology of Genesis 1:26-27 — the meaning of *tselem* ("image") and *demut* ("likeness") and their bearing on how ancient Israel related humanity to divinity.

Dan McClellan names it as "the best single monograph, in my opinion, on what's going on here, the most thorough discussion that's available," while noting he "quibbles with some things here and there, particularly with the conceptualization of monotheism and the kind of dichotomous notion of humanity and divinity."[^1]

## Image and Likeness (Imago Dei)

Garr's central argument is that *tselem* and *demut* place humanity on a graded continuum between God and the world rather than an absolute divide: because the *elohim* of the divine council are pictured as looking like both God and humans, human beings "resemble God because they resemble angelic godlike beings that resemble God." This fuses the two puzzles of Genesis 1:26 — the plural "let us" and the image — into a single scheme, and it treats the image primarily as a matter of **outward resemblance** that is then extended to representative function.
