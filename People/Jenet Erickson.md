# Jenet Erickson

Latter-day Saint social scientist and Associate Professor of Religious Education at Brigham Young University. Her research focuses on the well-being of women in the contexts of religious faith and work and family life.[^1]

## Research on Religiousness and Well-Being

Erickson is a coauthor of the BYU Studies report *Latter-day Saint Trends in the United States: Religiousness, Well-Being, and Retention* (2026), with [[Justin Dyer]], [[Sam Hardy]], Barbara Morgan Gardner, and David C. Dollahite. The report draws on five large US datasets to show that Latter-day Saints lead the nation in religious practice and active retention and rank at or near the top in spiritual peace and life satisfaction. See [[Latter-day Saints Rank High in Well-being and Spiritual Peace]] and [[LDS Retention Is Higher Than Most Other Christian Traditions]].[^1]

## On the Secular Alternative

The report's well-being data bear directly on Erickson's focus: those who are "spiritual but not religious" and the religiously unaffiliated — especially in the youngest generation — report the *lowest* levels of happiness and family-life satisfaction, undercutting the claim that one can shed organized religion without cost. See [[The Spiritual But Not Religious Underperform on Well-Being]].[^1]
