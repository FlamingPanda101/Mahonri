# Pope Victor I

**Role:** Bishop of Rome (c. 189 – 199 AD)
**Era:** Late 2nd century (ante-Nicene)
**Tradition:** Early Christianity / later claimed by Rome as an early pope

## Bio

Victor I was bishop of Rome at the close of the second century, best known for the **Quartodeciman controversy** — the dispute over whether Easter should be kept on the 14th of Nisan (the practice of the churches of Asia Minor) or always on a Sunday (the Roman practice). Victor attempted to **excommunicate the Asian churches** that refused to conform.

## In the Early-Papacy Debate

Victor I is a recurring exhibit in the argument over whether the early Roman bishop held universal jurisdiction. [[Jacob Hansen]] presses that Victor *tried* to cut off the Asian churches but the other bishops "didn't comply" — and, crucially, **Irenaeus of Lyons rebuked Victor** for the attempt. Hansen's inference: if Victor genuinely held supreme jurisdiction, his excommunication would have been binding and uncontested; that it was resisted and rebuked shows the bishops regarded one another as independent equals, not subjects of a Roman monarch.[^1] The Catholic reply distinguishes *validly holding* an authority from *prudently or successfully exercising* it, and treats excommunication as a distinct question from doctrinal primacy. See [[No Bishop Held Universal Jurisdiction in the First Three Centuries]].
