# Casey Griffiths

**Era:** Contemporary (2010s–2020s)
**Tradition:** Latter-day Saint (LDS)
**Role:** Historian of the Restoration; professor of Church history and doctrine (BYU)

## Bio
Casey Paul Griffiths is a Latter-day Saint historian and a professor in the Department of Church History and Doctrine at Brigham Young University. He specializes in the history of the Restoration and the Doctrine and Covenants, and is a familiar public voice explaining the documentary record behind Joseph Smith's early claims (the First Vision, the coming forth of the Book of Mormon, the Palmyra sites). His characteristic move is to walk a questioner through *what the sources actually say* — friendly and hostile alike — and to concede uncertainty candidly where the record is thin.

## Joseph Smith — Sincerity vs. Fraud
In the *Hello Saints* interview [[Hello Saints - Could He Have Been Lying]], Griffiths answers an Evangelical pastor's "could he have been lying?" questions by laying out a cumulative case against the con-man reading: the Book of Mormon's production trail (531 pages in three months, with no qualified collaborator), the interlocking testimony of family, the Whitmers, and even hostile Palmyra neighbors, and above all the absence of any payoff — see [[Joseph Smith Gained No Wealth and Died Affirming the Book of Mormon]]. He concedes the treasure-seeking and seer-stone background is largely true while denying it was the family's livelihood — see [[Joseph Smith Treasure Hunting Extent]].

## First Vision and the Multiple Accounts
Griffiths treats the four (or five) firsthand and eight-to-nine secondhand First Vision accounts as a strength, comparing the variation to the multiple New Testament accounts of Paul on the Damascus road — see [[First Vision Accounts Are No More Contradictory Than the Gospels]] and [[The Four Firsthand First Vision Accounts]].

## The Creeds "Abomination" and the Godhead
On the 1838 account's "abomination" language, Griffiths offers a *process* reading — the offense is a committee of well-meaning but power-invested men "closing the door" and voting on God's nature, versus God revealing his nature by appearing — see [[The Creeds Abomination Targets Teachings Not Christians]]. He frames the Godhead/Trinity gap as one of the two genuine, non-negotiable LDS–Protestant differences (with the open canon) — see [[The Godhead Model vs the Trinity Model]].
