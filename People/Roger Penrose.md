# Roger Penrose

British **mathematical physicist** (b. 1931), Emeritus Rouse Ball Professor of Mathematics at Oxford and 2020 Nobel laureate in Physics for his work on gravitational collapse and black holes (the Penrose–Hawking singularity theorems). Among his best-known results beyond relativity is an estimate of the precision of the universe's initial conditions: for the Big Bang to yield the low-entropy, ordered cosmos we observe, its starting state had to be selected to a precision of roughly **one part in 10^10^123** — a figure he uses to dramatize how *specified*, not merely improbable, the beginning was. He is also known for the (controversial) Orch-OR proposal that consciousness involves non-computable quantum processes.

## Why cited
[[Alex Arnold]] cites Penrose's 10^10^123 figure as a secular, Nobel-tier witness that the cosmos's initial low-entropy condition is finely specified — the cornerstone of [[The Universe's Low-Entropy Start Is Specified Not Just Improbable]] and part of the broader design case in [[Anthropic Fine-Tuning Points to a Designer]].[^1]
