# Fred Hoyle

British **astronomer** (1915–2001), Fellow of the Royal Society and long at Cambridge, a principal architect of the theory of **stellar nucleosynthesis** (the B²FH paper, 1957). He predicted the 7.65 MeV excited state of carbon-12 — the *"Hoyle state"* — without which stars could not forge the carbon that life requires; the delicacy of that resonance led him to write that "a superintellect has monkeyed with physics." An opponent of the Big Bang (he coined the name derisively, favoring steady-state), he also popularized the **"tornado in a junkyard"** analogy — that chance assembling life is as unlikely as a whirlwind through a scrapyard assembling a Boeing 747 — against undirected abiogenesis.

## Why cited
[[Alex Arnold]] calls Hoyle as a secular witness on two fronts of fine-tuning: the carbon-resonance "coincidence" and the junkyard/747 improbability of a chance origin of life. Both feed the cumulative design case in [[The Universe's Low-Entropy Start Is Specified Not Just Improbable]] and [[Anthropic Fine-Tuning Points to a Designer]].[^1]
