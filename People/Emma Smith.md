# Emma Smith

Emma Hale Smith (1804–1879) was the first and only publicly recognized wife of [[Joseph Smith]]. In this source she is mentioned because Joseph first met her during the 1825 treasure-hunting expedition for [[Josiah Stowell]], when Joseph went to Harmony, Pennsylvania, to search for a legendary lost Spanish silver mine.[^1] The detail ties the treasure-hunting episode directly into the founding biography of [[Joseph Smith]].

- See [[Josiah Stowell Hires Joseph Smith 1825]]

## As the Context of the Word of Wisdom
In the [[Let's Get Real with Stephen Jones - Joseph Smith's Biggest Discovery (Don Bradley E0039)|Let's Get Real interview]], [[Don Bradley]] places Emma at the heart of the [[Word of Wisdom]]'s deep context: the 1833 revelation came less than four months after she nearly died bearing [[Joseph Smith III]], and while she struggled with her postpartum health and (Bradley infers) probable caffeine dependence. The revelation's promises of "health in the navel, marrow in the bones," and strength to "run and not be weary" answered her household's real need, making the Word of Wisdom a likely shared sacrifice of both spouses rather than an abstract code. See [[Emma's Difficult Childbirth Is the Immediate Context of the Word of Wisdom]].[^2]

## Polygamy Testimony — Split Before and After 1860
[[Brian Hales]] argues Emma's statements about her husband's plural marriage divide at the 1860 founding of the RLDS Church (with her son [[Joseph Smith III]] as president on an anti-polygamy platform): *before* 1860 multiple unpressured witnesses (William McLellin, Lovina Smith Walker, Joseph Lee Robinson) record her *acknowledging* — even participating in — the Partridge-sister sealings; her categorical *denials* come after, filtered through RLDS leaders. See [[Emma Smith's Polygamy Testimony Splits Before and After 1860]] and [[The RLDS Church Were the Original Polygamy Deniers and Recanted]].[^3]

## Her Role in Bringing Forth the Book of Mormon
In the [[Lets Get Real with Stephen Jones - Emma Smith|Let's Get Real interview]] (E0059), [[Don Bradley]] argues Emma was divinely central to the Book of Mormon: the angel required Joseph to marry her to obtain the plates ([[The Angel Required Joseph to Marry Emma to Obtain the Plates]]), and she was his *first* scribe, at the very beginning of the lost book of Lehi ([[Emma Smith Was Joseph's First Book of Mormon Scribe]]). Her last testimony — that the unlearned Joseph dictated "hour after hour... beginning where he left off" — makes her a witness to the fulfillment of [[Isaiah 29.11-12|Isaiah 29:11-12]].[^4]

## Faith Without Seeing
Emma handled the wrapped plates but, so far as we know, never saw them or an angel; told in [[Doctrine and Covenants 25|D&C 25]] to "murmur not because of the things thou hast not seen," she models faith for the ordinary believer — "Emma is us." See [[Emma Smith Models Faith Without Seeing]].[^4]

## Why She Denied Polygamy, and Why She Stayed in Nauvoo
Bradley reconstructs Emma's later denial of Joseph's polygamy as loyalty, protection of her sons, and a belief that Joseph had rejected the practice ([[Why Emma Smith Denied Joseph's Polygamy]]), and shows she never left the church — the saints left her location, while Joseph's grave, inherited debt, and stewardship kept her in Nauvoo ([[Emma Smith Never Left the Church]]).[^4]

## Not an Argument but a Person
Bradley resists the critical habit of enlisting Emma against Joseph: the "duped wife" frame offers her pity, not respect, and contradicts her uncompromising testimony that her husband was a prophet. See [[Critics Turn Emma Smith Into an Argument Not a Person]].[^4]
