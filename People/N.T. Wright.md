# N.T. Wright

Anglican New Testament scholar and former Bishop of Durham, among the most influential living Pauline and early-Christianity scholars; author of the multi-volume *Christian Origins and the Question of God*.

## Romans 3:2 — "Oracles," Not "Words" of God
Quoted by [[Trent Horn]] on [[Romans 3.2|Romans 3:2]]: "Nowhere else in early Christian writings are Israel's scripture designated as God's oracles. Israel was entrusted with what the gentile nations might have perceived as divine oracles." Wright thus distinguishes the *logia* (oracles/pronouncements) from the category "Scripture," undercutting the use of the verse to prove Scripture is "the words of God." See [[Romans 3.2 Says Oracles Logia Not Words Logos]] [^1].

## Resurrection vs the Immortal Soul
Cited by [[Alex Arnold]] on *Moroni's Standard*: Wright argues that most contemporary Christians do not actually believe what the creed says ("the resurrection of the body") but rather **Platonic soul-immortality dressed in Christian language**, and that the New Testament promises "not life after death, but life after life after death" — a real intermediate state followed by bodily resurrection.[^2] Arnold stresses that Wright "is not LDS" and would likely not be sympathetic to LDS conclusions, yet "the person with the most rigorous case for the bodily resurrection is also the person most clearly documenting that mainstream Christianity has abandoned it in practice." See [[Soul Immortality and Bodily Resurrection Are Incompatible Traditions]] and [[LDS Resurrection Doctrine Recovers the Hebrew Whole-Person Framework]].

## Justification and the New Perspective on Paul
Wright is the most-cited authority in the vault's Pauline soteriology cluster. His reading — justification is God's present declaration of "who now belongs to God's people, and how can you tell" (covenant membership), and the gospel is the enthronement of King Jesus rather than a personal-salvation formula — is used to reframe the grace/works debate as a first-century covenant question, not a Reformation one. See [[Justification Is Covenant Entry Not a Guaranteed Final Verdict]], [[Pauls Question Was Gentile Inclusion Not Individual Merit]], and [[The Gospel Is the Good News of God's Kingdom Not a Doctrinal Formula]]. Popularized in [[Thoughtful Faith - Why Paul Sounds Like a Mormon]].
