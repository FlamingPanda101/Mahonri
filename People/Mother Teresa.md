# Mother Teresa

**Role:** Roman Catholic religious sister; founder of the Missionaries of Charity
**Era:** 1910–1997
**Tradition:** Roman Catholic

## Bio
Anjezë Gonxhe Bojaxhiu, known as Mother Teresa, was an Albanian-born Catholic nun who founded the Missionaries of Charity in Kolkata (Calcutta), India, in 1950 to serve the destitute and dying. She received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1979 and was canonized as Saint Teresa of Calcutta by Pope Francis in 2016. In popular usage her name is shorthand for a life of extraordinary charitable works.

## As the test case in the salvation debate
Because Mother Teresa was a lifelong Roman Catholic who — barring a private, unrecorded deathbed change of mind — held the Catholic doctrine that baptism is necessary for salvation, she becomes the emblem of [[Jacob Hansen]]'s reductio against [[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]]'s faith-alone theology: if trusting Christ *plus* any ordinance forfeits salvation, then even Mother Teresa "would end up in hell."[^1] Ryan declines to pronounce on her individual heart ("I can't know someone's heart. The Lord knows a person's heart"), holding only that *if* she finally trusted her works rather than Christ she is lost. Hansen uses the result to argue that consistent Calvinism paints God as a moral monster. See [[The Church Fathers Reductio Against Sola Fide on Baptism]] and [[Calvinism Turns God Into a Moral Monster That Breeds Atheism]].
