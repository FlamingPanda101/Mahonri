# Alan Segal

**Era:** 1945–2011
**Tradition:** Jewish; academic historian of religion
**Role:** Scholar of early Judaism and Christian origins (Barnard College, Columbia University)

## Bio
Alan F. Segal was a historian of ancient religion best known for *Two Powers in Heaven* (1977), a study of rabbinic polemics against the idea that there were **two divine powers** in heaven. His work is standard in the study of Second Temple Jewish conceptions of divine mediators and their bearing on early Christology.

## On Binitarianism Before Christianity
[[Alex Arnold]] cites Segal to establish that **binitarianism** — two distinct divine figures — "was a serious interpretive option in early Judaism before Christianity," so that Christianity's identification of Jesus as a second divine figure "was just a development from a framework that already existed" rather than a novelty imposed on Jewish monotheism. This supports the claim that a pre-reform Israelite theology of a second mediating divine figure (reconstructed by [[Margaret Barker]]) has textual and historical backing.
