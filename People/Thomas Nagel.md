# Thomas Nagel

American analytic **philosopher** (b. 1937), University Professor emeritus at New York University. His 1974 essay *"What Is It Like to Be a Bat?"* argued that the subjective, first-person character of experience resists any purely physical (third-person) reduction — a founding statement of what would later be called the hard problem. In *Mind and Cosmos: Why the Materialist Neo-Darwinian Conception of Nature Is Almost Certainly False* (Oxford UP, 2012) he pressed the case further, from an avowedly **atheist** standpoint, that materialism cannot account for consciousness, cognition, or value.

## Why cited
[[Alex Arnold]] presents Nagel as a hostile witness: "a philosopher from NYU and an atheist" who "wrote an entire book arguing that materialism cannot account for consciousness," to which the secular reaction was "light on the refutation and heavy on the irritation." Nagel's standing is what makes the concession load-bearing in [[The Hard Problem of Consciousness Defeats Materialism]].[^1]
