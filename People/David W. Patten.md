# David W. Patten

**Role:** Apostle of the early Restoration — one of the original members of the Quorum of the Twelve
Apostles (ordained 1835)
**Era:** 1799–1838
**Tradition:** The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints

## Bio
David W. Patten was among the first Latter-day Saint apostles of the modern dispensation and is
remembered as its **first apostolic martyr**. In Missouri in 1838 he led an armed rescue of Latter-day
Saints held by the state militia at the **Battle of Crooked River**, was mortally wounded at the front
of the charge, and — as recounted on *Latter Daily Saints* — died after telling his wife, "Stay true to
your testimony."[^1]

## Spirit-world mission
[[Jonah Barnes]] cites Patten as a Restoration echo of the ancient pattern in which slain apostles
continue their ministry among the dead: the Englishwoman [[Anne Booth]] reportedly saw a vision of "a
man from America" with "a mustache and dark features" arriving to teach the spirits in prison, and later
identified him as the deceased Patten. See [[The Apostles Were Martyred to Become Missionaries in the
Spirit World]].[^1]
