# Justin Barrett

**Full name:** Justin L. Barrett
**Role:** Cognitive scientist / psychologist of religion
**Era:** Contemporary (active 1990s–present)
**Tradition:** Academic (cognitive science of religion)

## Bio

Justin Barrett is an experimental psychologist and one of the founders of the cognitive science of religion. His research examines the mental machinery that makes belief in gods intuitive — including the "hyperactive agency detection device" and the gap between **intuitive** and **reflective (theologically correct)** god-concepts.

Dan McClellan cites "research by Barrett and others" for the finding that even Orthodox Christians, "when they're not actively contemplating specific features of the Orthodox conceptualization of deity, they will retreat to anthropomorphic representations of God" because anthropomorphism is "more intuitive, more natural."[^1]

## Intuitive vs. Reflective God-Concepts

Barrett's "theological correctness" research shows a systematic gap: in reflective, doctrinal contexts believers affirm an abstract, non-anthropomorphic deity, but in real-time reasoning tasks they revert to a humanlike God who occupies one place, acts in sequence, and has a body. McClellan uses this to argue that anthropomorphism is the cognitive **default** and the incorporeal/philosophical concept of God is a later, effortful **override** — which sat much closer to the surface in the biblical period.
