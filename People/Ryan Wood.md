# Ryan Wood

Ryan Wood is a Latter-day Saint podcaster and self-described **"Amateur Historian"** who runs a YouTube channel and audio podcast ("filling in the history") devoted to reading and narrating the **entire Joseph Smith Papers**. He says the project took roughly fifteen years — reading each volume as it appeared (about every six months, through COVID) — and that he has gone through the corpus multiple times (reading it, writing a historical essay, then recording the podcast); he has also read the 26-volume Journal of Discourses.[^1] He knows [[Richard Bushman]] personally, is candid that he "gave up on [[Hugh Nibley]] after two" volumes, and played the stake president of Nauvoo in the film *Six Days in August* (a succession-crisis sequel to *Witnesses*, dir. Mark Goodman).[^2]

## Reading the Whole Joseph Smith Papers
Wood presents the papers as "raw data" — repetitive, often only fully available on the JSP website — and argues that reading all of it forces a reader to "grow up" and drop both hostile and hagiographic expectations. His "top five craziest" takeaways anchor several concept notes below.

## Church History and the "Craziest Things"
- [[Oliver Cowdery Counterfeited Money After the Kirtland Bank Collapse]] — on [[Oliver Cowdery]]'s unauthorized note-printing and 1838 excommunication.
- [[The Kirtland Temple Was the House of the Lord and the First Chapel]] — his argument that Kirtland was really the first chapel.
- [[Multiple Independent Witnesses Attest the Kirtland Temple Pentecost]] — his "more miraculous than I thought" reversal.

## On Joseph Smith the Man
- [[If You Look for a Reason to Reject Joseph Smith You Will Find One]] — his faithful-but-eyes-open method.
- [[Joseph Smith's Restraint Is an Intellectual Testimony]] — his affirmative case from restraint.

## Historiography and Folk Belief
- [[The Church Does Not Hide Its History]] — his rebuttal to the concealment trope.
- Corroborates [[Oliver Cowdery Gift of the Rod]] and [[Avoiding the Pejorative Magic]] — that a seer-stone/divining-rod worldview was religiously normal, not "evil," in Joseph's era.
