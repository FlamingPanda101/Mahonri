# Pope Stephen I

**Role:** Bishop of Rome (254 – 257 AD)
**Era:** Mid 3rd century (ante-Nicene)
**Tradition:** Early Christianity / later claimed by Rome as an early pope

## Bio

Stephen I was bishop of Rome during the **rebaptism controversy** of the 250s. Against [[Cyprian of Carthage]] and the North-African and some Eastern bishops, Stephen insisted that heretics and schismatics who had been baptized outside the Church should **not** be rebaptized on returning ("nothing is to be innovated but what has been handed down"). Cyprian and a council of Carthage held the opposite. The dispute grew sharp but was cut short by the Valerianic persecution, in which both men were martyred.

## In the Early-Papacy Debate

Stephen I anchors both sides of the jurisdiction argument. The **Catholic** case (as pressed in the Davila debate) is that Stephen ruled locally against rebaptism and that his position ultimately prevailed and was ratified — evidence that Rome's judgment carried decisive weight.[^1] [[Jacob Hansen]]'s **counter** is that Stephen "never asserts his authority *over* Cyprian and excommunicated" him — he could not force compliance — and that Cyprian openly denied any "bishop of bishops," which shows the contemporaries did not yet regard the Roman bishop as a supreme monarch (the papal reading is read *back* onto Stephen by later centuries).[^2] Catholics respond that a later-formalized doctrine can still be genuinely present in seed. See [[No Bishop Held Universal Jurisdiction in the First Three Centuries]].
