# Oscar Cullmann

Oscar Cullmann (1902–1999) was a leading New Testament scholar and theologian of the Lutheran/Reformed tradition who taught at the University of Basel and the Sorbonne, prominent in salvation-history theology and in Catholic–Protestant ecumenical dialogue.

## Immortality of the Soul or Resurrection of the Dead?
Cited by [[Alex Arnold]] on *Moroni's Standard*: in a 1955 lecture at Harvard Divinity School — published in 1958 as *Immortality of the Soul or Resurrection of the Dead?* — Cullmann argued that soul-immortality and bodily resurrection "are two fundamentally different and incompatible doctrines and that Christianity adopted Plato rather than Paul on the specific question of what death actually is."[^1] Arnold stresses he "was not a radical or a fringe figure... a mainstream reform scholar," whose argument "was controversial among his peers exactly because it named the displacement so clearly." He is the mid-century precedent for [[N.T. Wright]]'s later critique. See [[Soul Immortality and Bodily Resurrection Are Incompatible Traditions]].
