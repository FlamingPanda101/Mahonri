# Jeff Robinson

**Full Name:** Dr. Jeff Robinson
**Era:** Contemporary (career spanning ~30 years as of 2025)
**Tradition:** Latter-day Saint (LDS)
**Role:** Marriage & family therapist; researcher on same-sex attraction

## Bio
Jeff Robinson holds a PhD in Marriage and Family Therapy from Brigham Young University,
where his doctoral dissertation researched same-sex attraction — specifically, what people
who said they had "changed" actually meant by that claim. For roughly 30 years since, he
has counseled Latter-day Saint individuals (mostly believing men who want to live the
gospel) who experience same-sex attraction. He delivered a talk titled "Thinking
Differently About Same-Sex Attraction" at a FAIR (FairMormon) conference, the basis for the
panel conversation ingested here, on which he appeared with his son [[Andrew Robinson]] and
[[Timber Harvard|Timber Harward]].[^1]

## Topics

### Same-sex attraction as "something you know how to do"
Robinson's signature reframe: SSA is best understood not as an innate identity but as a
learned capacity — "something you know how to do" — which, he argues, restores moral agency.
See [[Same-Sex Attraction Is Something a Person Learns to Do Not an Innate Identity]].[^2]

### The born-gay assumption
He argues (with Andrew) that "born gay" is an unproven assumption unsupported by science or
LDS prophets — predisposition is "far different than cause." See [[The Born-Gay Assumption Is Unsupported by Science and LDS Prophets]].[^3]

### The label critique and the gravity analogy
Robinson's gravity analogy — a label *names* a fact without *explaining* it — anchors the
panel's critique of "gay" as a totalizing identity. See [[Adopting the Gay Label Hinders Discipleship]].[^4]

### Developmental account and the focus mechanism
He offers a clinical account of onset (three traits, two patterns) and of entrenchment
(five factors, driven by overfocus). See [[Robinson's Developmental Account of Same-Sex Attraction]] and [[Overfocus and Overinterpretation Entrench Same-Sex Attraction]].[^5]

### "No sexual content, not homosexual"
His diagnostic test for separating platonic male feeling from homosexuality. See [[A Feeling With No Sexual Content Is Not Homosexual]].[^6]

### Pastoral counsel: shame, and listening first
He steelmans the affirming position on shame, argues humility (not pride) is the antidote,
and models a listen-first response to disclosure. See [[Pride Is Not the Antidote to Shame]]
and [[Listen Before Correcting When Someone Discloses Same-Sex Attraction]].[^7]
