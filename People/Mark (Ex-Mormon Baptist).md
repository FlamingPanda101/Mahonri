# Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)

**Known as:** Mark (surname not given on air)
**Era:** Contemporary (2020s)
**Tradition:** Baptist / non-denominational "believer on Jesus Christ" (former Latter-day Saint)
**Role:** Ex-Mormon critic of LDS soteriology; frequent commenter on [[Hayden Carroll]]'s channel

## Bio

Mark is the guest in the dialogue [[Lets Talk w Hayden Carroll - Ex-Mormon Baptist Says My Theology Is Wrong|"Ex-Mormon Baptist Says My Theology Is WRONG!"]] — by Carroll's account the first repeat critical commenter to accept an invitation to talk on camera.[^1] He describes growing up in a mostly Baptist home, then converting to the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints in college after missionaries gave him a Book of Mormon; reading it, he "felt... as if the very hands of God were touching my heart," a "clearly supernatural" experience he still affirms.[^2] He was an active member for "many years," held callings, and later describes himself as "a professional educator in the church."[^3]

He left over **soteriology**, not history: he says he "chose to believe the words of Jesus Christ" over what he had been taught, concluding he could never "merit the righteousness of God" and that eternal life is received now by simple belief. He insists he is "not a hater... not an anti-Mormon," still has LDS family, and would tell Carroll to "stay a Latter-day Saint" but "place your belief on Jesus Christ alone."[^4] He now attends "a country Baptist church," holds to **once-saved-always-saved**, is effectively **King James Only** (as a personal conviction), and rejects **Calvinism** ("I can't get past the T" — denying total depravity).[^5]

His characteristic method is to press a single present-tense verse — [[John 6.47|John 6:47]], "he that believeth on me *hath* everlasting life" — and to demand Carroll show where LDS theology teaches it, repeatedly citing [[Dallin H. Oaks]]'s 1998 statement that eternal life "can only be obtained after the final resurrection." Fairness note: Mark's own epistemology is admittedly subjective — he grounds biblical infallibility and KJV primacy in "personal conviction" and a heart-level "choice to believe," which Carroll presses as the same mechanism Mark rejects in [[Moroni 10.4-5|Moroni 10:3-5]].

## Topics

### Eternal Life as a Present Possession
His central thesis: eternal life is held *now* upon belief, not a future exaltation — the point on which he says LDS theology is "the opposite" of Jesus. See [[Eternal Life Is a Present Possession Not Only Future Exaltation]].

### Belief vs. Faith-as-Works
He sharply distinguishes **belief** (an easy, one-time choice of the heart) from **faith** (works/evidence, as in Hebrews 11), holding that salvation comes by belief alone. See [[Belief Alone Saves and Is Distinct From Faith as Works]].

### Once Saved, Always Saved
He holds salvation is irreversible — even apostasy or atheism could not undo it, because Christ cannot "unknow" a person. See [[Once Saved Always Saved Means Salvation Is Irreversible]].

### The Sinless Soul and the Sinful Body
His reading of [[1 John 3.9|1 John 3:9]]: the regenerate **soul** is made sinless and sealed, while the **body** still sins and dies. See [[The Soul Is Made Sinless While the Body Still Sins]].

### The Bible as Standard — Grounded in Personal Conviction
He judges LDS doctrine by an infallible (King James) Bible whose authority he rests on "personal belief" and a Moroni-10-style heart choice. See [[The Ex-Mormon Grounds His Bible on the Same Heart-Choice He Rejects in Moroni 10]].

### Universal Opportunity in Mortality
He answers the unevangelized problem with [[Titus 2.11|Titus 2:11]]: everyone gets at least one chance to encounter saving grace during mortality. See [[Everyone Gets One Chance to Encounter Saving Grace in Mortality]].
