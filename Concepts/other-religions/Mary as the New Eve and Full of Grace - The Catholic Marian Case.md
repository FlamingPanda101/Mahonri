# Mary as the New Eve and Full of Grace - The Catholic Marian Case

## The Claim

The ten Catholic interlocutors mount a *positive* scriptural-typological case that Mary's sinlessness is apostolic, not a late invention. The case does not rest on a single proof-text but on a convergence: Mary is **"full of grace"** ([[Luke 1.28|Luke 1:28]]), she is the **New Eve** of [[Genesis 3.15|Genesis 3:15]], and she is the woman of [[Revelation 12|Revelation 12]] whom the dragon cannot touch. Read together, these are held to show a woman preserved from sin from the beginning. This is the counter-claim to [[Marian Sinlessness Is a Later Development Not Apostolic]].

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. "Full of grace" is a title of perfected grace (rating: moderate; argument weight: primary)
At the Annunciation the angel does not merely favor Mary but *titles* her — Greek **κεχαριτωμένη (kecharitōmenē)**, which the Catholics gloss as "perfectly given all graces for the rest of time." They contrast [[Acts 6.7|Acts 6:7]], where Stephen is "full" with a *different* Greek word, arguing Mary's term is uniquely comprehensive.[^1] They frame this within a scriptural pattern that grace is "dispensed before time eternal" (2 Timothy 1:9, Galatians 1:15, Jeremiah 1:5), so Mary's fullness of grace is not a reward for a later act but a prior endowment. See [[Luke 1.28|Luke 1:28]].[^2]

### 2. The New Eve typology (rating: moderate; argument weight: primary)
Second-century writers (Justin, [[Irenaeus of Lyons|Irenaeus]]) teach that Mary "undoes the knot of Eve" — the disobedience of the first virgin is reversed by the obedience of the second.[^3] The Catholics press that Jesus deliberately calls Mary **"Woman"** (John 2:4; [[John 19.26-27|John 19:26-27]]) because *"Woman" was Eve's name before she fell into sin* — so in naming his mother "Woman," Jesus identifies her with the *sinless, pre-fall* Eve.[^4]

### 3. Genesis 3:15 — the woman the serpent cannot touch (rating: moderate; argument weight: primary)
The protoevangelium ([[Genesis 3.15|Genesis 3:15]]) sets "enmity" between the serpent and the woman. The Catholics read total enmity as meaning "the devil cannot touch her" — an incompatibility with sin, since sin would put her (partly) on the serpent's side.[^5]

### 4. Revelation 12 — the same woman, untouchable by the dragon (rating: moderate; argument weight: primary)
The apostle John, they argue, picks up the Genesis 3:15 woman in [[Revelation 12|Revelation 12]]: the woman who bears the Messiah cannot be reached by the dragon (the serpent of old), who therefore makes war instead on "the offspring of the woman." Linking Genesis 3:15 → Revelation 12 → John 19's "Behold thy mother," they claim a single apostolic identification of Mary as the untouchable woman.[^6]

### 5. Patristic titles of purity (rating: weak–moderate; argument weight: secondary)
[[Cyril of Alexandria]] at the Council of Ephesus (431) calls Mary the **"dove"** — alluding, they say, to [[Song of Songs 6.8-9|Song of Solomon 6:8-10]] ("my dove... the only one... pure"). Syriac fathers call her **"the fruit of original justice,"** meaning she possessed the original justice Adam and Eve had *before* the Fall.[^7] Later fathers who affirm her sinlessness explicitly present it as a belief received "from the very beginning," i.e. as apostolic.[^8]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

[[Jacob Hansen]]'s replies (with the general Protestant objection behind them):

1. **"Full of grace" does not entail sinlessness.** "A person can be full of grace the moment that they accept Jesus Christ. Everything we do is by grace." Being graced — even richly — is not the same as never having sinned; the inference from *kecharitōmenē* to *impeccability* is not in the word.[^9]

2. **The typology is polyvalent and read-in.** A dove "can be a sign of the Spirit... a sign of many" things; "Woman" is an ordinary (or merely respectful) address; and "cannot be touched by the dragon" most naturally means *protected*, not *sinless*. Hansen charges the Catholics with "acting as if there's no other possible interpretation" and working "the Marian dogma into those statements when it's not there."[^10]

3. **The positive witnesses cluster late.** By Hansen's dating, the explicit affirmations run from the fourth and fifth centuries onward — Ephesus "occurs literally right after Augustine dies" — while the *earlier* witness (Origen) has Mary sinning. Late, growing attestation is the signature of *development*, not apostolic deposit.[^11]

4. **It looks post hoc.** Hansen's overall diagnosis: the doctrine of original sin developed within the Catholic tradition, which then *needed* Mary to be sinless to fit the model, so the fathers' vague purity-language was retrofitted into a full dogma "in any way they can in order to make it work."[^12]

**The Catholic rejoinder** is that the convergence of independent lines (a titled fullness of grace, the Eve typology already present in the second century, and two scriptural "untouchable woman" texts) is better explained by a genuine apostolic seed than by coincidence, and that Revelation 12 and Genesis 3:15 are *first-century* data, not late. The disagreement, as in the companion note, ultimately turns on whether developed dogma faithfully unfolds these seeds or over-reads them.
