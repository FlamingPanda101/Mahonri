# Marian Sinlessness Is a Later Development Not Apostolic

## The Claim

[[Jacob Hansen]] (a Latter-day Saint, arguing self-consciously "as a historian," not from LDS presuppositions) contends that the Catholic Marian dogmas — specifically the **sinlessness of Mary** (that she never committed any sin) and, behind it, the **Immaculate Conception** and the **Assumption** — are *not apostolic*. His narrow thesis: whatever one thinks of Mormonism, the historical record does not show that the apostles taught Mary's total sinlessness as a binding doctrine; it is a later development retrojected onto the earliest sources.[^1] The stakes are high on the Catholic side: to knowingly reject these dogmas is, on Catholic teaching, mortal sin.[^2]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The New Testament universalizes sin and excepts only Jesus (rating: strong for the negative case; argument weight: primary)
[[Romans 3.23|Romans 3:23]] ("all have sinned, and come short of the glory of God") and [[2 Corinthians 5.21|2 Corinthians 5:21]] ("he hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin") state human sinfulness universally and carve out an exception *only for Christ*. "The Bible is replete that human beings, we all sin, and the only exception that's ever mentioned over and over is Jesus. But Mary is never mentioned in scripture as someone who is sinless."[^3]

### 2. Early fathers positively attest that Mary sinned (rating: strong; argument weight: primary)
Hansen adduces [[Origen]], who read Simeon's prophecy that "a sword shall pierce through thy own soul" (Luke 2:35) as *the sword of unbelief* — i.e., Mary was "scandalized" and fell short at the crucifixion; and he lists [[Basil the Great]] and [[Augustine]] as further witnesses that Mary was not held sinless in the earliest centuries.[^4] His point about *direction of development*: "there was a tradition in the church that Mary had sinned; it's very clearly in the historical record," and modern Catholics must now "go back and create these creative interpretations of scripture" to overturn their own earlier fathers.[^5]

### 3. The dogma's own definition-date is late (rating: strong; argument weight: primary)
Hansen turns the Catholics' own admission against them: "The Immaculate Conception wasn't dogmatically defined until the 19th century" (1854). So the early fathers were *not* bound to affirm Mary's sinlessness — which is why the record is mixed rather than unanimous.[^6]

### 4. The from-the-start criterion (rating: moderate; argument weight: secondary — a methodological premise)
If a teaching "came from the apostles and was a binding dogma on the church from the start, then someone would have mentioned it somewhere, and you wouldn't have church fathers contradicting the notion."[^7] The presence of contradicting fathers is, for Hansen, decisive against apostolic origin.

### 5. Deflecting the tu quoque (rating: n/a — clarification; argument weight: primary framing)
When Catholics answer "your church is less than 200 years old — that's Mormonism modernism," Hansen refuses the bait: "If Mormonism is false, that doesn't mean the Catholic teachings on Mary are apostolic. My claim is not that the Mormon church is true."[^8] The argument is a *historical* claim about apostolicity, not a proof of the Restoration.

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

The Catholic response, stated at full strength:

1. **Origen is a hostile, atypical witness.** The Catholics note that Origen "isn't a church father" in the honored sense (his views were later anathematized), so leaning on him is "a horrible epistemology." One dissenting or eccentric voice does not establish "the overall consensus of the church."[^9] A Latter-day Saint, they add, must himself grant that dissenting voices can exist beneath the level of an official promulgation.[^10]

2. **"All have sinned" is general, not absolute.** The linchpin verse is contested at the level of exegesis: Paul in [[Romans 3.23|Romans 3]] is quoting [[Psalm 14.1|Psalm 14]] ("none is righteous, no, not one"), yet *two verses later the same psalm* speaks of "the generation of the righteous," and the NT elsewhere calls various people "righteous." So Paul's "all" is being used *generally* — everyone, whether Jew or Gentile, stands under sin — not as a literal metaphysical claim that every individual without exception has personally sinned. If the "all" is not absolute, it does not by itself exclude a graced exception for Mary.[^11]

3. **Positive early data for Mary's purity.** Against the "silence" claim, Catholics cite the *positive* record: Mary "full of grace" ([[Luke 1.28|Luke 1:28]]); the second-century Eve/Mary typology (Justin, Irenaeus — Mary "undoes the knot of Eve"); Cyril of Alexandria at Ephesus calling her the "dove" (Song of Solomon 6:8-10); and Syriac fathers calling her "the fruit of original justice." The full positive case is developed in [[Mary as the New Eve and Full of Grace - The Catholic Marian Case]].

4. **Apostolic deposit ≠ written scripture; absence of proof ≠ proof of absence.** "The apostolic deposit of faith isn't limited to the written scriptures alone" — there is "the unwritten word of God that is preserved by the church"; and "lack of evidence isn't evidence of lack." A doctrine may be apostolic yet not explicitly recorded in the first three centuries.[^12]

5. **Late definition is not late origin (development).** That a doctrine is *defined* under challenge in 1854 does not mean it *originated* then — the same developmental logic the Catholic uses for the canon and the Trinity. Hansen's own tradition believes things not stated in the Bible; the parity cuts back. See [[Catholic Doctrinal Development Is the Same Double Standard]].

**Where the tension really sits.** Both sides *agree* that scripture nowhere explicitly says either that Mary sinned or that she did not (Hansen concedes the explicit statement is absent either way).[^13] The dispute is therefore over (a) the weight of the mixed patristic record, and (b) whether Rome's later, developed dogma is a faithful unfolding of an apostolic seed or a retrojection onto sources that point the other way. Hansen also concedes he approaches the documents *without* assuming the univocality or inspiration of scripture — a Catholic will note that this "neutral historian" posture is itself a contestable methodological choice, not a view from nowhere.[^14]

## LDS Relevance

Latter-day Saints reserve sinlessness to Christ alone and do not venerate Mary as immaculate, so Hansen has no confessional stake in the *positive* dogma — his interest is the *method* (apostolicity as the test, tradition read historically). Note the contrast with a *different* LDS line of attack in [[The Mary Problem for Catholic Theism]]: there Hayden Carroll *grants* the Immaculate Conception in order to weaponize it against Catholic theism (if God can make a sinless free person once, why not always?). The two LDS strategies are in tension — one denies Mary's sinlessness, the other provisionally concedes it — but they share the target: the coherence and apostolicity of the developed Roman Marian system.
