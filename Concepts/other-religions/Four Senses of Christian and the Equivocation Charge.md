# Four Senses of Christian and the Equivocation Charge

## The Claim
[[Devin Barnes|Devin]] argues that "Are Latter-day Saints Christians?" is only tractable once "Christian" is split into four distinct senses, because the critic wins only by **equivocating** between them — proving that the LDS fail one sense and quietly concluding they fail *the* term.[^1]

- **Descriptive** — one who worships Jesus Christ as divine Lord and Savior. Latter-day Saints clearly qualify.
- **Historical** — a movement that emerged from and claims continuity with the Christian tradition. They clearly qualify.
- **Confessional / creedal** — one who affirms the ecumenical creeds. Devin *concedes* the LDS are not creedal Christians.
- **Salvific** — one who is actually saved. Devin locates this "between each person and God," not a label a debater can assign.

The distinction disarms the negative case: showing the LDS are non-creedal (the confessional sense) does nothing to the descriptive or historical senses, so a blanket verdict of "not Christian" trades on sliding between definitions.

## Evidence
- The split tracks the concession-and-relocate pattern documented in [[Are Latter-day Saints Christians]] and [[Latter-day Saints Are Christians Just Not Creedal Christians]]: grant "non-creedal," keep "Christian," and move the real fight to the *content* of God and gospel.
- It pairs with [[The Creeds Cannot Be the Neutral Baseline - The Circularity of Creedal Disqualification]]: if disqualification runs through the confessional sense alone, and the creeds are not a neutral baseline, the equivocation is exposed rather than resolved.
- Devin's positive definition leans on orthopraxy — the dictionary ("believes in and follows the teachings of Jesus Christ") and Jesus's own mark of discipleship, to "love one to another" — see [[Christian Means Belonging to Christ by Doing the Fathers Will]].

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
Daniel (the negative) replies that the four senses are not equal: the **confessional / regenerate** sense is the historically and biblically *governing* one, so failing it is failing the term, not equivocating. On his view "Christian" has always meant regenerate-and-orthodox, and the descriptive sense ("worships someone named Jesus") is too thin to be the real question, since it would enfranchise any group that invokes Christ's name. Devin's rejoinder is that this merely *asserts* the confessional sense as primary — the very point in dispute — which is what [[The Creeds Cannot Be the Neutral Baseline - The Circularity of Creedal Disqualification]] denies.
