# An Impassible God Cannot Value the Worshiper

## The Claim
[[Brian Holdsworth]] argues that changelessness is a perfection: "between those two, the one who never changes is greater than the one who does or has changed in the past."[^1] The Ward Radio panel presses the entailment they find intolerable. If God by definition cannot be affected by anything external — if nothing a creature does can improve, diminish, please, or grieve him — then, the panel glosses, "what makes God so awesome and worth worshiping is that you have zero value to him."[^2] The dispute thereby sharpens to a single question: **what makes God worthy of worship?** The Thomist answer grounds worship in **metaphysical ultimacy** (justice requires giving honor to that from which you receive your very existence); the Latter-day Saint answer grounds it in God's **freely chosen virtue** and his actual power to save — "virtue is the product of choice, not metaphysical nature."[^3]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Impassibility entails the worshiper has no value — rate: strong (states the classical entailment, if pointedly).** The panel's climax: on a God whose "state cannot be reliant upon any other thing outside of himself," nothing you do "affect[s] God whatsoever," so "there is nothing about you that God can find valuable."[^2] This is the reactive-impassibility doctrine catalogued in [[God Is Impassible - Acting Without Being Acted Upon]], read as a liability rather than a perfection. *Argument weight: primary.*
2. **The Thomist ground of worship: metaphysical debt — rate: strong (fair statement of the Catholic position).** Jacob relays it accurately: for "Thomist Catholics... everything receives its existence from God, and therefore every creature is absolutely dependent on God, and justice requires giving honor to the one from whom you receive everything" — so "God is worthy of worship not because of what he does for us... but because of his nature."[^4]
3. **The LDS ground: chosen love, not nature — rate: strong.** "Why is somebody worthy of our honor and respect? It's because of their relationship to us... their choices, their actions." God "actually has the ability" to do otherwise but "chooses always to love and to do what's right — and that's why he's worthy of worship."[^5]
4. **The virtuous-robot analogy — rate: strong (illustration).** "If I have a robot that goes around and does nice things for everybody all day, is that a virtuous robot? No — not even if it does it better than any other human possibly could... because it can't do otherwise."[^3]
5. **The *Lectures on Faith* criterion — rate: moderate (LDS-internal standard).** Instead of "the greatest conceivable being," the standard is "the bare minimum attributes that God would need in order for him to be a being worthy of us putting faith in him — and it's being able to carry out the act of our salvation."[^6] To be worship-worthy "you have to be a personal being with agency" possessing "the fullness of virtue."[^7]
6. **The "eternal law" reply to 'God is subject to something more fundamental' — rate: moderate.** To the objection that a God dependent on eternal law cannot be ultimate, the panel answers that "eternal law is not an agent in the world; it is simply the fabric of reality," and "to be powerful you have to have agency."[^8] What classical theism reifies and worships as "God" — the impersonal, part-less, passion-less base of reality — Latter-day Saints identify as *eternal law*: "what they call God, we call the eternal law."[^9]

## Best Quotes
> "What makes God so awesome and worth worshiping is that you have zero value to him. There is nothing about you that God can find valuable." [^2]

> "Virtue is the product of choice, not metaphysical nature." [^3]

> "Instead of what is the greatest conceivable being, it's what is the bare minimum attributes that God would need in order for him to be a being worthy of us putting faith in him. And it's being able to carry out the act of our salvation." [^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**The strongest opposing case.** Impassibility is not coldness. The classical tradition insists God *is* love — but as pure, unchanging **act**, not a reactive emotion he is buffeted into (see [[God Is Impassible - Acting Without Being Acted Upon]]). A God whose inner state could be improved or worsened by creatures would be *dependent* on them, and so not the uncaused source of all — impassibility therefore *protects* divine perfection rather than emptying it, and the "you have zero value" gloss is a caricature: creatures have immense value to themselves and to one another under God's changeless love, even if they add nothing to an already-infinite plenitude. Worship as justice owed to the source of one's being (Aquinas) is a serious moral claim, not mere metaphysical bookkeeping. And "the unchanging is greater" tracks the intuition that a *perfect* being cannot improve, since any departure from perfection is a change for the worse (see [[Divine Immutability - God Cannot Change]]).

**The LDS response.** The panel bites the bullet: a being who *cannot* be moved cannot love responsively, and "love that cannot be affected" is not what the word means in any other relationship. Worthiness of worship is read not off metaphysical rank but off **character freely enacted** — a robot that does good by necessity is not virtuous, and neither is a God who "always loves" only because his nature leaves him no alternative.[^3] The Restoration's criterion (the *Lectures on Faith*) is not "that than which nothing greater can be conceived" but the being who actually possesses the attributes needed to save, exercised by choice, which is why he is worth trusting.[^6] To the charge that a God "subject to eternal law" cannot be ultimate, the reply is that eternal law is no rival *agent* — it is the impersonal fabric of reality, not a competing deity — so worship is reserved for the personal Father who freely acts within it, not for the metaphysical substrate.[^8][^9] This is the impassibility half of the broader greatness dispute in [[Greatest Means Fundamental Not Morally Best (The Mormon God Showdown)]]; the claim that classical impassibility is a post-biblical import is argued in [[Divine Impassibility Is Classical-Theist Heresy]] and [[Classical Theism Cannot Be Reconciled With the Bible]].
