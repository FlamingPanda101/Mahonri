# Greatest Means Fundamental Not Morally Best (The Mormon God Showdown)

## The Claim
[[Brian Holdsworth]] (in *The Mormon God Problem*) stages a "straight-up showdown, head-to-head stats comparison" between the God of classical/Nicene Christianity — the Trinity, "eternal, uncreated, uncaused, unmoved" — and the LDS Heavenly Father, "an exalted man," and asks "which is greater and which is more consistent."[^1] Opening from Anselm's definition of God as "that than which nothing greater can be conceived," he concludes the classical God wins by definition.[^2]

The Ward Radio panel's central response is **not** to deny that God must be "the greatest," but to expose an equivocation on the word. On Holdsworth's usage, *greater* means ontological **fundamentality** — position at the bottom of the causal chain, the uncaused cause — **not** moral goodness. The panel presses two points: (a) that metric is a Greek/Aristotelian presupposition Latter-day Saints do not share, so the "showdown" is decided by whichever contestant is allowed to write the finish line; and (b) the greatness proper to a *God worthy of worship* is the greatness of an **agent**, measured by the righteous use of agency, which ontological priority does not track at all.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The showdown and its stakes — rate: strong (accurate steelman).** Holdsworth: it "does actually matter because we're describing our object of worship," and the comparison pits the "blessed Trinity, eternal, uncreated, uncaused, unmoved" against "heavenly father, who is an exalted man."[^1] The panel grants the framing is coherent and worth answering rather than dismissing it. *Argument weight: primary.*
2. **"Greater" means fundamentality, not moral quality — rate: strong (the pivotal clarification).** Luke and Jacob repeatedly warn that Latter-day Saints misread the argument: "he's not saying greatest in like your own subjective moral qualities... He's talking about it in terms of fundamentality."[^3] Jacob, steelmanning: "What he means by greatest isn't moral quality... he means fundamentality in the chain of being."[^4] This is the same definitional engine catalogued in [[Perfect-Being Theology Reads the Bible Through a Greek Lens]].
3. **It is Aristotle's prime mover / uncaused cause — rate: strong (intellectual history).** The panel identifies the machinery: "isn't this all basically just Aristotle's old prime mover? That's exactly what it is," an infinite regress terminated by an unmoved, unaffectable first cause.[^5] See [[Unmoved Mover Reshaped God the Father]].
4. **The simplicity rider forces one God — rate: moderate.** Because only one thing can be the fundamental cause of everything else, "that's also why God has to be one... you can only have one thing that is the cause of all the other things in their model."[^6] The classical inference is stated positively in [[Divine Unity - There Can Be Only One God]].
5. **The metric is presupposed, not neutral — rate: strong (the core rebuttal).** KJ: "How could the Mormon god ever possibly hope to compete in this race... where the rules have been set up by one of the participants?" Jacob: "It's like I drew a picture and how could the picture you draw ever possibly come close to being the picture that I drew?"[^7] Because a god derived from philosophy will always score better on a philosophical test, "that doesn't mean it's true — it just means the assumptions... naturally lead to the conclusion that you want."[^8]
6. **Agent-greatness is measured by agency, not contingency — rate: strong (the reductio).** "It would be like saying Adam and Eve have to be the two greatest humans ever... because ontologically every other human is contingent upon Adam and Eve. And everybody knows that argument doesn't make sense, because the way to measure greatness in agents is by the use of that agency, not by ontological or contingent dependence on other things."[^9]
7. **Latter-day Saints already reject the ontological argument — rate: moderate (concession from the other side).** The panel relays a Catholic interlocutor's verdict on Holdsworth's video: "Mormons already reject the ontological argument, so using that to compare theisms is just preaching to the choir."[^10]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**The strongest opposing case.** The classical theist answers that the "equivocation" charge misfires, because for perfect-being theology fundamentality and moral perfection are not rival measures but the *same* perfection under two descriptions: goodness and being are convertible (a thing is good to the degree it is fully actual), so the purely-actual uncaused source of all being is *thereby* maximally good — see [[Divine Attributes Follow From Pure Actuality]]. Nor is the "you set the rules" complaint decisive: *some* definition of God is unavoidable, and the classical theist argues his is reached by reason *from the world* (motion, contingency, causal finitude), not merely stipulated. A god who is one contingent being among many really would reopen the atheist's "who made God?" — which is exactly why the classical camp thinks the showdown is a fair test of explanatory adequacy, not a rigged one (see [[Mormonism Rejects Classical Theism So the God Objection Bites]]).

**The LDS response.** The convertibility of being and goodness is *precisely* the disputed Greek axiom — assuming it to answer the objection begs the question. Measure the greatness of a *person* (an agent) and ontological priority drops out: Adam and Eve are ontologically prior to every human, yet no one calls them the two greatest, most worship-worthy humans, because agent-greatness is righteous agency exercised, not causal antecedence.[^9] The LDS God is "greater" in the sense that matters for worship — perfect in *chosen* love and able to save — while being an embodied, exalted person ([[Doctrine and Covenants 130.22|D&C 130:22]]); Holdsworth scores that embodiment as *lesser* only because he has already fixed "greatness" as fundamentality. And since Latter-day Saints reject the ontological argument's premises outright ([[Perfect-Being Theology Reads the Bible Through a Greek Lens]]), deploying it against them is, as even a Catholic reader of the video conceded, "preaching to the choir."[^10] The worship-worthiness half of this dispute — whether an unaffectable God can be worth worshiping at all — is developed in [[An Impassible God Cannot Value the Worshiper]].
