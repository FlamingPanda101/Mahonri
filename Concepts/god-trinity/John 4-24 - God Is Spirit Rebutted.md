# John 4:24 — "God Is a Spirit" Rebutted

## The Claim (Objection Rebutted)
[[John 4.24|John 4:24]] — "God is a Spirit" — disproves the LDS teaching that God the Father has a body of flesh and bone. As one soundbite has it, "you guys say that he has a body of flesh and bone … your view is incompatible with scripture."[^4]

## The one-line answer
"Yeah, God is spirit. He also has a body." A Ward Radio panel offers this quip as the in-group short answer — attributed uncertainly, the speaker cannot recall whether Spencer W. Kimball or Joseph Fielding Smith said it — then supplies the fuller responses below.[^5]

## The Rebuttal

### 1. Context: the woman at the well, about *where* to worship
Jesus is answering the Samaritan woman, who is barred from the Jerusalem temple. His point is that God can be worshipped **anywhere, in spirit** — not that God is exclusively immaterial.

> "He's like, 'God's a spirit and he'll be worshiped in spirit. You can pray to God. You can worship God wherever you need to.'" [^1]

Reading the prior verse confirms it: John 4:23 — "the true worshippers shall worship the Father in spirit and in truth" — frames verse 24 as being about *inward*, new-covenant worship replacing outward law-of-Moses ritual, "not talking about his physical qualities."[^6] Critics "hyper-focus on one word or one phrase in a verse and completely ignore the rest."[^7]

### 2. The Greek is qualitative, not possessive
The verse was written in Greek; *pneuma* here is qualitative.

> "God is a spirit is pneuma... which does not have any — it's qualitative. It's not possessive. So what this is essentially saying is God is spirit in the same way that God is love or God is truth." [^2]

"God is spirit" parallels "God is love" / "God is truth" — it characterizes how he is worshipped, not a metaphysical denial of embodiment. The panel underlines that the lexicon is *not* LDS: a Blue Letter Bible / Strong's entry glosses *pneuma* broadly ("breath, blast, or a breeze … the rational soul … divine"), so "God is spirit ⇒ formless, substanceless, no body" is "an incorrect interpretation, or at least … not totally fair or accurate to the original."[^8] And when a critic leans on "God is spirit" (no article) versus other texts that read "a spirit," that is a translation artifact — Koine Greek has no indefinite article.[^9]

### 3. Latter-day Saints affirm God has a spirit *and* a body
Just as humans are physical beings with a spirit, the Father has a glorified body of flesh and bone *and* a spirit; our spirit is what communicates with God. The verse encourages spiritual worship, it does not disembody the Father [^3].

### 4. Scripture itself calls the embodied Christ a "spirit"
The strongest consistency check: the same Bible calls the risen, embodied Christ "a quickening spirit" ([[1 Corinthians 15.45-46|1 Corinthians 15:45]]). If "God is spirit" proves God is bodiless, the same rule would make the resurrected Christ bodiless — which Trinitarians deny. So biblical "spirit"-language does not entail bodilessness. Full argument: [[The Risen Christ Is Called a Spirit Yet Is Embodied]].[^10]
