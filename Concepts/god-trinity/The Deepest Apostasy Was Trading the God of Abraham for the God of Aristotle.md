# The Deepest Apostasy Was Trading the God of Abraham for the God of Aristotle

## The Claim
Thoughtful Faith argues that the *strongest* evidence for the Great Apostasy is not the loss of centralized priesthood authority — that was only the **trigger**. The apostasy *itself* was the doctrinal corruption that followed once prophetic authority was gone, and its deepest instance is the **replacement of the biblical God with the God of Greek philosophy**: "What if the God of Abraham became the God of Aristotle?"[^1] Run classical theism to its conclusion, the video says, and "you don't just end up with a different explanation of God. You end up with a different God."[^2] This flips the standard Evangelical charge that Latter-day Saints worship "a different God/Jesus" (cf. [[Different Jesus Both Uncharitable and Precisely True]]) back onto creedal Christianity.

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Authority-loss was the trigger; doctrinal corruption was the apostasy (rating: moderate; framing)
The video's opening move distinguishes cause from effect: "the loss of centralized apostolic authority by itself is not apostasy. It's what triggered the apostasy. The apostasy itself was the corruption of the doctrines and practices of the church, which inevitably followed the loss of central prophetic priesthood authority."[^3] On this framing, without living prophets to correct drift, Christians "slowly adopted a concept of God that came not from prophets, apostles, or Jesus Christ, but from Greek philosophy."[^1] *Argument weight: primary — it is the thesis.* Compare the divine-family version of the same claim in [[Loss of the Divine Family Is the Core Apostasy]].

### 2. The imported God is Aristotle's, not Abraham's (rating: strong; intellectual history)
[[Aristotle]] reasoned — by "pure philosophy," not revelation — to an ultimate cause that is "uncaused, unchanging, unaffected, completely self-sufficient."[^4] [[Thomas Aquinas]] then gave it the vocabulary of *actus purus* ("pure actuality"), "the sheer act of to be itself," and divine simplicity, where "God's very essence is his existence."[^5] The video's point: "none of that language comes from scripture... Pure act, unmoved mover, absolute simplicity, these are not biblical categories. These are Greek philosophical categories."[^6] This is the same mechanism catalogued in [[Unmoved Mover Reshaped God the Father]] and [[Greek Philosophy Corrupted the Christian Concept of God]].

### 3. Scripture gets reinterpreted to fit the philosophy, not the reverse (rating: moderate; methodological)
Once God is *defined* as the impassible Absolute, "the Bible says one thing and the philosophical framework says another. And then scripture gets reinterpreted until it fits the philosophy."[^7] The Bible's God "speaks... rejoices... grieves... becomes angry... forgives... enters covenants... responds to people... acts in history" — a "loving heavenly Father" (e.g., the LORD who "repented of the evil," [[Exodus 32.14|Exodus 32:14]]) — yet classical theology says he only *appears* to do these things.[^8] This is the order-of-operations reversal argued in [[Perfect-Being Theology Reads the Bible Through a Greek Lens]].

### 4. The creeds locked the error in (rating: weak-moderate; psychological/institutional)
The video argues creedal Christians cannot easily disown the philosophical God because "for centuries and centuries, this conception of God has been written right into their creeds," and to call the creeds wrong "would be to admit... that an apostasy happened."[^9] *Argument weight: tertiary — a motive-explanation, not evidence for the thesis; presented candidly as such.*

### 5. The Restoration recovers the God-concept, not just authority (rating: moderate; LDS-internal)
The resolution: [[Joseph Smith]] "didn't simply restore priesthood authority. He restored our understanding of God. A personal God. A relational God... A God who looks remarkably similar to the God found in scripture" — an embodied Father of "body, parts, and passions" ([[D&C 130.20-21|D&C 130:22]]).[^10] See [[The Intuitive Personal God Points Back to the Family of Heaven]].

## Best Quotes
> "What if the God of Abraham became the God of Aristotle? What if the God who genuinely relates became the God who cannot be affected?" [^1]

> "When you follow classical theism all the way to its conclusion, you don't just end up with a different explanation of God. You end up with a different God." [^2]

> "It didn't merely lose the apostolic keys... It lost the God of the Bible and replaced him with the God of Aristotle." [^11]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**The strongest opposing case.** The classical theist denies any "trade" occurred. (a) Impassibility, simplicity, and immateriality are not Aristotle smuggled in but *conclusions the church drew from scripture itself* — "I AM" ([[Exodus 3.14|Exodus 3:14]]), "God is spirit" (John 4:24), "I change not" (Malachi 3:6), "no variableness" (James 1:17) — with the biblical "passions" read as accommodative anthropopathism, not literal mood-shifts in God. (b) [[Thomas Aquinas]] adopted Aristotle's mover precisely because he judged it *consonant with* revelation, so scripture and reason converge rather than compete. (c) The "Hellenization = corruption" thesis (Harnack's) is itself contested scholarship; many historians hold the Fathers *critiqued and transformed* Greek categories rather than capitulating — the vault records this rebuttal in [[Hellenization Was Not Corruption of an Original Pure Church]]. (d) The LDS alternative is not metaphysically innocent: an embodied, in-time, passible God raises its own problems (how is such a being omnipresent, or the necessary ground of all contingent things?), so the dispute is between two doctrines of God, not between "philosophy" and "the plain text."

**The LDS response.** The reply is not to deny that God is ultimate or self-existent but to *relocate* the answer to "what grounds God?" from divine *simplicity* to divine *self-existence*: the Father is the unoriginate, necessarily-divine person who was never composed or created (cf. [[Divine Unity - There Can Be Only One God]], [[Divine Simplicity - God Has No Parts of Any Kind]] for the classical alternative). The decisive move is exegetical: the Son's genuine embodiment and the Old Testament's grieving, relenting, covenant-making Yahweh are too central to the canonical narrative to dissolve into metaphor; the honest fork is *which* doctrine of God the biblical data actually support — and the "different God" verdict cuts against whichever side has re-described the God of the text to satisfy an outside axiom.
