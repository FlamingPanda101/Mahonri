# Formed the Spirit of Man Implies Pre-Existing Material Not Ex Nihilo

## The Claim (Objection Rebutted)
In the Las Vegas debate [[Sam Shamoun]] pressed [[Zechariah 12.1|Zechariah 12:1]] — the LORD "**formeth the spirit of man within him**" — as biblical proof that God *creates* human spirits, and therefore (with the ex-nihilo tradition) creates them out of nothing. Asked what passage says spirits are created, he cited it directly: "Zechariah 12:1, if you want to open it up."[^1] The Latter-day Saint rebuttal is that the verse's own verb defeats the ex-nihilo reading.

## The Rebuttal

### 1. "Form" (yāṣar) means shaping existing material (the central point)
The Hebrew verb is **יָצַר** (*yāṣar*) — to *form/fashion*, the potter's act on clay. Forming presupposes something to form. So "formeth the spirit of man" no more implies creation from nothing than a potter "forming" a pot implies the clay was conjured from nothing. As the guests put it: "formed" is "not create out of nothing" — the spirit is fashioned from **intelligence**, the eternal substrate.[^2] **Evidence rating: Strong; Argument weight: Primary.**

### 2. Same verb, obvious pre-existing stuff: Adam from dust
The decisive parallel is [[Genesis 2.7|Genesis 2:7]]: "the LORD God **formed** (*yāṣar*) man of the dust of the ground." Here *yāṣar* unmistakably operates on a pre-existing material (dust). The guests note it is "that same Hebrew word to form that Adam was formed out of pre-existing."[^2] If *yāṣar* in Genesis 2:7 works with a substrate, *yāṣar* in Zechariah 12:1 does too — so the verse Shamoun offered *for* creation *ex nihilo* actually supports **organization from pre-existing material**, the LDS view of spirits formed from eternal intelligence.

### 3. A same-milieu counter-verse: Wisdom 11:17
When Shamoun separately produced [[2 Maccabees 7.28|2 Maccabees 7:28]] ("God made them of things that were not") for *ex nihilo*, the guests answered afterward with [[Wisdom 11.17|Wisdom 11:17]], from the same apocryphal corpus: God's hand "made the world of matter without form" (Greek *ex amorphou hylēs*, "out of formless matter"). One apocryphal creation text is thereby answered by another that explicitly affirms creation from formless matter — see [[2 Maccabees 7.28 Does Not Teach Creation Ex Nihilo]].[^3]

### 4. Ex nihilo is the later, Hellenized reading
The guests characterize the ex-nihilo interpretation of these texts as a product of "Hellenistic influence... Greek philosophy and Greek religion seeping into the old church."[^4] This matches the broader case that "create" (*bārā'*) meant *organize/order* and that creation *ex nihilo* is a late-2nd-century development — see [[Bara Means Organize and Ex Nihilo Is a Second-Century Invention]] and [[Creation Ex Nihilo Is Not Biblical]].

## Best Quotes
> "So formed could be — not create out of nothing, right? ... that same Hebrew word to form that Adam was formed out of pre-existing." [^2]

> "The wisdom of Solomon 11:17... says thy almighty hand that made the world of matter without form, which is completely a counter to what he was mentioning before." [^3]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **Verbs are not decisive by themselves.** A defender of *ex nihilo* will say lexical range does not settle theology: *yāṣar* is a general verb for divine making and can be used loosely of any origination; Zechariah 12:1's point is God's sovereign authorship of the human spirit, not a metaphysics of raw materials. Genesis 2:7 uses dust because a *body* is in view; a *spirit* has no obvious material, so the parallel may not carry.
- **Apocryphal texts cut both ways.** Citing Wisdom 11:17 concedes the relevance of a corpus Latter-day Saints do not treat as canonical scripture; a critic notes the guests were themselves *stumped in the moment* by 2 Maccabees 7:28 because they "don't read the Catholic version of the Bible," and only found the counter afterward.
- **The strongest LDS reply** does not rest on the single verb but on the whole pattern: multiple "creation" verbs (*bārā'*, *yāṣar*, *ʿāśāh*) used of forming/ordering, plus same-period Hellenistic-Jewish texts (Wisdom 11:17) that explicitly presuppose formless matter, plus the late emergence of *ex nihilo* as a doctrine. On that cumulative case, Zechariah 12:1 is at best neutral and at worst counter-evidence for Shamoun's use of it.
