# Gospel of Thomas Teaches Self-Knowledge as Divine Sonship

## The Claim
Gospel of Thomas logion 3 — "the kingdom is inside of you and it is outside of you; when you come to know yourselves… you will realize that it is you who are the sons of the living father; but if you will not know yourselves, you dwell in poverty" — reads, [[Jonah Barnes]] argues, like restored doctrine: **salvation is to know who you are, a child of God**; it implies a pre-mortal identity we have forgotten; and "living father" means the *resurrected* God. [^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The kingdom is interior, not a place or program.** *Argument weight: Secondary.* Against those who say "the kingdom is in the sky" or "in the sea," Jesus locates it *within*: "the kingdom is inside you." Barnes: "salvation is in your soul… the point isn't to set up a new government." [^2]
2. **Self-knowledge is the condition of salvation.** *Argument weight: Primary.* "You need to know yourself… understand where you came from and what your potential is. That is salvation — to understand you're a child of God." Failing to know yourself is itself the "poverty" the saying warns of. [^3]
3. **It implies a forgotten pre-mortal identity.** *Argument weight: Primary.* "Have we forgotten who we are? Did we pass through a veil and now we no longer remember that we are sons of the living father?" Barnes reads the summons to *re-*know oneself as presupposing a prior existence, tied to the "before we existed" sayings he says recur across the apocrypha. [^4]
4. **"Living" father means resurrected.** *Argument weight: Secondary.* Throughout Thomas, "living" denotes post-crucifixion life — "the living Jesus" is the risen Jesus who "came back and took up his body again." So "sons of the *living* father" invokes an embodied, resurrected God, not an abstract spirit. [^5]

## Best Quotes
> "When you come to know yourselves, then you will become known, and you will realize that it is you who are the sons of the living father." (Logion 3, as read on air) [^1]

> "Salvation is to understand you're a child of God… Did we pass through a veil and now we no longer remember that we are sons of the living father?" [^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **This is textbook Gnostic soteriology, not obviously LDS.** "Know yourself and you are saved; ignorance is the fall" is the classic *gnosis* pattern (self-knowledge as the saving thing) that the ancient heresiologists targeted — the panel itself jokes "maybe this is Gnostic." A critic will say Thomas here teaches salvation-by-esoteric-knowledge, which is precisely what the LDS gospel of covenant, ordinance, and grace is *not*.
- **"Sons of God" language is pervasive and generic.** Divine-sonship imagery runs through the canonical gospels and Paul ("children of God," Romans 8; "ye are gods," [[John 10-35's Unbreakable Scripture Is the Deification Text Ye Are Gods|John 10:34-35]]) without requiring pre-mortal existence. Reading Thomas 3 as pre-existence is a possible gloss, not a demonstrated meaning.
- **"Kingdom within" ≠ divinization.** "The kingdom of God is within you" also appears at Luke 17:21 and is read by most traditions as God's reign in the heart, not a claim that the hearer is ontologically divine. The distinctively LDS payoff is imported, not stated.
- **Source caution.** This is a devotional reading offered on a comedy podcast ("30 seconds of prep"), tier C — suggestive resonance, not exegetical proof. Its real strength is that Thomas's *vocabulary* (know yourself, sons of the living father, poverty vs. divine potential) is strikingly congenial to a deification theology; see [[Debate - Theosis and Deification]].
