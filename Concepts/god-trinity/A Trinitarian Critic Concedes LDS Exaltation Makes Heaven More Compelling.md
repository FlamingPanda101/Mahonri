# A Trinitarian Critic Concedes LDS Exaltation Makes Heaven More Compelling

## The Claim
In the Las Vegas debate the anti-Mormon polemicist [[Sam Shamoun]] conceded, on camera, that the Latter-day Saint vision of the afterlife — becoming like God, eternal marriage, eternal increase — is **more compelling than the Protestant heaven**, which he characterized as a "celibate bachelor forever" with no clear purpose. This is a hostile-witness admission that the LDS doctrine of exaltation ([[Human Deification in Mormonism|deification]]) answers a question — *what is heaven actually for?* — that he thinks his own tradition answers poorly.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The "what do you have to offer?" argument (primary)
Shamoun framed it as his own evangelistic experience run in reverse: "When I talk to Protestants... I say, okay, let's say I joined Protestant and I'm saved. Now what's heaven going to be like? ... You're going to be a bachelor forever. You're going to be a celibate. What's your purpose when you're in heaven? ... the Latter-day Saint view is that we can become like God. Doesn't that sound so much cooler? ... there's no incentive for me to switch to Protestantism."[^1] **Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Strong** as a recorded concession (its force is rhetorical/comparative, not a proof of doctrine).

### 2. "Mormon heaven sounding better all the time" (secondary)
Reacting to the exchange on embodiment and eternal family, Shamoun quipped that "Mormon heaven [is] sounding better than our heaven all the time."[^2]

### 3. A retracted caricature (secondary)
Shamoun also told the guests: "I thought you guys believed that. I'm sorry, I've been telling people that's what you believe and I'm not going to do that anymore" — walking back a caricature of LDS afterlife belief he had been repeating.[^3] Cf. [[Gods of Their Own Planets Is a Caricature of Exaltationist Theosis]].

### 4. The guests' framing: eternal, not created, offspring (secondary)
Pressed on "spirit babies," the guests kept returning to the point that Latter-day Saints do not believe gods *create* spirits from nothing — "spirits are eternal" — and that lurid "celestial sex" imagery is a caricature ("it's not that big of a deal... maybe, who cares"), deflating the "cult" charge while keeping the substance: exaltation is real, its mechanics are unrevealed.[^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **Appeal is not truth.** That a doctrine is *attractive* is no evidence it is *true*; a critic will say a heaven with eternal families and godhood is exactly what one would invent if one wanted converts, and "more comforting" cuts toward wishful thinking, not toward accuracy.
- **The classical answer to "what is heaven for."** Historic Christianity does have a purpose for heaven — the **beatific vision**, unending worship and union with God — which its defenders regard as the highest conceivable good; Shamoun's "bored celibate bachelor" framing is a caricature of that tradition just as "celestial sex" is a caricature of the LDS one. On this view he traded one strawman for another.
- **Concession under social pressure.** The admission comes inside the same unusually warm exchange that produced the "change of heart" (see [[Christlike Love Softened a Hostile Debater - The Las Vegas Miracle]]); a skeptic will discount an on-the-spot, good-natured concession as rapport, not considered theology. The apologetic value is therefore modest: a hostile source found LDS exaltation *appealing and less caricatured than he'd thought* — not that he judged it true.
