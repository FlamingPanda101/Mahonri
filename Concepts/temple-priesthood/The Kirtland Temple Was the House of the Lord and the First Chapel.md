# The Kirtland Temple Was the House of the Lord and the First Chapel

#type/claim

## The Claim
Reading the Joseph Smith Papers led [[Ryan Wood]] to conclude that "Kirtland Temple" is a mild **misnomer**: the building the Saints erected in Kirtland was functionally the church's **first chapel** — a "House of the Lord" for worship — not a temple in the later Nauvoo/endowment sense. The full temple concept, he argues, was still being revealed and did not arrive until Nauvoo.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The Independence plat imagined ~24 temples.** The original city plot for Independence, Missouri laid out something like **24 temples** in the center of town, each with its own distinct purpose — evidence that "temple" in the early 1830s did not yet mean the single endowment house it means today.[^2] *Evidence rating: Moderate — Wood recalls the number ("I want to say ~24") from the plat.*

2. **Kirtland lacked the defining temple features.** Unlike Nauvoo — which had a baptismal font and dedicated ordinance rooms — Kirtland "had none of that." Architecturally it was a large hall that could be **partitioned**, with a tiered set of pulpits, i.e. a meetinghouse design the church still echoes today.[^3] *Evidence rating: Moderate-to-strong — the structural contrast is well documented.*

3. **The Saints called it the "House of the Lord," not "the temple."** Contemporaries "did not call it the Kirtland Temple colloquially"; they said **House of the Lord** — the same reverent phrase Latter-day Saints still apply to an ordinary chapel.[^4] *Evidence rating: Strong — a documentary/terminological claim; the dedicatory revelation [[D&C 109]] repeatedly calls it a "house."*

4. **The point is elevating, not deflating.** Wood is explicit that he does not mean to "diminish anything": if the Kirtland House of the Lord was really the prototype chapel, then the ordinary chapel is "based on an inspired design," not a lesser-than building.[^5] *Evidence rating: N/A — theological framing.*

## Best Quotes

> "The Saints back then never used that term. They used House of the Lord … I believe it was our first chapel."[^4]

> "It's actually based on an inspired design."[^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
This is Wood's stated **opinion**, and a specialist can push back. (a) The Kirtland House of the Lord *was* revealed as more than a meetinghouse: [[D&C 109]] is a temple-dedication prayer, and the [[The Kirtland Dedication Recapitulates Christ's Final Week|dedication week]] delivered priesthood keys ([[D&C 110]]) and washings/anointings — proto-temple ordinances, not merely Sunday worship. So "just a chapel" understates it. (b) The Independence plat's many "temples" may reflect a broad early usage rather than proof Kirtland was non-temple. A fair synthesis: the *endowment* temple with fonts and ordinance rooms is a Nauvoo development, but Kirtland was already a **transitional** sacred house — genuinely a "House of the Lord" that both prefigured the modern temple and modeled the modern chapel. That reconciles Wood's insight with the building's real ordinance history rather than collapsing it into an ordinary chapel.
