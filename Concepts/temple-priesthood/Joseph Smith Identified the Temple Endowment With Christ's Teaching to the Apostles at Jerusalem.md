# Joseph Smith Identified the Temple Endowment With Christ's Teaching to the Apostles at Jerusalem

## The Claim

[[Jonah Barnes]] argues that [[Joseph Smith]] — years before any of the "forty-day" apocrypha were available to him — **independently identified** the content of the risen Lord's forty-day / Jerusalem instruction as the temple **endowment**. Two data points carry it: (1) [[D&C 95|D&C 95:8-9]] (Kirtland, 1833) commands the Saints to build a house in which the Lord will "endow… with power from on high," calls it "the promise of the Father," and says to "tarry even as mine apostles at Jerusalem" — quoting the exact Luke/Acts language back onto the Kirtland endowment; and (2) Joseph's 1843 discourse (as the hosts report it) that Christ trained the original Twelve in "the very same order of things" as Joseph's own restored ordinances. Put together, Barnes says, Joseph is filling Luke's Lacuna with temple ordinances: "when you go to the temple, you are experiencing Luke's lacuna."[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. D&C 95:8-9 reproduces the Luke/Acts pattern almost verbatim
The 1833 revelation reads: "I gave unto you a commandment that you should build a house, in the which house I design to **endow** those whom I have chosen with **power from on high**. For this is the **promise of the Father** unto you; therefore I command you to **tarry, even as mine apostles at Jerusalem**." Every load-bearing phrase — *endow, power from on high, the promise of the Father, tarry as the apostles at Jerusalem* — is lifted from [[Luke 24.49|Luke 24:49]] and [[Acts 1.1-5|Acts 1:4]]. Joseph is explicitly modeling the Kirtland endowment on what the apostles were told to wait for in Jerusalem.[^2]

> **Evidence rating:** Strong that Joseph read the Luke/Acts "tarry-and-be-endowed" language as an *endowment* pattern and applied it to the temple; the verbal overlap is exact. **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 2. Chronology: the identification predates the forty-day texts
Barnes stresses that D&C 95 came "before any of the myriad forty-day texts were discovered." The major witnesses later invoked for a secret forty-day ordinance — the Nag Hammadi library (1945), Clement's *Letter to Theodore* / Secret Mark (1958/1973), and the rest of the cluster — surfaced long after 1833/1843. So the fit between Joseph's claim and those texts is not a retrofit; the claim is prior to the evidence now marshaled for it.[^1]

> **Evidence rating:** Moderate — the dating is correct and the point is real; it establishes Joseph was *not copying* those texts, not that his reconstruction is thereby proven. **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 3. The 1843 discourse: Christ trained the apostles in "the very same order of things"
As the hosts report it, Joseph taught in 1843 that Christ, head of the church, "gave the original Twelve the power to administer in the ordinances of the gospel," and that Joseph's own divinely appointed ordinances "were designed by Christ himself, who trained the holy apostles of old to engage in the very same order of things." Barnes's gloss: "He said Christ taught his apostles the temple rituals… and my temple rituals are those temple rituals." Joseph never uses the phrase "forty days," but the identification of the restored ordinances with the apostolic ordinances is explicit.[^3]

> **Evidence rating:** Moderate — the identification is genuinely in Joseph's teaching about ordinance-continuity; "temple rituals" and the precise wording are Barnes's paraphrase of the 1843 discourse, not a verbatim Joseph quote (see steelman). **Argument weight:** Secondary.

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

A critic answers: **(a) A Restoration text cannot be its own proof.** D&C 95 shows that *Joseph* read Luke 24:49 / Acts 1:4 as pointing to an endowment and built the Kirtland ordinance on that reading — a plausible reading of "endued with power from on high" taken alone — but it does not independently establish that the first-century apostles received a *temple ordinance*; it presupposes what is to be shown. **(b) Circularity.** Using an 1833/1843 revelation to reconstruct a 1st-century practice, then citing 1st-century apocrypha to confirm the revelation, risks arguing in a circle. **(c) The 1843 wording is a paraphrase.** The extant accounts of Joseph's 1843 discourses come through clerks' notes; "the very same order of things" and especially "temple rituals" are the hosts' rendering, and should be checked against the documentary record before being pressed. **(d) Simpler reading of the pattern.** Joseph reusing the Pentecost "power from on high" language for Kirtland is exactly what one expects of a Restorationist consciously imitating the New Testament — imitation, not independent access. A Latter-day Saint reply: the chronology still bites — the imitation was made *before* the forty-day apocrypha were on the table, so the later convergence of Joseph's temple ordinances with those texts (garments, washings, anointings, new names, prayer circles) is not something he could have copied; see [[Early Christian Catacomb Art and Vestments Depict Temple-Style Ordinances]] and [[The Lost Forty-Day Teaching Points to a Suppressed Temple Endowment]].
