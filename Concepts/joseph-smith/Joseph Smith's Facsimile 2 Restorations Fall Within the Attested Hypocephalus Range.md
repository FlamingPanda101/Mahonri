# Joseph Smith's Facsimile 2 Restorations Fall Within the Attested Hypocephalus Range

## The Claim
A standard criticism is that the missing portions of [[Facsimile 2]] were simply *invented* by Joseph Smith (or his engraver, Reuben Hedlock), so the published facsimile is a fabrication and its explanations worthless. Egyptologist [[John Gee]] concedes the object was genuinely damaged and reconstructed — but argues that **every restored element falls within the attested range of real hypocephali**. The solar boat, the multi-headed central figure, and the upright orientation each match documented parallels; if Joseph were inventing freely, his choices would not keep landing on genuine Egyptian forms. Gee calls the orientation "another thing Joseph Smith got right."[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Facsimile 2 was demonstrably incomplete in Joseph's day (rate: strong; B-tier, concession).** The rim inscription runs about two-thirds of the way around and then "drops out," with hieratic characters from elsewhere in the papyri inserted to fill it; the central figure was "missing the head," and the top-right quadrant was gone. Gee states the damage plainly — this is not contested.[^2]

2. **Reuben Hedlock engraved the reconstruction under severe constraints (rate: strong; B-tier + primary journals).** Facsimiles 2 and 3 carry "engraved by Reuben Hedlock"; from Joseph Smith's journals we know Hedlock did the work. He was carving into metal, filling blank space, working at reduced size, and flipping everything mirror-image — "and he has no idea what he's looking at." Gee: "under those conditions, the facsimiles are amazing." So the restoration is a known, human, 1842 engraving process, not a hidden forgery.[^3]

3. **The restored solar boat has parallels (rate: moderate; B-tier, comparanda).** Joseph put a solar bark with the sun-god Ra (crown of light) into the missing top-right quadrant. "Oddly enough you get solar barks" in other hypocephali — some with one boat, some with two. The insertion matches attested examples of the genre.[^4]

4. **The central figure's head-count is in range (rate: moderate; B-tier, comparanda).** The central figure "usually has multiple heads" — typically four; here it has two. But "there are other hypocephali where it's just two heads in the central figure." Gee's verdict: "It's in the range." A free inventor had no reason to land on an attested two-head variant.[^5]

5. **The counterfactual cuts against fabrication (rate: moderate; B-tier, argument).** "If Joseph is just pulling stuff out of thin air, he can put anything... Why not put the one with the deceased before the three gods?" That the restorations repeatedly match real hypocephalus motifs — rather than arbitrary or impossible scenes — is what one expects if he (or Hedlock) was reconstructing toward the genre, not confabulating.[^4]

6. **The upright orientation matches how Egyptians placed them — "another thing Joseph Smith got right" (rate: strong; B-tier, Egyptology).** Egyptologists reorient hypocephali to put the cow figure right-side-up at the top, and "the places where they've put one of these at the head of the mummy is our best indication of the correct orientation." Facsimile 2 is oriented that way — "another thing that Joseph Smith got right, or Reuben Hedlock," because "that's actually the way... the Egyptians oriented these things."[^6]

**Implication.** The reconstruction of Facsimile 2 is real and admitted, but it is bounded by the attested corpus. "Joseph fabricated the missing parts" overstates: the restored elements are documented hypocephalus forms, and at least the orientation is a positive hit.

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The strongest critical reply concedes the parallels but relocates the burden: "within the range of the genre" is a low bar. A hypocephalus corpus that is *customizable and never identical* (see [[The Hypocephalus Is a Passport Through the Gates of the Afterlife]]) will contain *some* parallel for almost any plausible motif, so matching the range is weak evidence that Joseph restored the *correct original*. A boat, two heads, and an upright orientation are common enough that landing on them is unremarkable; and the truly diagnostic material — the figure *identifications* Joseph captions — is unaffected by whether the drawing was plausibly reconstructed. The LDS reply is modest and stated as such by Gee: this shows the restorations are "defensible" and "in the range," and the orientation is a genuine hit, which is enough to defeat the strong charge of free fabrication even if it does not prove the original read as Joseph said.
