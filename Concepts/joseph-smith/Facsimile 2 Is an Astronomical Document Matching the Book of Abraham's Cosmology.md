# Facsimile 2 Is an Astronomical Document Matching the Book of Abraham's Cosmology

## The Claim
If Joseph Smith simply invented meanings for [[Facsimile 2]], "this can mean anything." Yet he consistently read it as **astronomical** — his journals call it "the astronomical cut" — and Egyptologist [[John Gee]] confirms that hypocephali genuinely carry astronomical, solar, and creation associations that match the [[Book of Abraham]]'s cosmology (Kolob, the sun, the creation; [[Abraham 3.2-3|Abraham 3]]). Three specific figures Joseph tied to the sun, the four quarters of the earth, and the firmament each have real Egyptological support for those exact themes. Because the interpretations are *not* constant across Egypt, no one can say Joseph was "totally wrong" or "totally right" — but the astronomical register he claimed is demonstrably there.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Joseph and his contemporaries filed Facsimile 2 under astronomy (rate: strong; primary + B-tier).** In Joseph Smith's journals Facsimile 2 "is always the one on astronomy" — "the astronomical cut." Gee adds, "I have a whole article on that" — the association of hypocephali with astronomy is real, not a Joseph Smith novelty.[^2]

2. **It maps onto the Book of Abraham's own outline (rate: moderate; B-tier, textual).** The end of Abraham chapter 1 outlines the book: "first the creation and then the planets and the stars." Facsimile 2 (the astronomical piece) "would come after the creation" in that outline — but the Book of Abraham breaks off at chapter 5 (the creation, not even reaching Adam and Eve), so "we don't have" the section that would have explained Facsimile 2. The facsimile sits where the astronomy belongs, in a text whose astronomical chapter (Abraham 3) names Kolob.[^3]

3. **The cow (figure 5) genuinely carries a solar/creation meaning (rate: strong; B-tier, Egyptology).** Joseph read a solar connection here. Egyptologically the identification varies by locale — examples from Akhmim equate the cow with Hathor, those from Thebes (the provenance of this papyrus) with Mehet-weret — "but the interpretation of these is not constant in Egypt." Still, "there absolutely is a solar connection": the fullest form is "the great cow who gave birth to the sun," a creation myth in which the cow rises from the primordial waters and bears the sun on her horn; the Esna astronomical text lets it be "both the earth and the sun at the same time." [[Stephen Smoot]]'s dissertation treats part of this, and the "great cow gave birth to the sun" reading is also attested at the Ptolemaic temple gates at Karnak.[^4]

4. **Figure 6 (the four quarters) matches the four winds/corners (rate: moderate; B-tier, artifact).** Joseph explained figure 6 as "this earth in its four quarters." A sarcophagus in the Cairo Museum identifies those characters with the **four winds**, "being associated with different parts of the earth" — the four corners. (Ancient Egyptians were not consistent about *which* figure maps to *which* corner, but together they represent the four quarters.) A BYU Studies *Guide to the Book of Abraham* article (Gee, Muhlestein, Smoot, Thompson) treats figure 6.[^5]

5. **Figure 4 (the firmament) has an Edfu parallel (rate: moderate; B-tier, Egyptology).** Joseph read figure 4 as "signifying expanse of the firmament." At the temple of Horus at Edfu, an Egyptologist identified Horus spreading his wings in the creation myth "as signifying the firmament or the heavens above" the temple — a neat parallel for Joseph's firmament reading.[^6]

6. **The counterfactual (rate: moderate; B-tier, argument).** "If Joseph Smith is just making this all up... he can make it into any whatever fantastical thing. He happens to know it's involved with astronomy, with creation, with the sun, the moon, and the stars" — and "demonstrably we have those connections." A free inventor had no reason to keep landing on the solar/creation/astronomical themes that Egyptology independently attaches to hypocephali.[^7]

**Implication.** Facsimile 2's genre carries exactly the astronomical–solar–creation content Joseph Smith claimed for it, and that content matches the Book of Abraham's cosmology. This does not prove any single caption "correct" against the shifting Egyptological glosses, but it defeats the notion that Joseph's astronomical reading is arbitrary.

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The critic's best reply leans on Gee's own candor. (a) **Multivalence cuts both ways**: if the cow can be Hathor *or* Mehet-weret, earth *or* sun depending on locale, then a "solar hit" for Joseph is nearly unfalsifiable — a broad theme like "astronomy/creation" will match almost any funerary-cosmological artifact, so the fit is cheap. (b) **Post-hoc selection**: Joseph's specific captions (Kolob = "first creation," particular gods, particular governing planets) are *not* what Egyptologists read; the defense retreats to general themes precisely where the specific claims fail. (c) **Interested comparanda**: the Edfu, Esna, and Karnak parallels are assembled by LDS Egyptologists, and Gee repeatedly concedes "we wish we knew more" and "we really can't be overconfident one way or another." The honest steelman: the *general* astronomical register is real, but generality is what makes it weak evidence for Joseph's *particular* readings. The LDS reply is that the argument is deliberately modest — a convergence of independent themes, not a decoded translation — and that the same interpretive humility ([[Egyptologists Disagree Across Time and Place on What a Drawing Means]]) which blocks a confident "Joseph is wrong" also blocks a confident "Joseph is right."
