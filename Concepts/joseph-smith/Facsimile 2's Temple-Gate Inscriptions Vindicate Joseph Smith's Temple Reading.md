# Facsimile 2's Temple-Gate Inscriptions Vindicate Joseph Smith's Temple Reading

## The Claim
Critics treat Joseph Smith's Facsimile 2 explanations as failed translations. Egyptologist [[John Gee]] points out that the *one* block of text on [[Facsimile 2]] that can actually be read — the middle register on the left — is exactly the block Joseph explained as pertaining to "the holy temple of God," and that this temple framing has **genuine ancient parallels**: the phrasing occurs on Ptolemaic **temple gates at Karnak**, and such gate-inscriptions appear literally on *gates* — fitting a document whose native function is to pass the deceased *through gates* (see [[The Hypocephalus Is a Passport Through the Gates of the Afterlife]]). Gee calls it "a bullseye for Joseph Smith," even though Joseph translated nothing.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Only one block is readable, and Joseph flagged exactly that one (rate: strong; B-tier + primary).** Almost all of Facsimile 2's text is incomplete or ungrammatical; "the only block of text we can read is that middle register on the left" (figures ~8–12). Of that block Joseph Smith wrote that if the world can find it out, "so let it be"; of the rest he wrote it "will be given in the Lord's own due time." Joseph's confidence and his deferral track the readable/unreadable divide.[^2]

2. **Joseph assigned the readable figures to the temple (rate: strong; primary source).** For figures 8–11 Joseph wrote that they "contain writings that cannot be revealed unto the world, but is to be had in the holy temple of God," with figures 9–11 "not to be revealed at the present time," and figures 12–21 to "be given in the... time of the Lord." He explicitly located this material in a *temple* context.[^3]

3. **That temple phrasing has a real Ptolemaic parallel (rate: moderate–strong; B-tier, published).** Gee: "there actually are... you can find parallels to that phrasing in the Ptolemaic temple gate at Karnak" — and adds, "I have an article on this." A host who separately attempted the left-block hieroglyphs found "the phrasing Joseph Smith gives for those explanations actually has some kind of ancient precedent," which "you actually find... in Egyptian temples."[^4]

4. **Gate-texts on a pass-through-gates amulet is a fitting hit (rate: moderate; B-tier, argument).** "If this is to help you pass through the gate, it's interesting that you actually find them on gates — gates of the temple." The convergence of (a) a hypocephalus whose function is gated passage, (b) Joseph's temple reading, and (c) real temple-*gate* inscriptions is why Gee counts it "a bullseye for Joseph Smith, even if... he doesn't translate anything."[^1]

5. **The rim inscription independently yields a temple reference (rate: moderate; B-tier, Egyptology).** Reading the surviving rim, Gee confirms "two references to the temple of the Benu bird" (the phoenix) at ancient Heliopolis — another temple connection in the part that can be read, though one damaged word remains genuinely disputed ("no real agreement on what that second word is").[^5]

**Implication.** On the only stretch of Facsimile 2 that can be read, Joseph Smith's reading is *temple*, and the Egyptological parallels for that stretch are *temple-gate* texts on an amulet built for gated passage. That is a positive convergence, not a failed translation — and it dovetails with Joseph's explicit refusal to translate the rest (see [[Joseph Smith Declined to Translate the Hypocephalus Characters of Facsimile 2]]).

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
A critic answers on three fronts. (a) **Selection**: Joseph "explained" many figures confidently and deferred others; that the deferred ones happen to be the unreadable ones can be read as convenient rather than prophetic, and calling a match on the readable block a "bullseye" over-weights one data point. (b) **Vagueness**: "to be had in the holy temple of God" is general enough that a temple-gate parallel is not a tight, falsifiable prediction — Egyptian funerary texts are pervasively temple- and afterlife-themed, so a temple hit is nearly guaranteed. (c) **Interested reading**: the parallels are drawn by LDS Egyptologists (Gee's own articles), and one key rim word remains disputed, so "temple of the Benu bird" is partly reconstruction. The steelman grants Joseph a real hit on the readable block while denying it generalizes to the contested identifications. The LDS reply is that Gee frames this precisely — a genuine but bounded convergence ("even if he doesn't translate anything"), offered as one converging line alongside the astronomical fit ([[Facsimile 2 Is an Astronomical Document Matching the Book of Abraham's Cosmology]]) and the temple-order pattern ([[The Facsimiles Follow the Temple Order of Altar Cosmos and Throne]]).
