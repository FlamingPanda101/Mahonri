# Critics Turn Emma Smith Into an Argument Not a Person

#type/rebuttal

## The Claim
Critics of Joseph Smith often enlist [[Emma Smith]] as a witness *against* him — posing as sympathetic to the wife supposedly duped by a "conniving" fraud. [[Don Bradley]] argues this rhetorical move fails on its own terms: it can only offer Emma **pity, not respect**, and it flatly contradicts her lifelong, uncompromising testimony. The apologetic point is methodological — the moment we "turn her into an argument versus a person," we stop taking the actual Emma seriously.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The co-opting move, made explicit.** Critics "represent themselves as kind of being on Emma's side," present her as "duped by this terrible conniving person, Joseph," and thereby imply "Emma's on our side." Bradley's rejoinder: "If Emma Smith were here, she would be outraged... at being used that way."[^2] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong as characterization of the rhetoric; the outrage is inference from her testimony.)*
2. **Pity versus respect.** "The best that critics can say... toward Emma is pity... But do we have pity for Emma or do we have respect for Emma?" The critical narrative — that she was "the dupe of an obvious fraud" — insults "her own intellectual and spiritual perceptions of the man she knew perhaps better than anyone else in the world."[^3] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong — a sound logical point: the "duped wife" frame requires demeaning Emma's intelligence.)*
3. **Her uncompromising testimony.** Emma "was uncompromising in her testimony that her husband was a prophet of God, that the Book of Mormon is his word, and that the restored gospel is true." Even when she questioned whether *polygamy* was right, "she did not question whether her husband's heart was right... whether he was sincere as a prophet... whether God had chosen him."[^4] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong — consistent across her recorded statements; cf. [[Emma Smith Models Faith Without Seeing]].)*
4. **She knew Joseph best.** As Joseph's wife for over seventeen years and closest companion through his prophetic career, "Emma Smith knew Joseph Smith better than anyone else," so "her perceptions of the man deserve to be taken seriously" rather than discounted whenever they are inconvenient to the critic.[^5] *(Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Strong.)*

## Best Quotes
> "Emma Smith would be outraged at being used that way. Emma Smith was uncompromising in her testimony that her husband was a prophet of God, that the Book of Mormon is his word, and that the restored gospel is true."[^2]

> "The critical narrative about Emma... is that she was the dupe of an obvious fraud. How much credit does that give to Emma and to her own intellectual and spiritual perceptions of the man she knew perhaps better than anyone else in the world?"[^3]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The strongest version of the critic's position is not that Emma was stupid but that intelligent, close witnesses are routinely mistaken about intimate partners — spouses of frauds and cult leaders often defend them sincerely, so Emma's nearness is as much a source of bias as of insight, and her testimony cannot simply settle the question of Joseph's authenticity. On this reading, invoking Emma's belief is itself an argument-from-authority that the apologist deploys no less than the critic.

The reply grants the general caution but presses the specific asymmetry Bradley names. First, the objection concedes the actual point of this note: Emma must be engaged *as a person* whose perceptions carry evidential weight, not wheeled out as a rhetorical prop for either side. Second, the "duped spouse" model has to explain away not a vague loyalty but Emma's detailed, first-hand testimony about the *translation* — content she scribed and heard dictated beyond Joseph's known capacity ([[Emma Smith Was Joseph's First Book of Mormon Scribe]]) — which is evidence, not mere devotion. Third, Emma is a *hostile-leaning* witness on the sub-question of polygamy: she resisted it and even denied it, yet never wavered on Joseph's prophetic sincerity — exactly the pattern a genuinely persuaded, clear-eyed witness would show, and the opposite of a credulous dupe. Taking Emma seriously cuts against the critic more than for him.
