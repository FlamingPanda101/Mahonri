# Why Emma Smith Denied Joseph's Polygamy

#type/rebuttal

## The Claim
In her final interview with her son [[Joseph Smith III]], [[Emma Smith]] appeared to deny that Joseph practiced polygamy at all. Against the historical consensus (which [[Don Bradley]] fully shares, having researched Joseph Smith and polygamy full-time for two years for [[Brian Hales]]) that Joseph *did* practice it, Bradley offers a **motive reconstruction**: Emma's denial is best explained not as evidence Joseph was monogamous, but as Emma following Joseph's own pattern of public denial, believing Joseph had *rejected* polygamy before his death, and acting to protect her sons from the fate that plural marriage had brought her husband.[^1] This complements the source-critical dating argument in [[Emma Smith's Polygamy Testimony Splits Before and After 1860]].

## Supporting Evidence
1. **Emma was following Joseph's own example of public denial.** Joseph treated plural marriage as secret and sacred and denied it publicly while living, telling associates "you've got to keep this secret because otherwise... people will kill me." Emma denying it publicly after his death may simply be "taking her cues from Joseph."[^2] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate-to-strong — Joseph's own secrecy is well documented; cf. [[Joseph Smith Never Issued a Blanket Denial of Polygamy]].)*
2. **She believed plural marriage had gotten Joseph killed — and wanted to protect her sons.** Shortly after the martyrdom Emma said "it was the secret things that got Joseph and Hyrum killed." Bradley connects this to the real causal chain: William Law's charges over Joseph's plural marriage to Maria Lawrence fed the *Nauvoo Expositor* crisis that led directly to Carthage. Emma "does not want to pass it to her sons and have them get killed as well."[^3] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong — Emma's "secret things" statement is on record, and the Expositor-to-Carthage chain is historically solid.)*
3. **She may have believed Joseph had abandoned polygamy before his death.** The best accounts say Joseph *burned* the original of [[Doctrine and Covenants 132|D&C 132]] shortly before going to Carthage (June 1844), possibly after asking Emma to burn it. If Emma understood that as Joseph *rejecting* the revelation — a view also held by Nauvoo stake president William Marks — she could deny Joseph "practiced polygamy" while being loyal to what she took to be his final position: that it was a false or abandoned principle she need not perpetuate.[^4] *(Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Moderate — the burning is attested but the accounts are mixed, and Emma's inference is Bradley's reconstruction.)*
4. **A respectability motive.** Emma told the Relief Society, "If we will be respectable here in Nauvoo, we can be respected every place." Polygamy was the single biggest thing 19th-century America held against the Latter-day Saints; Emma may have judged that denying it protected the reputation of the restored gospel she believed in.[^5] *(Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Moderate — Emma's quote is documented; the application is interpretive.)*

## Best Quotes
> "It was the secret things that got Joseph and Hyrum killed... She believed plural marriage had gotten her husband killed. She does not want to carry it on. She does not want to pass it to her sons and have them get killed as well."[^3]

> "Joseph was denying it publicly. Emma, if Emma denies it publicly, she's taking her cues maybe from Joseph even after he's passed away."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
A denier replies that this is special pleading: it assumes the very thing in dispute (that Joseph practiced polygamy) and then explains away Emma's first-person, repeated denial with a chain of inferred motives. If Emma really participated in the Partridge-sister sealings, why would even fear for her sons drive her to a flat denial rather than a careful silence? And the "Joseph burned the revelation / abandoned polygamy" thread, if pressed, could be turned *against* the practice's legitimacy.

The response is twofold. First, Emma's denial does not stand alone against the record — a large, interlocking body of testimony (plural wives, officiators, and pre-1860 statements by Emma herself) establishes the practice; her late denial is the datum needing explanation, not a counterweight to all the rest (see [[Emma Smith's Polygamy Testimony Splits Before and After 1860]]). Second, motive reconstruction is not special pleading when the motives are *independently documented*: Emma's own "secret things got Joseph killed" statement, the historical Expositor-to-Carthage chain, the burning accounts, and her respectability concern are all on the record. Bradley's account also refuses to make Joseph a spotless hero — [[Doctrine and Covenants 132|D&C 132]]:56 has the Lord ask Emma to "forgive my servant Joseph his trespasses" — so the reconstruction is candid about the human drama rather than sanitizing it.
