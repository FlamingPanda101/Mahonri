# Nephite Temple and Priesthood Outside the Levitical Line

## The Claim (Objection)
[[Greg (Collierville Pastor)]] argues the Book of Mormon contradicts the Old Testament and so cannot be scripture: the Nephites — descendants of **Joseph** (through Lehi and his son Jacob), *not* of **Levi** — build a temple and establish a priesthood in the Americas, outside both the Aaronic/Levitical line and God's chosen sanctuary in Zion. On the Mosaic order, "you cannot have an unauthorized temple," and "you cannot establish a priesthood unless you're a Levite... of the line of Aaron." The Book of Mormon therefore "abandoned the story that God established."[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The temple must be at God's chosen place — Zion/Jerusalem.** Greg cites 2 Chronicles 6:6 (God chose Jerusalem "that my name might be there") to argue the legitimate temple is fixed at "his holy hill in Zion," so a Nephite temple in the New World is unauthorized.[^2] See [[2 Chronicles 6.6|2 Chronicles 6:6]]. *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate* — a real feature of the centralized-cult texts, though those texts postdate Lehi's departure (see rebuttal).

2. **Priesthood restricted to Aaron's line.** Under the Law, priestly office belongs to Levi, specifically the sons of Aaron; the Nephites, of Joseph, cannot legitimately hold it. "They're not Levites... you had to be of the line of Aaron."[^3] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate.*

3. **No stated authorization in the text.** Greg presses that the Nephi narrative contains "no statement" granting authority for the temple and priesthood — it "just build[s] it."[^4] *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Weak* — an argument from silence, which the rebuttal contests directly.

## Best Quotes
> "They're not Levites, and you cannot establish a priesthood unless you're a Levite... you had to be of the line of Aaron."[^3]

> "You cannot have an unauthorized temple. God said that his temple is in Jerusalem. It's on his holy hill. It's on Mount Zion."[^2]

> "They abandoned the priesthood God established. They abandoned the temple God established. This is a departure from God's story."[^1]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
[[Hayden Carroll]]'s rebuttal is direct — divine authorization overrides the ordinary rule:

- **God authorized it by revelation.** "They were given permission from God to do so." In LDS doctrine, Nephi's vision (1 Nephi) confers the authority to build the temple and administer priesthood ordinances. "It's not illegitimate if he authorizes it."[^5] *This meets Greg's argument-from-silence head-on:* the claim is not that Nephi acted without warrant but that the warrant was direct revelation, which the narrative reports.

- **The Levitical restriction is covenant-order-specific.** Carroll concedes the key point *within its frame*: "within the Israelite confines, I absolutely agree" a non-Aaronic priesthood would be illegitimate. His contention is that God, as the lawgiver, can make exceptions outside those confines — so 2 Chronicles 6:6 describes the covenant arrangement of *that* time and place, not an absolute, permanent restriction binding a prophet God has separately commissioned.[^6]

- **The pre-Reform first-temple context (vault steelman).** The strongest scholarly support for Carroll's side is that Lehi (c. 600 BC) left Jerusalem *before* the Deuteronomistic centralization hardened, carrying an older, pre-Reform first-temple theology in which the Jerusalem monopoly and later priestly boundaries were not yet the settled norm. On that reading a Nephite temple is far less anomalous than Greg's post-exilic proof-text (Chronicles) implies. See [[Nephi Wrote as a First-Temple Priest-King]] and [[The Book of Mormon Matches Pre-Reform First Temple Theology]]. Carroll also gestures at Hebrew literary features in the Book of Mormon as positive evidence of ancient authenticity.[^7]

**Fair assessment:** Greg's objection has real force *if* the Mosaic/centralized-cult order is treated as an absolute standard binding all times and places; Carroll's rebuttal has real force *if* direct revelation can authorize exceptions and *if* Lehi predates the centralization the objection assumes. The exchange turns, as so much of the debate does, on whether God's revealed order is fixed by the closed text (Greg) or open to authorized new revelation (Carroll).
