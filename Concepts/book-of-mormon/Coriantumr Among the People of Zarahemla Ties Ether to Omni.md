# Coriantumr Among the People of Zarahemla Ties Ether to Omni

## The Claim
[[Jeff Markham]] highlights a striking cross-record connection: a single verse in the short book of Omni quietly resolves a cliffhanger left at the very end of the Jaredite record in Ether. [[Coriantumr]], the last Jaredite king, is left "as if he had no life" after his people's mutual annihilation ([[Ether 15.32|Ether 15:32]]) — the reader is not told whether he died. Only in [[Omni 1|Omni 1:21]] do we learn he survived: the people of Zarahemla had discovered "one Coriantumr," who "dwelt with them for the space of nine moons." Neither passage cross-references the other; the reader must connect them. Markham offers it as one of his [[The Book of Mormon's Undesigned Internal Consistencies Argue Against Fabrication|undesigned internal consistencies]], with the added weight that it bridges two *separate records* — the Jaredite and the Nephite — through one otherwise-idle detail.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The unresolved end of the Jaredites.** [[Ether 15.32|Ether 15:32]] closes the Jaredite civil war with Coriantumr and Shiz as the last two combatants; after beheading Shiz, "Coriantumr fell to the earth, and became as if he had no life." Read alone, the verse leaves his fate genuinely ambiguous — a natural place for a story to simply stop.[^1] *Evidence rating: Strong. Argument weight: Primary.*

2. **The one-verse resolution, a whole book away.** [[Omni 1|Omni 1:21]] records that the people of Zarahemla "gave an account of one Coriantumr, and the slain of his people. And Coriantumr was discovered by the people of Zarahemla; and he dwelt with them for the space of nine moons." The stray Jaredite survivor reached the Mulekites and lived among them nine months ("moons" being a Hebraic idiom for months) before dying — answering the Ether question the reader may not even have known to ask.[^1] *Evidence rating: Strong. Argument weight: Primary.*

3. **The link is undesigned and dictation-separated.** The Omni account sits on the small plates and, in the [[The Book of Mormon Was Translated Mosiah-First|Mosiah-first dictation order]], was dictated well before Moroni's abridgment of Ether — which appears "more than 60 modern chapters later in the dictation sequence." The nine-moons detail serves no later purpose in Omni and is never revisited; nothing in Ether 15 points forward to it. A fabricator improvising forward has no motive to plant a survivor in a throwaway Omni verse and then leave the Ether ending ambiguous so the two silently match.[^1] *Evidence rating: Moderate. Argument weight: Secondary.*

## Best Quotes
> "Did he die? No, according to an obscure verse more than 60 modern chapters later in the dictation sequence." [^1]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**Objection.** This can be read as ordinary authorial design: a writer building a Jaredite backstory would naturally invent a lone survivor to explain how the people of Zarahemla came to possess an account of the Jaredites' destruction (they later show Mosiah I a large engraved stone about Coriantumr, Omni 1:20-22). So the "coincidence" is just a plotted setup paying off.
*Response.* Grant that it *can* be plotted — but the shape of the payoff is what Markham presses: on the dictation timeline the Omni aside comes first and introduces a detail (a stranger's nine-month stay) that is never needed again, while the Ether cliffhanger is left deliberately open, so the connection lands only when a reader troubles to bridge two separate records that never cite each other. That is the signature of an undesigned coincidence rather than a signposted callback. The general naturalistic alternatives — literary genius, a borrowed source, or supernatural-but-non-divine authorship — are treated and answered in [[The Book of Mormon's Undesigned Internal Consistencies Argue Against Fabrication]]. **Note:** this is the *last Jaredite king* Coriantumr; a later Nephite-era dissenter of Zarahemla lineage also bears the name in Helaman 1, whose Hebrew etymology is discussed in [[Coriantumr Decodes as the Hebrew Title Sar-On-Tom-Ur]].

*Honest limits:* Markham presents this as one of ~120 cumulative data points on FAIR, not a standalone proof; a single shared name and fate across two records is suggestive evidence, weighted by the dictation constraints, rather than demonstration.
