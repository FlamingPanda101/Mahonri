# The Book of Mormon Teaches Universal Moral Principles Shared Across Scripture

#type/claim

## The Claim
[[The Bald Coder]] built a second, non-generative **embedding-model** pass over four scriptures (Old Testament, New Testament, Book of Mormon, Quran) and measured each verse's **cosine similarity** to natural-language "probes" drawn from named frameworks of universal morality — the **UN Universal Declaration of Human Rights**, an academic moral standard, and **Pew Research**. He reports that the Book of Mormon communicates principles such as care, equality, liberty, dignity and justice at rates comparable to the Bible, and that across all four texts the aggregate moral profiles are **at least 95% similar**. The intended conclusion: the Book of Mormon "teaches good ethical principles," and those principles are broadly shared by the world's major scriptures.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **A "care / no-harm" probe, quantified.** For the principle that "people should be cared for and no one should be harmed," the share of verses expressing the idea was New Testament **14%**, Book of Mormon and Old Testament **10%**, Quran **6.5%** — the Book of Mormon tracking the Old Testament and just behind the New.[^2] *(Evidence rating: Weak / model-generated — a share-of-verses figure from a hand-written probe, not a validated moral-content measure. Argument weight: Secondary.)*
2. **Breadth of principles tested.** The same method was run for roughly twenty named moral attributes — liberty, dignity, equality, life, justice, expression, solidarity, honesty, tolerance, civic duty, hard work, peace, generosity, obedience, independence — and "all four sacred texts score high in each of these universal principles," some ranking higher than others by attribute.[^3] *(Evidence rating: Moderate. Argument weight: Primary.)*
3. **A worked equality example across all four texts.** For "equality" the top-ranked verse in each text was — Book of Mormon: [[2 Nephi 26.33|2 Nephi 26:33]] ("he denieth none that come unto him, black and white, bond and free, male and female... all are alike unto God, both Jew and Gentile"); New Testament: [[Galatians 3.28|Galatians 3:28]] ("neither Jew nor Greek... for ye are all one in Christ Jesus"); Old Testament: [[Hosea 2.23|Hosea 2:23]] ("I will say to them that were not my people, Thou art my people"); Quran 49:13 ("O mankind, indeed we have created you from male and female... the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous").[^4] *(Evidence rating: Moderate — the verses genuinely express egalitarian themes; the model surfaced them by meaning, not keyword. Argument weight: Primary.)*
4. **Cross-text moral convergence at ≥95%.** Taking the cosine similarity of every text's full set of moral-attribute scores, "no matter which book you do or do not believe in, all four texts align in terms of morality"; the *lowest* pairwise similarity — Quran vs. Old Testament — is still 95%.[^5] *(Evidence rating: Moderate. Argument weight: Primary — this convergence, not any single text's edge, is the video's real finding.)*

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**Steelman of the skeptic.** Three fair objections. (1) *Shared morality is expected, not evidential.* A 19th-century text produced inside the Judeo-Christian moral stream will of course echo the ethics of the Bible; high moral resonance measures cultural lineage and authorial values, not divine origin — so it can't discriminate an inspired book from a devout human one. (2) *The convergence cuts against uniqueness.* If the Book of Mormon, Bible and Quran are ≥95% alike on morality, that *undermines* any claim that one uniquely carries God's ethic; the author embraces this ecumenical reading, which is a very different apologetic than "the Book of Mormon is uniquely good." (3) *Anachronistic and model-bound.* The probes are keyed to a *modern* framework (the 1948 UDHR, Pew), so a high score shows the ancient/19th-century text *resonates with* today's values, not that it grounds them; and the embedding model is itself trained on human text, so [[A Data Analysis of Scripture Is Only as Objective as Its Model]] still applies — the numbers are illustrative, not probative.

**Response (the note's own weighting).** The finding is best read modestly, exactly as the creator frames it: as a *corroboration* that the Book of Mormon invites moral living, aimed at a secular audience, not as proof of inspiration. Its strength is that a transparent, non-generative method reproduces the intuition that the book is ethically serious; its limit is that "teaches good ethics" is necessary but nowhere near sufficient for "is scripture." It belongs alongside [[Book of Mormon Evidences]] as rhetorical framing, not as evidence of historicity.
