# The Book of Mormon's Undesigned Internal Consistencies Argue Against Fabrication

## The Claim
Jeff Markham argues that the Book of Mormon contains roughly **120 "internal consistencies"** — subtle factual details planted in one passage and only redeemed dozens of chapters (weeks of dictation) later, with no contradiction. These are *undesigned coincidences*: the text does not flag them, gains no obvious literary payoff from them, and would suffer no visible harm if they were dropped — yet they interlock. Set against the documented dictation conditions ([[Joseph Smith]] producing ~270,000 words in ~60 working days with no notes, no source manuscript, face in a hat, and no revisions), Markham argues that the bookkeeping load of tracking this many latent details is unaccountable on any naturalistic "literary genius" or "automatic writing" theory.[^1][^2] He is careful to bracket this as separate from and *additional to* chiasmus, Hebraisms, and scriptural intertextuality — "this is details, factual details, built into the narrative"[^11] — and to call it a "party trick" that is a gateway to devotional study, not the point of the book.[^3]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **"He fell *again* to the earth" (the signature case).** In [[Alma 27.17|Alma 27:17]] Ammon is so overcome with joy at reuniting with the sons of Mosiah that "he fell **again** to the earth." The word *again* is only redeemed ~20 chapters earlier in [[Mosiah 27.11-13|Mosiah 27:11-13]], where Alma and the sons of Mosiah first fall to the earth in terror before the rebuking angel. The two fallings invert each other — terror then joy, the culmination of the same conversion — and nothing in Alma 27 signals the callback.[^1] *Evidence rating: Strong. Argument weight: Primary.*

2. **A reign-timeline that arithmetically closes.** Mosiah 6:4 records Mosiah beginning to reign at age 30, "about 476 years from the time that Lehi left Jerusalem." Twenty-three chapters later, Mosiah 29:46 has him die "in the thirty and third year of his reign, being sixty and three years old… five hundred and nine years from the time Lehi left Jerusalem." The numbers reconcile in three independent ways: 63 − 30 = 33 years reigned, and 509 − 476 = 33 years elapsed.[^4] *Evidence rating: Strong. Argument weight: Primary.*

3. **A king's characterization held across 14 chapters.** In Mosiah 7:10 the newly-arrived Ammon is arrested because King Limhi was outside the city walls with his guards. Only in Mosiah 21:19 do we learn the reason: "the king himself did not trust himself without the walls of the city unless he took his guards with him, fearing that he might by some means fall into the hands of the Lamanites," because Limhi's people were then in bondage to the Lamanites. The earlier scene's odd detail (a king patrolling his own perimeter under guard) is a consistent trait, not a throwaway.[^5] *Evidence rating: Moderate. Argument weight: Secondary.*

4. **Internal geography consistent across 56 chapters.** Alma 6:7 places the city of Gideon east of Zarahemla, across the river Sidon. Fifty-six chapters later, Alma 61:5 has Pahoran flee the captured capital to "the land of Gideon" to gather men — a movement that only makes sense if Gideon sits just across the river, exactly as established. Markham notes this holds regardless of which real-world map model one adopts: everyone agrees the *internal* geography (Rosenvall counted 200+ geographic descriptors) is somehow self-consistent.[^6] *Evidence rating: Strong. Argument weight: Primary.*

5. **A conversion re-told with audience-tailored variation.** Mormon narrates Alma the Younger's conversion in Mosiah 27; Alma re-tells it in his own voice to his son Helaman in [[Alma 36.6-13|Alma 36:6-13]]. Roughly ten specific details recur *in the same order* (going about to destroy the church, the angel's voice like thunder, the earth trembling, falling to the earth, three days speechless and limbless, the "gall of bitterness," calling on Jesus). Yet the accounts differ exactly as genuine re-tellings do: Alma omits the priests fasting and praying for him (a public detail) and adds intimate ones ("encircled about by the everlasting chains of death"), because he is speaking privately to his son rather than for the record. Markham likens this to the multiply-attested [[First Vision Accounts Differ by Audience Not Contradiction|First Vision accounts]] — variation by audience is a mark of authenticity, not contradiction.[^7] *Evidence rating: Strong. Argument weight: Primary.*

6. **A 400-year prophecy planted and fulfilled book-to-book.** In Alma 45:10 Alma secretly prophesies to Helaman that 400 years after Christ manifests himself the Nephites will dwindle and be destroyed. The thread is picked up by Samuel the Lamanite (Helaman 13:9) and closed by Moroni: "Behold, four hundred years have passed away since the coming of our Lord and Savior" (Mormon 8:6) — a countdown maintained across the width of the record.[^8] *Evidence rating: Moderate. Argument weight: Secondary.*

7. **The 30 Jaredite kings, reversed then unwound.** Ether 1:6-32 lists ~30 Jaredite kings in reverse genealogical order (Ether back to Jared); the narrative from Ether 6 onward then runs the same 30 *forward* in exact order, while weaving in a dozen non-royal "mingling" names without ever confusing the succession. Detailed under [[The Dictation Feat - Reversed Jaredite Kings and a Hundred Names of Christ]], to which Markham's independent treatment (and the Shiblon/Shiblom scribal-error point) has been added.[^9] *Evidence rating: Strong. Argument weight: Primary.*

**The amplifier — dictation conditions.** Markham stresses (citing [[Brian Hales]]) that the text was a "verbatim run-on sentence" of ~270,000 words with "no edits, no corrections": a slip could not be scratched out and repaired later, so every one of these latent details had to be tracked live and closed correctly, sometimes 60 chapters after being opened. On his uniform-output model the number of open details Joseph would have had to juggle at once "gets up into the 30s."[^2][^10] See [[Dictation Speed and Method Argue Against Fabrication]].

**More worked examples (Markham's FAIR series).** The same catalogue was published on FAIR in 15 parts; the concluding installment adds cases from the *shortest* books, captured in [[The Small-Plates Concluding Books Interlock With the Nephite Record]] (consecrated offices, a planted prophecy of lost inheritance, and a wars-then-peace setup in Jacob, Omni, and Words of Mormon) and [[Coriantumr Among the People of Zarahemla Ties Ether to Omni]] (a one-verse aside in Omni resolving a Jaredite cliffhanger in Ether).[^17]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

**Objection 1 — Joseph was simply a literary genius / automatic writer.** The strongest naturalistic reading is that Joseph possessed a rare gift for "automatic writing" that let him extemporize a long, self-consistent narrative. Oral traditions (epic bards, improvisers) do sustain long internally-consistent stories, so raw feats of memory are not unprecedented.
*Response (Markham):* Even granting the gift, the comparison class collapses. Tolkien needed ~12–17 years and a doctorate for a world *with* a written map and endless revision; Joseph had ~65 days, no map, no notes, no editing, and a scribe. If Joseph were uniquely capable of this, why is his entire later literary output so meagre by comparison, and why no sequel? Markham points to [[Brian Hales]]'s *Interpreter* work on the automatic-writing analogues and to [[Matt Vallejo]]'s statistical modelling ([[The Stick of Joseph - The Statistical Impossibility of the Book of Mormons Origin|Stick of Joseph — Statistical Impossibility]]) that Joseph's speed sits ~4.66σ beyond the 1820s baseline.[^13][^14] A forger would also not *set traps for himself* — opening seven or eight demonstrable "plot holes" on day one and only closing them 60 working days later invites critics to catch an error; yet the errors are not there.[^15]

**Objection 2 — the parallels are borrowed (View of the Hebrews / Spalding).** Critics propose a source text supplied the raw material.
*Response:* Source-theft can explain shared *themes*, never *undesigned internal cross-consistency* — a callback in Alma to a datum in Mosiah is not in any proposed source. See [[View of the Hebrews Is Not Scripture or a Novel]] and [[Spalding Theory of Book of Mormon Origin]]; also [[No Naturalistic Source Accounts for the Whole Book of Mormon]].

**Objection 3 — demonic/supernatural authorship.** Some critics, conceding the text exceeds Joseph's natural capacity, retreat to "he was deceived by Satan" or another supernatural being.
*Response (Markham):* This concedes the whole naturalistic project — the debate is now over *which* supernatural agency. And it is self-defeating on its own terms: a book whose measurable effect is to move millions to forsake sin and come to Christ is not plausibly the work of Satan ("By this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another").[^16]

*Honest limits:* Markham self-rates the whole exercise as a "party trick" and evidence, not proof; the "no revisions" framing is a simplification (the printer's manuscript does carry edits); and the total count (~120) is his own lay tally, published on FAIR, not an audited scholarly corpus.[^3]
