# Ridiculed Book of Mormon Names Resolve to Hebrew Roots

## The Claim
[[Rob Kay]] answers the standard anti-Mormon charge that names like *curelom*, *cumom*, and *Shez* are obvious inventions ("gibberish"): each, he argues, **re-vocalizes to a Hebrew root that fits the creature or character's narrative role**. The same holds for names that merely *look* foreign — *Lachoneus* (Greek-seeming) and *Abinadi*/*Nehor* — which resolve to Semitic roots matching what those figures *do* in the text. These are worked examples of the method in [[Book of Mormon Names Are Phonetic Transliterations of Spoken Hebrew]]; individually they are suggestive, not probative. [^1]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Curelom ≈ *shor*, the ox / useful beast — rate: moderate
[[Ether 9.19|Ether 9:19]] lists cureloms and cumoms alongside **elephants** as "beasts most useful unto man." Kay: "I've never in my life in Hebrew heard of a curelom, but I have heard of *shor*" (שׁוֹר) — the Torah's quintessential useful bovine. He also gives its gematria (*shin* 300 + *vav* 6 + *resh* 200 = **506**) to argue variant spellings share one value — a numerological flourish, not a phonetic proof. [^1]

### 2. Cumom ≈ the root *shamem*, "a large / awesome creature" — rate: weak-moderate
Kay parses *cumom* via *Proverbs 30:28*'s *semamit* (a small creature "in kings' palaces," rendered spider or gecko), where the *-it* ending marks *small* size. Strip the diminutive and the root *shamem* denotes something "big, awesome," even "dreadful" — hence a large beast of burden (he jokes: a giant lizard, or a "buffalo and giants" reading). [^2]

### 3. Shez ≈ *shetef*, a sweeping flood — rate: moderate
*Shez* first appears at *Ether 14:18*: "there went a fear of Shez throughout the land … he sweepeth the earth before him." Kay ties *Shez* (*shin-zayin*) to *shetef* — "a flood or an outburst, something overflowing" — matching the "sweeping" destructive imagery of the verse. (Co-host [[Dave Butler]] notes *Shez* is effectively a hapax-type form.) [^3]

### 4. Lachoneus — Greek-looking but Semitic, "cross over / flee" — rate: moderate (a clean function-match)
Critics read *Lachoneus* (like *Timothy*) as an anachronistic Greek name. Kay derives it instead from a root meaning "to leave / go / cross over" (cf. the Torah portion *Lech-Lecha*, Abraham's departure) plus *nus*, "to flee / escape." This matches the man's one decisive act in [[3 Nephi 3.12-13|3 Nephi 3:12-13]]: Lachoneus decrees that the people **gather in and flee** the Gadianton robbers — "cross over, get up and get out." [^4]

### 5. Abinadi and Nehor — names that describe their bearers' fate — rate: moderate
**Abinadi** = *avi* ("my father") + *nad/nud* ("to wander, flee, be driven out / exiled") → "father of my exile"; fitting, since Alma (introduced as "a young man," [[Mosiah 17.2|Mosiah 17:2]]) becomes Abinadi's disciple and *the exiled one*. **Nehor** Kay ties to *nahar* ("river," the Eden river of *Genesis 2:10*, a "conduit" in the Zohar) — Nehor is executed on the hill Manti "between the heavens and the earth" (Alma 1), and *Manti* he glosses as "the place of the spring / fountainhead" (Hebrew *maʿyan*). [^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
This is Kay's most persuasive-*looking* material and also where the method's core weakness bites hardest. (a) With a full Hebrew lexicon and free re-vocalization, **some root can be found to "match" almost any short consonant string**, and knowing the character's story in advance lets the analyst *select* the gloss that fits — the very move a skeptic rejects. (b) The phonetics are often **loose**: *shor* → *curelom* and *shamem* → *cumom* require dropping and adding consonants. (c) **Gematria (shor = 506) is numerology, not etymology** — equal numeric values don't establish a linguistic relation. (d) Function-matching (Lachoneus "flees," Shez "sweeps") is exactly the post-hoc reasoning flagged in [[Book of Mormon Names Are Phonetic Transliterations of Spoken Hebrew]].

The residue worth keeping: a few cases are *internally constrained* — Lachoneus's flee-and-gather edict and Abinadi's exile role are matches the text itself supplies, and the "Greek-looking name is actually Semitic" reply answers a real critic talking point (reinforced by the vault's [[Q X and W Are Absent From Book of Mormon Names as From Ancient Hebrew]]). But collectively these are **illustrations of a reading method, not independent evidence**. The decisive contrast: unlike Kay's re-vocalizations, [[Nahom and NHM Converge with Nephis Eastward Turn|Nahom / NHM]] is attested by *external, material inscriptions* — the difference between level-1 attestation and level-2 (or here, level-3) analyst-supplied wordplay in [[Attestation Versus Wordplay - Two Levels of Evidence]].
