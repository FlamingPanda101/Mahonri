# Coriantumr Decodes as the Hebrew Title Sar-On-Tom-Ur

## The Claim
[[Rob Kay]] argues that the "Jaredite" name **Coriantumr** is not opaque gibberish but a **Hebrew titular construct decoded verbatim in the text that names him**. Reading the initial *C* as an *S* (as Hebrew has soft sibilants), he segments it *Sar-On-Tom-Ur*:
- **Sar** (שַׂר) — prince / mighty one;
- **On** (אוֹן) — strength / virility;
- **Tom** (תֹּם) — complete / whole / perfect (a "*tav* seal");
- **Ur** (אוּר) — light / wisdom.

The larger contention: there are **no genuine Jaredite names** in the Book of Mormon — Moroni translated Ether *from* the Jaredite language *into* his own Hebrew-based tongue, so every "Jaredite" name we have is already Hebrew. This is the most speculative move in the source (the host asks aloud whether Kay is "just making this up"), and belongs to the phonetic-reconstruction method of [[Book of Mormon Names Are Phonetic Transliterations of Spoken Hebrew]]. [^1]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The name is decoded in the same passage that introduces him — rate: moderate (a genuinely constrained correspondence)
[[Helaman 1.15-34|Helaman 1]] describes Coriantumr as "a large and mighty man" whom his sponsors suppose "could stand against the Nephites **with his strength and also his much wisdom**." Kay maps this onto his segmentation in *exact sequence*: **Sar** (mighty man) → **On** (strength) → **Ur** (wisdom). He calls it a classic Hebraism — "the name and the verse are the same content written twice: once compressed [the name] … and one expanded [the description]." This internal control is what raises it above free association. [^2] [^3]

### 2. "No genuine Jaredite names" — rate: moderate (a real apologetic move, but unfalsifiable as stated)
Kay's premise for reading a Jaredite name as Hebrew: Moroni is the translator of Ether, rendering it into his Hebrew-based language, which is then Englished by Joseph Smith — "I don't think there's any real Jaredite [name] per se." This coheres with the vault's principle that the book is a *translated* ancient text ([[Read the Book of Mormon as a Translated Ancient Text]]). [^1]

### 3. Symbolic overlay: Coriantumr as an adversarial "prince" of a past age — rate: weak (esoteric)
Kay layers a Kabbalistic reading: *Sar* can denote an angelic/adversarial **prince** (an Enoch-style Watcher), and *Tom* as "the end of a completed age" frames Coriantumr as a "leftover" of the destroyed Jaredite/Babel cycle — the weapons-and-metallurgy machinations behind him echoing the Watchers of *1 Enoch*. Presented by Kay himself as "painting a picture," not mechanical etymology. [^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
This is the source's weakest-grounded claim, and the hosts nearly say so. (a) **Re-slicing a polysyllable into four Hebrew monosyllables is maximally flexible** — with a large lexicon and free re-vocalization, almost any long string can be carved into "prince-strength-complete-light"-type roots, so the *procedure* can succeed on invented names too. (b) The "**decoded in the verse**" step is **circular** when the analyst chooses which of the description's words count as the match. (c) "**No Jaredite names**" is convenient in the worst way: it converts every counter-example (a name that *doesn't* parse as Hebrew) into "Moroni translated it," removing any possible test — and it cuts against the book's own claim that some Jaredite words were carried over untransliterated.

The one real constraint worth crediting: the **sequential** match — *mighty / strength / wisdom* appearing in Helaman's description in the same order Kay reads them out of the name — is checkable against the text and is not nothing; the "encode the meaning next to the name" convention is independently documented for attested names by [[Matthew Bowen]] ([[Book of Mormon Names Show Authorial Wordplay]]). But because the segmentation itself is unconstrained, the honest verdict is: *suggestive as illustration of a Hebraic habit, not evidence that survives a skeptic*. The host's own reaction — "is he just making this up though?" — should travel with the claim.
