# Believers Already Place Themselves Above Scripture

#type/claim

## The Claim
[[Dan McClellan]]'s *tu quoque* against the inerrantist: the person who insists the Bible is the final authority already sits **above** the text in practice. Believers have quietly discarded scripture's endorsements of "slavery... genocide... misogyny" and "just accepted that renegotiated package" — which is "the word of God **as we dictate it, not as we find it in the text**."[^1] And they flatly reject the Bible's plain statements — a fetus valued below a born human, [[Exodus 22.29|Exodus 22]]'s firstborn sacrifice, Matthew 5's law-in-force, internal contradictions — "because you place yourself and your tradition above scripture."[^2] So "the Bible as final authority" is already false in the reader's own conduct; the operative authority is the tradition doing the selecting.

## Supporting Evidence
1. **Uncomfortable teachings already removed.** "We've already removed so many uncomfortable teachings and we've just accepted that renegotiated package. That is the word of God as we dictate it, not as we find it in the text."[^1] He lists slavery, genocide, and misogyny as endorsements no modern believer will defend on the plain text.[^3] *(Evidence rating: Strong on the descriptive fact of selective application — cf. the slavery parade case in [[The Bible Has No Inherent Meaning Apart From Interpretation]]; Argument weight: Primary.)*
2. **Fetus as property.** In the Hebrew Bible "a fetus is quite clearly designated a piece of property that is far less valuable than a born human whose... accidental destruction by a third party is punishable by a fine" (Exodus 21:22–25) — "and you refuse to accept that."[^2] *(Evidence rating: Moderate — the standard critical reading of Exodus 21:22, though the rendering of the Hebrew is itself contested; Argument weight: Secondary.)*
3. **Commanded firstborn sacrifice.** "We can look in [[Exodus 22.29|Exodus 22]] where child sacrifice is commanded. You will refuse to accept that because you place yourself and your tradition above scripture."[^2] See [[Surviving Traces of Human Sacrifice in the Hebrew Bible]]. *(Evidence rating: Moderate; Argument weight: Secondary.)*
4. **Matthew 5's law-in-force.** "Matthew 5 where Jesus says the law of Moses will continue in force, that every last law will have to be followed by Jesus' disciples or they will be least in the kingdom of heaven. You will absolutely refuse to accept that."[^2] See [[Dual Chronological Markers Keep the Law in Force Until the Eschaton]]. *(Evidence rating: Moderate; Argument weight: Secondary.)*
5. **Internal contradictions.** Believers "will absolutely refuse to accept" that the scriptures contradict each other "because your tradition tells you that they're not allowed to contradict, and the tradition is the real authority."[^2] The conclusion: "texts do not have inherent meaning."[^4] *(Evidence rating: Moderate — leans on his separately-argued contradiction catalog; Argument weight: Primary to the thesis.)*

## Apologetic Engagement
**Steelman McClellan.** The descriptive core is undeniable and worth conceding: no modern believer defends chattel slavery, Canaanite herem-warfare, or the property-status of women by quoting the verses that once licensed them. That *is* moral judgment operating on the text, and an inerrantist who claims to submit to "the plain word" while silently filtering it is not being fully honest about the interpretive work already happening (the point developed in [[The Bible Has No Inherent Meaning Apart From Interpretation]] and [[We Negotiate With the Text - It Does Not Speak for Itself]]). McClellan is right that "no interpretive authority is operating here" is a false description of anyone's actual practice.

**The informed response.** (1) *Selective reading is not arbitrary overruling — it can be principled.* The distinction McClellan collapses is between (a) overruling the text by preference and (b) reading it through genre, covenantal progression, and later revelation. Christians do not "reject" Exodus 22:29 the way one crosses out a line; they read the Mosaic covenant as fulfilled and transformed in Christ (the very tension Matthew 5 raises and the New Testament resolves) — a hermeneutic with internal warrant, not a veto. (2) *His proof-texts are contested exegesis, not settled plain-sense.* Exodus 21:22's "fetus as mere property" turns on a disputed reading of the Hebrew; Exodus 22:29 as a *command* to sacrifice children is one reconstruction among several (Israel's own law elsewhere condemns child sacrifice, and the firstborn were *redeemed*); Matthew 5:18–19 is read by many scholars as affirming the Law's abiding *validity* under its Christological fulfillment, not its unchanged *observance*. The vault's own notes register these as live debates, not knockdowns. (3) *The moral advance runs* through *scripture, not against it.* Abolition did not defeat the proof-texts by external fiat but by pressing scripture's deeper trajectory — the image of God, the Exodus, "neither bond nor free... in Christ" — so that the Old Testament became, in the abolitionists' hands, "a hammer" (see [[The Old Testament Was a Hammer for Abolitionists]], and the contrary case in [[The New Testament Does Not Teach Abolition]]). That is meaning *constraining* the reader, the opposite of "no inherent meaning." (4) *McClellan is right that someone always governs the reading — so name them.* The honest response to "you already stand above the text" is not to deny it but to identify the interpretive authority openly, which is exactly what a magisterium or a living prophetic office does and what bare *sola scriptura* cannot.

## LDS Relevance
This argument lands hardest on *inerrantist* Protestantism and glances off the Latter-day Saint, who never claimed to stand *under* an inerrant text in the first place. The eighth Article of Faith already qualifies the Bible "as far as it is translated correctly," and the Restoration supplies exactly the "someone who decides" McClellan says is unavoidable — but names it: **continuing revelation through living prophets**, an accountable and disclosed authority rather than a hidden committee of tradition. So the LDS reader can grant McClellan's premise (moral and doctrinal judgments do govern how scripture is applied) without his conclusion (that this is arbitrary or embarrassing): where he sees only "the word of God as we dictate it," the Restoration offers "the word of God as He continues to reveal it," reading the older covenant forward under new light (the priesthood revelation of 1978 being developed as revelation resolving a prior practice, not a preference overruling a verse). The candid half of McClellan's point should still be kept: proof-texting a single ancient statute as a self-interpreting oracle ignores genre, covenant, and the whole counsel of scripture. See [[The Bible Has No Inherent Meaning Apart From Interpretation]] for the LDS handling of the same slavery example.
