# Translated Correctly Is Not an Escape Hatch but Vindicated Honesty

#type/rebuttal

## The Objection Being Answered
Critics have read the eighth **Article of Faith** — "We believe the Bible to be the word of God as far as it is translated correctly" (written by [[Joseph Smith]] for a newspaper editor in 1842) — for 180 years as "a convenient escape hatch, just a way of dismissing scripture whenever it's inconvenient."[^1]

## The Rebuttal
[[Alex Arnold]] argues the qualifier reflects exactly what two centuries of mainstream manuscript research has confirmed: that "the biblical text was transmitted through human hands over many centuries in conditions where editors made deliberate changes and institutional pressures shaped what survived."[^2] That was "a totally epistemically honest position in 1842," and it is "even more defensible now because we have the manuscript evidence to show that it's true."[^2] The phrase is not an ad-hoc dodge invented to escape hard verses; it is a general, *falsifiable* stance about transmission that the evidence has since vindicated.

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The 1842 claim precedes the evidence that confirms it.** The Dead Sea Scrolls (1947), the critical demonstration of the [[1 John 5.7|Comma Johanneum]] as a late interpolation, and the documentary/Deuteronomistic reconstructions all post-date the Articles of Faith — so the qualifier cannot have been reverse-engineered from them. See [[What the Deuteronomists Cut Is What the Creeds Replaced]].[^2] *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong.*
2. **"Translated correctly" tracks a real, conceded phenomenon.** That the text was altered in transmission — scribal changes, doctrinally motivated insertions, institutional selection — is not an LDS peculiarity but the ordinary finding of textual criticism. See [[Manuscripts Were Altered by Scribes Over Centuries]] and [[The Bible Was Transmitted and Canonized by Fallible Human Process]]. *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong.*
3. **It is a reliability standard, not a license.** The clause affirms the Bible *is* the word of God (a high view), while declining to stake that authority on the inerrancy of every transmitted word — the same posture argued in [[Reliability Not Infallibility Standard]]. *Argument weight: Secondary.*

## Best Quote
> "That was a totally epistemically honest position in 1842. But it's even more defensible now because we have the manuscript evidence to show that it's true."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
A critic can reply that the *practical use* of the clause still looks selective: Latter-day Saints invoke "translated correctly" chiefly where a verse cuts against LDS distinctives, which reintroduces the escape-hatch worry at the level of application even if the general principle is sound. The fair response is to hold the two apart — the *principle* (transmission is imperfect) is a mainstream, evidence-backed claim independent of any single verse; whether a *particular* appeal to it is warranted has to be argued case by case on textual grounds (manuscripts, versions, patristic citation), exactly as with the Comma, rather than asserted. Conceding the second point is what keeps the first honest. A further critic move — that the qualifier does no work because the Church still uses the KJV — misreads it: it governs *authority*, not *edition*.
