# Choose to Believe Then Get the Evidence Is Unfalsifiable

#type/claim

## The Claim
Dan McClellan and Dan Beecher target a specific apologetic move: **you don't get the evidence for God until you first choose to believe.** Their exhibit is mathematician [[John Lennox]]'s "red Ferrari" analogy from the *Diary of a CEO* interview — Lennox tells the skeptic there is a red Ferrari parked outside just for him, and "you just have to get up and go look," because arguing on the couch will never settle it; once you sincerely commit to finding out, you will find it is true.[^1] McClellan argues this has the identical structure to Carl Sagan's parable of **the invisible dragon in the garage** (*The Demon-Haunted World*): every proposed test is pre-empted by a new caveat, so the claim is "unverifiable, unfalsifiable" — "we're not providing evidence. We're telling you why you don't get to have any evidence."[^2] The fatal tell, he says, is the treatment of *ex-believers*: those who looked and found no Ferrari are told "you didn't really look."[^3]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **Lennox's red-Ferrari analogy.** On *Diary of a CEO*, Lennox answers a skeptic's demand for evidence with: there's a Ferrari outside for you; we can "argue about it all night long," but "you just have to get up and go look" — and once you sincerely try, "you're going to find out that it's true."[^1] *(Evidence rating: Strong — a fair report of a public analogy; Argument weight: Primary.)*
2. **The Sagan structural parallel.** Sagan's invisible-immaterial-silent dragon absorbs every test; McClellan maps the apologetic onto it: "when it comes to ideas about God... it's always unverifiable, unfalsifiable. Every question is sidestepped."[^2] *(Evidence rating: Strong on the structural point; Argument weight: Primary.)* See [[Carl Sagan]].
3. **The claim reduces to "believe first."** "Fundamentally, the claim is you don't get to know it's true until you choose to believe" — knowledge is gated behind a prior act of belief, inverting the normal order of evidence then assent.[^4] *(Evidence rating: Strong; Argument weight: Primary.)*
4. **The unfalsifiable escape for apostates.** Anyone who "went outside" and found "no Ferrari" is met with "you didn't really do it... if you did, you would have found it" — no outcome can count against the claim.[^3] *(Evidence rating: Strong — a textbook unfalsifiability marker; Argument weight: Primary.)*
5. **Interviewer's parity challenge.** Steven Bartlett pressed Lennox that the same fulfillment/"veil lifted" experiences are documented "all around the world" in other traditions — "what's special?" — and (per the hosts) Lennox retreated to "the dragon's invisible, can't you see" rather than answering.[^5] *(Evidence rating: Moderate — the hosts' characterization of the exchange; Argument weight: Secondary.)*

## Best Quotes
> "We can sit on the couch and argue about it all night long and you will not know whether or not it's out there. You just have to get up and go look." *(Lennox's Ferrari analogy, as recounted)*[^1]

> "It's always unverifiable, unfalsifiable... We're not providing evidence. We're telling you why you don't get to have any evidence."[^2]

## Apologetic Engagement
**Steelman the hosts.** The critique is sharp and partly correct. An apologetic whose *only* content is "commit first, evidence later" is unfalsifiable, and the "you didn't really try" rejoinder to sincere ex-believers is a classic unfalsifiability move that should embarrass anyone who uses it — McClellan is right that a serious apologist "would acknowledge" a genuine sincere search that came up empty. The cross-tradition parity that Bartlett pressed is the genuinely hard problem (it recurs in [[Reading With the Holy Spirit Is Special Pleading]]).

**The informed response.** (1) **Lennox is describing knowledge by acquaintance, not special pleading.** Some truths are only accessible through participation — you cannot know what a symphony sounds like by arguing about it, and (as McClellan himself concedes on this very episode) *qualia* are real and non-transferable. That is a recognized epistemic category, not a dodge. Scripture frames faith the same way: "O taste and see that the LORD is good" (Ps 34:8); "If any man will do his will, he shall know of the doctrine" (John 7:17). The knowing follows the doing because the relevant evidence is experiential, exactly as with music, love, or grief. (2) **The Christian case is not** ***only*** **"commit then see."** The tradition also advances public, pre-commitment evidence — the historical case for the resurrection, contingency and fine-tuning arguments, the Book of Mormon witnesses — so Bartlett's "no evidence until you believe" charge lands against the *weakest* version, not the whole. Lennox himself elsewhere argues from cosmology and the rationality of the universe, not bare fideism. (3) **Falsifiability is available if you build it in.** The honest form of the invitation specifies in advance what would count against it — which is precisely what distinguishes a real experiment from Sagan's dragon. (4) **The parity problem is the real residue**, and the answer is a fruits-and-outcomes test (Matt 7:16) rather than the raw feeling — which is falsifiable in principle. See [[Heads Faith Wins, Tails Reason Loses - The Unfalsifiability Charge]] and [[Christian Metaphorical Reading Is Unfalsifiable Retreat]] for the general form of this charge and reply.

## LDS Relevance
Latter-day Saints must own this critique more than most, because **Alma 32** frames faith as exactly the "try it and see" structure Sagan attacks: "experiment upon my words," plant the seed, and observe whether it "beginneth to swell within your breasts" (Alma 32:27-28). Two things rescue it from the invisible-dragon charge. First, Alma builds in a **falsification test**: "if it be a true seed... it will begin to swell"; "if it grow not... behold it is not good, and... ye must cast it out" (Alma 32:32,38) — a real experiment names the outcome that would count against it, and Alma does. Second, the promised confirmation is meant to be checked by **fruits over time** (Alma 32:41-43; Moroni 7:16-17; Matt 7:16), not a single unrepeatable feeling immune to review. Moroni 10:4 likewise conditions the witness on asking "with real intent" — which the critic hears as the unfalsifiable "you didn't really try," so the LDS apologist has to specify, in advance and publicly, what a sincere-but-failed search would look like and grant that it happens. The strongest LDS position concedes Sagan's point against pure fideism and stakes the claim on an experiment with a stated, observable failure condition. See [[Do Not Argue Spiritual Experience to Be Valid]].
