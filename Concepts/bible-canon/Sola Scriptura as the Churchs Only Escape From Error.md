# Sola Scriptura as the Church's Only Escape From Error

## The Claim
The host's third argument: *sola scriptura* (as veto-power, see [[Sola Scriptura as the Bibles Veto Power Over Tradition]]) is the church's **only means of self-correction** — "the only way to ensure that we don't get hopelessly trapped in a false system of belief."[^1] The Catholic and Orthodox model, which makes the church the final judge of whether the church has erred, is an **unfalsifiable circle**; only a standard *outside* the institution — the apostolic deposit in the earlier documents — can regulate it.[^2]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The regulatory function requires an external standard.** If the Catholic (or Orthodox) Church taught something in 2026 that contradicted the faith of the original apostles, a member could only detect it by holding the teaching up to the apostolic deposit in the New Testament and seeing the contradiction. "We can't perform this regulatory function unless we recognize that the Bible has more weight than later teachings."[^2] *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate — assumes the deposit is legible enough to serve as a check.*

2. **The rival churches can't be told apart from inside.** Catholic, Eastern Orthodox, and Oriental Orthodox each claim to be the one true church whose teaching matches the apostles'. With no external test, a member "led astray" has no way to "ever come to recognize that I've been led astray."[^3] *Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Moderate.*

3. **The circle: reinterpret the verse or gatekeep its meaning.** Point out that doctrine X seems to contradict verse Y and the reply is either "your interpretation of Y is wrong; the church's reading removes the conflict," or "you can't even know what Y says without the church." Either way "the Catholic and Orthodox Church essentially become unfalsifiable… No matter how egregious the error, no matter how stark the contradiction," the church's authority is assumed to reject the very evidence against it.[^4] *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate — a sharp structural point, contested below as symmetric.*

4. **The boss-investigating-himself analogy.** It is "like if your boss gets accused of improper conduct and an investigation gets launched, but then the boss who is the very person being investigated is the person leading the investigation who gets to make the final call. His judgment is the very thing that's under question."[^5] *Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Strong as an illustration of viciously circular self-adjudication.*

5. **The asymmetry of escape — the modest conclusion.** The claim is not that *sola scriptura* guarantees you never err; the host grants he has held false views (he cites abandoning penal substitutionary atonement after new evidence). The point is directional: a Protestant who errs "stays humble and engages in certain epistemic virtues and continues to look at evidence" and can escape; someone who has adopted the Catholic/Orthodox "we set the standard, you don't interpret" method has no exit, because the same circular reasoning will always re-secure the church. The method "makes it way more probable that we will not… find ourselves in a trap where we are methodologically hopeless."[^6] *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate.*

## Best Quotes
> "If the Catholic or Orthodox Church ever was wrong about anything… we would never be able to recognize that error under this methodology, because they will always just assume the validity of their church and… use that as a basis to reject the error that you have found."[^4]

> "It's like if your boss gets accused of improper conduct and an investigation gets launched, but then the boss who is the very person being investigated is the person leading the investigation who gets to make the final call."[^5]

> "It makes it way more probable that we will not ever find ourselves in a trap where we are methodologically hopeless from ever getting out."[^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

**The "escape" relocates the infallible authority to the self — and its fruit is schism.** The Protestant has not stepped outside an unaccountable final judge; he has *become* one. When two sincere believers read the same verse oppositely, *sola scriptura* supplies no final interpreter, so "you just go with your own" — the reader is his own last court of appeal. The historical fruit is not tidy self-correction but endless division. See [[No Final Interpreter - The Protestant Adjudication Problem]]. The "regulatory function" can flag that *something* seems off, but it cannot tell two disagreeing correctors which of them is right, so it equally licenses walking *into* a new error with full confidence. See [[The Infinite Regress of Infallible Interpreters]].

**The unfalsifiability charge is symmetric.** Every ultimate authority is, by definition, not falsifiable by a higher one — otherwise it would not be ultimate. The Protestant's own final standard (his interpretation of Scripture) is equally the thing "under question," and "I just follow the evidence and stay humble" is exactly what every schismatic and heretic has also claimed. See [[Every Worldview Has a Self-Authenticating Ultimate Standard]] and [[A Fallible List of Infallible Teachings - The Symmetry of Uncertainty]]. The Catholic reply: a *living* magisterium at least provides a public, non-private adjudicator, whereas privatized judgment guarantees the disagreement is never resolved at all.

**LDS response — the real exit is living prophetic authority, not a book or a conscience.** The Latter-day Saint grants the dilemma's force against both a self-sealing institution and a self-adjudicating reader, and answers with a third option: living prophets and apostles who can themselves receive *new* correction from God, so the channel of revelation stays open rather than sealed in either a closed text or a private opinion. The self-correction argument also assumes the apostolic deposit survives intact and perspicuous enough to serve as the check — which the Restoration denies (plain and precious truths lost through a fallible transmission). See [[The Restoration Answers an Authority Problem Scholarship Cannot Solve]].
