# Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church

The Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church is one of the Oriental Orthodox (non-Chalcedonian) churches, with roots in the fourth-century Christianization of the Kingdom of Aksum. *Tewahedo* ("made one") signals its miaphysite Christology. It is notable for maintaining the broadest biblical canon in Christendom — an 81-book canon that includes works absent from other traditions.

## Relevance

The Church is cited in the vault as the sole tradition to preserve [[1 Enoch]] in its full scriptural canon (in Ge'ez/Ethiopic). This bears on arguments about the fluidity of the biblical canon and the contested boundary between canonical and extracanonical scripture — a theme relevant to Latter-day Saint claims about an open canon.

## How the Aksumite Canon Preserved 1 Enoch (McClellan)

[[Dan McClellan]] explains *why* this one tradition retained 1 Enoch: the kingdom of Aksum was "far enough away from what was going on in northern Africa and in the broader Roman Empire that they were independent of the debates and the councils and the decisions related to the canon." [^1] A version of the [[Septuagint]] was carried there — "probably by missionaries of one kind or another" — and the Aksumite church "maintained everything that was a part of that Septuagintal corpus as the canon," including 1 Enoch, [[Jubilees]], and other texts (plus later works composed in Ethiopia). [^2] McClellan's summary line: **"the only Bible that ever included First Enoch still includes First Enoch"** — because the communities that rejected the book "did so before they ever created their biblical canon." [^2] This is a load-bearing datum for [[The Book of Enoch Was Not Taken Out of the Bible It Predates the Canon]]: exclusion elsewhere preceded canon-formation, while inclusion here persisted precisely because Aksum stood outside those decisions.
