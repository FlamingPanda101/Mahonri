# Reading With the Holy Spirit Is Special Pleading

#type/claim

## The Claim
Dan McClellan argues that the claim "you can only understand the Bible if you have the Spirit with you" is **"the definition of special pleading"** — an appeal to a privileged epistemic level "that we just cannot achieve," which cannot be measured, tested, or scrutinized.[^1] His core disproof is the **parity problem**: two sincere readers who both believe they are "reading with the Holy Spirit" reach *contradictory* conclusions, so at most one is doing it right — and there is no way to tell which. The more economical explanation, he says, is that the felt witness ("burning in the bosom," "a revelation from God") is really **intuition**, "the same thing that is responsible for optical illusions and for the way cartoons work."[^2][^3]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The special-pleading charge.** McClellan reports being told he "cannot understand the Bible" without the Spirit; both hosts classify this as textbook special pleading — a claim to an epistemological level no outsider can reach or check.[^1] *(Evidence rating: Strong as a description of the move's logical form; Argument weight: Primary.)*
2. **The parity disproof.** "Yes, you think you're reading using the Holy Spirit. That person over there thinks that they're reading using the Holy Spirit and you've come to differing conclusions." Either one of them is doing it wrong (with no test to say who) or both are simply leaning on their own understanding.[^4] *(Evidence rating: Strong — a genuinely hard problem for spiritual-witness epistemology; Argument weight: Primary.)*
3. **Cognitive science of religion.** The hosts invoke the spectrum from intuitive to reflective cognition: an idea that surfaces without conscious derivation feels "given" and is labeled revelation, but it is intuition — the same faculty behind optical illusions. Crucially, "so many of our intuitions are rooted in our identity politics and the world views that we have become accustomed to," so they *confirm* prior identity rather than tracking truth.[^3][^5] *(Evidence rating: Moderate — mainstream CSR framing, applied polemically; Argument weight: Primary.)*
4. **The qualia dodge.** McClellan anticipates the retreat to *qualia* (non-transferable experience, like describing red to a person born blind) and calls appealing to it for spiritual knowledge "special pleading as well" — it exempts the claim from scrutiny by definition.[^6] *(Evidence rating: Moderate; Argument weight: Secondary.)*
5. **Cross-tradition parity and the demonic escape hatch.** The same "veil lifted from my eyes" conversion is reported entering Muslim and Buddhist belief and LDS testimony; the standard rescue — "that's actually the devil... disguised as an angel of light" — is applied *only* to the rival's identical experience, which McClellan notes is unfalsifiable.[^7][^8] *(Evidence rating: Strong on parity, and the demonic move is a recognized unfalsifiability problem; Argument weight: Secondary.)* See [[When Catholics Argue Like Jews - The Demonic Charge Is Unfalsifiable]].

## Best Quotes

## Apologetic Engagement
**Steelman the hosts.** This is the strongest material in the episode, and it should be conceded generously. The parity problem is real: [[1 Corinthians 2.9-16|1 Corinthians 2:14]] ("the natural man... neither can he know them, because they are spiritually discerned") and [[Matthew 11.25-26|Matthew 11:25]] ("hid these things from the wise... revealed them unto babes") *are* deployed to place the critic's objection out of bounds without answering it. The cognitive-science point is well-established — intuitions do track identity and can misfire like optical illusions — and the "that's Satan disguised as an angel of light" move, applied only to the other guy's identical burning, is exactly the unfalsifiable maneuver the vault flags elsewhere ([[Burning in the Bosom vs Self-Evidencing Light]]). Any honest defense of Moroni 10:4-5 has to face this squarely.

**The informed response.** Four replies. (1) **Private access is not a public trump card.** McClellan himself later grants that privately held belief is fine ("blast off, man") and that the real offense is deploying spiritual authority to *control* others; the apologetic tradition agrees — D&C 50 tells Latter-day Saints to reject any influence that does "not edify," and "by their fruits ye shall know them" (Matt 7:16) supplies a public, shareable test. The witness was never meant to end a debate by fiat, so the critique is decisive against the *abuse* and not against the doctrine. (2) **The parity argument proves too much.** McClellan concedes on the same episode that "our brain is... absolutely unreliable" and that reasoners "misunderstand each other all the time" — yet no one concludes we should discard reason; we refine it. The identical structure ("faculty X yields contradictory outputs across sincere users, therefore X is not truth-tracking") would retire perception, memory, and reason alongside the Spirit. A test that deletes every faculty singles out none. See [[Reason Is Emergent From Intuition]] and [[The Rider and the Elephant - Intuition Drives Reason]]. (3) **LDS epistemology is study-then-confirm, not intuition-instead-of-thought.** D&C 9:7-9 requires one to "study it out in your mind" *first*; the confirming witness is a check on reasoning already done, which meets the intuitive-vs-reflective objection head-on rather than fleeing to raw intuition. See [[Spiritual Gifts Differ - Not Everyone Receives the Burning]]. (4) **Reformed epistemology answers the "unscrutinizable" charge.** A belief formed by a reliable faculty (Plantinga's *sensus divinitatis* / the internal witness of the Spirit) can be *warranted* without being publicly demonstrable — precisely as memory and perception are warranted though not provable to a determined skeptic. Non-transferability is a feature of experiential knowledge generally, not a special exemption invented for religion. See [[Perfect Certainty of Experience Not of Interpretation]] and [[Spiritual Experience Is the Lens Not the Picture]].

## LDS Relevance
The critique targets the Latter-day Saint "burning in the bosom" (D&C 9:8) and Moroni 10:4-5 ("ask God... by the power of the Holy Ghost ye may know the truth of all things") more directly than any other tradition, and McClellan raises the LDS case by name. The honest LDS rejoinder does not deny the parity data; it (a) relocates the witness from public trump card to personal warrant, (b) points to the study-first structure of D&C 9 as the disanalogy with mere gut feeling, and (c) accepts a fruits-and-continuing-revelation test (Moroni 7:16-17; Matt 7:16) as the falsification criterion the parity objection rightly demands. The weakest LDS move — dismissing a sincere outsider's contrary witness as satanic while trusting one's own identical feeling — is one the vault already flags as unfalsifiable; see [[When Catholics Argue Like Jews - The Demonic Charge Is Unfalsifiable]] and [[Praying to Know Truth and the Discernment-of-Spirits Objection]].
