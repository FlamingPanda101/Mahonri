# Sola Words of the Covenant - Redemption Then Covenant Writings

## The Claim
[[Greg (Collierville Pastor)]] answers the Latter-day Saint challenge "where did God ever establish *your* canon?" with a coined phrase — **"sola words of the covenant."** His thesis: God's revelatory pattern is always **redemption first, then covenant-writings** that stand as a *witness* against His people. Because Christ has already redeemed and given the everlasting covenant, the writings that immediately followed His work (the apostolic New Testament) are the closed witness-book of that covenant — "what other words do we need?"[^1] The canon is therefore not an arbitrary list but the unfolding of a single **story** from Genesis to Revelation, which closes when the story closes.[^2]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The Old Testament pattern: Exodus 20 → Deuteronomy 31.** God redeems Israel out of Egypt, *then* gives the terms of the covenant ("I have redeemed you... therefore this is how you shall live"), and Moses writes them in a book placed beside the ark "that this book may be a witness against you." *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate.* The pattern is textually real (see [[Exodus 20.3|Exodus 20:3]], [[Deuteronomy 31.24-26|Deuteronomy 31:24-26]]); the disputed step is whether "witness-writings" implies a *closed, self-defining* canon.[^3]

2. **Josiah's reform: the writings, not the prophet, bring revival.** Greg's marquee case (2 Kings 22-23): King Josiah discovers the lost Book of the Law and is cut to the heart — "it's not the prophet who cuts Josiah to the heart, it's the writings." Josiah gathers the nation, tears down the idols, and holds Israel's first Passover in centuries. "What brought the restoration? Scripture brought the restoration. Not the prophet."[^4] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate* — a strong rhetorical parallel, though a prophetess (Huldah) is in fact consulted in the same account (2 Kings 22:14).

3. **"Living oracles" (Acts 7:38) names the writings, not a person.** In Stephen's speech Moses receives the "living oracles" — the *written* covenant law — and Greg argues scripture never applies "living oracles" to a living prophet, undercutting the LDS use of the phrase for the living prophet. "That to me just demolishes the view."[^5] See [[Acts 7.38|Acts 7:38]]. *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Moderate* (contested — see steelman below).

4. **The canon "tells a story" that closes at Revelation 22.** From the garden of Genesis to the restored garden-city of Revelation, "it starts in a garden and it ends in a garden." Greg likens canonicity to a coherent narrative canon (Star Wars, comics) — what "fits the story" belongs, and the story reaches its resolution in Christ's return, leaving "nothing else that needs to be said."[^2] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Weak-to-Moderate* — this is a coherence criterion, and Carroll's central objection targets exactly this move.

5. **The New Testament points forward only to Christ, never to a later prophet.** The Old Testament anticipates the Messiah and a new covenant; the New Testament anticipates only Christ's *return* (Jude's "faith once for all delivered," the "everlasting covenant" of Ezekiel 37 / Hebrews 13) — never a Joseph Smith or a further covenant. "There's no room for a new covenant in the everlasting covenant."[^6] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate.*

## Best Quotes
> "I'm a huge proponent of a phrase that I have coined... Sola words of the covenant. When God redeems, he then gives the terms of the covenant."[^1]

> "It's not the prophet who cuts Josiah to the heart, it's the writings... What brought the restoration? Scripture brought the restoration. Not the prophet."[^4]

> "If living oracles is never viewed to refer to as a person in the scriptures, why should I look for that under Joseph Smith? That changes the game."[^5]

> "Genesis through Revelation tells a story... It starts in a garden, and it ends in a garden... There's nothing else that needs to be said."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
[[Hayden Carroll]] presses the argument's central gap and never lets Greg fill it:

- **"Where did God say *this* is the canon?"** The pattern (redemption → writings) may be real, but it never yields a *divine designation of the specific 66 books*. Describing a narrative is not the same as authorizing a list. "You're telling a story. This is not justifying the canon."[^7] This is the vault's [[Authorization - Divine Designation Required for an Infallible Canon|authorization argument]] and [[The Canon Question Is Logically Prior to Canon Authority|the logical-priority problem]]. *This is Carroll's strongest point and, on the transcript, Greg does not produce the requested designation.*

- **A fallible list of infallible books.** Carroll turns Greg's own hero against him: [[R.C. Sproul]] conceded Protestants have "a fallible list of infallible books." The canon was in fact settled by centuries of church debate and councils — the deuterocanon (Maccabees, Tobit, Wisdom) was disputed until Trent, and the "story" criterion is a post-hoc rationalization, not a revealed rule. See [[A Fallible List of Infallible Teachings - The Symmetry of Uncertainty]] and [[The Deuterocanon Was Disputed Until Trent Not Removed]].[^8]

- **The circularity charge.** Greg ultimately grants that "the highest authority is always circular" and that he presupposes the Bible's truth ("my sheep hear my voice"). Carroll's rejoinder: "if you believe the Bible's true because the Bible says the Bible's true, why can't I say that about the Book of Mormon?" — the coherence/self-authentication move proves too much.[^9] See [[The Self-Authenticating Canon Refuted]].

- **The story is still open — "God still speaks."** Carroll accepts a narrative canon but denies it is *closed*: the same God who authored the story can continue it through living prophets (he affirms Brigham Young's willingness to prefer "the living oracle" over all written scripture). Greg's "it fits the story, therefore it's canon" cannot exclude the Book of Mormon, which claims to continue the very same story of Christ.[^10]

**Fair concession to Greg:** his positive case does answer the frequent charge that Protestants have *no* account of the canon — he offers a covenant-witness rationale, not mere fideism, and the "no new covenant after the everlasting covenant" point is a genuine biblical-theological argument, not just an assertion. The dispute is whether a coherent rationale can rise to the *infallible designation* the LDS challenge demands.
