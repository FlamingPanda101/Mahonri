# P.Oxy 5575 Dates a Thomas-Synoptic Text to the Second Century

## The Claim
A newly published papyrus — **P.Oxy 5575**, excavated at Oxyrhynchus in Egypt — is a Koine Greek fragment carrying Gospel of Thomas material, and paleographers have dated it to the **2nd century (mid-to-late 100s AD)**, "as early as any New Testament manuscript in existence." Because earlier scholarship dismissed Thomas as a 3rd-4th century Gnostic latecomer (known mainly from the Coptic Nag Hammadi copy), a securely 2nd-century *Greek* witness "blows the doors off" that dating and, [[Jonah Barnes]] argues, the whole story critics tell about the canon. [^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The provenance is the richest manuscript site in the field.** *Argument weight: Secondary.* Oxyrhynchus, excavated from 1898, is "a flipping gold mine of manuscripts"; the very first fragment ever pulled from it (P.Oxy 1) was itself a piece of the Gospel of Thomas, later identified once the complete Coptic copy surfaced at Nag Hammadi in 1945. Barnes claims "40% of all New Testament manuscripts come from Oxyrhynchus." [^2] *(Evidence rating: Weak on the exact figure — see Steelmanning; the site's centrality to the earliest papyri is real.)*
2. **It is Greek and northern, not Coptic and southern.** *Argument weight: Primary.* Unlike the Nag Hammadi Thomas (4th-century Coptic, far south), P.Oxy 5575 is Koine Greek from Oxyrhynchus up north — "closer geographically, closer linguistically to the original gospels" — removing two of the excuses used to date Thomas late. [^3]
3. **The dating rests on paleographic comparanda.** *Argument weight: Primary.* The publication (in an Egyptian journal) prints a table of dated comparison hands — Barnes cites samples dated 157, 161, 178, and 185 AD — whose script closely matches the fragment, yielding a mid-to-late 2nd-century date. [^4]
4. **That would put it among the very earliest Christian manuscripts.** *Argument weight: Primary.* On the panel's timeline the apostles die c. 50-80 AD; the earliest defensible date for P52 (the [[John 18.37|John 18]] fragment, the current record-holder) is c. 125. A 157-161 Thomas fragment would sit "in the club of three texts," predating the earliest copies of Luke and all the letters of Paul, Peter, and John. [^5]

## Best Quotes
> "Paleographers have dated this now to the 2nd century, so 100s AD, which is as early as any New Testament manuscript in existence." [^1]

> "This would be in the club of three texts. This would predate the Gospel of Luke… all the letters of Paul, all the letters of Peter, all the letters of John." [^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **An early copy is not an early composition, still less an early *content*.** Even a securely 2nd-century fragment dates *this manuscript*, not the origin of every saying in Thomas — and certainly not any distinctively Latter-day Saint reading of it. Barnes himself concedes the fragment "doesn't say anything that supports Mormon doctrine, particularly."
- **Paleographic dates carry wide error bars.** Dating a hand by comparanda routinely spans 50-75 years, and specialists dispute individual assignments; a headline "2nd century" is a probable range, not a fixed point, and some would read the same hand into the early 3rd century.
- **The mainstream inference runs the other way.** The dominant view in Thomas scholarship (e.g. Mark Goodacre, Simon Gathercole) is that Thomas *knows and reworks the finished Synoptics* — it preserves Matthew's and Luke's editorial fingerprints — which makes the interwoven fragment most naturally a *later harmony*, not proof that Thomas is primitive. See the "downstream" reading in [[P.Oxy 5575 Interweaves Thomas With Matthew and Luke]].
- **The source is a comedy podcast working from memory.** The panel admits "30 seconds of prep," misremembers the publishing scholar's name, and garbles P52 (calling it "a verse from Mark" before self-correcting to John 18) — so the numbers should be checked against the actual editio princeps before being leaned on. This is why the [[Ward Radio - The Gospel of Thomas and the P.Oxy 5575 Fragment|source]] is rated authority tier C.
