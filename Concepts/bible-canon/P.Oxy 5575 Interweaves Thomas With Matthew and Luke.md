# P.Oxy 5575 Interweaves Thomas With Matthew and Luke

## The Claim
When the tiny P.Oxy 5575 fragment is reconstructed and matched to known texts, it does not read as one gospel: its ~100 recoverable words run **Thomas → Luke 12 / Matthew 6 → Thomas**, weaving Gospel-of-Thomas sayings together with Synoptic material like a miniature Diatessaron. [[Jonah Barnes]] argues that *either* explanation of this weave — "upstream" or "downstream" — is bad for a univocal, infallible, sufficient Bible and good for the Restoration's case that the canon is a living, human process. [^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The reconstruction is standard papyrology, not LDS spin.** *Argument weight: Secondary.* From a single legible word in Koine Greek ("worry" / "what you will eat"), scholars estimate line length and locate the passage, then piece the fragment together — "this is not my work, these scholars have translated this." The colored overlay shows Thomas at top, then Luke 12 and Matthew 6, then back to Thomas. [^2]
2. **It behaves like a gospel harmony.** *Argument weight: Primary.* The panel compares it to Tatian's Diatessaron — someone has "compiled it all into the greatest hits," interleaving Matthew, Luke, and Thomas's unique elements (it even uses a *different word for birds* than Matthew or Luke). [^3]
3. **Upstream theory: a lost source birthed all three.** *Argument weight: Primary.* One published option is that an original document "birthed Matthew, Luke, and Thomas" — each took a portion. If so, this stratum would be **older than all of them**, and Thomas's "secret sayings" would predate the canonical gospels, collapsing the "Gnosticism is a later corruption" timeline. [^4]
4. **Downstream theory: a mid-2nd-century compiler fused them.** *Argument weight: Primary.* The alternative is that someone "zipper-toothed" Matthew, Luke, and Thomas together in the mid-2nd century — which means some early community treated Thomas as *equal in standing* to the Synoptics, "within the lifetime of Origen… contemporary with the letters of Ignatius." [^5]
5. **Either way the canon story is destabilized.** *Argument weight: Primary.* Upstream makes Thomas primitive; downstream makes it co-equal early scripture. Both undercut the picture of a Bible that "dropped like an album at midnight" — fully formed, univocal, and self-evidently bounded — which is the constructive payoff toward [[God Himself Not a Book Is the Ultimate Authority]] and [[A Living Canon Is a Feature Not a Bug]]. [^6]

## Best Quotes
> "These aren't separate manuscripts. This tiny fragment… incorporates Luke and Matthew and Thomas unique elements." [^3]

> "So either way, upstream or downstream, it becomes very difficult to ignore all the scholarship. If you're a creedal Christian believing that the Bible is univocal and infallible and totally sufficient, this nukes that stuff." [^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **A harmony is the *ordinary* explanation — and it favors the deflationary reading.** A 2nd-century text that stitches Matthew + Luke + Thomas together is most simply a *later* compilation that presupposes those sources (the "downstream" option). That is the mainstream default (cf. Nicholas Perrin's argument that Thomas depends on the Diatessaron), and it does not make Thomas primitive or authoritative — only *used*.
- **"Different word for birds" can point downstream, not upstream.** Lexical variation is exactly what a paraphrasing harmonizer produces; it is not evidence of an independent early source.
- **Equal *use* is not equal *authority*.** That some 2nd-century community read Thomas alongside Matthew and Luke shows the era's textual fluidity (already documented in [[Canon - Early Fluidity and Debate]]) — but fringe or heterodox groups using a text is not the mainline church canonizing it. The eventual canon's *stability* is itself a datum the argument has to explain away.
- **The dichotomy is too clean.** "Upstream or downstream" omits live third options: shared oral tradition feeding both Thomas and the Synoptics independently, or a fragment that is neither Thomas nor a harmony but a related sayings text. The published editors' own framing is more cautious than the panel's "this nukes that stuff."
