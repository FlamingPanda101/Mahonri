# The Silent Years Argument Excludes the Apocrypha From the Canon

## The Claim
Pressed in cross-examination for a *criterion internal to scripture* by which the exact 66-book canon can be known (and the Catholic/Orthodox extra books excluded), [[Quinn Fryberg]] offers the **silent years**: Old Testament revelation closes around Nehemiah's era, after which God ceases speaking through prophets for roughly **400 years** until the New Testament era begins. Because the intertestamental (deuterocanonical) books were written *during* that prophetic silence, they are disqualified as scripture. Fryberg presents this as an argument that "you might actually be able to get rid of those [seven books]" on the timeline itself rather than by an arbitrary list.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence (Fryberg's case, with assessment)

### 1. A prophetic-silence period is a real Second Temple Jewish tradition — rate: moderate, has genuine grounding
Fryberg appeals to [[Josephus]], who "tells us there is a canon" and "actually numbers the books in the Old Testament."[^2] Josephus (*Against Apion* 1.38-42) does describe a fixed, numbered Jewish canon and states that the "exact succession of the prophets" failed after Artaxerxes — the classic proof text for an intertestamental cessation of prophecy (echoed in 1 Maccabees 9:27, "there had not been such distress since the prophets ceased to appear"). So the *notion* of a closed prophetic era is not invented by Fryberg; it reflects an ancient Jewish self-understanding.

### 2. The criterion is a *dating* rule, not a table-of-contents — rate: framing
The argument does not claim the canon lists itself; it claims a *principle* — revelation belongs to periods when God was speaking through prophets — that, combined with the historical dating of the books, sorts the disputed writings out. Fryberg concedes it is "a decent inference," not an explicit statement of the text.[^3]

## Best Quotes
> "You can actually make an argument about the timeline of Revelation in the Old Testament ending with Nehemiah's time frame, and then there are silent years... and all of those books are in between. So you might actually be able to get rid of those."[^1]

> "If those are these silent years when God is not speaking through prophets and revealing scripture... those books come during those years."[^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**Carroll's rebuttal — the criterion is not *in* the canon, and the text never states it.** Carroll presses that the 66 books nowhere teach "no scripture may be written during the silent years"; it is an inference the reader supplies, and the *dating* of which books fall in the gap is an **external historical judgment**, not an internal canonical mechanism. When Carroll asks "does the Bible say that?" Fryberg answers only that it is "a decent inference."[^3] So even if sound, the silent-years rule is supplied *from outside* the collection — which is precisely the negative's thesis that identifying the canon requires an authority the canon itself does not provide (see [[The Canon Question Is Logically Prior to Canon Authority]]).

**Further tensions the steelman must grant:** (a) the argument proves too little and too much — it addresses the Old Testament gap but says nothing about *New Testament* canon boundaries (why these 27 and not, e.g., 1 Clement or the Shepherd of Hermas); (b) the "silence" tradition is itself a *Jewish tradition* recorded in Josephus and 1 Maccabees — extrabiblical sources — so the Protestant is leaning on tradition to bound scripture; and (c) some of the very deuterocanonical books excluded (1 Maccabees) are among the historical sources *for* the silent-years framework, making the reasoning uncomfortably circular. **Fryberg's best reply:** the criterion need not be a verse-level proof text to be a *principled, non-arbitrary* pattern the inspired history exhibits, and every canon (including the LDS and Catholic canons) finally relies on some judgment about *when and through whom* God spoke. Cf. [[The Deuterocanon Was Disputed Until Trent Not Removed]] and [[Two Rival Canon Traditions Neither Settled Before Trent]] for the historical messiness the clean-timeline picture must absorb.
