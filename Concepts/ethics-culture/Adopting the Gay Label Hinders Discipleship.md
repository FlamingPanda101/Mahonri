#type/claim

# Adopting the Gay Label Hinders Discipleship

## The Claim
The panel argues that adopting **"gay" as a totalizing self-label** — an identity and a
category, rather than an adjective describing some feelings — is not merely unhelpful but a
"core piece of difficulty" for a person who wants to live the gospel. Once the label is
worn as identity, unrelated traits (tender-heartedness, musical taste, disliking sports)
become "evidence" of the category, and the culture's binary of "straight vs. gay" tells a
teenage boy with first same-sex feelings that he simply *is* one kind of person. The
antidote the panel presses (crediting Presidents [[Russell M. Nelson]] and
[[Dallin H. Oaks]]) is to keep the primary labels — *son of God, disciple of Christ, child
of the covenant* — in first position.[^1][^2]

## Supporting Evidence (as presented)

### 1. The gravity analogy — a label names, it does not explain
Robinson: we say a pen falls "because of gravity," then talk as if we have *explained* it,
when "we don't know exactly why gravity happens." The reasoning goes in a circle: *"Why is
that person attracted to other men? Well, he's gay. How do you know he's gay? Because he's
attracted to other men."* Naming a fact ("gay") is mistaken for accounting for it.[^3]
> **Evidence rating:** Strong as analogy; **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 2. The label makes everything "evidence"
"As long as they maintain that label, then suddenly all kinds of things about themselves
are evidence of that" — being tender-hearted, a music preference, not liking sports or deer
hunting — none of which is intrinsically sexual, "but... all part of being gay." Robinson:
"No, they're not. There are many, many people who have all of those attributes who don't
experience same-sex attraction."[^4]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate; **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 3. The two-category culture traps the adolescent
"As a culture... we've said there's a kind of person who is gay. That's a category. And
everybody is either in the straight category or gay category." So a teenage boy with new
feelings "knows he's in that category... knows that's who he is" — "a major problem for
someone who wants to live the gospel."[^5]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate; **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 4. Prophetic backing — the label is an adjective, not a noun
Andrew relays [[Dallin H. Oaks]]: *"homosexuality... is not a noun that describes a
condition. It's an adjective that describes feelings or behavior."* And President Nelson's
emphasis that when any other label "takes precedent or... displaces those primary labels
[son of God, disciple, child of the covenant], then that's where we start to go
sideways."[^6][^2] [[Timber Harvard|Timber Harward]] models this personally: he "kind of
hate[s] the term," uses "gay" only as a "necessary evil" for search visibility, and
identifies as "a son of God who happens to experience same-sex sexual feelings... it's a
very small facet of my mortal experience."[^1]
> **Evidence rating:** Strong (verbatim leader quotation + lived application); **Argument weight:** Primary.

## Best Quotes
> "We've labeled that fact as gravity, but then we talk about it as though we have explained it... Why is that person sexually attracted to other men? Well, he's gay. How do you know he's gay? Because he's sexually attracted to other men. This just goes in a circle."[^3]

> "I don't identify myself that way. I see myself as a son of God who happens to experience same-sex sexual feelings, but that's not a defining feature of who I am... it's a very small facet of my mortal experience."[^1]

> "Homosexuality... is not a noun that describes a condition... It's an adjective that describes feelings or behavior." — President Oaks, quoted by Andrew Robinson[^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
For many people, naming their orientation is protective, not corrosive: coming out and
using an accurate word for a real, stable feature of oneself reduces the shame, secrecy,
and isolation that the research links to poor mental-health outcomes, and family/community
acceptance is associated with markedly lower suicidality. Faithful Latter-day Saints have
themselves used "gay" or "same-sex-attracted" descriptively without abandoning covenants
(e.g., the North Star community; the Church's own former *mormonandgay.org* site permitted
"gay" as a descriptor) — so refusing the word can read as erasure. The gravity analogy also
cuts both ways: a label can be an honest, useful *description* even when it explains
nothing, exactly as "gravity" is.

The panel's reply is that its target is narrower than "never use the word": it is the label
as **totalizing identity/category**, not as adjective. On that narrower point the note and
its critics substantially converge — [[Dallin H. Oaks]]'s "adjective, not a noun," and
Timber's "I *happen to* experience" both keep the descriptive use while resisting the
identity use. The genuinely contested residue is empirical and personal: whether de-
emphasizing the label, on balance, lowers distress (the panel's clinical claim) or instead
adds a layer of self-suppression for those it does not fit — a live disagreement that the
note should not paper over.

## LDS Connection
The label critique is the panel's most-repeated theme and the pivot of its praise for
President Nelson's and President Oaks's counsel on labels; it presupposes the reframe in
[[Same-Sex Attraction Is Something a Person Learns to Do Not an Innate Identity]] and feeds
directly into [[A Feeling With No Sexual Content Is Not Homosexual]] (which strips the
"evidence" out from under the category). Andrew ties it to [[Isaiah 5.20|Isaiah 5:20]] —
"Satan's playground is definitions and words and labels." Tier C, with Oaks/Nelson quoted
as authoritative LDS ecclesiastical voices.
