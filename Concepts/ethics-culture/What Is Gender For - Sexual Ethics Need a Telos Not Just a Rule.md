# What Is Gender For — Sexual Ethics Need a Telos Not Just a Rule

#type/claim

## The Claim

[[Jacob Hansen]] argues that every argument about gay marriage, pornography, chastity, and sexual morality "ends up floating in midair" until a prior question is answered: **what is gender for?** The rules around sex "make no sense unless you know the purpose behind sexuality itself."[^1] This reframes the whole debate as **teleological before it is deontological** — you cannot evaluate a rule about male and female without first knowing what male and female are *for*.

On this framing, the stock Christian answers fail not because they reach the wrong conclusion but because they are **assertions, not explanations**. "Because God said so," "because it's unnatural" (Hansen: going to the bathroom on a toilet is also unnatural), and "it just seems wrong" all leave the operative question untouched — *what actual purpose is being violated?*[^2] A rule with no stated purpose cannot answer that.

The deeper premise: **morality is about the ideal, not merely the permissible.** "Morality is not about what is permissible. It's about what is ideal."[^3] Asking "what is the ideal sexual order for civilization?" is a question Hansen claims most people — secular or religious — have never seriously posed. Christianity, he says, "is about aligning ourselves with an ideal," and an ideal presupposes a purpose. Notably, he insists the ideal "cannot be imposed by force" — people must be free to seek it by conscience, so long as they don't violate others' rights;[^3] the argument is about *what is ideal and why*, not about legislation (see [[Consent Answers What Is Permissible Not What Marriage Is For]]).

Hansen's payoff: the purpose question sorts the three major worldviews. **Secularism** answers with consent and so never reaches purpose; **creedal Christianity** makes gender and marriage temporary and so cannot ground a purpose that survives death; **the Restoration** makes gender and family eternal and so can. He notes Jesus himself answered the "why gender" question teleologically — pointing back to Genesis, "made them male and female… the two shall become one flesh" ([[Matthew 19.3-9|Matthew 19:4-6]]) — men and women as "two complementary parts of a complete whole."[^4]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Rules are unintelligible without a purpose.** "The rules around sex make no sense unless you know the purpose behind sexuality itself." Without a telos, debates over gay marriage "float in midair."[^1] *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: this is the load-bearing framing claim, not itself an empirical datum — it stands or falls on whether ethics is teleological.*

2. **"Assertions, not explanations."** "Because God said so," "it's unnatural," "it seems wrong" — "none of those are actually explanations. They're assertions. The question remains, why?"[^2] Parallels [[Principles Are More Fundamental Than Commandments]]: a bare command without its underlying principle cannot be reasoned about. *Argument weight: Primary.*

3. **Ideal, not permissible.** The category shift from *permitted* to *ideal* is what lets the question even be asked; a permission-ethic (consent) cannot pose it.[^3] *Argument weight: Secondary — developed fully in the consent note.*

4. **Jesus grounds the rule in a purpose.** Christ answers "why gender exists" by returning to the creation pattern (Genesis via [[Matthew 19.3-9|Matthew 19:4-6]]): male and female exist "so that they can be joined together and become one thing."[^4] The biblical hint appears already in [[Genesis 2.18-19|Genesis 2:18-19]] — Adam is in a sinless, deathless, perfect world and *still* "it is not good that the man should be alone."[^5] *Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Moderate — the exegesis is defensible but the leap from "complementary pair" to a full teleology is a theological reading.*

## Best Quotes

> "Every discussion about gay marriage, pornography, chastity, and sexual morality ends up floating in midair. The reality is that the rules around sex make no sense unless you know the purpose behind sexuality itself." [^1]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

**The divine-command reply.** A creedal Christian or divine-command theist can deny that "because God commanded it" is a *mere* assertion. On their view, God's commands express his perfect, holy nature and wisdom; a command so grounded is *reason enough*, and the demand for a further human-legible telos is itself a modern-rationalist assumption, not a neutral standard. Hansen's "assertions vs. explanations" cut presupposes that a good rule must be teleologically transparent to us — which the tradition need not grant (cf. [[Divine Command Theory Is Metaethics Not Ethics]], [[God's Nature as the Standard - The Third Horn of Euthyphro]]).

**The natural-law reply.** Catholic natural-law theology already supplies a purpose for gender *without* needing eternal marriage — the procreative-and-unitive structure of the sexed body in *this* life (Aquinas). So "creedal Christianity has no answer to *what is gender for*" overstates; it has a temporal-teleological answer Hansen sets aside (pressed in [[If Marriage Ends at Death Sexual Ethics Reduce to Arbitrary Command]]).

**The secular reply.** A naturalist can argue that *teleology itself is the error*: biology retired final causes centuries ago, sex has evolutionary *causes* but no cosmic *purpose*, and deriving a normative "ideal" from a claimed natural purpose is exactly the is-ought slide Hume and Moore flagged (see [[You Cannot Derive an Ought From an Is]]). On this view "what is gender for?" is a category mistake, and consent-based ethics is a *feature*, not a failure — it refuses to smuggle metaphysics into other people's bedrooms.

**Response.** Hansen's thesis does not require that purposes be *biologically* read off nature; it locates the telos in a revealed eternal order, and concedes freedom of conscience so the objection "you're legislating metaphysics" misfires against him specifically. The live crux is therefore not *whether* purpose-talk is legitimate in the abstract but *which* account of the purpose is true — which is why he treats secularism and creedal Christianity as the real rivals (the following two notes), not as nonsense.
