#type/claim

# Pride Is Not the Antidote to Shame

## The Claim
The panel's pastoral thesis (drawn from an article by [[Andrew Robinson]] and
[[Timber Harvard|Timber Harward]], "[gay] pride is not the antidote to shame"):
affirmation *does* relieve the real, crushing shame many with SSA carry — but it relieves
it by offering a fixed gay identity ("that's how God made you, you're perfect as you are"),
and then **strands the faithful disciple** at the unanswered question *how do I then live a
covenant life?* [[Jeff Robinson]] steelmans the affirming side sympathetically, then argues
the durable antidote to shame is not pride but **humility and charity** — relief found in
"something better" than the label, by reorienting toward God.[^1][^2]

## Supporting Evidence (as presented)

### 1. The shame is real, and affirmation genuinely relieves it
Robinson insists lay members grasp "the level of shame and emotional pain" — "I grew up
thinking I'm a terrible sinful person... nobody would love me... this is disgusting." When
someone says, in effect, "that's who you really are, God made you this way, you're perfect
just the way you are," people "find relief in that."[^1]
> **Evidence rating:** Strong (a fair, non-strawman statement of the affirming position); **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 2. Why the relief is fragile — the unanswered "how do I live a life?"
Robinson's core objection: affirming voices (whether liberal therapists or well-meaning
members) can relieve the shame but "won't go the distance." The disciple asks, "okay, but
what do I do about my faith in the gospel... how do I live a life?" — and the answer keeps
returning to "stop hating yourself" (plus "maybe the church will change someday," or "in
the next life it'll be made better"), never actually answering how to *proceed* with a
covenant life now.[^3]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate; **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 3. Pride and shame are "two sides of the same unhealthy coin"; humility is the antidote
[[Timber Harvard|Timber Harward]]: the solution "is humility — humbly submitting ourselves
to God's will" and charity; as he strives to do that, "SSA is just not the issue it was."
Andrew grounds it in [[Ezra Taft Benson]]'s teaching that pride is *enmity* — "pride is the
exact opposite of love," so gay pride cannot cure shame; only love (of God and neighbor)
does.[^2][^4]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate (devotional-theological framing); **Argument weight:** Secondary.

### 4. The label as Satan's "playground of definitions"
Andrew reads [[Isaiah 5.20|Isaiah 5:20]] ("call evil good and good evil... put... bitter
for sweet") as diagnosing a war over definitions: relief built on redefining oneself into a
fixed identity is a counterfeit of the relief found in the primary labels (son of God,
disciple, child of the covenant).[^4]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate (scriptural application); **Argument weight:** Secondary.

## Best Quotes
> "Someone comes along and says, 'That's how God made you, you're perfect just the way you are,' and they find relief in that... Then if we come along and say you don't need to be that way, they'll say, 'You want me to go back to where I hated myself?'"[^1]

> "You need to accept yourself and stop hating yourself. Then, what do I do about the church? Well, I don't know, but you stop hating yourself. But they won't go the distance and tell them how to live a life."[^3]

> "Pride and shame... those are two sides of the same unhealthy coin. The solution is humility — humbly submitting ourselves to God's will and having charity."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The most serious objection is that self-acceptance is, for many, genuinely **life-saving**:
family and community acceptance is associated with sharply lower suicidality among LGBTQ
people, and shame-based approaches with higher risk. Reducing "gay pride" to the sin of
pride/enmity can dismiss hard-won dignity and read the language of a marginalized group
uncharitably; and a critic will note the panel's own alternative ("humility, submit to
God") does not, by itself, answer the same "how do I live a life?" it presses on affirming
counselors — it answers it only *given* the prior commitment that same-sex relationships are
against God's plan, which is the very point in dispute.

The note's claim is narrower than "self-acceptance is bad," and that narrowing is where it
holds: it targets self-acceptance *specifically framed as embracing a fixed, immutable gay
identity*, and its complaint is a real gap — many affirming responses do relieve shame
without offering the covenant-keeping member a livable path forward, leaving "wait for the
church to change" as the only horizon. The panel members repeatedly disclaim any wish to
shame those who struggle (Timber: "I definitely don't want to shame anyone... struggling
with sexuality"). The genuinely contested premise — that a flourishing life requires
*not* adopting that identity — is argued elsewhere in the vault, not assumed away here;
compare the empirical counter-case in [[Mixed-Orientation Marriages and LGBTQ Latter-day Saints Show Higher Wellbeing]].

This is the pastoral payoff of the whole panel: the label critique
([[Adopting the Gay Label Hinders Discipleship]]) and the focus mechanism
([[Overfocus and Overinterpretation Entrench Same-Sex Attraction]]) converge on a claim

offers a relief that also answers "how do I live a life?" It is the doctrinal counterpart to
