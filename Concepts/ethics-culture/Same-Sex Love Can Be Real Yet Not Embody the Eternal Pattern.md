# Same-Sex Love Can Be Real Yet Not Embody the Eternal Pattern

#type/claim

## The Claim

Having built the purpose-first case (see [[What Is Gender For - Sexual Ethics Need a Telos Not Just a Rule]]), [[Jacob Hansen]] applies it to actual same-sex couples with unusual charity. The LDS position, he argues, **need not deny that same-sex love is genuine, faithful, committed, or fulfilling.** He states plainly that he does not mean "same-sex relationships cannot be loving or that they cannot be deeply fulfilling," and adds, "if it were me, I don't know that I could live a life alone."[^1] Latter-day Saints "can acknowledge all of that," and "can even be happy when people find the companionship that they're longing for."[^2]

The disagreement is relocated. **"The question is not whether love exists. The question is whether that relationship can embody the pattern God is preparing his children for."**[^2] On this frame, the commandments are not expressions of disgust — the point is "not because God thinks certain sexual behaviors are yucky, but because he is trying to guide his children toward an eternal pattern, a family pattern, a pattern of a father, a mother, and a child."[^3] The union of male and female is cast as "not merely about reproduction" but about "becoming something in communion with another person, something eternal, something divine."[^3]

The load-bearing distinction: **"Exaltation is not merely about affection. It's about participation in a divine order"**[^2] — the same pattern in Genesis ([[Genesis 1.26-27|Genesis 1:27]], [[Genesis 2.24|Genesis 2:24]]), reaffirmed by Christ ([[Matthew 19.3-9|Matthew 19:4-6]]), and restored by modern revelation. So a relationship can be full of real love and still not be the *form* that exaltation, as Hansen defines it, consists in. (The complementarity metaphysics behind "the male-female form" is developed in [[Male and Female Balance Is Required for the Divine Image and Human Flourishing]]; the doctrinal-change version of the debate in [[Gay Marriage Is Not Analogous to the Priesthood Ban or Polygamy]].)

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Explicit concession of genuine love.** Hansen refuses to say same-sex relationships can't be loving or fulfilling, and personalizes the difficulty of a life without companionship.[^1] This is a deliberate anti-strawman move that strengthens the argument's credibility (cf. the pastoral posture of [[Meet People Where They Are Without Surrendering the Doctrine]]). *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong — it is a stated concession, not an inference.*

2. **The reframed question.** The dispute is shifted from *the reality of the love* (conceded) to *whether the relationship embodies the eternal pattern* (the actual disagreement).[^2] *Argument weight: Primary.*

3. **Commandments as invitation, not disgust.** The rule is framed teleologically — guidance "toward an eternal pattern… a father, a mother, and a child" — explicitly denying a revulsion-based rationale.[^3] This is what distinguishes the LDS grounding from the "it's unnatural / it seems wrong" assertions the video rejects at the outset. *Argument weight: Secondary.*

4. **Exaltation as participation, not affection.** The union is "something eternal, something divine," and exaltation is "participation in a divine order," so affection — however real — is a necessary but insufficient feature.[^2][^3] *Argument weight: Primary — this is the hinge the whole application turns on.*

## Best Quotes

> "This is not to say that I believe same-sex relationships cannot be loving, or that they cannot be deeply fulfilling. And to be honest, if it were me, I don't know that I could live a life alone." [^1]

> "The question is not whether love exists. The question is whether that relationship can embody the pattern God is preparing his children for. Because exaltation is not merely about affection. It's about participation in a divine order." [^2]

> "Not because God thinks certain sexual behaviors are yucky, but because he is trying to guide his children toward an eternal pattern, a family pattern, a pattern of a father, a mother, and a child." [^3]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

**Love and fidelity are the theologically weightiest features — so the "pattern" inverts the priorities.** The strongest affirming reply grants the framing and turns it: if God *is* love, then a covenant of faithful, self-giving love images him most fully, and an account that admits loveless-but-heterosexual unions into the eternal pattern while excluding faithful same-sex love has arguably ranked *form* above *the very thing scripture most exalts*. The affirming Christian says the "divine order" should be read as an order of *love*, which same-sex covenants can instantiate.

**Granting the good while withholding its end can wound more, not less.** A candid objection: telling someone their love is real, good, and even enviable — yet permanently barred from its fulfillment — can be experienced as a *crueler* message than a blunt prohibition, because it concedes everything except the outcome. The pastoral gentleness may not soften the underlying "no"; it may sharpen it. This is a cost the view should own, not paper over.

**Scriptural room for non-standard vocations.** Affirming theologians read the Genesis/one-flesh texts as describing a *norm* rather than an *exhaustive rule*, and point to Jesus's own words on those "born eunuchs" and "eunuchs for the kingdom" ([[Matthew 19.3-9|Matthew 19]]:12) and Paul's relativizing of marriage in 1 Corinthians 7 as scriptural space for lifelong callings outside the male-female-child pattern. The pattern, they argue, is central without being the only sanctioned path.

**Response.** Hansen's frame concedes the love and even the tragedy, so it does not rest on denying anyone's experience; the disagreement is explicitly about *what exaltation is* — participation in a gendered, eternal divine order — not about the worth of the affection. That relocation is honest, but it does not by itself *establish* that the divine order is gendered-and-eternal; it presupposes the metaphysics argued for elsewhere (the eternal-gender case of [[If Marriage Ends at Death Sexual Ethics Reduce to Arbitrary Command]] and [[Male and Female Balance Is Required for the Divine Image and Human Flourishing]]). So the affirming steelman is not refuted here — it is *correctly located*: the real crux is whether the eternal order is truly male-female in form, which is why the whole debate reduces to purpose ([[What Is Gender For - Sexual Ethics Need a Telos Not Just a Rule]]) rather than to the reality of anyone's love.
