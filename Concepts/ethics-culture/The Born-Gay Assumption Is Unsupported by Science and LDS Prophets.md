#type/claim

# The Born-Gay Assumption Is Unsupported by Science and LDS Prophets

## The Claim
[[Jeff Robinson]] and [[Andrew Robinson]] argue that the widely-held premise that people
are "born gay" is an **unproven assumption**, not an established fact — and that it is
supported by neither the science nor the teaching of Latter-day Saint prophets. There is a
*predisposition* (some genetic/temperamental element), but predisposition is "far
different than cause"; no research has demonstrated a determinative genetic cause, and no
prophet has taught that God makes people gay. Andrew's pastoral point: when a teacher
answers a youth's "why would God allow someone to be born gay?" either with a good-faith
reason *or* with an honest "I don't know," **both answers silently reinforce the
born-gay assumption** — and thereby go against both the (silent) science and the (silent)
prophets, while warping the youth's picture of God's fairness.[^1][^2]

## Supporting Evidence (as presented)

### 1. Predisposition is not cause
Robinson: *"if there wasn't some sort of genetic element to it, we'd study it in
amazement — it's the only thing in human behavior that has no genetic component. But
that's far different than cause."* A predisposing vulnerability exists; a deterministic
"gay gene" that *causes* orientation has not been shown.[^1]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate; **Argument weight:** Primary. (Accurately concedes a biological *contribution* while denying biological *determination*.)

### 2. The research does not exist to settle it
Robinson notes the candidate causal factors that *have* been examined — father-son
relationships, childhood experiences — "none of them have proved definitive," and that
rigorous causal research is effectively unfundable: *"You can't do research on this.
Nobody's going to fund research on this."*[^3]
> **Evidence rating:** Weak-to-moderate; **Argument weight:** Secondary. (This cuts both ways — see the steelman: absence of funded proof is not proof of absence.)

### 3. Prophets have not taught it, so affirming it can mislead
Andrew: science "hasn't shown" a clear genetic component "and neither have prophets.
Prophets haven't said that either." Teachers who answer the youth's question with a
good-faith reason *why* God made someone gay, or with "I don't know why God would allow
someone to be born [that way]," are "inadvertently reinforcing the hidden assumption that
they're born gay," and "inadvertently going against the brethren."[^2]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate (accurate as to the absence of such a teaching); **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 4. The stakes: a "fair God"
Andrew ties the assumption to theodicy: if "God made somebody this way" and the way is
forbidden, then "this isn't fair... my view of God just gets really warped." Identifying
"born gay" *as an assumption* and questioning it "and then all of a sudden... God's not to
blame."[^4]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate; **Argument weight:** Secondary.

## Best Quotes
> "If there wasn't some sort of genetic element to it, we'd study it in amazement. It's the only thing in human behavior that has no genetic component. But that's far different than cause. Far different than cause."[^1]

> "Science hasn't shown that. And neither have prophets. Prophets haven't said that either... teachers are inadvertently reinforcing the assumption when they give one of those responses. And when they do that, they're actually going against the brethren."[^2]

> "You can't do research on this. Nobody's going to fund research on this."[^3]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The strongest reply is that the panel understates the science. Twin, family, and
genome-wide studies indicate a **real but partial** heritable contribution to sexual
orientation; the large 2019 genome-wide association study found many small-effect variants
(no single "gay gene," but a genuine polygenic signal), alongside prenatal-hormone and
fraternal-birth-order findings. The mainstream scientific bodies hold that orientation
arises from a complex mix of biological and environmental factors and is **not** simply a
developmental error or a chosen behavior — so "not proven to be genetically *determined*"
is true but is a much weaker claim than "the born-gay idea is unsupported." Robinson's
"nobody funds this research" is also questionable as stated; considerable orientation
research exists. And prophetic *silence* on causation is not evidence *against* a
biological contribution — it is simply silence.

Where the note's claim survives: it is genuinely correct that (a) no study has shown
orientation to be **strictly genetically determined**, (b) predisposition ≠ cause is a
sound methodological distinction, and (c) the Church's official position (e.g.,
[[Dallin H. Oaks]]) has deliberately *declined* to pronounce on ultimate causation. The
defensible version is narrow — "science has not established that people are *simply born*
gay, and the Church has not taught it" — not the broader suggestion that biology plays
little role. Note too that the pastoral payoff (restored agency, an unblamed God) does not
depend on the *empirical* question either way; it depends on the label critique in
[[Adopting the Gay Label Hinders Discipleship]].

## LDS Connection
This note is the assumption-audit at the heart of the panel's method ("pull the weeds so
faith can grow"): rather than answer *why* God would make someone gay, question whether
He did. It pairs with [[Same-Sex Attraction Is Something a Person Learns to Do Not an
Innate Identity]] (the positive reframe) and defends a "fair God" against a theodicy the
born-gay premise would otherwise create. Tier C, Andrew (educator) and Robinson
(clinician); the scientific counter-case is the stronger *empirical* voice and is carried
in full above.
