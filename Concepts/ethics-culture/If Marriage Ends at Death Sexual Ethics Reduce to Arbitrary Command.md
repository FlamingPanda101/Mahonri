# If Marriage Ends at Death Sexual Ethics Reduce to Arbitrary Command

#type/claim

## The Claim

This is [[Jacob Hansen]]'s candidate for "the real reason," aimed not at secularism but at **creedal Christianity**. In the creedal tradition, "Christians believe marriage ends at death. It's simply a mortal institution — something for this life only."[^1] Hansen's argument is that this concession is *self-undermining* for sexual ethics: **if marriage and gender are temporary, then God's intense concern for male-female union has no eternal purpose to appeal to, and the male-female rule collapses into arbitrary command.**

The reasoning: "If marriage is temporary and gender is temporary, then why does gender matter so much to God?"[^2] On the creedal view, "the beauty of the masculine and feminine interplay… all become mere temporary biological arrangements."[^2] From this Hansen draws a sharp parity problem: "If it's just biological and temporary, then what is the meaningful difference between a gay couple and an infertile male and female couple?"[^3] Since an infertile heterosexual couple also cannot reproduce, reproduction alone cannot be the differentiator — so with no *eternal* purpose in play, the insistence on male-and-female specifically looks unmotivated. The conclusion: "Sexual morality in the creedal Christian world becomes grounded essentially in arbitrary command rather than some larger eternal purpose."[^4]

The contrast (developed in [[What Is Gender For - Sexual Ethics Need a Telos Not Just a Rule]]) is that only a framework in which **gender itself is eternal** can answer the "why male-and-female, and why does God care forever?" question with a *purpose* rather than a *fiat*. See [[Anthropomorphic God Grounds Heavenly Mother and Eternal Families]] for the eternal-family metaphysics this leans on.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Creedal marriage is mortal-only.** The mainstream Christian reading of [[Matthew 22.30|Matthew 22:30]] ("in the resurrection they neither marry nor are given in marriage") treats marriage as a this-life institution. The video illustrates with a believer's own words: "we won't be married to the same people in heaven that we were on earth… we are going to be so fulfilled in heaven that we won't need the fulfillment of marriage."[^1] *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong as a description of the majority creedal position.*

2. **Temporary gender drains the "why."** If male and female dissolve at death, the gendered familial world (husband, wife, child, grandparent) has no continuation, so God's weighting of it looks disproportionate to a merely biological, expiring arrangement.[^2] *Argument weight: Primary.*

3. **The infertile-couple parity problem.** With reproduction unavailable to both a same-sex couple and an infertile heterosexual couple, and no eternal telos to distinguish them, the creedal defender lacks a *principled* line — the difference reduces to bare stipulation.[^3] *Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: this is the sharpest edge of the argument — and the point most vulnerable to the counter below.*

4. **Therefore: command without purpose.** The residue is "arbitrary command rather than some larger eternal purpose"[^4] — which, per [[What Is Gender For - Sexual Ethics Need a Telos Not Just a Rule]], is exactly the "assertion, not explanation" that the whole video targets. *Argument weight: Secondary — it is the conclusion the prior three points drive toward.*

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

**"Temporary" does not entail "arbitrary" — the natural-law answer.** This is the strongest creedal reply. Catholic and classical-Protestant natural-law theology grounds the male-female norm in the **procreative-and-unitive structure of the sexed body in this life** (Aquinas), a purpose that needs no eternal marriage at all. Marriage can be a temporal creation-ordinance with a real *this-worldly* telos, so Hansen's dilemma ("either eternal or arbitrary") is a **false dilemma** that omits temporal teleology. Many things are non-arbitrary yet mortal (feeding the hungry, keeping promises); temporariness alone never implies arbitrariness.

**Penultimate is not meaningless.** The creedal reading of [[Matthew 22.30|Matthew 22:30]] is that earthly marriage is *fulfilled and transcended* in a greater reality — union with God, the marriage of the Lamb — much as a betrothal is completed, not annulled, by the wedding. The widow in the clip says exactly this: not that marriage was pointless, but that its good is surpassed. A good that points beyond itself is not thereby arbitrary (compare the temple or Sabbath, temporary yet purposeful). See [[Matthew 22-30 - No Marriage in Resurrection Rebutted]] for the LDS parsing of the same verse.

**The divine-command defender bites the bullet.** A creedal theist can say commands rooted in God's holy nature are not "arbitrary" even absent a human-legible telos, because arbitrariness means *without reason*, and God's nature is the reason (see [[God's Nature as the Standard - The Third Horn of Euthyphro]]). "Arbitrary" is doing contested work in Hansen's argument.

**The parity problem cuts both ways.** The infertile-couple point rebounds on the LDS view: the Church itself sanctions and seals infertile and post-menopausal male-female marriages *for eternity*, so appeal to eternal *reproduction* cannot be the LDS differentiator either. The LDS answer must be the *form* of the union (an eternal male-female pattern), not its fertility — which means the honest LDS line is closer to the natural-law "form of the act" answer than the video's framing admits.

**Response.** Hansen's claim survives in a narrower form: natural-law's *temporal* telos still leaves unanswered *why God should care about it eternally* and still faces the infertile-couple parity at the level of reproduction — so it explains the rule for *this* life but not God's evident weighting of it as ultimate. The LDS move relocates the telos from reproduction to an **eternal male-female form** (the father-mother-child pattern of [[Same-Sex Love Can Be Real Yet Not Embody the Eternal Pattern]]), which is precisely what answers "why forever." But the steelman lands a real hit: "temporary therefore arbitrary" is too strong as stated, and the cleanest reading of Hansen is comparative (the eternal frame grounds the norm *better*), not that creedal Christianity's ethic is literally groundless.
