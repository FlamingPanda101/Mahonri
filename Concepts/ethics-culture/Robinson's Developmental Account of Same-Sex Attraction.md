# Robinson's Developmental Account of Same-Sex Attraction

## The Claim
[[Jeff Robinson]] offers a clinical, **developmental** account of how same-sex attraction
typically forms in the men he has counseled — his answer to his clients' near-universal
"why do I have these feelings? How did it happen?" He describes three predisposing
childhood **traits**, then two common **onset patterns**. He is careful to frame this as a
predisposition and a developmental narrative — the "how did it happen" — explicitly *not* a
proven cause, and not deterministic.[^1][^2] (The maintenance side — the five factors that
"lock it in" — is treated in [[Overfocus and Overinterpretation Entrench Same-Sex Attraction]].)

## Supporting Evidence (as presented)

### 1. Three predisposing traits
Robinson reports that his clients, in childhood/adolescence, tend to share three traits (in
varying strength): **(a) unusually emotionally sensitive** ("I felt things more deeply, I
cry more easily, I'm more tender-hearted"); **(b) unusually introspective / self-analytical**
("I overthink... I think myself into knots"); and **(c) an unusually strong sense of right
and wrong** — a strong desire "to be right, to be correct, to be normal... worthy," broadly
construed.[^1]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate as clinical pattern-observation; **Argument weight:** Primary.

### 2. Onset pattern A — "overly chaste"
About half fit this: warned young about chastity, the boy resolves "I'm not going to think
sexual thoughts about women... but nobody warned me about men." Robinson reports that, asked
which they'd feel guiltier about — fantasy about a man or a woman — "over half will say...
the woman," often "twice as guilty."[^3]
> **Evidence rating:** Weak-to-moderate (striking but uncontrolled clinical anecdote); **Argument weight:** Secondary.

### 3. Onset pattern B — "felt different / rejected by other men"
The rest more often report early feeling "different from or rejected by other men" — father,
brothers, or peers — "and it wasn't a good different." That produced envy, longing to belong,
and fascination/curiosity about other males; at puberty the easily-triggered arousal fused
with that already-intense focus on men, sometimes via same-sex pornography sought out of
curiosity.[^4]
> **Evidence rating:** Weak-to-moderate; **Argument weight:** Secondary.

### 4. A lived case that only partly fits — Timber
[[Timber Harvard|Timber Harward]] tests the model against himself: the classic "father
wound" story (associated with [[Joseph Nicolosi]]'s reparative theory) "was never my
story." But he *can* see how early factors — a first sexual encounter with a boy his age,
peer conflict, feeling alone and ostracized, "not having any close male friends" — "may have
become sexualized for me." Crucially, he adds that *knowing the cause* "hasn't fixed the
issue... it doesn't make it go away," though it brings "some closure."[^5]
> **Evidence rating:** Moderate (firsthand, and notably self-critical of the model); **Argument weight:** Primary (it both illustrates and limits the account).

## Best Quotes
> "Individuals who experience same-sex attraction seem to have three traits in common... one, they're unusually emotionally sensitive... two, unusually introspective, self-analytical... and three, an unusually strong sense of right and wrong."[^1]

> "'I'm not going to think sexual thoughts about women,' but nobody warned me about men... over half will say, 'I feel more guilty about the woman.'"[^3]

> "Coming to understand what caused it hasn't fixed the issue. It hasn't made my same-sex attraction go away just by knowing the cause." — Timber Harward[^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
This is the note most vulnerable to the charge that it revives the **discredited reparative
/ developmental model** (weak fathers, unmet male-bonding needs, sexualized deficits)
associated with [[Joseph Nicolosi]] and rejected by the American Psychological Association
and allied bodies as scientifically unsupported and linked to harm. The traits Robinson
lists (sensitivity, introspection, moral scrupulosity) are common in the general population
and unfalsifiable as "predisposition"; the guilt-asymmetry and "felt different" narratives
are retrospective self-report, subject to recall bias and to the very cultural scripts the
panel elsewhere critiques. And Robinson himself concedes no research confirms any of it.

What keeps the note honest is that it front-loads these limits: Robinson explicitly (a)
distances himself from single-cause father-wound theory, offering multiple patterns; (b)
rates the account as clinical observation, not proof, and unfundable/unconfirmed; and (c)
lets [[Timber Harvard|Timber Harward]]'s testimony *undercut* the model's therapeutic
promise — Timber's case doesn't fit the father-wound story, and understanding the cause did
**not** remove the attraction. Read as "etiology," the account overreaches; read as "a
shame-reducing narrative that gives clients closure on *how did it happen*," which is how
Robinson and Timber both frame it, it is more defensible. The distinction matters and the
note preserves it.

## LDS Connection
This supplies the "how did it happen" content that the reframe in [[Same-Sex Attraction Is Something a Person Learns to Do Not an Innate Identity]] gestures at, and it is deliberately
paired with [[The Born-Gay Assumption Is Unsupported by Science and LDS Prophets]]:
predisposition and development, not genetic determination. Its therapeutic value, per the
panel, is closure and reduced shame — *not* a promise of change, a limit Timber states
outright. Tier C; Robinson's clinical status leans B, the empirical ceiling set by his own
concession and the professional-consensus critique carried above.
