# Apply the Extraordinary Evidence Standard to Materialism Itself

## The Claim

[[Alex Arnold]] argues that philosophical materialism — the claim that "matter and energy operating under natural laws" is "the complete and sufficient explanation for everything that exists" — is a *positive metaphysical claim* that carries its own burden of proof, and that when Carl Sagan's own standard ("extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence") is applied *symmetrically*, materialism is "the least defensible position on the table," not the rational default.[^1] The claim that a fine-tuned universe of conscious beings who independently produced identical theological frameworks "arose by accident with no guiding intelligence" is "about as extraordinary a claim as human thought has ever produced" — making "God exists look pretty modest by comparison."[^2] The materialist's escape hatches — the multiverse, self-resolving abiogenesis, consciousness reducing to neural complexity — are each themselves faith claims held "without any observational support."[^3]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Materialism is a substantive claim, not the absence of one (Argument weight: Primary).** New atheism spent two decades "conflating the deliverances of empirical science with the conclusions of philosophical materialism" — treating "because science explains *how* things work, materialism is therefore true." But "science studies the physical world; materialism is the claim that the physical world is all there is," so the second claim "needs its own evidence." The confidence borrowed from science "doesn't really belong there."[^4]

2. **Two burden-shifting tactics named (Argument weight: Primary).** (a) *Definitional retreat* — redefining atheism as "merely the absence of belief" to escape the burden while still positively arguing "that consciousness is just neural complexity, that religion evolved from fear and ignorance, and that the universe arose through natural processes." (b) *Russell's Teapot* — insisting theism "bears the entire burden of proof because nobody's proved a teapot orbits the sun." Both establish only the narrow point that "you aren't required to believe a claim with no supporting evidence" — and whether theism is in that category "is exactly what this episode addresses."[^5]

3. **The standard applied symmetrically indicts the multiverse (Argument weight: Secondary).** The multiverse posits "an infinite number of unobservable, untestable entities specifically to avoid the conclusion that this universe was designed." By the atheist's own test — "unfalsifiable, unobservable, and requiring belief in entities beyond detection" — "the multiverse itself is a faith claim," not in the religious sense but "in the epistemic sense of holding a belief without any observational support."[^3]

4. **Both positions require faith, so the question shifts (Argument weight: Primary).** "The multiverse is a faith claim. Abiogenesis resolving itself is also a faith claim. Consciousness turning out to be reducible to neural complexity is... a faith claim. None of those have current evidentiary support. They have hope."[^6] Once both positions are seen to require commitments, "there is no neutral ground," and "the question shifted from faith or reason to which account does the available evidence actually support."[^7] Arnold enlists [[David Berlinski]], who argues in *The Devil's Delusion* that new-atheist confidence "vastly exceeds its evidential warrant."[^8]

## Best Quotes

> "The claim that all this happened by accident with no guiding intelligence is in fact the most extraordinary faith claim in the room." [^9]

> "Because science explains how things work, materialism is therefore true. But those aren't the same claim." [^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

**Objection 1 — Lack of belief really is the null hypothesis.** The atheist may insist that withholding assent to an unproven claim is epistemically free; the theist is the one asserting an entity, so the teapot analogy holds and no positive materialist metaphysic is entailed.
*Response:* Arnold concedes the narrow point — you needn't believe an unsupported claim — but notes most atheists do not *behave* as mere abstainers: they positively argue consciousness is neural, religion is fear, and the cosmos is self-caused. Those are claims about reality, not states of mind (compare [[Atheism Is a Worldview With the Burden of Proof]]). The "lack of belief" definition is reserved for the burden question and abandoned everywhere else.

**Objection 2 — This is tu quoque; two faith claims don't make theism true.** Showing that materialism also rests on unproven commitments doesn't establish design; it may just leave genuine agnosticism.
*Response:* Correct, and Arnold explicitly declines to claim proof. The move is not "materialism has faith, therefore God," but "neither side stands on neutral ground, so compare which framework the *available* evidence fits better" — an inference to the best explanation, not a proof (compare [[Skepticism Must Be Applied Consistently to Atheism Too]] and [[Extraordinary Claims Require Extraordinary Evidence - Craig vs Hitchens]]).

**Objection 3 — The multiverse is independently motivated.** Multiverse cosmology "falls out" of inflation and string theory rather than being invented to dodge fine-tuning, so the "faith claim" charge is unfair.
*Response:* See [[Multiverse as Ad-Hoc Escape from Fine-Tuning]] — the rebuttal grants the theoretical pedigree but notes the *current* empirical status is zero direct observation, and that a generating mechanism capable of producing life-permitting universes is itself specified.

## LDS Connection
The Restoration refuses to let naturalism pose as the neutral default and treats the existence and character of God as a genuine, investigable question — Arnold closes by telling LDS viewers they "already live inside a framework that... has no structural conflict with science."[^7] This note concerns method and burden of proof, shared ground with all theists; doctrinal specifics diverge only later.
