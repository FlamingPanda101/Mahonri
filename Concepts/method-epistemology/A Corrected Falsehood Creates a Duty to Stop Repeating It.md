# A Corrected Falsehood Creates a Duty to Stop Repeating It

## The Claim
A Ward Radio host argues for a **reciprocal intellectual-honesty obligation**: once a *specific* factual claim has been corrected with primary sources — his example is "Joseph Smith was just a treasure digger" — the critic is "morally obligated to stop repeating that trope," even though he remains entirely free to keep rejecting the faith.[^1] The point is carefully bounded: correcting a fact does not obligate anyone to believe Joseph was a prophet, only to stop asserting the debunked particular. "I haven't told you you must believe that he was a prophet ... you don't have to stop saying Mormons are wrong. But ... you're now morally obligated to stop saying Joseph Smith was just a treasure digger."[^2] The move mirrors the "intellectual honesty pledge" [[Devin Barnes]] posts atop his own site, which the host says should run in both directions.[^3]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The obligation attaches to the particular, not the conclusion — rate: strong (a clean distinction)
The argument does not demand conversion or even agreement; it isolates the narrow duty not to re-assert a claim one now knows to be false. This keeps it from being a rhetorical trap: the critic keeps his verdict ("Mormons are wrong") and surrenders only a falsified premise.[^2] This is the constructive complement to [[Recycling Old Lies Is Anti-Mormon Standard Practice]] — where that note diagnoses the *tactic* of citing pre-rebuttal sources, this states the *duty* the tactic violates.

### 2. It generalizes the honesty pledge symmetrically — rate: moderate
Barnes's website opens with a pledge — "you deserve to know the rules I operate by ... if I violate any of these commitments, I want you to know about it."[^3] The host's contribution is that a reader who demands such honesty of an apologist incurs the same standard: "there should almost be an intellectual honesty pledge for people that are going to read your website."[^3] The apologist grants critics "plenty of reasons why you can disagree with our faith and be a rational and kind person" — so the demand is not to stop criticizing, only to "not have to lie to kick it."[^4]

### 3. The treasure-digging case is the worked example — rate: illustrative
The specific trope invoked — that Joseph was "just some benile treasure digger who stumbled upon some trinket and invented a religion" — is one the vault treats at length: folk treasure-seeking was an ordinary cultural practice, not evidence of fraud (see [[Treasure Hunting as Historical Cultural Practice]], [[Treasure Law and Biblical Precedent]]). The host's claim is that once shown "primary sources and historical materials" on this, continuing to reduce Joseph to a con-man treasure-digger becomes dishonest rather than merely mistaken.[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **"Morally obligated" overstates it.** Repeating a claim you have merely *heard* corrected, but not been persuaded the correction is right, is at most careless, not immoral. The critic may reasonably think the apologist's "primary sources" are contested or spun; he owes diligence, not deference. The duty is real only where the correction is genuinely decisive and understood — otherwise "you're now obligated to stop saying X" is itself a burden-shift.
- **The line between fact and interpretation is where the fights actually are.** "Treasure digger" is partly factual (did the Smith family money-dig? yes) and partly interpretive (does that make him a fraud?). A critic can honestly keep the fact while dropping the caricature — so the obligation bites on the *spin*, not the underlying datum, and the two must be kept distinct or the demand overreaches.
- **It cuts toward apologists too.** The symmetry the host invokes obligates the LDS side to retire *its* corrected talking points with equal speed — a standard popular apologetics does not always meet (compare [[Recycling Old Lies Is Anti-Mormon Standard Practice]], which concedes "recycling" is a property of all popular polemics).

## Best Quotes
> "You've been corrected. I haven't told you you must believe that he was a prophet ... you don't have to stop saying Mormons are wrong. But I kind of feel like you're now morally obligated to stop saying Joseph Smith was just a treasure digger."[^2]

> "You don't have to lie to kick it."[^4]
