# Christlike Love Softened a Hostile Debater - The Las Vegas Miracle

## The Claim
Two Latter-day Saint content creators, [[Latterday Logic]] (Devin) and [[Gospel Guardian]] (Braden), debated [[Sam Shamoun]] — a polemicist with a reputation for "steamrolling people, insulting them personally, insulting their mothers" — on Brian Davila's channel "The Way." Rather than winning on points, they adopted a deliberate posture of Christlike love and respect, and Shamoun responded by publicly declaring he had *lost the debate because his heart was changed*. The panel offers this as **"exhibit A" that debate can make "the love of Jesus Christ manifest,"** i.e. that right practice (orthopraxy) can persuade where combative argument (weaponized orthodoxy) cannot.[^1][^2]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Shamoun's own on-camera reversal (primary)
In a ten-minute response on his own channel, Shamoun said his heart was changed: "It became harder for me to want to debate anyone after this debate. After seeing how beautiful and loving those two young men were, it's hard for me to debate... I fell in love with these people."[^3] Before filming he told the room, "I know all of you are wanting a UFC blood bath, but that's not going to happen today. I love these two."[^4] **Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong** for the fact of the reversal (it is his recorded testimony), weaker as proof of a durable change.

### 2. The strategy was love, deliberately chosen (primary)
The guests say that the night before, both independently felt they should "pray that this becomes a constructive friendly conversation" though it "seemed impossible."[^5] They fasted, and Gospel Guardian bought Shamoun a personalized triple combination (Book of Mormon set).[^6] They opened the debate with prayer and gifted him the scriptures.[^7]

### 3. Prepared "gotchas" abandoned mid-debate (secondary)
Devin recounts lining up "zingers" and a "doctrinally sound, logical trap he could not get out of," then repeatedly getting the impression "don't say that" and having to improvise — reading the softened outcome as the fruit of the Spirit rather than of rhetoric: "clearly here this isn't something that we did... this is obviously something by the Spirit."[^8]

### 4. The method thesis (the point)
Answering the perennial "why debate at all — you're not baptizing anyone," the guests conclude: debate is worthwhile "not only... to make a strong case for Latter-day Saint theology, but to have the love of Jesus Christ be made manifest." A man known for cruelty "completely had a change of heart."[^2] This is a concrete instance of [[Orthopraxy over Orthodoxy]].

## Best Quotes
> "It became harder for me to want to debate anyone after this debate... it's hard for me to debate. I don't know what to do. I fell in love with these people." — Sam Shamoun [^3]

> "Here's exhibit A of why debates could be a good idea. Not only just to make a strong case for Latter-day Saint theology, but to have the love of Jesus Christ be made manifest." [^2]

> "I don't consider it anything less than a miracle. Through the Spirit of God and through the love of Jesus Christ, the hardest people can be softened, and bridges can be built where nobody thought possible." [^9]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The panel steelmans the skeptic itself. Host [[Cardon Ellis]] cautions against over-reading one event: "Let's not start giving him human-being status yet... let's see two or three other well-behaved debates" before crediting a real change of character.[^10] Fair objections a critic would press:
- **One anecdote, no conversion.** Shamoun did not change his theology or join the Church; an emotional response to likeable people is not evidence that a doctrine is true. Warmth and truth are independent variables.
- **Selection and framing.** This is an LDS channel reviewing its own performance and playing clips it chose; the "miracle" reading is self-flattering and unfalsifiable ("it was the Spirit").
- **Non-repeatable.** The same men admit they *expected* the opposite from Shamoun's entire prior record; if love reliably softened polemicists, his 25-year track record would not exist. The honest reading is that this is a hopeful data point about tone and Christian charity in disagreement — not a demonstration that Latter-day Saint claims are correct, which still turns on the theological arguments (monarchy of the Father, creation, exaltation) canvassed elsewhere in the same source.
