# The Ex-Mormon Grounds His Bible on the Same Heart-Choice He Rejects in Moroni 10

## The Claim (Rebuttal)
**[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** left the Church because "the words of Jesus Christ" in the Bible "did not match what I had been taught," and he judges Latter-day Saint doctrine by the standard of an **infallible King James Bible**. **[[Hayden Carroll]]** rebuts not the Bible but the *standard's justification*: pressed on how he knows the Bible is infallible, Mark repeatedly answers "personal belief" and "personal conviction" — and, decisively, concedes he believes the Bible "the same way that investigators believe Moroni 10:3-5 is true. You just simply read it and said, 'Okay, I believe that.'"[^1] The rebuttal: Mark's authority for critiquing Mormonism rests on the *identical* subjective heart-choice ([[Moroni 10.4-5|Moroni 10:3-5]], the "burning in the bosom") that, as a critic, he treats as unreliable when a Latter-day Saint uses it. A standard that reduces to "I chose to believe it" cannot do the external, adjudicating work his critique needs.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Mark states the parallel himself.** On why he trusts the Gospels' accuracy: "That is personal belief. I choose to believe that that is what happened. How do members know that Moroni 10:3-5 is God's test of truth? You heard it and you made a choice in your heart... and that's what belief is."[^2] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Strong* — this is not Carroll's characterization but Mark's own equation of his Bible-warrant with the Moroni-10 mechanism.

2. **"The Bible doesn't even mention the Bible."** To Mark's "I'm convinced the Bible is what it says it is," Carroll notes the canon does not name or delimit itself; believing the 66-book collection is infallible is therefore a commitment brought *to* the text, not read *from* it.[^3] See [[Knowing Scripture Is the Word of God Requires an Authority Outside Scripture]] and [[The Word of God Fallacy - The Load-Bearing Premise Is Unproven]]. *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate.*

3. **King-James-onlyism rests on feeling, not language.** Mark affirms the KJV / Textus Receptus as "the most correct of all translations" and "a supernatural book," but concedes he does not read Greek or Hebrew; it is "a personal conviction... a personal belief I have."[^4] Carroll: "so this is just a feeling that you have essentially." See [[The Translation Question Has No Objectively Correct Answer]]. *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Moderate* — it shows the standard is doubly subjective (this collection, in this translation, on conviction).

4. **The "justify your standard" demand.** Carroll's general apologetic move: "before you judge me by a particular standard, I would like you to justify that standard" — and when Protestant critics "can't even tell me why they believe in it, it really doesn't do a lot for their arguments."[^5] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate.*

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**Mark's side has real replies**, and fairness requires stating them:

- **Perspicuity: you don't need a scholar.** Mark's consistent answer is that Scripture's saving message is plain — "a third grader can understand" John 6:47 — so ordinary readers can rightly grasp it without a teaching magisterium or the biblical languages. On this view "personal conviction" is not arbitrary but the natural response to a self-evidencing text (the Protestant doctrine of the Spirit's internal witness). Carroll's own positive model is not far off — he too grounds ultimate authority in the Spirit, not a book — which blunts the charge of a *unique* Mormon subjectivity; see [[Burning in the Bosom vs Self-Evidencing Light]].

- **Not all convictions are equal.** A defender could argue Mark's belief in the Bible is *better* warranted than a burning-bosom witness — by historicity, manuscript evidence, and continuity — even if Mark, in the moment, articulates it only as "personal belief." His gestured "overwhelming evidence supports the claim that the Bible is the word of God" is a promissory note toward an evidential (not merely fideistic) case. The rebuttal lands only to the degree Mark *actually* rests on conviction rather than argument — which, on this transcript, he largely does.

- **Symmetry cuts both ways.** If the heart-choice discredits Mark's Bible, the same symmetry pressures the Latter-day Saint's Moroni-10 witness. Carroll's aim is therefore not to destroy testimony-based knowledge but to level the field: neither side may wield "you just have a subjective feeling" as a one-way weapon. That is a tu-quoque (a real logical limitation), not a positive proof of Restoration claims — an honest reader should note the argument neutralizes rather than vindicates.
