# Calvinism Turns God Into a Moral Monster That Breeds Atheism

## The Claim (Objection Rebutted)
Reviewing his debate with the Reformed evangelist [[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]], [[Jacob Hansen]] argues that consistent Calvinism does not merely get a doctrine wrong — it disfigures God's *character* into "a moral monster... not worthy of worship," and that this very portrait is a leading cause of modern unbelief. The engine is double predestination: God could give everyone "a new heart" and save them all, but withholds it from most, creating the majority of humanity precisely so that "they would go to hell so that God could display his justice."

## The Rebuttal

### 1. The Calvinist God authored the Fall itself (Argument weight: Primary)
On Ryan's radical-sovereignty view, the Fall was not a risk God permitted but a script he wrote: God "chose to put Adam and Eve in the garden with the tree knowing that they would eat of it. He created the tree that would damn humanity forever. He created the snake that was in the garden."[^1] There was "no tree in the Garden of Eden that they could have eaten from" that God had not himself placed to bring about the outcome he desired.[^2] See [[Decreeing All Things Makes God the Author of Sin]] and [[Meticulous Providence Makes God the Author of Evil]].

### 2. Most are created for hell, to display an attribute (Argument weight: Primary)
Hansen presses the purpose Ryan assigns to reprobation: "God created most people for that purpose so that they would go to hell so that God could display his justice."[^3] God "chooses only to regenerate a small portion of humanity by giving them a new heart while leaving others so that he can torture them forever in hell."[^4] A God whose mercy and justice both *require* a permanent population of the damned is, Hansen argues, seeking his own display at his creatures' expense — the indictment developed in [[Hell for Gods Glory Is Narcissism Not Love]] and [[The Calvinist God Cannot Love Because He Controls Everything]].

### 3. This "moral insanity" manufactures atheists (Argument weight: Primary)
Hansen's distinctive move is that the Calvinist portrait is itself an evangelistic stumbling block: "This sort of insanity is the reason you have atheists like [[Christopher Hitchens]] writing best-selling books called *God Is Not Great*. When you grow up thinking that this is what Christianity is, it's no wonder people abandon Christianity thinking it's moral insanity."[^5] The cost is real: a God presented this way repels the very people the gospel seeks. Cf. [[Problem of Evil and the Harms of Religion - Craig vs Hitchens]].

### 4. Wrong character is worse than wrong ontology (Argument weight: Secondary)
Hansen closes by inverting the usual Protestant charge against Latter-day Saints: "What's worse — a Latter-day Saint who may disagree with you on the matter of God's ontology, or a Protestant who turns the loving God of the Bible into a moral monster?"[^6] Misdescribing God's *metaphysics* is a smaller error than misdescribing his *heart* — the thesis of [[Getting Gods Character Wrong Is Worse Than Getting His Ontology Wrong]].

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The Reformed steelman (Ryan): sovereignty, justice, and the potter's rights
Ryan's position, fairly stated, rests on God's absolute sovereignty and the display of *both* justice and mercy: God "could save everyone," but "he desires to show his different attributes... his attribute of justice. It's why he has a hell where he punishes people. His attribute of mercy, so he saves people who put their trust in Jesus."[^7] The scriptural backbone is [[Romans 9]] — the potter shapes vessels of wrath and vessels of mercy from one lump, and "who are you, O man, to answer back to God?" God owes salvation to no one, so passing over the reprobate is *justice*, not cruelty, and the contrast magnifies grace. Ryan adds that Jesus' own warning that "few there be that find it" shows a mostly-lost humanity is biblical, not monstrous.[^8]

### Why Hansen judges the steelman to fail
A justice that punishes creatures for a guilt God himself scripted (evidence 1) is theater, not justice; and a glory that *requires* manufacturing eternal misery is the narcissism of a being eliciting praise from puppets (see [[Hell for Gods Glory Is Narcissism Not Love]]). Because the choice to believe is itself God's gift and not the creature's ([[Regeneration Precedes Faith So We Only Seem to Choose God]]), the damned cannot be the authors of their own damnation. **Fair note:** Hansen's target is *consistent* double-predestinarian Calvinism; many Reformed thinkers reject the "hell glorifies God" framing, and the LDS alternative relocates rather than dissolves the mystery, positing co-eternal agents whose choices God does not cause.
