# The Freethinking Argument - Determinism Undermines Rationality

## The Claim

The argument [[Tim Stratton]]'s ministry (Freethinking Ministries) is named after: **a mind whose every state is determined by something outside itself cannot rationally infer anything**. Whether the determiner is physical causation (atheism) or God's decree ([[Calvinism]]), determinism removes the free evaluation that reasoning requires, so a determinist has no grounds to trust that his own conclusions — including determinism — were reached by good reasoning rather than merely caused. This is the epistemological sibling of [[The Deity of Deception Argument Against Exhaustive Divine Determinism|the Deity of Deception argument]].

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Reasoning is incompatible with a fully determined mind (Argument weight: Primary)

Stratton: "reasoning... is not compatible with having everything about your mind determined by somebody else. So... even if it was my greatest desire to reason, I couldn't reason if I was a passive cog or if something or someone else was determining everything about my mind."[^1] Inference is an act of *weighing* reasons and moving to a conclusion because it is warranted — an activity a "passive cog" cannot perform.

### 2. A determined reasoner cannot adjudicate between rival conclusions (Argument weight: Primary)

[[Josh Klene]] states the argument the ministry is named for: "if you are determined to reason then you can't actually trust your reasoning faculties, because some people reason differently and so... there is no objective standard, then we... don't know which one is right — so how can we actually trust a god" that determines it all.[^2] If God (or physics) determines person A to affirm p and person B to affirm not-p, neither is in a position to say his own faculty tracked the truth.

### 3. Compatibilism's escape is unfalsifiable (Argument weight: Secondary — Critique)

Klene anticipates the compatibilist reply that we are "free... to do that which we most greatly desire." He argues it becomes unfalsifiable: "you say, well, I reasoned and I was able to overcome my greatest desires — [the compatibilist says] well, but your greatest desire was to reason, and so it was still compatibilistic." That move "kick[s] the can down the road every time," refusing ever to let determinism go.[^3] See [[Compatibilism]].

## Best Quotes

> "Even if it was my greatest desire to reason, I couldn't reason if I was a passive cog, or if something or someone else was determining everything about my mind." — [[Tim Stratton]][^1]

> "If you are determined to reason, then you can't actually trust your reasoning faculties." — [[Josh Klene]][^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### Objection 1: Reliable determined processes yield knowledge (compatibilist)
**Steelman (Calvinist/compatibilist):** Rationality does not require the ability to have concluded otherwise; it requires that your belief-forming process reliably tracks truth. A calculator is fully determined yet reliably correct. If God determines a truth-aimed reasoning process, its determined outputs can be knowledge. "Free" inference in the libertarian sense is not needed — only a good process.

**Response:** The proponent replies that a calculator does not *evaluate reasons* — it executes; the analogy concedes that a determined system only mimics inference. And, echoing the [[The Deity of Deception Argument Against Exhaustive Divine Determinism|Deity of Deception argument]], if the *same* determiner also fixes rival, mutually-contradictory conclusions in other minds, the individual has no internal way to certify his own process as the reliable one.

### Objection 2: The argument proves too much (it would indict God's foreknowledge)
**Steelman:** If determination destroys rationality, does God's exhaustive foreknowledge (which even the Molinist affirms) not likewise "fix" what you will conclude? The Molinist owes an account of why *middle knowledge* preserves freedom where determinism does not.

**Response:** The Molinist's stock reply is that foreknowledge is not causation: God's middle knowledge of what you *would freely* conclude presupposes your libertarian freedom rather than removing it (see [[Molinism]] and [[Predestination Does Not Entail Determinism]]). The critic may still press whether "counterfactuals of creaturely freedom" can be true without a determining ground.
