# The Church Does Not Hide Its History

#type/rebuttal

## The Claim
Against the recurring anti-Mormon trope that the church "hides" or "sterilizes" its history, [[Ryan Wood]] argues the reverse: Latter-day Saints are, if anything, *obsessed* with their own history, and the church spends money to **publish and preserve** the very documents critics say it conceals. The free, exhaustive Joseph Smith Papers are Exhibit A.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The Joseph Smith Papers are free — with more online than in print.** Anyone can read the primary sources at no cost, and the website carries *more* than the published volumes. A church trying to bury its record does not fund a 26-volume critical edition and give it away.[^2] *Evidence rating: Strong — verifiable and central.*

2. **The church owns and gives away the alleged "plagiarism" sources.** For a century critics claimed Joseph plagiarized *View of the Hebrews* (also the Spalding-manuscript and Captain-Kidd theories — "15 theories … all debunked"). So the church **bought the rights** to those books and publishes them for free: "The rights to *View of the Hebrews* are owned by the church … and they give it away for free." Wood reads this as "insane confidence."[^3] *Evidence rating: Strong — see [[BYU Published View of the Hebrews]], [[No Contemporary Accusation of Plagiarism]], [[The Plagiarism Charge Boomerangs on the Critic]].*

3. **Critics work inside the Church History Library — and thank it.** Wood notes that anti-Mormon books routinely carry acknowledgments like "thank you to the Church History Library for allowing me to research." You cannot both be a welcomed guest in an archive and call that archive a cover-up: "How can you say the church is hiding its history … publishing the crap they just gave you?"[^4] *Evidence rating: Strong.*

4. **Even the editors flag uncertain documents.** The JSP editors openly mark material they cannot verify ("we can't really verify this") and file it accordingly — transparency about the limits of the record, not suppression.[^5] *Evidence rating: Moderate-to-strong.*

## Best Quotes

> "The rights to *View of the Hebrews* are owned by the church … and they give it away for free. To me that exudes such an insane confidence."[^3]

> "How can you literally say the church is hiding its history [when you are] a welcome guest for free in the Church History Library, publishing the crap they just gave you?"[^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The strongest version of the critic's case is not "the documents are locked away" but "the church long **downplayed** the hard parts in its correlated teaching materials, and only issued the Gospel Topics essays acknowledging the seer stone, polygamy math, and Book of Abraham problems under external pressure." That is a real historiographical debate. Two replies. (a) **Archival transparency and pedagogical simplification are different questions.** That primary sources were always preserved and are now free answers the "hiding" charge directly; whether Sunday-school lessons should have been more candid is a separate, fair critique — and generalizing a subject for a lay or child audience ("arithmetic before calculus") is not the same as lying. (b) The **incentive structure** cuts the other way: as the interview notes, "the church is hiding its history" is a claim that "gets clicks" and is, for some, a revenue stream — which is a reason to scrutinize it, not evidence for it. Compare [[The Pipeline and Sugarcoating Charges Against the Church Are Overstated]].
