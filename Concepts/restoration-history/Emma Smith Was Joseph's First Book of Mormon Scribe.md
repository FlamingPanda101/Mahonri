# Emma Smith Was Joseph's First Book of Mormon Scribe

#type/claim

## The Claim
Before [[Martin Harris]] or Oliver Cowdery ever wrote a word, [[Emma Smith]] was Joseph Smith's *first* scribe for the Book of Mormon — and [[Don Bradley]] argues her scribal work belongs at the very **beginning** of the translation, in the lost book of Lehi (the 116 pages). Piecing together Emma's own recollections, Bradley concludes that Joseph and Emma "as husband and wife" *initiated* the translation of the Book of Mormon together, a collaboration the angel had required from the start.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **Emma scribed before Martin Harris.** Bradley states plainly that "before Martin becomes a scribe, Emma becomes a scribe."[^2] From clues in Emma's statements he then reconstructs *where* in the text she wrote. *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate — Emma's role as an early scribe is well attested; the placement is Bradley's reconstruction.)*
2. **The "Sariah" and "walls of Jerusalem" clues place her at the start of Lehi's narrative.** Emma recalled that the unlearned Joseph could not spell the name "Sariah" phonetically, and that he was shocked to find in the text that Jerusalem had walls, making her check the Bible to confirm it — he had feared he "must be understanding this wrong."[^3] Both the name Sariah and the walls of Jerusalem would first appear at the *beginning* of Lehi's story (the flight from Jerusalem and Nephi's return for the brass plates) — i.e., in the lost book of Lehi. Emma's presence for those "firsts" locates her scribal work at the very opening of the translation.[^4] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate-to-strong — the reconstruction is inferential but the details almost necessarily occur early in any telling of Lehi's departure.)*
3. **The "February" letter corroborates the same early window.** Joseph, who "didn't like to write" and wrote poorly, tried to date a February 1828 letter to Martin Harris and badly misspelled "February"; Martin later cited exactly this as proof of Joseph's ignorance. Bradley reconstructs that Joseph, unable to write or even dictate a coherent letter, handed the task to Emma — the same husband-and-wife pairing seen in the translation.[^5] *(Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Moderate — a reconstruction, but it independently converges with Martin's and Emma's testimony.)*
4. **Emma's dictation testimony describes the method firsthand.** In her last interview she said Joseph would "dictate to me hour after hour," and after meals or interruptions "could at once begin where he had left off without either seeing the manuscript or having any portion of it read to him" — impossible, she said, for one so "ignorant and unlearned."[^6] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Strong — a specific, first-person eyewitness account of the translation process.)*
5. **Why Emma?** She was far more educated than Joseph and could do the writing he could not — one plausible reason the angel required her ([[The Angel Required Joseph to Marry Emma to Obtain the Plates]]), and a reason the translation began as a shared labor.[^1] *(Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Moderate.)*

## Best Quotes
> "So where did Emma act as scribe? At the beginning of the book of Lehi, which means at the very beginning of the translation process. Emma Smith was Joseph's first scribe for the translation of the Book of Mormon. So it's actually Joseph and Emma together who are initiating the translation of the Book of Mormon as husband and wife."[^4]

> "Joseph Smith could neither write nor dictate a coherent, well-worded letter, let alone a book like the Book of Mormon... it is marvelous to me, a marvel, and a wonder as much so as anyone else."[^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The placement rests on inference, and Bradley is candid that he is "piecing things together." The book of Lehi manuscript is lost, so we cannot directly verify that Sariah and Jerusalem's walls first appeared there; the extant [[Book of Mormon]] text of 1 Nephi (the small plates, translated last — see [[The Book of Mormon Was Translated Mosiah-First]]) is not in Emma's handwriting. Emma's recollections are also decades-late, given by a devoted widow. A skeptic could grant Emma an early scribal role while doubting the precise "book of Lehi" localization.

The defense is the *convergence*: (a) the details Bradley uses (a proper name, a city's walls) are precisely the kind that must appear at the opening of a narrative that begins in Jerusalem, so their "firstness" is not arbitrary; (b) three independent strands — Emma's Sariah/walls memory, Martin Harris's "February" memory, and Emma's dictation testimony — all point to the same earliest window; and (c) the same anomalies (spelling unpronounced names, surprise at the content) independently argue Joseph was receiving rather than composing the text ([[The Seer Stone Swap and Jerusalem Walls Show Joseph Was Receiving Not Composing]]). The reconstruction is inferential but tightly constrained.
