# Strang's Witnesses Saw Plates Not an Angel

## The Claim
The [[The Strang Plates Have Witnesses Just Like the Book of Mormon|parity objection]] conflates two categorically different witness claims. [[James Strang]]'s witnesses attest only that a physical stack of metal plates existed — a natural, easily explained fact. [[Joseph Smith]]'s *decisive* witnesses, the [[Three Witnesses]], attest that an **angel** ([[Angel Moroni|Moroni]]) showed them the plates — a supernatural claim with no naturalistic explanation. Seeing a stack of tin is simply not the same order of testimony as seeing an angel, so equal "witness counts" do not yield equal evidence. This is the specific Strangite application of the vault's [[Natural vs Supernatural Witnesses|two-tier witness structure]].

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Strang's witnesses are natural-only.** No Strangite ever claimed a *supernatural* encounter validating the plates. The hosts' challenge: "Show me the testimony of someone who saw the angel Laban. That would be cool... I don't care if people saw a stack of tin." [^1] *Argument weight: Primary.* A signed statement that copper or tin plates were dug up establishes, at most, that metal plates were dug up.

2. **Joseph's case adds three supernatural witnesses.** "There's three men that see the angel Moroni. That's what's incredible." Merely finding metal sheets "doesn't mean he's a prophet just cuz he found copper sheets." [^2] The Three Witnesses' claim is of a different category — foretold in the Book of Mormon itself (see [[Ether 5.2-4|Ether 5:2-4]], "in the mouth of three witnesses"). *Evidence rating: Strong (as testimony).*

3. **The witnesses held their supernatural testimony against their own interest.** The three "fall out of favor with Joseph Smith and go to their deathbed saying, 'No, I saw the angel and I saw the plates.' That's not even in the same category of claim." [^3] Estrangement without recantation is what makes the supernatural tier costly; see [[Witnesses Never Recanted Despite Estrangement]].

4. **The naturalist must defeat both tiers at once.** "You have the physical experience of the plates and then you have a more supernatural experience... Both of those things together working in tandem makes the Book of Mormon witnesses incredibly strong." [^4] A hallucination theory must still account for the handled physical plates; a fraud theory must still account for three men's persistent angelic testimony. Strang's case presents only the first, easily-naturalized tier — "it seems like they're just kind of larping a little bit on Beaver Island." [^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
A naturalist replies that **subjective visionary experience is exactly what religious excitement, expectancy, or suggestion would produce**, so the "supernatural" tier is not an independent evidentiary track — it is the very phenomenon under dispute (the same steelman recorded in [[Natural vs Supernatural Witnesses]]). On this reading, that Strang's followers reported no angel shows only that his movement generated less visionary fervor, not that Joseph's angel was real. The hosts' reply is that the physical and supernatural tiers *reinforce* each other: you cannot dissolve the angelic testimony as mere psychology without still explaining the tangible, hefted, leaf-turned plates that some of the same and neighboring witnesses handled — "Is it possible in a naturalistic world to get three guys to see an angel? And then to go through the rest of their lives not denying that experience?" [^5]

## Best Quotes
> "Show me the testimony of someone who saw the angel Laban. That would be cool... I don't care if people saw a stack of tin." [^1]
