# A Large Reserve Fund Is Prudent Stewardship Not Hoarding

## The Claim
[[Nathan Apffel]]'s headline charge on the Tucker Carlson Show: the Church "make[s] so much profit in the market" — "25 billion last year in interest alone" — that it "could fund the church in perpetuity just off a percentage of the interest," since "the entire global LDS Church costs around $7 [billion] to run." Yet "they still demand that their congregants give them 10% a year."[^1][^2] The implied conclusion: a body that could self-fund forever has no honest reason to keep collecting tithing — so it is hoarding. [[Jacob Hansen]] answers that the argument "shows an insane lack of understanding of how money works": a large reserve is prudent stewardship for a growing global church, not a hoard.[^3]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The numbers are the critic's own aggressive estimates.** "No one knows exactly what the totals are and he is using the most aggressive estimates."[^1] The "$25 billion interest / fund-it-forever" claim is asserted, not audited. *Argument weight: primary. Evidence rating: moderate — the rebuttal concedes it cannot prove the real figure either, only that the critic cannot.*
2. **"Fund it forever" assumes markets never change.** "Markets change. The church is not simply playing a 20 or 30 or even a 100-year game... Talking about any market continuing to perform a certain way into perpetuity just shows you don't know what you're talking about."[^2] *Weight: primary. Rating: strong.*
3. **The reserve insures a fast-growing, low-income global membership.** Members in developing countries "consume billions and billions from the church in Salt Lake, but send nothing back";[^4] more than half of members are now outside the US, and "without... tithing paid by members of the church in the US... the church could not maintain its programs."[^5] If a severe US depression cut that tithing, "the reserve fund would be what would be supporting the church throughout the rest of the world."[^6] *Weight: primary. Rating: strong — this is the load-bearing rebuttal: the fund is insurance for the global mission.*
4. **The fund is not actually vast per member.** "A hundred billion only adds up to about $6,000 per member of the church" — hardly "an endless supply of money"; and "since when is having a significant savings account a bad idea?"[^7] *Weight: secondary. Rating: moderate — reframes the headline number.*
5. **Saving against future famine is the biblical pattern.** Apffel himself anticipates the Joseph-of-Egypt analogy (storing grain for seven years of famine, Genesis 41); the video accepts the analogy and extends it — the Church "makes plans" for "the next 50 to 100 or even 200 years" of demographic and economic change.[^3][^8] *Weight: secondary. Rating: moderate.*

## Best Quotes
> "Markets change. The church is not simply playing a 20 or 30 or even a 100-year game." [^2]

> "A hundred billion only adds up to about $6,000 per member of the church." [^7]

> "If there were a severe depression, and that declined, the reserve fund would be what would be supporting the church throughout the rest of the world." [^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
Steelman Apffel: if a fund can already cover annual operating costs off interest alone, then continuing to require tithing looks less like need and more like accumulation — and "why keep piling up a reserve you may never spend?" is a fair question, especially paired with "why not give more to charity?" The video honestly concedes these are "real questions" it will address separately.[^9] The rebuttal, though, defeats the specific inference *self-funding-capable ⇒ hoarding*: a reserve sized to survive a multi-decade downturn while carrying a membership that is shifting toward regions that cannot fund themselves is exactly what prudent stewardship of a global institution looks like — the same logic as a sovereign wealth fund or a university endowment. Whether the reserve is *larger than* that prudential need is the legitimate open question; that it exists at all is not evidence of greed. See [[Church Wealth and Welfare Are Evidence Not Scandal]] and [[The SEC Settlement Does Not Prove the Church Hid Its Wealth]].
