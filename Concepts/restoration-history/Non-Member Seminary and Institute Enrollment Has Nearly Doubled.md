# Non-Member Seminary and Institute Enrollment Has Nearly Doubled

#type/claim

## The Claim
A Church newsroom release relayed on *Latter Daily Saints* (Ep. 186) reports that worldwide
enrollment in the Church's **seminary and institute** program now exceeds one million students,
and that this total **includes 85,695 "friends of other faiths" — non-members — whose numbers
have nearly doubled in the past two years.** The hosts credit the *Invite a Friend* initiative
(launched April 2025 under [[Dallin H. Oaks]]) and read the figure as evidence that outsiders
are voluntarily studying Latter-day Saint scripture in record numbers — a goodwill/interest
signal that cuts against a simple "the Church is shrinking" narrative.[^1][^2]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The primary datum is specific and Church-sourced.** The release names **85,695** non-member
   ("friends of other faiths") enrollees and states their enrollment "has nearly doubled in the
   past two years." A precise, recently published figure from the Church's own newsroom is the
   strongest element here. *Evidence rating: Strong (primary statistic); Argument weight: Primary.*[^1]
2. **Total enrollment context.** The hosts put the non-member figure against a total that "exceeds
   one million students" worldwide, noting 85,000 is "how many missionaries we have in the whole
   world" — i.e., the non-member cohort alone rivals the full-time missionary force in size.
   *Evidence rating: Moderate (host gloss of the release); Argument weight: Secondary.*[^3]
3. **A named mechanism, not just a trend.** The growth is tied to a concrete program — *Invite a
   Friend* (April 2025) — in which [[Dallin H. Oaks]] "encouraged youth to attend seminary and
   invite friends," framing institute as preparation "to love and lead like Jesus Christ." A
   causal mechanism makes the doubling more than a statistical blip. *Evidence rating: Moderate;
   Argument weight: Secondary.*[^2]
4. **Peer invitation is the driver.** The hosts note the release's own logic — a trusted friend is
   the most effective "advertisement" — so the growth is friend-to-friend, not paid outreach,
   which they take as a sign of genuine social buy-in among LDS youth. *Evidence rating: Weak
   (interpretive); Argument weight: Tertiary.*[^4]

## Best Quotes

> Latter-day Saints now exceeds [over a million] students... including 85,695 friends of other faiths
> whose enrollment has nearly doubled in the past two years." [^1]

> of people." [^3]

> President Oaks: "I invite you to consistently attend institute... I also encourage you to invite
> friends to share in these same blessings. I promise that your time in institute will bring the
> Savior's peace, joy, and divine love." [^8]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The hosts **steelman their own statistic**, which is the honest thing to do with it:
- **Enrollment is not conversion — or even attendance.** In much of Utah seminary is a slotted part
  of the high-school schedule and skipping it does not affect school attendance, so a non-member can
  "enroll" as a free period and never show up — the hosts joke about classmates who signed up and
  "would go skiing... in the winter semester," and about promo stickers reading "seminary: get out of
  school for an hour free."[^5][^6] A headcount of enrollees therefore overstates real engagement, and
  says nothing about **retention** or baptisms.
- **No retention data is given.** The release (as relayed) reports an intake number, not how many
  non-member enrollees continue, convert, or stay active; the hosts can only *hope* "at least one" of
  the 85,000 eventually joins.[^7] The apologetic weight is thus limited to "interest/exposure," not
  "growth in membership."
- **Selection and definition effects.** "Friends of other faiths" is a self-reported category tied to
  a specific outreach push; a doubling off a small, program-driven base is easier than sustained
  organic growth, and the two-year window coincides with the initiative's launch.

**What survives:** a real, Church-published figure showing a sharp rise in non-members voluntarily
signing up to study LDS scripture — a modest but genuine data point of outward interest and social
goodwill. It should be cited as *exposure/interest*, not as evidence of net membership growth or
retention, both of which this source does not measure.
