# Smartphones and Screen Time Predict Youth Religious Deidentification

## The Claim
In longitudinal youth data, owning a smartphone and heavy video/screen consumption in the mid-teen years predict later deidentification from the Church — but the effect appears to operate *through* the erosion of daily spiritual experience rather than as an independent cause. The lesson is less "the internet disproves faith" and more "screens crowd out the practices that anchor faith."

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Smartphone ownership nearly triples the odds of leaving (rated: strong, longitudinal).** Using the FFYD panel to predict deidentification between 2020 and 2024, youth with a smartphone were "nearly three times more likely to deidentify (13.0%) compared to those without a smartphone (4.9%)," controlling for gender and parental income.[^1]

2. **A clean dose-response for video-watching (rated: strong).** Time spent watching video clips (e.g., YouTube) tracked deidentification almost linearly: 7.6% of those who watched none deidentified, rising to 12.1% at one hour a day, 16.3% at two hours, and 21.6% at three hours.[^2]

3. **The effect is mediated by spiritual experience (rated: moderate, methodological).** When the digital-media variables were entered into the same regression as daily spiritual experiences and social/political concerns, they "were no longer statistically significant." The authors infer digital media influences retention *through* its impact on spiritual experience — and by amplifying dissonance with the faith's social positions — rather than independently.[^3] This dovetails with the finding that felt daily connection with God is the top protective factor. See [[Daily Connection With God Is the Top Predictor of Retention]].

4. **Consistent with prior research (rated: moderate, cited literature).** The report notes prior work linking more social-media use among young adults to a "pick and choose" approach to faith and less certainty in one's religion's exclusive truth, and linking more adolescent screen time to diminished religious commitment and less scripture reading.[^3] See [[The Online Echo Chamber Accelerates Disaffiliation]].

## Best Quotes

> "Youth with a smartphone were nearly three times more likely to deidentify (13.0%) compared to those without a smartphone (4.9%)."[^1]

> "While only 7.6% of those who watched no video clips deidentified... 21.6% of those who watched three hours a day deidentified."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

- **Correlational, with obvious confounds.** Smartphone ownership co-varies with age, independence, family strictness, and household culture. Although the model controlled for gender and parental income, families who withhold smartphones likely differ from those who don't in exactly the traits (religious intensity, parental monitoring) that also drive retention.
- **Reverse causation / selection.** Youth already drifting from faith may seek out more screen time and more video, so heavy use could be a symptom as much as a cause; the panel design helps but cannot fully close this.
- **The report's own caution cuts against overclaiming.** Because the digital-media effect vanishes once spiritual experience is controlled, the honest reading is not "phones cause apostasy" but "phones displace the daily spiritual practices that keep youth connected." Apologetic use should keep that nuance.
- **Limited population.** The FFYD sample is drawn from Utah, Arizona, and California with Latter-day Saints oversampled, and the numbers of non-smartphone owners are small (11% of the sample), limiting precision and generalization.
