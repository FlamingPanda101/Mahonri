# The Strangite Plates Argument Concedes Joseph Smith's Plates Existed

## The Claim
Deploying [[James Strang]]'s plates against the Book of Mormon backfires. To run the parallel at all, the critic must grant that Strang's plates *physically existed* (they are well attested) — and by the identical evidential standard must grant that [[Joseph Smith]]'s gold plates physically existed (they are better attested). Far from neutralizing the Book of Mormon, the argument forces the critic onto Latter-day Saint ground: "you'd be stuck saying that Joseph's plates existed." [^1] The existence of the plates was never the disputed point; the *miracle* is — and the parallel leaves that untouched.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **Both plate-sets are historically documented — denying one denies the other.** "We have more documentation for those two claims than most historical things that we consider fact." [^2] A critic who accepts Strang's plates on the witness record cannot consistently reject Joseph's plates, which rest on a larger record (roughly twenty people handled or moved them). *Argument weight: Primary.*

2. **The physical-plate evidence actually favors Joseph.** Forging copper or tin plates was trivial; forging inscribed *gold-alloy* plates as a poor teenage farm boy — with no cultural knowledge that ancient peoples wrote on metal plates — is the far harder feat. "Gold alloy is a lot harder to come across as a poor farm boy than like copper," and "there's no working theory for... who would have made Joseph's plates." [^3] So the parity the critic invokes cuts the LDS way. *Evidence rating: Moderate (comparative plausibility).*

3. **Only one of the two is miraculous, and that is the whole point.** Enemies of Strang noted early on that his plates' metal "is very similar to the metal we use" for common objects — a "pretty clear plausible theory" of natural manufacture. [^4] Grant that both sets of plates existed and Strang's has a naturalistic origin while Joseph's does not; the concession therefore *sharpens* the asymmetry rather than dissolving it (see [[Strang's Witnesses Saw Plates Not an Angel]]).

4. **The hosts openly invite the argument.** "We actually invite you to do so, because then you'd be stuck saying that Joseph's plates existed. We have no issue saying that Strangite plates existed, but... you're going to have a lot harder time explaining why Joseph Smith's plates existed." [^1] The Strangite case is welcomed as *evidence for* the gold plates, not against them.

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
A critic can consistently grant that *both* sets of metal plates existed and still deny divine origin to *either* — the existence of engraved metal is not the existence of a god or an angel, so conceding physical plates costs the naturalist nothing they were not already willing to pay. This is a fair move, and the hosts effectively agree: existence was never their claim. Their rejoinder is that the parallel was supposed to *equalize* the two prophets, and instead it (a) commits the critic to the reality of Joseph's plates and (b) throws the remaining burden entirely onto the un-paralleled items — the angelic Three Witnesses and the 65-day dictated translation — for which no naturalistic account exists. The argument that was meant to lower Joseph to Strang's level instead isolates exactly what makes Joseph's case singular.

## Best Quotes
> "You would be forced to say that the Strang plates existed and you'd be forced to say that Joseph Smith's plates existed. One of those things is incredibly miraculous and the other is not that crazy — and you're forcing yourself into accepting our position." [^5]

> "We have no issue saying that Strangite plates existed, but... if you are against the restoration and Joseph Smith as a prophet, you're going to have a lot harder time explaining why Joseph Smith's plates existed." [^1]
