# Tithing Is Biblically Commanded and the New Testament Raised the Standard

## The Claim
On the Tucker Carlson Show, documentary filmmaker [[Nathan Apffel]] argues that "tithing is not scriptural" for a modern-day Christian — that it "only flared back in the last couple hundred years," and that "you can go through the history of Christianity from Christ on and Christians weren't tithing."[^1][^3] [[Tucker Carlson]] amplifies the point. [[Jacob Hansen]] answers that this is "demonstrably false": tithe-paying is commanded across the Bible, and the reason the first Christians did not pay a 10% tithe is that they lived a *higher* law — consecrating 100% — not a lower one.[^5]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **Tithing is commanded throughout the Old Testament.** "Tithe paying is commanded in the Bible. Abraham paid tithes to [[Melchizedek]]" ([[Genesis 14.18|Gen 14:18]]).[^1] [[Genesis 28.20-22|Jacob vowed]] to "give the tenth" to God; after the Exodus [[Moses]] "commanded that they should give a tenth to the Lord"; and Malachi frames withholding it as robbing God: "Will a man rob God?... In [[Malachi 3.8-10|tithes and offerings]]."[^2] *Argument weight: primary. Evidence rating: strong — multiple explicit, undisputed texts.*
2. **The first Christians gave MORE than a tithe, not less.** The reason the New Testament church wasn't "tithing" is that "they were living the higher law of Jesus and consecrating 100%": the Jerusalem saints were "of one heart and soul," "no one said any of the things that belonged to him was his own," and they sold their possessions and distributed "to all" ([[Acts 4.32-35|Acts 4:32-35]]; cf. Acts 2:44-45).[^3] *Weight: primary. Rating: strong.*
3. **God enforced consecration seriously — Ananias.** Luke underscores how "important" the commitment was: [[Acts 5.1-11|Ananias]] sold property, secretly "kept back a part," lied about it to [[Peter]], and fell dead — "you have not lied just to human beings, but to God."[^4] *Weight: secondary (illustrative). Rating: strong for the narrower point that New Testament giving was a real, God-backed obligation.*
4. **So the dichotomy is false.** "To imply that God did not expect his people to contribute financially to building the kingdom in the Bible is demonstrably false and just shows a person does not know what they're talking about."[^5] Tithing is the lesser, preparatory form of a giving principle the gospel *intensifies* — not a post-biblical invention. *Weight: primary. Rating: moderate — the "higher law" framing is an LDS reading, but the underlying biblical giving texts are not in dispute.*

## Best Quotes
> "Tithe paying is commanded in the Bible. Abraham paid tithes to Melchizedek. Jacob covenanted to give the tenth unto God." [^1]

> "The first Christians were not giving 10%. They were living the higher law of Jesus and consecrating 100%." [^3]

> "To imply that God did not expect his people to contribute financially to building the kingdom in the Bible is demonstrably false." [^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
Apffel's strongest form: the New Testament epistles never institute a fixed 10% tithe on Christians; Mosaic tithing was tied to the temple and the Levites, and the specific practice of a modern church collecting 10% is not commanded by Christ or Paul. The video fairly grants the historical half of this: "he does go on to say that tithing was only a thing in the Old Testament."[^6] But the inference "therefore God did not expect financial consecration" does not follow. The New Testament replaces a floor (10%) with a ceiling (all things in common), and treats withholding as a lie "unto God" (Ananias). Whether the modern Church is right to restore the *tithe* specifically — rather than full consecration — is a separate question about tithing as a preparatory law; see [[Tithing Is a Command for Spiritual Formation Not a Celestial Toll]].
