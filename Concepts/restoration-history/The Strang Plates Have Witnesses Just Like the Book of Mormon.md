# The Strang Plates Have Witnesses Just Like the Book of Mormon

## The Claim (the objection)
Critics of the Book of Mormon argue that [[James Strang]]'s plates were attested by witnesses exactly as [[Joseph Smith]]'s gold plates were, so the [[Three Witnesses|Book of Mormon witnesses]] carry no special evidential weight. In the strongest form the video reports, an ex-Latter-day Saint from the mainstream ("Brighamite") church lost his faith "upon realizing that there's just as much evidence Strang wasn't lying as there is that Joseph Smith wasn't lying." [^1] Strang's case is offered as a parallel that neutralizes the witnesses apologetic: if a second man produced witnessed plates a few years later, witnessed plates prove nothing.

## Supporting Evidence (steelmanned for the objection)

1. **The Strang plates really existed and were displayed.** The hosts concede the point without reservation: "Yes, the Strang plates existed... they were at one point on display" (later destroyed). [^2] *Argument weight: Primary.* The parallel is not a fabrication — there genuinely were physical Strangite plates.

2. **Multiple witnesses saw them and never recanted.** "Several people saw these plates, too, when Strang claimed to dig them up and never recanted their testimonies, either" — the Voree plates carried a signed witness statement, structurally like the Book of Mormon's. [^3] *Evidence rating: Moderate.*

3. **Strang used the same apparatus and narrative template.** Strang translated by "the same devices... referred to in Mormon scripture as the Urim and Thummim," [^4] and the resulting story — a lost people "all slain in battle," the last survivor "recording the story of his people" — is, the hosts admit, "not too dissimilar to the ending of the Book of Mormon in Moroni chapter 10." [^5] The formal resemblance is real.

4. **A Book of Mormon witness even joined him.** [[Martin Harris]], one of the original Three Witnesses, affiliated with the Strangites and was sent on a mission for them — the single data point critics cite to fuse the two movements' credibility. [^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
The hosts grant every factual premise above and locate the entire dispute in the *kind* of witness, not the *existence* of plates:
- The comparable set is only the Book of Mormon's **natural** witnesses; Strang has no parallel to the **supernatural** Three Witnesses who saw an angel. See [[Strang's Witnesses Saw Plates Not an Angel]] and [[Natural vs Supernatural Witnesses]].
- Conceding the parallel actually forces the critic to grant Joseph's plates existed, too. See [[The Strangite Plates Argument Concedes Joseph Smith's Plates Existed]].
- The narrative parallel is imperfect: Strang's "plates of Laban" yielded a new law-code (the Book of the Law of the Lord), whereas the Book of Mormon specifies that Laban's brass plates already held "the five books of Moses" plus the Lehite genealogy from the tribe of Manasseh [^7] — see [[1 Nephi 5.11|1 Nephi 5:11]] — so even the alleged plagiarism gets the source-text's contents wrong.
- [[Martin Harris Among the Strangites|Harris's brief affiliation]] never included a miraculous Strangite testimony and ended in his expulsion for preaching the Book of Mormon.
