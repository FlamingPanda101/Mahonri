# Martin Harris Among the Strangites

## The Claim
The one Book of Mormon witness critics cite as a Strangite convert — [[Martin Harris]] — does nothing to equate the two movements. Harris briefly affiliated with [[James Strang]]'s church (embittered at being left out of the Utah migration), and was even sent on a mission to England for it, but he never testified to anything *miraculous* for Strang, was **not** an official witness of Strang's plates, would not stop preaching the Book of Mormon and the [[Angel Moroni|angel Moroni]], and was effectively expelled for it. He died buried with a Book of Mormon and Doctrine and Covenants — not the Book of the Law of the Lord.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The affiliation was social, not evidential.** Harris "was like a part of the Strangites for a second 'cause he was sad that he didn't go to Utah." [^1] *Argument weight: Primary.* His joining reflects personal grievance over the westward split, not a new revelation.

2. **Sent to preach for Strang, he preached Joseph instead.** Strang "even sent Martin Harris to England" to gain followers; [^2] Harris "got like kicked out of this church because he wouldn't stop... he wouldn't shut up about the Book of Mormon. It's all he wanted to talk about." [^3] The Strangite mission collapsed precisely because Harris's testimony was to *Joseph's* plates and angel.

3. **No miraculous Strangite testimony, and no official witness status.** "I don't think we have any accounts of him witnessing anything miraculous to vindicate the Strang claims," [^4] and the record shows "he was not an official witness of the Strangite plates." [^1] His name never appears on a Strangite witness statement — so he cannot be counted as evidence *for* Strang.

4. **His deathbed testimony was constant to the Book of Mormon.** Harris "went to his deathbed saying that he saw the angel Moroni. Much more spectacular claim," [^4] and "was buried with a Book of Mormon in one hand and a Doctrine and Covenants in the other hand" — "he wasn't buried with the Book of Laban." [^5] See [[Witnesses Never Recanted Despite Estrangement]].

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
A critic notes that Harris was famously credulous — he cycled through several movements after leaving the church — so his Strangite phase shows how loosely a "witness" holds his allegiances, which should discount his Book of Mormon testimony as well. The steelman has bite: Harris's institutional wandering is well documented. The reply is that Harris's drift is exactly what makes his *unchanging core testimony* evidentially costly rather than cheap: across every institutional realignment — Strangite, Shaker-adjacent interest, years outside the church — he abandoned organizations but never the specific, falsifiable claim that he saw an angel and the plates, and he returned to the Utah church to die affirming it. A witness who would follow his feelings into Strang's camp and *still* not manufacture a Strangite angel-story is not a man tailoring testimony to his affiliations. See [[Strang's Witnesses Saw Plates Not an Angel]].
