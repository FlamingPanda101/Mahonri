# The Angel Required Joseph to Marry Emma to Obtain the Plates

#type/claim

## The Claim
[[Emma Smith]]'s place in the Restoration was not incidental: according to multiple accounts, the angel who directed Joseph to the plates told him to **bring Emma to the hill as his wife**. [[Don Bradley]] draws the striking implication — "Emma's involvement was not optional... for whatever reason, Joseph needed Emma to bring forth the Book of Mormon."[^1] She was, in Bradley's phrase, "handpicked" by revelation.

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The angelic instruction to marry Emma.** Bradley reports "multiple accounts" (which he details in his book on the lost 116 pages) that the messenger who led Joseph to the plates told him, in about January 1827, to marry Emma and bring her to the hill on the next September 22nd — which Joseph did.[^2] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate — attested in more than one account, though the sourcing is early-Latter-day-Saint recollection.)*
2. **Emma was brought to the hill when the plates were obtained.** An account from Emma's first cousin (Joseph Lewis, on her mother's side) has Emma accompanying Joseph to the hill Cumorah; by one telling she went up to the stone box but turned her back as he lifted the stone.[^3] Her physical presence at the retrieval matches the angelic charge to bring her. *(Argument weight: Secondary. Evidence rating: Moderate — a relative's later account, which Bradley himself flags as uncertain in detail.)*
3. **The requirement is restated as a settled point.** Late in the interview Bradley reiterates that Joseph "is told by the messenger, he needs Emma to get the plates" — treating Emma's necessity as a fixed feature of the founding narrative, not a one-off remark.[^4] *(Argument weight: Primary. Evidence rating: Moderate.)*
4. **A converging natural reason.** Lucy Mack Smith records that Joseph, lonely since his brother Alvin's death (about three and a half years earlier), desired the companionship of marriage; Emma for her part said she "preferred him to anyone else." Revelation and human affection ran together — but the decisive factor Bradley stresses is the revelation.[^5] *(Argument weight: Tertiary. Evidence rating: Moderate — [[Lucy Mack Smith]]'s memoir plus Emma's own statement.)*

## Best Quotes
> "Emma was handpicked. The angel said, to get the plates, you need to bring Emma... Emma's involvement was not optional. Emma — Joseph needed Emma to bring forth the book."[^1]

> "The messenger who had led Joseph to the plates, the angel told him to bring Emma to the hill as his wife. He tells him this apparently in January of 1827... Marry her, bring her to the hill next September 22nd. And Joseph does."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
A critic will note that the "angel told him to marry Emma" tradition is not first-person from Joseph and comes through later Latter-day Saint recollection, and that a naturalist reading is available: Joseph, in love and rebuffed by Emma's father Isaac Hale, had ordinary reasons to elope, and a providential gloss could be added afterward. Bradley concedes uncertainty in the surrounding details (e.g., exactly what Emma did at the hill).

The defense does not rest the *doctrine* on this account; it rests an *interpretation* of Emma's centrality on it. The claim's force is cumulative with the rest of Emma's story: if she was also Joseph's first scribe ([[Emma Smith Was Joseph's First Book of Mormon Scribe]]) and the recipient of her own revelation ([[Doctrine and Covenants 25]]), then an early angelic charge that she be brought to the work coheres with — rather than strains against — the documented pattern of her indispensable role.
