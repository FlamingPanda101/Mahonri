#type/claim

# The New Testament Contradicts Itself on Faith and Works

## The Claim
Answering an Evangelical who faults Mormonism for not sharing the New Testament's supposed grace-alone gospel, [[Dan McClellan]] denies the premise: **"no text in the New Testament endorses grace alone salvation as [Protestants understand] it today. And several of them directly contradict it."**[^1] The Synoptic Gospels and John make "trusting *and* obeying God" the "main part of salvation"; Paul's polemic against "works" targets the *law of Moses*, not obedience as such; and James "says the exact opposite of what Paul is saying," probably "engaging directly with Paul's own argument" and "pseudographically written decades later."[^2][^3] On McClellan's reading the New Testament is not a harmonized witness to sola fide that Mormonism departs from — it is internally divided, and Protestant sola fide is read *into* it.

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The Gospels condition salvation on obedience, not bare faith (rating: Strong; textual).** "When we look in the Gospels, particularly the Synoptic Gospels, but also the Gospel of John, what we see is an insistence on trusting and obeying God. That's the main part of salvation."[^2] This matches passages like Matthew 7:21 ("not every one that saith… Lord, Lord") and Matthew 25:31–46, and undercuts the claim that the whole New Testament teaches faith-only. *(Argument weight: Primary.)*
2. **Paul's "works of the law" = the Mosaic law (rating: Strong; New Perspective).** "Even in Paul, the works of the law are primarily talking about the works of the law of Moses… not saying grace operates entirely independently of any agency of our own."[^2] This is the mainstream New Perspective on Paul (Dunn, Sanders, Wright); it dissolves the apparent Paul-vs-obedience tension and is developed in [[Works of the Law Means Mosaic Boundary Markers Not Obedience as Such]]. *(Argument weight: Primary.)*
3. **James contradicts Paul (rating: Moderate/contested; critical-scholarship).** McClellan: "James… says the exact opposite of what Paul is saying and is probably engaging directly with Paul's own argument, or at least how Paul was being read when the letter of James was pseudographically written decades later."[^3] The lexical core is solid — "faith only" appears once in the New Testament, at [[James 2.24|James 2:24]], inside a negation (see [[Faith Alone Appears Once in Scripture and Is the Verse That Denies It]]) — but the further claims that James is *pseudepigraphic* and *genuinely contradicts* Paul (rather than complementing him) are contested critical positions, not neutral data. *(Argument weight: Primary — this is the note's distinctive, foil-worthy claim.)*
4. **Doctrinal "grace vs works" balance shifts over time (rating: Moderate; historical).** McClellan notes that grace-alone is "more salient" in Mormonism today while the "works based salvation of the 20th century is less salient," paralleling the Protestant Reformation's own renegotiation of the same texts.[^4] *(Argument weight: Secondary.)*

## Best Quotes
> "No text in the New Testament endorses grace alone salvation as David understands it today. And several of them directly contradict it."[^1]

> "What we see is an insistence on trusting and obeying God. That's the main part of salvation. And even in Paul, the works of the law are primarily talking about the works of the law of Moses."[^2]

> "James… says the exact opposite of what Paul is saying and is probably engaging directly with Paul's own argument… when the letter of James was pseudographically written decades later."[^3]

## Apologetic Engagement
**Steelman the critique.** McClellan's core observation is honest and strong: the New Testament authors were not systematic theologians harmonized in advance, and its soteriology is genuinely diverse. The Gospels really do condition final judgment on discipleship and obedience (Matt 7:21; 25:31–46); the New Perspective reading of Paul's "works of the law" is mainstream and well-evidenced; and James really does say "by works a man is justified, and not by faith only." A naive prooftext sola fide — "the whole New Testament teaches grace alone" — does not survive contact with these texts. This is exactly why the Evangelical "false religion" charge against Mormonism misfires: the grace-alone standard it wields is not the uniform teaching of the New Testament it claims to defend.

**The informed LDS response.** Latter-day Saints can accept McClellan's *negative* point (the New Testament does not teach Protestant sola fide) without accepting his *critical* framework. (1) The Restoration **resolves** the alleged Paul–James contradiction rather than embracing it: Paul describes initial justification *before God* by grace through faith, apart from the *Mosaic* law; James describes the vindication and life of that faith in works ("faith… made perfect" by works, James 2:22). Read this way — the LDS both/and of "by grace… after all we can do" ([[After All We Can Do Means In Spite of All We Can Do]]) — the two are complementary, not contradictory. (2) LDS **agree** with McClellan (and the New Perspective) that Paul's target is the law of Moses, which is why the Restoration affirms both grace and gospel obedience/ordinances (Articles of Faith 1:3–4). (3) LDS **reject** the reductive premises McClellan layers on top — that James is a late pseudepigraphon, that the tension is a real contradiction, and (elsewhere) that texts carry no inherent meaning (see [[The Bible Has No Inherent Meaning Apart From Interpretation]]). Canonically James is apostolic, and the Restoration's claim is precisely that the *same* gospel of faith, repentance, baptism, and endurance is taught consistently across scripture and by living prophets — not renegotiated to suit the age.

## LDS Relevance
A double-edged foil. On the narrow soteriological point McClellan is an **ally**: his reading of the New Testament aligns with LDS synergism (grace *and* covenantal obedience) far better than with Protestant sola fide, so a critic like "David" who brands Mormonism a "false religion" for teaching works is judging it by a grace-alone standard the New Testament itself does not hold — see [[Synergism Is Ancient and Sola Fide Is the Real Innovation]]. But he is also a **foil**: his fuller package (biblical self-contradiction, James as pseudepigraphon, meaning-as-negotiation, a 19th-century Book of Mormon) is incompatible with the Restoration's claim to a consistent, revealed gospel. Latter-day Saints should take the exegetical gift and decline the naturalistic frame.
