# Being With Heavenly Father vs a Reward in Glory

## The Claim
Much of the debate hinges on what "heaven" *is*. [[Jacob Hansen]] (LDS) holds that heaven is a **spectrum of kingdoms of glory**: everyone who accepts any measure of Christ inherits some degree of reward, and the highest — being with Heavenly Father (exaltation) — is attained through covenant faithfulness and full sanctification. [[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]] (Protestant) holds that **heaven simply is being with the triune God**; scripture's "different rewards" are different *capacities to serve*, not tiers of access to God, and the lower LDS kingdoms are therefore not "heaven" at all.

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Hansen: heaven is a spectrum of kingdoms (Argument weight: Primary — LDS side)
"Heaven is a spectrum is the way to think of it."[^1] He grounds it in D&C 88: "there are many kingdoms, for there is no space where there is no kingdom. And there is no kingdom where there is no space either greater or lesser," describing three levels as a depth of *relationship* — the lowest a relationship with the Spirit, the next with the Son, and exaltation the fullness, "to sit on a throne with him as Revelation says."[^2] Entry to *any* glory is "by just having faith in Jesus Christ," while the *degree* tracks obedience.[^3]

### 2. Hansen: a patristic witness to three tiers — Papias (Argument weight: Secondary — LDS side)
Hansen reads [[Papias]] (writing c. 110 AD, plausibly a hearer of the apostle John): "there is this distinction between the habitation of those who produce 100-fold and those who produce 60-fold and those who produce 30-fold... The first will be taken to heaven, the second will dwell in paradise, the last will inhabit the city. For... the Lord declared, 'In my Father's house are many mansions.'"[^4] See also [[Degrees of Glory vs Heaven]].

### 3. Ryan: the lower kingdoms are not heaven, because you are not with God (Argument weight: Primary — Protestant side)
Ryan reframes: "there's these lower levels of heaven — it's not heaven because you're not even with God. You're not with the triune God... I wouldn't want to be in a place where I don't even get to be with my Heavenly Father or Jesus Christ. That's terrible."[^5] Reading LDS sources himself, he notes D&C 76 assigns the terrestrial kingdom a visit from Christ "but not by Heavenly Father," and the telestial a visit from the Holy Ghost "but not by the Father or the Son."[^6] (Hansen counters that the manual Ryan quotes, *Gospel Principles*, "isn't a manual we currently use."[^7])

### 4. Ryan: "many mansions" puts all believers in the Father's house (Argument weight: Primary — Protestant side)
From John 14, Ryan argues the "many mansions/rooms" are *within* the Father's house: "anyone who's united to Christ in faith gets to be in his Father's house. Not, okay, you're out in a shack somewhere... You're in your Father's house."[^8]

### 5. Ryan: differing rewards are differing capacities, not different heavens (Argument weight: Secondary — Protestant side)
Rewards are "different capacities that people will have in heaven to glorify God, different responsibilities" — the parable of the minas (Luke 19: ten cities, five cities), like an archangel and other angels — yet all "have peace with God" (Romans 5:1) in the same heaven. The parable of the laborers ([[Matthew 20.1-16|Matthew 20:1-16]]) gives every worker the same denarius, so the deathbed convert and the lifelong saint "enjoy the same heaven being with God."[^9]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The Latter-day Saint steelman (Hansen): differentiated glory is biblical and merciful
Hansen argues differentiated glory is scriptural (differing rewards, "many mansions," Papias' three habitations) and *maximally merciful*: almost everyone who will accept any of what Christ offers receives some inheritance of glory; only those who knowingly reject the light end in outer darkness. Even the "least in the kingdom" (Matthew 5:19) is still *with* God, differing only in capacity to glorify Him. This coheres with the LDS view of eternal progression and deification (see [[Human Deification in Mormonism]]).

### The Protestant steelman (Ryan): heaven is binary — with the triune God, or loss
For Ryan the biblical hope is one place — being with the triune God on the new earth, "the blessed hope," where God "will wipe away every tear." "Rewards" and "responsibilities" vary *within* that single heaven, but proximity to God does not; a realm defined by being cut off from the Father (LDS terrestrial/telestial) is exactly what scripture would call *loss*, not a lesser heaven. This is the binary insisted on in [[Salvation Is Binary In Christ or Out of Christ Not a Spectrum]].

**Where it turns:** the two men use "heaven" in different senses. Hansen: any degree of glory/reward *is* heaven, so the model is inclusive and graduated. Ryan: only being with the Father counts as heaven, so the LDS lower kingdoms — however glorious — describe people who "don't get to be with their Heavenly Father," which he treats as a deprivation rather than a reward. The dispute over *reward vs. relationship* is therefore partly semantic and partly a real disagreement about whether nearness to God itself comes in degrees.
