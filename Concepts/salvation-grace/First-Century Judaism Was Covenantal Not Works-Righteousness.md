# First-Century Judaism Was Covenantal Not Works-Righteousness

## The Claim
The popular picture — that first-century Jews (and, by extension, Latter-day Saints) ran a "works-based religion where you tried to earn your way to heaven through good works" — is a **historical caricature with essentially no first-century evidence behind it**.[^1] [[Jacob Hansen]] argues that the actual framework of Second-Temple Judaism was what [[E.P. Sanders]] named **covenantal nomism**: God graciously elects Israel first, gives the covenant and its law second, and obedience is the *faithful response* to grace, not a wage that purchases heaven. Sin was expected, not fatal — the whole sacrificial apparatus presupposes it — so Israel was called "not to perfection, but to covenant faithfulness."[^2] The decisive test Hansen puts to critics: *"Can you show me where a first-century Jew actually says that?"*[^3]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Israel's own story runs grace-first, law-second (Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Strong — narrative structure of Exodus is uncontested)
"Did God say, 'Obey me perfectly and then I'll rescue you from Egypt?' No. He rescued them first, then he gave them the law."[^4] The Exodus precedes Sinai; the Decalogue itself opens by grounding the commands in the prior rescue ("I am the LORD thy God, which have brought thee out of the land of Egypt," [[Exodus 20.3|Exodus 20]]). "Grace and covenant came first, obedience came afterward... The entire story of Israel begins with divine mercy, not human achievement."[^4] This is the indicative-before-imperative structure at the heart of covenantal nomism.

### 2. The sacrificial system presupposes ongoing sin, not sinless perfection (Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Strong)
"Why did God establish sacrifices for his covenant people? Why a day of atonement? Why priests? Why repentance? Why forgiveness? Because the Jews knew that people would still sin. They did not expect sinless perfection. They expected covenant faithfulness."[^2] A religion built around atonement, repentance, and forgiveness is structurally *incompatible* with a merit-ledger in which one earns heaven by flawless performance — the built-in remedy for sin is the tell.

### 3. The first-century question was covenant vindication, not individual post-mortem destiny (Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate — mainstream in NT scholarship)
"First-century Jews did not think like a modern Protestant. Their primary question was not, 'How do I get to heaven when I die?' The question... was, 'Will God vindicate his covenant people?'"[^5] The hope was the resurrection and a renewed earth, not escape to a disembodied heaven — Martha's confidence at Lazarus's death is that "he shall rise again in the resurrection at the last day" ([[John 11.24|John 11:24]]), not that he is now in heaven forever.[^6] For the fuller eschatology (Sheol, the day of the Lord, and how D&C 76 recovers it) see [[Joseph Smith Reconstructed First-Century Soteriology Without Knowing It]].

### 4. The scholarly consensus abandoned the caricature (Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Moderate — the New Perspective is contested)
"Modern scholarship from E.P. Sanders to [[James Dunn]] to [[N.T. Wright]], and even many scholars who disagree with them on other issues, have largely abandoned the caricature of Judaism as a religion based on earning salvation through works. Jews of Paul's day saw themselves as a covenant people living under God's gracious election."[^7] "One of the biggest discoveries in New Testament scholarship over the last 50 years is that most Christians have misunderstood the entire framework of first-century Judaism."[^8]

### 5. The evidential challenge: produce the primary source (Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Strong as a burden-shift)
The argument's sharpest form is a demand for evidence: "the next time someone implies Paul is addressing people who thought they could earn their way to heaven, ask them one simple question. Can you show me where a first-century Jew actually says that?"[^3] The works-righteousness picture is an *assumption* read back onto Paul's opponents, not a documented Second-Temple position.

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The "old perspective" reply: Judaism was variegated, and some strands sound meritorious
The strongest rebuttal is that Sanders overstated the *uniformity* of Second-Temple Judaism. The *Justification and Variegated Nomism* project (D.A. Carson, Peter O'Brien, Mark Seifrid) marshals texts — 4 Ezra's grim arithmetic of the few who are saved, some Qumran and rabbinic passages that weigh merits against transgressions — to argue Judaism was "variegated," with genuinely merit-oriented currents, not monolithically gracious. A Reformed exegete adds that Paul's own **wage-versus-gift** antithesis (Romans 4:4-5) and "if by grace, then no more of works" (Romans 11:6) read most naturally as opposing *merit as such*, not merely ethnic boundary markers.

### The response
Even granting variegation, two things hold. First, "variegated" *already concedes* that the monolithic works-righteousness caricature is false — which is all this claim needs. Second, the evidential burden is unmoved: to justify reading Paul's opponents as heaven-earners, one must still produce a first-century Jew who asserts it, and the dominant, documented pattern remains gracious election answered by covenant faithfulness. Whether Paul's polemical "works" targets *merit* or *boundary markers* is the genuinely open, contested question — pursued in [[Works of the Law Means Mosaic Boundary Markers Not Obedience as Such]] and [[The Works-Righteousness Reading of Paul Is a Reformation Retrojection]].

## LDS Relevance
This dissolves the top Evangelical objection at the root: if the "Jews-earned-heaven" trope has no first-century basis, then the parallel charge that Latter-day Saints run a works-based salvation ("Mormons are like the Jews who thought that they could earn their way to heaven"[^9]) collapses with it. The LDS both/and — saved by grace, judged by covenant faithfulness — is not a corruption of the gospel but the *original* covenantal shape of it. See [[LDS Salvation Is by Grace Not Earned by Works]] and [[Synergism Is Ancient and Sola Fide Is the Real Innovation]].
