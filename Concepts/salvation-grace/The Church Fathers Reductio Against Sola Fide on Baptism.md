# The Church Fathers Reductio Against Sola Fide on Baptism

## The Claim (Objection to Sola Fide)
[[Jacob Hansen]] (LDS) turns the patristic record against [[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]]'s claim that baptism is unnecessary for salvation. If sola fide is true and adding baptism as a condition forfeits Christ, then the early Christians who held **baptismal regeneration** — a roster that includes named martyrs and, on Hansen's telling, essentially all Christians for the first 1,500 years — are all in hell. Ryan replies with Matthew 7 ("depart from me, I never knew you"), the claim that most fathers never addressed the question, and the narrow-road warning that a majority *are* lost.

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The named-fathers reductio, including martyrs (Argument weight: Primary — objection)
Hansen lists early Christians who taught that one had to be baptized — "Irenaeus... Justin Martyr... Cyprian of Carthage... Hippolytus of Rome, St. Patrick, St. Nicholas, St. Francis of Assisi, St. Augustine, St. Thomas Aquinas" — and draws the implication: "all of those people are actually in hell, including... four of those people are martyrs who died for the faith."[^1] See [[Irenaeus of Lyons]], [[Justin Martyr]], [[Cyprian of Carthage]], [[Hippolytus of Rome]], [[Augustine]], and [[Thomas Aquinas]].

### 2. It scales to nearly all historic Christians (Argument weight: Primary — objection)
Hansen extends it to "the majority of Christians" of "the first 1500 years," for whom "baptismal regeneration was the kind of standard Christian belief in all the writings," plus figures like a hypothetical Mother Teresa.[^2] Cross-reference the LDS/Catholic historical case in [[The Unanimous Early Church Read Baptism as Saving]].

### 3. Ryan: Matthew 7 — many "mighty works," yet lost (Argument weight: Primary — Protestant reply)
Ryan answers that doing impressive works is no guarantee: in [[Matthew 7.21|Matthew 7:21-23]], people plead "Lord, Lord, haven't I prophesied in your name and cast out demons and done many mighty works," and Jesus says "Depart from me. I never knew you." So "there are people who do a lot of... good works for the world who would end up in hell."[^3] He adds that he judges no individual's heart — "I don't make any judgment on those people because I don't know what they truly believed."[^4]

### 4. Ryan: most fathers never said baptism is necessary (Argument weight: Secondary — Protestant reply)
"A lot of them we don't even have any writings about baptism in particular and whether it's necessary... we only have a selection of them that spoke about baptism." He cites that "even in [[Tertullian]]'s day" some Christians held "faith was enough to justify Abraham without water, without baptism, so therefore it's the same with us" (Romans 4).[^5]

### 5. Ryan: the narrow road predicts a majority lost (Argument weight: Secondary — Protestant reply)
"The road to hell is broad and many are on that path; the road to heaven is narrow and few there be that find it. So I'm just following what Christ says here."[^6] A large number of the lost is, for Ryan, exactly what Jesus foretold, not a reductio.

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The Latter-day Saint / Catholic steelman (Hansen): patristic consensus is decisive where it speaks
The objection's force is historical and moral: the students of the apostles' students (Irenaeus in the circle of John; Justin; Cyprian) and virtually every father who addressed the topic affirmed baptism's saving necessity, so a doctrine that damns them plus the great martyrs is prima facie implausible — it would mean the visible church erred on a salvation-essential from the sub-apostolic age. See [[The New Testament Repeatedly Says Baptism Saves]] and [[Baptism Is Gods Work Not a Human Work That Earns Salvation]].

### The Protestant steelman (Ryan): scripture over consensus, and baptism as obedient sign
Ryan's reply has three moves: (1) *sola scriptura* — the fathers can be, and were, wrong; consensus does not overturn Romans 4; (2) baptism is a genuine, commanded good, "just not as a way to gain salvation" — an act of obedience, not a cause of it (see [[Salvation Is by Faith Alone and Baptism Is a Non-Saving Act of Obedience]]); (3) he refuses to pronounce any individual damned, holding only that *if* someone finally trusts their baptism/works rather than Christ, they are lost. He also disputes the historical premise, insisting the "necessary" claim rests on "a selection" of fathers, not the whole.

**Where it turns:** Hansen presses that among fathers who *did* address baptism, "most of them did" affirm its necessity, so silence does not rescue sola fide; Ryan presses that patristic testimony cannot bind conscience against Paul, and that Jesus' own narrow-road teaching means a shocking number of professed believers being lost is a *feature* of the gospel's warning, not an absurd consequence of it.
