# Belief Alone Saves and Is Distinct From Faith as Works

## The Claim
**[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** draws a sharp lexical line between **belief** and **faith**. *Faith*, he says, "means works... it's synonymous with works" — the doing that flows from conviction (Hebrews 11: "By faith Abel did something. By faith Noah did something... the substance of that which is unseen"). *Belief*, by contrast, "is a decision that is simply made in your heart or in your mind." Salvation, on his reading of [[John 6.47|John 6:47]], is offered on **belief** — not faith-as-works: "What Jesus is saying is not that you have to do anything. He just says if you will believe. It's the easiest commandment there is... all you have to do is do it one time."[^1] This is the mechanism behind his present-possession thesis (see [[Eternal Life Is a Present Possession Not Only Future Exaltation]]): because saving belief is a single, effortless heart-choice, eternal life can be had instantly.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The belief/faith split is lexically motivated.** Mark grounds the distinction in Hebrews 11's "by faith X *did*" pattern (faith = enacted) versus the Gospel of John's bare "believe and have life."[^1] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Weak-to-Moderate* — it is a real intuition (faith often connotes fidelity, belief connotes assent), but Greek does not sustain a clean two-word system: John's Gospel uses the verb *pisteuō* ("believe") for saving response, cognate with the very *pistis* Mark assigns to "works."

2. **It dissolves the works-righteousness worry.** If saving belief requires no act, then no one can boast, backslide into scorekeeping, or lose assurance over performance — the pastoral payoff Mark repeatedly stresses ("I don't have to struggle daily").[^2] *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Weak as proof, strong as motivation.*

3. **"The easiest commandment."** Belief is framed as maximally accessible — "a third grader can understand" John 6:47 — which Mark uses to argue no teaching authority or theological training is needed to be saved.[^3] *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Weak* (it assumes the disputed reading of the verse).

## Best Quotes
> "Faith means works. It's synonymous with works. Belief, on the other hand, is a simple choice that is made in your heart or in your mind."[^1]

> "What Jesus is saying is not that you have to do anything. He just says if you will believe. Of all the commandments, belief is the easiest, and all you have to do is do it one time."[^1]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**[[Hayden Carroll]] denies the belief/faith wedge and reads the same Greek word the opposite way.** His case:

- ***Pistis* means allegiance, not bare assent.** Where Mark makes belief effortless and faith works-laden, Carroll (following Matthew Bates) reads *pistis* as **allegiance/loyalty** to Christ — a response that inherently includes trust *and* faithfulness. See the fuller treatment in [[Faith Is Allegiance to Christ Not Mere Mental Assent]].[^4] On this view Mark has bisected one biblical reality into two English words and then kept only the half that asks nothing.

- **Ephesians already integrates it.** Carroll cites Ephesians 2: "we're saved by grace through faith, which is then manifested in works. God ordained... good works."[^5] Works are neither the price of salvation nor absent from it; they are its evidence — see [[Works Are the Fruit of Faith Not the Price of Salvation]]. James 2 (a live "faith without works is dead") points the same way; cf. [[James 2-24 - Justified by Works Not Faith Alone]].

- **Jesus ties life to doing, not mere assent.** [[Matthew 7.21|Matthew 7:21]] ("not every one that saith... Lord, Lord... but he that *doeth* the will") and the whole Sermon on the Mount cut against "believe once and do nothing" — see [[Jesus Taught Salvation Involves Doing Not Faith Alone]]. Mark himself concedes the LDS point that genuine belief produces action ("are you going to do a bunch of things because you believe? Absolutely"), which quietly reintroduces the works Mark's definition tried to exclude.

- **Fair point for Mark:** his distinction is a recognizable, historically Protestant instinct (assent *fiducia* vs. mere *notitia/assensus*), and it rightly resists any scheme where the sinner earns standing. The disagreement is not "grace vs. works" — both deny earning — but whether covenant obedience is *internal to* saving faith (Carroll) or a mere downstream option (Mark). See also [[Sola Fide vs Faith Plus Ordinances]].
