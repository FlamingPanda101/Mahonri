# Regeneration Precedes Faith So We Only Seem to Choose God

## The Claim
Reviewing his debate with the Reformed evangelist [[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]] (of needgod.net), [[Jacob Hansen]] isolates the engine of Ryan's soteriology: **monergism**. On this view no one freely chooses God; God first gives the sinner a "new heart" (regeneration), and only that God-given heart then produces faith. So when Ryan says "from our perspective, yes, we do make the choice," Hansen underlines *from our perspective* — the choosing is real to us but is wholly caused by God's prior, unconditional act. Faith itself is therefore not a contribution: it is "an empty hand" with "no intrinsic value," valuable only because of its object, Christ.

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. "From our perspective" we choose — but God does the choosing (Argument weight: Primary)
Pressed on whether we can "reach up to God and take his hand," Ryan concedes only a perspectival choice: "From our perspective. Yes, we do make the choice."[^1] Hansen's gloss: "A Calvinist like him does not believe that we choose God but rather that God chooses us. The idea is that no one chooses God unless he gives them a new heart."[^2] The asymmetry is personal — the only reason Hansen does not believe as Ryan does is that "God has chosen not to regenerate me but did choose to regenerate him."[^3]

### 2. The cut-brakes car analogy makes the mechanism visible (Argument weight: Primary — illustration)
Hansen's shared analogy: humanity is a family in a car with cut brakes, rolling toward an ocean cliff — helpless sinners who cannot save themselves. In the traditional/synergist picture, "Jesus shows up in a helicopter and... says to everyone in the car, take my hand," and each person may "grab his hand and hold on to it or not."[^4] In Ryan's Calvinist picture, God "does everything": he "gives some people new hearts so that they will take his hand and then does not give new hearts to other people," even though "he could give a new heart to everyone."[^5] The response we feel ourselves making is real, but only the regenerate are given the capacity to make it.

### 3. Ezekiel 36 and Ephesians 2: the dead cannot raise themselves (Argument weight: Secondary — Reformed prooftexts)
Ryan grounds the mechanism in scripture: "You got [[Ephesians 2.1-5|Ephesians 2]] where it says we're dead in our trespasses and sins and he makes us alive... He takes out the heart of stone, gives us a new heart... [[Ezekiel 36]] talks about that."[^6] By nature "we don't want God," so "thankfully, God does something in us to give us that desire to have him."[^7]

### 4. Faith is "an empty hand" with no intrinsic value (Argument weight: Primary)
To keep salvation from becoming a boast (Romans 4:5, "to the one who does not work but believes"), Ryan insists "faith is not a work" and "faith is like an empty hand... it has no intrinsic value... because it's about the object of our faith. It's not about our faith itself." He is saved "not because my faith is amazing" but "because Christ has infinite value."[^8] Cf. [[Ephesians 2.8-9|Ephesians 2:8-9]] and [[John 3.18|John 3:18]] ("condemned already").

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The Reformed steelman (Ryan): monergism alone protects grace
If a corpse cannot quicken itself ([[Ephesians 2.1-5|Eph 2:1-5]]) and the heart of stone must be replaced before it can love God ([[Ezekiel 36]]), then any human choice *prior* to grace would be a work, and salvation would be partly self-earned — exactly what Paul denies (Romans 4:5). Making faith an "empty hand" is precisely how the Reformed keep 100% of the credit on Christ: the value is in the object grasped, not in the grasping. This is the same logic behind [[Justification - Infusion vs Legal Fiction|imputed righteousness]], and it is why [[Seeking God Versus Arbitrary Election|a Reformed reader will say even our "seeking" is itself prevenient grace]].

### The Latter-day Saint / synergist reply (Hansen): a caused choice is not a choice
Hansen's counter is that a "choice" God unilaterally causes is no real choice at all, and that this is the whole problem: it makes God, not the person, the deciding cause of who is saved and who is damned (see [[Calvinism Turns God Into a Moral Monster That Breeds Atheism]]). If God "could give a new heart to everyone" but withholds it from most, the outcome is God's, not ours — which both guts the sincerity of Reformed evangelism (see [[The Regeneration Rejoinder - Calvinist Evangelism Is Self-Defeating]]) and makes genuine love impossible, since [[Coerced Love Is Not Love - Agency Against Irresistible Grace|love cannot be coerced]]. The LDS alternative affirms real, un-caused agency responding to a universally offered grace, so that [[Predestination Does Not Entail Determinism|predestination need not collapse into determinism]].
