# All We Can Do Is Defined as Repentance in Alma 24

## The Claim
The single most-weaponized phrase against Latter-day Saint soteriology — "it is by grace that we are saved, **after all we can do**" ([[2 Nephi 25.23|2 Nephi 25:23]]) — is **defined by the Book of Mormon itself**. At [[Alma 24.11|Alma 24:11]] the Anti-Nephi-Lehies use the identical phrase, "all that we could do," and there it means one specific thing: **to repent**. Read the crux verse with that internal definition and it says "saved by grace after we *repent*" — a gift accepted, not a checklist of works completed before grace activates.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The "defined term" method.** [[Jeff Harmon]], drawing on Angel Studios' litigation experience, notes that a legal document fixes a capitalized *defined term* by its own in-text definition. Applied to scripture: where a distinctive phrase recurs, let the text define itself rather than importing an outside meaning.[^1] *(Argument weight: Primary — the whole reading rests on this move.)*
2. **Alma 24:11 supplies the definition — twice in one verse.** "since it has been all that we could do... to repent of all our sins and the many murders which we have committed... for it was all we could do to repent sufficiently before God that he would take away our stain." The speakers, self-described "most lost of all mankind," name repentance as the entirety of what lay in their power.[^2] *(Evidence rating: Strong — verbally identical phrase, same authorial tradition.)*
3. **The intratextual syllogism.** If 2 Nephi 25:23's "all we can do" (A) is the same phrase (B) that Alma 24:11 defines as "repent" (C), then A = C: "by grace we are saved after we repent." Harmon frames it as "money-balling the Book of Mormon" — the same word-frequency exegesis critics apply to the Bible, turned on the Book of Mormon.[^3]
4. **"Accept the gift of repentance" guards the reading.** Harmon deliberately phrases it "after we accept the *gift* of repentance" (Elder D. Todd Christofferson's language), so repentance is not smuggled back in as a new work. Otherwise the perfectionist relapses into "I just haven't repented *enough*" — trading one works-treadmill for another.[^4]
5. **Corroborated by the Doctrine and Covenants.** The Lord repeatedly reduces the required message to repentance: "Say nothing but repentance unto this generation" (D&C 11:9); "the thing which will be of the most worth unto you will be to declare repentance unto this people" (D&C 15:6). Repentance can be "all we can do" precisely because it is the one thing chiefly asked.[^5]
6. **A fresh key that complements the old ones.** Traditionally Latter-day Saints answered the objection with a *historical-idiom* argument — that in 1830s English "after all we can do" meant "in spite of / despite all we can do" — plus [[Steven Robinson|Stephen Robinson]]'s bicycle parable (a child's few cents cannot buy the bike; the parent pays all the rest) and [[Brad Wilcox]]'s "Jesus doesn't make up the difference, but he makes all the difference." Harmon's is a different route: an *intratextual word-study*, not an appeal to period dictionaries. See [[After All We Can Do Means In Spite of All We Can Do]].[^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **Rival Latter-day Saint reading.** The older "despite / apart from all we can do" idiom (granted even by the Evangelical [[Kyle Beshears]]) *negates* human contribution outright, whereas the defined-term reading still makes repentance a temporal prerequisite ("after we repent"). A grace-alone critic could say Harmon quietly reintroduces a condition the "despite" reading avoids. **Response:** repentance received as an accepted *gift* (a grace-enabled turning, *metanoia*) is not a meritorious work, so both readings terminate in grace-alone; they differ on mechanism, not outcome — see [[Conflating Salvation with Exaltation Breeds Works Burnout]].
- **"Just another reinterpretation."** An Evangelical may answer that this moves the goalposts from the plain "do your part, then grace covers the shortfall," and that the LDS *system* (ordinances, covenants, temple worthiness, exaltation tiers) remains synergistic however one parses a single verse. **Response:** the same prooftext standard would convict Protestants via James 2:24, Matthew 7:21, and Acts 2:38 — see [[The Prooftext Double Standard on Grace and Works]] — and Alma 24:11 is a *textual* datum, not an ad hoc gloss.
- **Is it really a "definition"?** A cautious exegete can object that Alma 24:11 merely *uses* similar words in a narrow context (the Anti-Nephi-Lehies' repentance from murder); the Book of Mormon has no formal definitional apparatus, so calling it a "defined term" over-reads a thematic parallel as a lexical-technical one. **Response:** even as a thematic cross-reference within one authorial tradition (Nephi and the abridger Mormon), the verbally identical phrase points the reader to repentance as the intended content of "all we can do."

## LDS Connection
This is a flashpoint verse with Evangelical critics who read it as proof of works-salvation. The defined-term reading anchors the Restoration squarely in salvation by grace ([[LDS Salvation Is by Grace Not Earned by Works]]) using the Book of Mormon's own vocabulary. The episode frames it provocatively as showing that the traditional works-forward emphasis — associated in the title-joke with [[Bruce R. McConkie]] ("an official petition to update the footnotes... Bruce R. McConkie done messed up, according to Angel Studios") — needs correcting; the panel offers this as a lighthearted quip rather than a documented critique of McConkie's writings.[^8]
