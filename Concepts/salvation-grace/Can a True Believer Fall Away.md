# Can a True Believer Fall Away

## The Claim
[[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]] (Reformed) holds **eternal security**: a genuine believer is "kept for Jesus Christ" and cannot finally fall away, so he claims to be 100% sure of his salvation. [[Jacob Hansen]] (LDS) holds that a real believer, as a **free agent**, retains the possibility of rejecting Christ, so no one can claim absolute certainty of the future; salvation is conditioned on *abiding* / enduring to the end.

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Ryan: the believer is kept by God (Argument weight: Primary — Protestant side)
Ryan's assurance rests on divine keeping, not his own strength: "God's going to sustain me to the end. 1 Corinthians 1:8, guiltless in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ... Jude 1... I'm kept for Jesus Christ," plus Philippians 1:6 (God "who began that good work... will bring it to completion").[^1] He cites John 10 — the sheep "hear his voice... they will never perish, and no one will snatch them out of" his hand[^2] — and Romans 8:1 (no condemnation; nothing can separate). Asked whether he *could* reject Jesus, he answers, "I never would. Just like God never lies... God would never let that happen for someone who belongs to him."[^3]

### 2. Hansen: agency entails the real possibility of falling away (Argument weight: Primary — LDS side)
Pressed on whether he is 100% guaranteed to remain with Heavenly Father, Hansen answers, "Yes — if I abide in Christ." When Ryan asks if his abiding is itself guaranteed, Hansen concedes: "I have to [abide] because I'm an agent. There is a possibility."[^4] For Hansen, libertarian agency means perseverance is genuinely up to the person, so certainty attaches to a *present* relationship, not to a future outcome.

### 3. John 15 — was the branch ever in the vine? (Argument weight: Primary — the crux text)
Reading [[John 15.1-6|John 15]], Hansen argues a branch truly "in me" must abide or be "cast forth... and burned," and that keeping Christ's commandments is how one abides (v.10). He challenges Ryan: "How can a branch fail to remain in the vine if it was never actually in the vine in the first place?"[^5] Ryan answers that the unfruitful branch is a *false* believer — "in the church we have true believers and we have false believers"; someone who "looks like he's in Christ" but was never savingly united, since true union inevitably bears fruit (v.5).[^6]

### 4. Hebrews 10:26 and 1 Corinthians 9:27 (Argument weight: Secondary — LDS side)
Hansen cites [[Hebrews 10.26|Hebrews 10:26]] ("if we go on sinning deliberately, there no longer remains a sacrifice for sins") and [[1 Corinthians 9.27|1 Corinthians 9:27]], where even Paul disciplines himself "lest... I myself should be disqualified" (*adokimos*, the word for being rejected by God).[^7] Ryan replies that Hebrews 10's sin is the specific apostasy of abandoning Christ for the old sacrifices — and its warnings ("hold fast," v.23) are the very *means* God uses to keep believers — while 1 Corinthians 9's context is Paul's *ministry*, not his salvation.[^8]

### 5. The self-deception press (Argument weight: Secondary)
Hansen turns Ryan's certainty against him: "Are you 100% sure you're not self-deceived? ... I've talked with other people who ended up abandoning Christ, and they all said the same things as you."[^9] Ryan answers that his confidence is in the *object* of his faith, not introspection: "I don't know their heart, but I do know what I'm trusting in" (2 Timothy 1:12, "I know whom I have believed"). This is the mirror of the argument developed in [[Calvinism Cannot Ground Assurance of Salvation]].

## Best Quotes

> "I have to [abide] because I'm an agent. There is a possibility." — Jacob Hansen[^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The Protestant steelman (Ryan): perseverance of the saints
True saving faith is God-sustained, so those genuinely in Christ *will* endure; Ryan even affirms "we have to continue trusting in Jesus to the end," but adds "everyone who does truly believe will endure" (1 Corinthians 1:8). The warning passages target apostates or, in 1 Corinthians 9, credible ministry; John 15's fruitless branches were never truly united (1 John 2:19). Assurance is therefore not arrogance but trust in God's promise — "if God promises me something, I can be sure he's going to deliver." His pastoral refrain: rely on Christ, because "you're the weakest link."

### The Latter-day Saint steelman (Hansen): conditional security and real agency
If agency is real, the possibility of rejecting God is real; abiding (John 15:10), enduring to the end (Matthew 24:13), and not "sinning wilfully" (Hebrews 10:26) are genuine conditions the agent must meet. Hansen does not deny God's help — he affirms God softens hearts and is "the author and finisher of our faith" — but insists a salvation that *cannot* be refused would make the person a non-agent. Certainty of one's *present* covenant relationship is warranted; a guarantee about one's *future* free choices is not. The Catholic form of this dispute is in [[Assurance of Salvation - Catholic vs Protestant]].

**Where it turns:** Ryan grounds certainty in monergistic keeping (God will not let his own fall), so the warnings are means, not open possibilities; Hansen grounds uncertainty in libertarian freedom (a real branch can stop abiding), so the warnings describe live outcomes. The self-deception exchange is the pressure point: Hansen says the apostate "said the same things as you," so Ryan's introspective certainty is unreliable; Ryan says certainty rests on Christ's finished work and promise, not on reading his own heart.
