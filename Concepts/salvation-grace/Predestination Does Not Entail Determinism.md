# Predestination Does Not Entail Determinism

## The Claim (Objection Rebutted)

Calvinism looks biblically inevitable only because two words have been quietly redefined. [[Josh Klene]] and [[Tim Stratton]] argue that **sovereignty** need not mean "meticulous deterministic control of every molecule," and **predestination** need not mean "deterministic ordering of all things." Strip out those idiosyncratic definitions and the [[Predestination|predestination]] passages of Scripture no longer entail determinism. See [[Predestination]].

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The whole steelman rests on two definitions (Argument weight: Primary)

Klene's concession-and-turn: "to steelman Calvinism, all you have to do is accept two definitions of two different words — sovereignty and predestination. If you believe that sovereign means he must have a meticulous deterministic control over every molecule of the universe, including the human will... and if predestination means... he has to order everything deterministically, then you could easily come to the Calvinist understanding of scripture."[^1] The entire system is downstream of those two definitions.

### 2. The definitions are historical novelties (Argument weight: Primary)

"My quibble is that sovereignty has never, ever meant that... until about ten minutes ago... And predestination doesn't have to equate to meticulous deterministic control either."[^2] Klene's positive method: rival readings are decided by **parsimony** and **authorial intent** — "what makes the most sense and is the simplest given all of the data, especially going back to authorial intent."[^3]

### 3. Stratton's deductive wedge between the two words (Argument weight: Primary — Deductive)

Stratton reports "a deductive argument that ends with *therefore, predestination and determination ought not be conflated*." Seeing the word "predestination" and assuming it means "determination" lets you make sense of *some* passages "but it violates other big sections of scripture" — the passages that "heavily imply or entail libertarian freedom, or what the LDS church often calls agency."[^4] A view "that makes sense of the whole of scripture" cannot keep the predestination texts by discarding the agency texts.

### 4. The historical mechanism: reacting to Pelagius (Argument weight: Secondary — Historical)

Both trace the hardening of these definitions to church controversy. Stratton: "I would just blame Augustine."[^5] Klene: Calvinism was "a big reaction... to Pelagianism" — and in over-correcting, its architects "started reading any sort of work in scripture as any sort of human will or effort," which he calls "a poor exegesis of what 'works' means in Romans." Words "got redefined so that... we can really knock out... the Pelagians," definitions "instantiated" by the time of the **Synod of Dort**.[^6] Compare [[The Pelagianism Erratum]] and [[The Pelagian Controversy on Grace and Human Ability]].

## Best Quotes

> "Sovereignty has never, ever meant that... until about ten minutes ago. And predestination doesn't have to equate to meticulous deterministic control." — [[Josh Klene]][^2]

> "A mistake that many have made is just seeing the word predestination and assuming that that means determination." — [[Tim Stratton]][^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### Objection 1: The Calvinist definitions are the biblical ones
**Steelman (Calvinist):** These are not novelties smuggled in "ten minutes ago" but the plain sense of Romans 9's potter and clay, Ephesians 1's election "before the foundation of the world," and Isaiah's "I will accomplish all my purpose." Divine sovereignty *just is* meticulous providence; anything less makes God a spectator whose plans creatures can veto.

**Response:** The Molinist grants exhaustive providence but locates it in God's [[Molinism|middle knowledge]] rather than causal determination — God ordains the world (predestination) by selecting among feasible free outcomes he foreknows, so the texts are satisfied without conflating foreordination with causation. Romans 9's "hardening," on this reading, concerns the *corporate*, historical outworking of the covenant, not a decree fixing each individual's eternal fate. See [[Romans 9]].

### Objection 2: Compatibilist freedom preserves both sovereignty and responsibility
**Steelman:** Reformed thinkers are compatibilists: God determines, yet humans act freely by doing what they most desire. So "determinism" is not the puppetry the critics allege.

**Response:** Klene's rejoinder is that compatibilism becomes unfalsifiable and, more decisively, that a will whose deepest desires were themselves fixed by another is not the author of its acts in the sense moral responsibility requires (developed in [[The Freethinking Argument - Determinism Undermines Rationality]] and [[Decreeing All Things Makes God the Author of Sin]]).
