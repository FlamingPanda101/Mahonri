# Faith Is Allegiance to Christ Not Mere Mental Assent

## The Claim
On the debate's stated topic — *the message of salvation* — [[Hayden Carroll]] agrees with [[Greg (Collierville Pastor)]] that salvation is "by grace... through faith," but defines **faith** (Greek *pistis*) as **allegiance**, not bare intellectual assent. Citing the evangelical New Testament scholar Matthew Bates (*Salvation by Allegiance Alone*), he argues Paul's word meant an "unceasing dedication to Christ" that necessarily shows up in action and covenant obedience — including baptism — while insisting "it's not the baptism that saves us."[^1] The upshot: professed faith contradicted by one's actions is evidence of *no* faith.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **The lexical claim: *pistis* = allegiance.** "Faith in the Greek, in the word *pistis*, is allegiance... the idea that we have allegiance to Christ — not that we're going to be perfect, but that we have an unceasing dedication to Christ and to what he's asking us to do."[^1] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate* — Bates's reading is a real, published scholarly proposal, though contested (see steelman).

2. **Faith is evidenced by works.** "If somebody says they accept Jesus Christ but their actions show otherwise, that's evidence that they actually don't have faith in him."[^2] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Moderate* — this framing (works as *evidence*, not *price*) is one many evangelicals share; the live question is whether covenant ordinances are *necessary* expressions.

3. **Baptism is the expression, not the cause.** Because Christ commanded baptism, one who genuinely has faith "will choose to be baptized" — but Carroll is emphatic: "it's not the baptism that saves us. I think that's often where we get misunderstood."[^3] See [[Baptism Is Gods Work Not a Human Work That Earns Salvation]] and [[Sola Fide vs Faith Plus Ordinances]]. *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Moderate.*

4. **Grace-first framing (closing statement).** "I am saved on the merits of Jesus Christ. Nothing I can do can merit my salvation... He requires allegiance, which is faith. And I won't be perfect, but luckily Christ doesn't require perfection."[^4] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Strong* as a statement of the LDS grace/works synthesis — cf. [[LDS Salvation Is by Grace Not Earned by Works]].

## Best Quotes
> "Faith in the Greek, in the word *pistis*, is allegiance... an unceasing dedication to Christ and to what he's asking us to do."[^1]

> "It's not the baptism that saves us. I think that's often where we get misunderstood."[^3]

> "I am saved on the merits of Jesus Christ. Nothing I can do can merit my salvation... He requires allegiance, which is faith."[^4]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
[[Greg (Collierville Pastor)]] holds classic evangelical **sola fide**, and his case is strong:

- **Nothing to earn — faith alone is the instrument.** "There's nothing you have to do to earn it. You can just trust Jesus and be born again and have life everlastingly."[^5] Works are the *fruit* of a regenerated heart, never any part of the *instrument* of justification (see [[Works Are the Fruit of Faith Not the Price of Salvation]]). On this view, "allegiance" language risks smuggling the fruit back into the root.

- **"It is finished" leaves nothing to add.** Greg's closing: Christ's finished work means "you don't need anything else. You don't need another covenant... You don't need to make your own covenants... feel closer to God by taking covenants. You can just rest in the everlasting covenant."[^6] The LDS system of ongoing temple covenants is, to him, precisely the addition *sola fide* excludes.

- **Where they actually agree — and don't.** Both affirm that living faith produces works (cf. James 2, [[James 2-24 - Justified by Works Not Faith Alone]]); the disagreement is whether covenant ordinances are *evidential fruit* (which both can grant) or *necessary conditions* of the faith itself (the LDS/Bates lean). *A fair point that cuts toward Greg:* Matthew Bates is himself a Protestant, and his "allegiance" thesis was advanced within — and contested inside — evangelical scholarship; the LDS appropriation goes beyond Bates by tying allegiance to restored **priesthood ordinances**, which Bates does not. So the citation supports "faith is more than assent," not the specifically LDS covenant framework. *A fair point that cuts toward Carroll:* the "allegiance" reading blunts the caricature that Latter-day Saints try to *earn* heaven — Carroll explicitly denies any human act can merit salvation.
