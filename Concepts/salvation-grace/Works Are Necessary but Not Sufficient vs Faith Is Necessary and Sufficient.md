# Works Are Necessary but Not Sufficient vs Faith Is Necessary and Sufficient

## The Claim
The debate's central fault line is a modal one. [[Jacob Hansen]] (LDS) frames works as **necessary but not sufficient** for salvation: a saving faith is a *living* faith that necessarily produces works (James), so works are a required condition even though they never "earn" anything. [[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]] (Reformed) counters that trusting Christ is **both necessary and sufficient** — works are the *fruit* of justification, never a condition of it — and that making works necessary forfeits Christ entirely (Galatians 5).

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. Hansen's syllogism: works → living faith → salvation (Argument weight: Primary — LDS side)
Hansen builds a deductive argument: "If B requires A and C requires B, then C requires A. A is works. B is living faith... works are required to have living faith and living faith is required to receive salvation," so works are required for salvation.[^1] He states the category directly: "It's works are necessary but not sufficient. That's the category that you should use."[^2] His car-on-the-cliff picture keeps grace primary while making works a condition: "Your works will never be sufficient to get you back up that hill. But your works are required to take the hand of Jesus."[^3] *(Evidence rating: Moderate — the syllogism is valid only if "living faith" analytically contains works, which is the contested premise.)*

### 2. The James engine: dead faith cannot save (Argument weight: Primary — LDS side)
Hansen grounds the "living faith" premise in James: "Can dead faith save us? James says, 'As the body without the spirit is dead, so faith without works is dead.'" He presses that James creates two categories — living faith and dead faith — and only the former saves.[^4] See [[James 2.14-26|James 2:14-26]] and [[James 2-24 - Justified by Works Not Faith Alone]].

### 3. Ryan: faith is necessary AND sufficient — else Paul and Jesus lied (Argument weight: Primary — Protestant side)
Ryan refuses the "necessary but not sufficient" category for faith itself: "Believing in Jesus is necessary for salvation and... sufficient. It's both necessary and sufficient to trust in Christ."[^5] His warrant is the plain apostolic promise: when Paul tells the Philippian jailer "believe in the Lord Jesus Christ and you'll be saved" (Acts 16:31), or Jesus says "whoever believes has eternal life" (John 6:47), "Paul would be lying if believing was insufficient."[^6]

### 4. Ryan: the justified man is the one who does NOT work (Argument weight: Secondary — Protestant side)
From [[Romans 4.4-5|Romans 4:4-5]], Ryan notes the text justifies "the one who does not work but believes" — the person is explicitly *not working* — and that "faith is not a work according to the apostle Paul." He calls faith "an empty hand" with "no intrinsic value... because it's about the object of our faith," Christ, not the faith itself.[^7]

### 5. Ryan: any added condition severs from Christ (Argument weight: Secondary — Protestant side)
From [[Galatians 5.2-4|Galatians 5:2-4]], Ryan argues that trusting Christ *plus* an act of obedience (circumcision, baptism, an ordinance) means "he's not a partial savior. He's a complete savior. So either you have him entirely or you don't have him at all."[^8]

## Best Quotes
> "It's works are necessary but not sufficient. That's the category that you should use." — Jacob Hansen[^2]

> "Believing in Jesus is necessary for salvation and... sufficient... Otherwise, you're calling Jesus and Paul a liar." — Ryan[^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The Protestant steelman (Ryan): works evidence faith; they do not condition it
Ryan concedes works are real and expected, but assigns them a different role — *evidence* before others, not a cause of justification. Reading [[James 2-24 - Justified by Works Not Faith Alone|James 2:24]], he takes "justified by works and not by faith alone" in the sense of *vindicate*: "my works vindicate or justify me before people... you can't look inside my heart, but you can look at my works" (James 2:18).[^9] On this reading the "faith alone" James denies is *dead* faith (mere profession), not the Reformation's living *sola fide*. He also cites Genesis 15:6 — Abraham "believed God and it was counted to him as righteousness" *before* any obedience — so justification precedes works. See [[Faith Alone Appears Once in Scripture and Is the Verse That Denies It]] and [[James - Justification Before Men Reply Rebutted]].

### The Latter-day Saint steelman (Hansen): "faith alone" is the phrase scripture explicitly denies
Hansen answers that James 2:24 is the *only* verse pairing "faith" and "alone," and it *negates* the pairing; James further says Abraham's "faith was completed by works" (James 2:22), so faith has "an intended end" that includes works.[^10] Crucially, Hansen denies that his works *earn* anything — they are covenant faithfulness *responding* to grace ("I haven't even mentioned the word earn"), so the Reformed "either merit or grace" dichotomy misframes the LDS position. The deeper LDS move is to relocate the question from *entry* to *covenant maintenance* (see [[Justification Is Covenant Entry Not a Guaranteed Final Verdict]]).

**Where it turns:** Ryan's rebuttal is that James' own resolution (v.18, "I will show you my faith by my works") makes works the *demonstration* of a faith that already justifies, whereas Hansen's rebuttal is that a "faith" guaranteed to be inert unless it produces works is, by James' definition, not yet saving faith — so the disagreement reduces to whether "living faith" is faith-plus-works or faith-that-will-work. Compare the LDS synthesis in [[Faith and Works - Two Blades of the Scissors]] and [[Works Are the Fruit of Faith Not the Price of Salvation]].
