# Conflating Salvation with Exaltation Breeds Works Burnout

## The Claim
A pastoral and soteriological diagnosis: Latter-day Saint culture often **merges salvation with exaltation**. Salvation is secured by faith and repentance — received as a gift of grace; exaltation is the *covenant path* (likened to the parable of the talents, "the kingdom of heaven, which is after salvation"). When the two are collapsed, members "jump over" faith and repentance straight to covenant-keeping and perfection-seeking, and the result is a works-treadmill that ends in burnout: "you're either going to snap and turn to Jesus, or you're going to snap and leave."[^1][^2]

## Supporting Evidence
1. **The conflation named.** [[Jeff Harmon]] distinguishes salvation (which *follows* faith and repentance) from the covenant path / exaltation (tied to the parable of the talents). Merging them makes people "never truly find faith and repentance," so they "just work and work and work" — "we jump over salvation to get to exaltation... you can't have the one without the foundation of the other."[^1] *(Argument weight: Primary.)*
2. **Repentance is *metanoia* — direction, not a perfection-destination.** The word means to *turn the heart* to Christ. It does not mean you will not fail again, only that you keep turning back. This corrects the "ABCs of repentance" model (admit / ... / don't do it again — and if you do it again, "you didn't repent"), which fuses repentance with flawless performance.[^3]
3. **Sin as "missing the mark."** The Greek *hamartia* is an archery term — missing the target. Repentance is "taking another shot"; God trains rather than condemning on the first miss. "All we can do is take another shot."[^4]
4. **Givens on fear vs. healing.** [[Terryl Givens]]'s *All Things New* traces the fear attached to repentance to a translation shift — "by your faith you are *healed*" later rendered "by your faith you are *saved*" (attributed in the episode, tentatively, to [[William Tyndale]]). A "wounded → healed" register (how the Book of Mormon speaks) is truer and less fear-driven than "damned → saved."[^5]
5. **Deathbed repentance and the thief on the cross.** If repentance is the turning that "all we can do" names, a genuine last-moment turn to Christ is efficacious — the panel affirms it "valid" (the thief on the cross), while hedging that *exaltation*, not bare salvation, is the real aim, so this is no license for presumption.[^6]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
- **The antinomian worry (cheap grace).** Does decoupling salvation from the covenant path license "a life of sin and debauchery" so long as you turn at the very end? The host raises exactly this — "grace kicks in, baby." **Steelman:** this looks like fire-insurance grace that hollows out discipleship. **Response:** the panel locates the *goal* not in bare salvation but in exaltation — the covenant path and the talents — and treats genuine repentance as a reorientation of the heart that bears fruit over time, not a one-time transaction. See [[Repentance as Willingness Not a Cleared Ledger]] and [[Works Are the Fruit of Faith Not the Price of Salvation]].
- **Perfectionism is real, but the cure is not laxity.** The diagnosis targets a burnout culture, not the call to "become perfect." The vault develops the healthy corrective elsewhere: [[Reading Perfection in Context Cures the Inward Checklist]], [[Maladaptive Perfectionism Leaves Mercy Out of Becoming Perfect]], [[Self-Worth Decoupled From Performance Combats Perfectionism]], and the clinical dimension in [[Scrupulosity Is an Anxiety Disorder Expressed Religiously]].
- **Popular-level, not a systematic theology.** This is a podcast pastoral diagnosis (a tier-C source), not a rigorous account of the salvation/exaltation distinction. The formal doctrine (salvation/immortality vs. exaltation/eternal life in the celestial kingdom) is treated in [[Degrees of Glory vs Heaven]] and [[Human Deification in Mormonism]].

## LDS Connection
The reading pairs with [[All We Can Do Is Defined as Repentance in Alma 24]]: if "all we can do" is repentance, then the works-heavy self-image many Latter-day Saints carry is a cultural overlay, not the scripture's demand. Naming the salvation/exaltation conflation lets grace do its work at the foundation ([[LDS Salvation Is by Grace Not Earned by Works]]) without denying the covenant path that builds on it.
