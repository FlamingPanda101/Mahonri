# TRUMP - The Molinist Alternative to TULIP

## The Claim

[[Tim Stratton]], a former five-point Calvinist, replaces the Calvinist **TULIP** with the Molinist acronym **TRUMP** — a soteriology that keeps God's exhaustive foreknowledge (via [[Molinism|middle knowledge]]) while restoring genuine libertarian freedom. Where TULIP makes salvation a unilateral, determined act, TRUMP makes it a freely-entered "true love relationship."[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. First, the two Calvinisms being replaced (Argument weight: Primary — Definitional)

Stratton distinguishes **TULIP (five-point) Calvinism** from **ED Calvinism** — "exhaustive divine determination" — the view "most academic and scholarly Calvinists hold today."[^2] TULIP = **T**otal depravity, **U**nconditional election ("God just decides who's saved... before we're even born"), **L**imited atonement, **I**rresistible grace, **P**erseverance of the saints.[^3] The pastoral sting he presses: asked "what's the good news for the non-elect?", the consistent Calvinist answer is "there is none. You were made so that you could suffer and go to hell."[^4]

### 2. The TRUMP replacement, letter by letter (Argument weight: Primary)

Announcing "instead of tulip, now as a Molinist, I share the acronym Trump,"[^5] Stratton offers:[^6]

- **T — Total depravity**, redefined: "there's not one aspect of humanity that's not been infected with sin," but this "doesn't mean I'm totally dead" (against the Calvinist "spiritually dead" reading).
- **R — Resistible prevenient grace**, "as opposed to the irresistible grace. I say it's quite resistible."
- **U — Unlimited atonement**: "the life, death, and resurrection of Jesus is powerful enough to save all people."
- **M — Middle knowledge**: "God knows who will freely choose to reject his love and grace from eternity past, but nothing forces them" — the piece that "preserves God's foreknowledge and our free will."
- **P — Perseverance of free saints**: the redeemed keep libertarian freedom in heaven yet never sin, being "too smart to sin" — "you could, but you never would."[^7]

### 3. Perseverance without determinism, via soul-making (Argument weight: Secondary)

Stratton grounds heavenly sinlessness not in a divine override but in learned experience: "these light momentary afflictions prepare us for an eternal weight of glory" (2 Corinthians 4:17). Having seen what taking perfection for granted cost Satan and Adam, the redeemed *freely* will never sin again — "that's how God can guarantee, without determining, the perseverance of the saints."[^8] This is Stratton's Molinist version of a [[Soul-Making Theodicy - Hick and Joseph Smith|soul-making]] account.

## Best Quotes

> "Instead of TULIP, now as a Molinist, I share the acronym TRUMP... M is middle knowledge — God knows who will freely choose to reject his love and grace from eternity past, but nothing forces them." — [[Tim Stratton]][^6]

> "You could, but you never would sin in heaven. That's how God can guarantee, without determining, the perseverance of the saints." — [[Tim Stratton]][^8]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### Objection 1: TULIP is the biblical gospel; TRUMP smuggles in autonomy
**Steelman (Calvinist):** The five points are exegetically derived (Romans 9, Ephesians 1, John 6), and "resistible prevenient grace" makes the decisive difference in salvation a human contribution, reintroducing boasting. If grace can be finally resisted, then what saves is my non-resistance — a work.

**Response:** Stratton answers that unlimited atonement plus resistible grace keeps salvation entirely God's *provision* while making damnation entirely the creature's *refusal*; not-resisting a gift is not earning it (see [[Grace Is Unconditional Love Not an Earned Wage]] and [[Coerced Love Is Not Love - Agency Against Irresistible Grace]]).

### Objection 2: Middle knowledge does not actually secure freedom
**Steelman:** If God, by selecting which world to actualize, thereby fixes exactly who is lost, the predestination problem returns at the level of the divine decree — Molinism "does not solve predestination." See [[Molinism Does Not Solve Predestination]].

**Response:** The Molinist replies that God is constrained by which worlds are *feasible* given creatures' free counterfactuals, so he actualizes the best feasible balance rather than authoring each choice — but the LDS critique presses that a God who selects the world still bears responsibility for its casualties, a tension the vault treats under [[Counterfactuals of Creaturely Freedom Constrain Freedom Not Ground It (Goff vs Molinism)]].
