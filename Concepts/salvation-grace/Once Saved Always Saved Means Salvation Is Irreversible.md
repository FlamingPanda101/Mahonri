# Once Saved Always Saved Means Salvation Is Irreversible

## The Claim
**[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** holds that saving belief, once real, **cannot be lost**: "once the Savior knows you, it is irreversible." Pressed with the hardest case — "if you became an atheist right now, would you still be saved?" — he answers "Absolutely."[^1] His argument rests on the permanence of *being known* by Christ: in Matthew 7 the rejected are told "I never knew you," and "you can't unknow somebody." He analogizes salvation to biological sonship: as one always carries a father's DNA, the saved carry a "spiritual DNA," and the relation is irreversible.[^2] Note this is a **non-Calvinist** eternal security — Mark explicitly rejects TULIP and total depravity — so the permanence flows from the nature of belief and God's knowing, not from unconditional election.

## Supporting Evidence

1. **"I never knew you" implies knowing is the hinge — and knowing is permanent.** Mark reads [[Matthew 7.21|Matthew 7:21-23]] as turning on relationship ("I never knew you"), inferring that once Christ *does* know a person, that knowledge cannot be revoked.[^2] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Weak* — the verse establishes that the rejected were never known, not that the known can never fall; the inference is a possible but non-necessary reading.

2. **The sonship / DNA analogy.** A son cannot cease to be his father's offspring; likewise the born-again cannot un-become God's child.[^2] *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Weak* — see the steelman, where the analogy is turned against him.

3. **It follows from his soteriology.** If eternal life is a present *possession* ([[Eternal Life Is a Present Possession Not Only Future Exaltation]]) received by a one-time belief, then its loss would contradict the "hath" — so irreversibility is the consistent corollary of his system, not an add-on. *Argument weight: Primary (for internal coherence); Evidence rating: Moderate.*

## Best Quotes
> "I believe once the Savior knows you, it is irreversible." — "So if you became an atheist right now, would you still be saved?" — "Absolutely."[^1]

> "He said, 'I never knew you,' to the rejected. In Matthew 7, you can't unknow somebody, Hayden."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**[[Hayden Carroll]] rejects unconditional eternal security as unscriptural**, and his replies are pointed:

- **"Not taught in the scriptures whatsoever."** Carroll's flat verdict on the atheist-still-saved claim.[^1] Restoration scripture makes continuance conditional: [[3 Nephi 27.17|3 Nephi 27:17]] warns that "he that endureth not unto the end" is "cast into the fire" — the enduring, not merely the initial believing, is in view.

- **The sonship analogy cuts the other way.** To "you can't stop being your father's son," Carroll answers that one *can* "legally remove myself from him" — disinheritance and legal disaffiliation are real, even though biology is unchanged.[^2] So the analogy, examined, actually models a relationship that *can* be severed by the will of a party, which is the opposite of what Mark needs.

- **"I never knew you" under-determines his thesis.** The verse says the rejected were *never* known; it is silent on whether the once-known can apostatize. Carroll's broader point (see [[Eternal Life Is a Present Possession Not Only Future Exaltation]]) is that biblical assurance need not entail *inability to walk away* — a person retains agency.

- **Fair point for Mark:** his position is a mainstream evangelical one with real texts behind it (John 10:28, "no man shall pluck them out of my hand"), and it is *not* the Calvinist version he is often lumped with — a distinction worth preserving; contrast the specifically Calvinist assurance problem in [[Calvinism Cannot Ground Assurance of Salvation]]. The live question between them is conditional security (LDS: endure to the end) vs. unconditional security (Mark), each with prooftexts — a genuine intra-Christian dispute, not a Mormon idiosyncrasy.
