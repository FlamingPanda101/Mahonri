# Calvinism Cannot Ground Assurance of Salvation

## The Claim

Calvinism destroys the very assurance of salvation it prizes. Its own confessions — the **Westminster Confession** and the **London Baptist Confession** — concede that some who believe themselves saved are not, a phenomenon [[John Calvin]] himself named **evanescent grace**. Since you cannot rule out that you are among the self-deceived, "there is no answer" to how you know you are truly elect.[^1]

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The confessions concede false assurance (Argument weight: Primary)

[[Hayden Carroll]] presses it as "one of the strongest arguments against Calvinism": "the confessions that they adhere to — particularly the Westminster and the London Baptist confession — say that there are some who think they're saved and they're not actually saved."[^2] The guests supply the technical name: **evanescent grace**, and note "Calvin brought it up himself," so it is "part of the system," not a modern discovery.[^3]

### 2. The self-deception trap has no exit (Argument weight: Primary)

"How do you know that you're not self-deceived? And there is no answer to it. They'll say, 'Well, I feel, or I believe' — but then they tell you that you can't do that."[^1] The illustration: the man "who just left your congregation, who said all these things last week" — professed the marks of election — "and now he's not [there]. That's what self-deception is. You can't know."[^4] Any internal test (feelings, perseverance, fruit) is exactly what the apostate also passed.

### 3. A deity of deception cannot certify anything (Argument weight: Secondary)

[[Tim Stratton]] ties the problem to his [[The Deity of Deception Argument Against Exhaustive Divine Determinism|Deity of Deception argument]]: "If a deity of deception assures you of salvation, do you really have assurance of salvation? And you don't."[^5] If God determines some to hold false beliefs — including, possibly, a false belief that they are saved — then the divine "assurance" is worthless as evidence.

### 4. The "backup plan" reductio (Argument weight: Tertiary — Rhetorical)

Carroll's practical needle: if Calvinism is true, "God will eventually regenerate me" if I am elect and will not if I am not, so nothing I do now changes the outcome — "Calvinism is my backup plan. I'm going to continue to be a Latter-day Saint, because if their doctrine is true, God will drag me... You'll never freely choose to be a Protestant."[^6] The point dramatizes that on ED Calvinism, deliberation about one's own salvation is idle.

## Best Quotes

> "How do you know that you're not self-deceived? And there is no answer to it." — [[Hayden Carroll]] / guests[^1]

> "If a deity of deception assures you of salvation, do you really have assurance of salvation? And you don't." — [[Tim Stratton]][^5]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### Objection 1: Assurance rests on Christ's finished work, not introspection
**Steelman (Calvinist):** Assurance is grounded objectively in the promises of God and the finished work of Christ, apprehended by faith, and confirmed by the Spirit's witness (Romans 8:16) and the fruit of a changed life (1 John). Evanescent grace describes the *hypocrite's temporary experience*, not a defeater for the believer's warranted confidence; the possibility of counterfeit money does not make real money unknowable.

**Response:** The proponents reply that the counterfeit analogy fails precisely because, on ED Calvinism, the *same God* both grants and withholds the internal marks and determines the believer's assessment of them — so the believer has no independent standpoint to distinguish true from evanescent grace. This is why the argument is stronger against determinism than against a synergistic view where assurance tracks a relationship the agent can actually verify he is in.

### Objection 2: Non-Calvinist views face the mirror problem
**Steelman:** If salvation can be finally resisted (Stratton's own [[TRUMP - The Molinist Alternative to TULIP|TRUMP]]), then no one can be sure he will not later apostatize — so the Molinist has *less* assurance, not more.

**Response:** Stratton distinguishes present relationship from future guarantee: one can know one is *now* in a true love relationship with God even while remaining free, whereas the Calvinist worry is that one may *never have been* elect at all despite every present sign. The Catholic form of this dispute is treated in [[Assurance of Salvation - Catholic vs Protestant]].
