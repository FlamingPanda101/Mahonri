# The Soul Is Made Sinless While the Body Still Sins

## The Claim
Confronted with [[1 John 3.9|1 John 3:9]] — "whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin... he cannot sin" — **[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** must reconcile "cannot sin" with the fact that believers still sin daily. His solution is a **soul/body dualism of sanctification**: when Christ gives eternal life "he saves your soul, not [the body]." The regenerate *soul* is made "sinless" and "sealed up until the day of redemption," while the *body* remains sinful, sins daily, and dies ("the wages of sin is death" falls on the body).[^1] So the person genuinely "cannot sin" at the level of the soul that is saved, even as the flesh continues to transgress. He supports the split with [[Romans 7.17|Romans 7:17]] ("it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me" — old man vs. new man) and argues this is the *only* way to satisfy the [[Matthew 5.48|Matthew 5:48]] command to "be perfect": the soul is made perfect and sealed now.[^2]

## Supporting Evidence

1. **It preserves the plain force of "cannot sin."** Rather than soften 1 John 3:9 to "does not sin habitually," Mark keeps "cannot" absolute and relocates it to the soul.[^1] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Weak* — it honors one verse's wording at the cost of importing a body/soul distinction the verse never states (see steelman).

2. **Romans 7 supplies a two-selves vocabulary.** Paul's "inward man" vs. "outward man," "old man" vs. "new man," and "no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me" are read as literal partition of a sinning body from a sinless regenerate self.[^2] *Argument weight: Primary; Evidence rating: Weak-to-Moderate* — the language is real, but Romans 7's "I" is one of the most contested referents in the NT (regenerate? pre-regenerate? rhetorical Everyman?).

3. **It grounds instant perfection and sealing.** The sinless soul, "sealed up until the day of redemption," lets Mark say Christ "has already perfected my soul... it's done" — undergirding both his assurance and his eternal-security claim.[^3] *Argument weight: Secondary; Evidence rating: Weak* (depends on the prior two).

## Best Quotes
> "Your soul can be made sinless and sealed up until the day of redemption, when Jesus Christ... brings you as a sinless, perfect person. And that is the only way to meet the Matthew 5:48 requirement."[^2]

> "It is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth within me... The old man is sinful... but inside, that is the part that is made sinless."[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning
**[[Hayden Carroll]] presses a single, repeated hermeneutical objection: the text does not say what Mark needs it to say.**

- **"You're adding to the text."** Carroll's core charge: 1 John 3:9 makes no soul-versus-body distinction — "it doesn't say the soul." When Mark says the soul is what is saved and the body what sins, Carroll asks directly, "where does it say that you physically sin but your soul does not?" and gets no verse that states it.[^1][^4] The reading is a theological *inference* imported to rescue "cannot sin," not the verse's own claim.

- **Regeneration makes one new self, not two.** To the old-man/new-man material Carroll replies: "I become a new man. I don't have an old man *and* a new man — that's a contradiction." When Mark says "you have a soul and you have a body... I'm talking to the real Hayden," Carroll answers that a decision to sin is still *his* decision: "that's still me."[^5] Splitting the agent so the "real" self never sins threatens moral responsibility for the sins the person actually commits.

- **The method problem generalizes.** Carroll notes Mark reads 1 John 3:9 hyper-literally while adding an unstated body/soul gloss, yet accuses Latter-day Saints of "adding context" when they read Moroni 10:32 or D&C 45 — an inconsistency in interpretive rule. See [[The Ex-Mormon Grounds His Bible on the Same Heart-Choice He Rejects in Moroni 10]].

- **Fair point for Mark:** his instinct answers a real exegetical pressure — 1 John 3:9's stark "cannot sin" *does* strain against 1 John 1:8 ("if we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves"), and every tradition needs some device (habitual-sin reading, positional vs. progressive sanctification, ideal-vs-actual) to hold them together. Mark's soul/body sealing is one such device; it is not obviously worse than the "continuous tense" reading, only differently located. The dispute is which harmonization the text licenses, and Carroll's "you added it" cuts against any harmonization that inserts unstated terms — including, a critic might note, some of his own.
