# Works Constitute Saving Faith Rather Than Merely Displaying It

## The Claim
The most common Protestant harmonization of James — that works are merely the *downstream evidence* of an already-saving faith, a display for others, not a cause of justification — is, on [[Dan McClellan]]'s reading, presuppositional eisegesis: it "renegotiates what's going on in James so that you can make James align with Paul."[^1] For James, McClellan argues, the relation runs the other way: "the faith was contingent on the works... the works are what put in the reps for the faith,"[^2] faith is "brought to completion" by works ([[James 2.20-23|James 2:22]]), and faith "is not an ends in and of itself" but "a component that contributes to creating a better world."[^3] Works are thus *constitutive* of saving faith, not a scoreboard for it.

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The harmonization is named and diagnosed as circular (rated: strong, methodological)
McClellan states the target reading fairly — works are "the downstream outcome of a saving faith," so "you got to have the faith and then the works are just this magical thing that happens" — and identifies its proof-text as [[James 2.14-26|James 2:18]] ("show me your faith apart from your works and I by my works will show you faith").[^4] He then diagnoses the move: "this is pretty presuppositional because... you're renegotiating what's going on in James so that you can make James align with Paul... you approach the text, you figure out which text agree with the presuppositions you've brought to it, then you reinterpret all the other texts to bring them into alignment."[^1][^5] The reading is driven by a prior commitment to *sola fide*, not by James.

### 2. James makes faith contingent on works, not vice versa (rated: strong, primary text)
Commenting on "by works was faith made perfect" ([[James 2.20-23|2:22]]): "James pretty clearly saying that the faith was contingent on the works... Not the other way around. The works are what put in the reps for the faith, so the faith could get stronger."[^2] This inverts the Protestant *ordo* (faith → works) into James's own sequence (works complete faith), which the "mere demonstration" reading cannot accommodate.

### 3. Reducing works to a display turns faith into a social signal (rated: moderate, sociological)
McClellan observes that the harmonizing reading "turns faith into something that you are required to display to others so that they can accept you. It really does make it more of a social club";[^6] faith becomes "an identity marker and... a bit of costly signaling, as a way to put on display to others that your faith is good enough."[^7] The point of James, by contrast, is external and concrete: "faith minus works equals dead... faith plus works equals salvation,"[^8] because "the point is to make a better world."[^3]

## Apologetic Engagement
**Steelman (the harmonizing side).** The "works demonstrate faith" reading is not baseless: James 2:18 really does frame works as what *shows* faith ("show me your faith... I by my works will show you faith"), and Reformed theology has always insisted that *fides* is *fiducia* — a living trust that necessarily bears works — so "faith alone, but the faith that justifies is never alone" ([[Faith Alone but Never Alone]]). On that view James and Paul agree that a workless faith is dead, and "contingent on works" overstates a text that still calls Abraham's *belief* what was "counted for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6). The demonstration reading is a coherent theological position, not mere evasion.

**Where it helps.** McClellan's critique lands the methodological point the vault's apologists make against the Protestant escape routes (see [[James - Different Kind of Faith Reply Rebutted]], [[James - Justification Before Men Reply Rebutted]]): the harmonization is reverse-engineered from *sola fide* rather than read out of James, and James 2:18's "show me" is being asked to carry a whole soteriology it cannot bear. His "faith is contingent on works" is a sharper form of the vault's synergism than the softer "works are the fruit of faith."

**Where it diverges.** McClellan's positive frame is horizontal and this-worldly — faith matters because "the point is to make a better world," a component of social flourishing, not primarily a vertical, saving relationship with God. The Latter-day Saint reads James's works as covenantal obedience that *sanctifies* and unites the disciple to Christ, not merely as social utility; grace remains the sole saving cause and works its appointed expression (see [[Works Are the Fruit of Faith Not the Price of Salvation]]). So the LDS view should take McClellan's exegetical inversion (works complete faith) while resisting a purely humanistic reduction of what that faith is *for*.

## LDS Relevance
This note refines the vault's faith-and-works cluster with a critical scholar's demonstration that the Protestant harmonization is presuppositional rather than exegetical — useful precisely because it comes from outside the apologetic camp. It also gently corrects an over-soft LDS slogan: "works are merely the fruit of faith" can drift toward the very demonstration reading McClellan critiques. James's own language ("faith made perfect by works," faith "contingent on" works) supports the stronger LDS claim that faith and works are mutually constitutive — faith is *allegiance* enacted, not assent inspected (see [[Faith Is Allegiance to Christ Not Mere Mental Assent]]).
