# Kimball vs McConkie - Does LDS Grace Teaching Contradict Itself

## The Claim (Objection to LDS Soteriology)
[[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]] argues that Latter-day Saint teaching on grace is internally at war with itself. He sets President [[Spencer W. Kimball]] — who called "saved alone by the grace of God... belief in Jesus Christ alone" a doctrine "originated by Satan" — against apostle [[Bruce R. McConkie]], who preached "we are saved by grace without works. It is a gift from God." [[Jacob Hansen]] answers that the two are not contradictory: grace is the sole *saving power* (McConkie), while Kimball condemns *cheap grace* — a mere profession of faith without covenant faithfulness.

## Supporting Evidence

### 1. The Kimball quote: grace-alone as a "doctrine of Satan" (Argument weight: Primary — objection)
Ryan reads President Kimball: "One of the most fallacious doctrines originated by Satan and propounded by man is that man is saved alone by the grace of God — that belief in Jesus Christ alone is all that is needed for salvation." He presses Hansen: "Do you agree with that quote?"[^1]

### 2. The McConkie quote: "saved by grace without works" (Argument weight: Primary)
Hansen produces a counter from apostle Bruce R. McConkie, in general conference at the same era: "Does salvation come by grace without works? It surely does, without any question, in all its parts, kinds, and degrees. We are saved by grace without works. It is a gift from God. How else could it come?"[^2] The two apostolic statements appear, on their face, to affirm opposite things about grace alone.

### 3. Hansen's harmonization: cheap grace vs. saving power (Argument weight: Primary — LDS response)
Hansen accepts Kimball "in the sense that he's talking about it — cheap grace," adding "we do reject cheap grace."[^3] He locates Kimball's target in *profession* rather than *possession* of faith: the error is thinking grace is "contingent merely on a mere confession of faith." Ryan rejects the framing — "It doesn't say cheap grace. You make up something" — insisting Kimball plainly condemns "belief... alone," which Ryan reads as directly opposite to [[Ephesians 2.8-9|Ephesians 2:8-9]].[^4]

### 4. Hansen: LDS scripture centers grace (Argument weight: Secondary — LDS response)
Hansen quotes Book of Mormon grace texts: 2 Nephi 2 — "Salvation is free... no flesh can dwell in the presence of God save it be through the merits and mercy and grace of the Holy Messiah"[^5] — and 1 Nephi 12, "because of their faith in the Lamb of God, their garments are made white in his blood." See [[LDS Salvation Is by Grace Not Earned by Works]] and the classic verse [[2 Nephi 25.23|2 Nephi 25:23]].

### 5. Ryan: the Doctrine and Covenants conditions salvation on obedience (Argument weight: Primary — objection re-pressed)
Ryan answers with LDS scripture that appears to condition cleansing and exaltation on works: D&C 76:52 — "by keeping the commandments they might be washed and cleansed from all their sins"[^6] — and D&C 25:15 — "Keep my commandments continually, and a crown of righteousness you shall receive, and except you do this, where I am, you cannot come." From these he concludes the operative LDS doctrine is a "performance view of salvation," whatever the grace quotations say.[^7]

## Best Quotes
> "One of the most fallacious doctrines originated by Satan... is that man is saved alone by the grace of God." — Spencer W. Kimball, read by Ryan[^1]

> "We are saved by grace without works. It is a gift from God. How else could it come?" — Bruce R. McConkie, read by Jacob Hansen[^2]

## Counter-Arguments / Steelmanning

### The Protestant steelman (Ryan): the statements plainly conflict, and the D&C settles it
Kimball's words are not about "cheap grace" — that phrase is imported; he explicitly condemns "belief in Jesus Christ alone," the very thing Paul affirms. And whatever McConkie says about grace, the binding scripture (D&C 76:52; 25:15) conditions being washed and entering God's presence on *keeping the commandments continually*. So the McConkie quote functions rhetorically while the D&C encodes a works-conditioned salvation — an internal contradiction the LDS harmonization only papers over.

### The Latter-day Saint steelman (Hansen): two registers, not two doctrines
Grace is the sole *cause* of salvation (McConkie), and precisely because it is real grace it produces covenant faithfulness; Kimball attacks the counterfeit — a verbal profession with no living faith or transformation (the same living-vs-dead-faith distinction of [[Works Are Necessary but Not Sufficient vs Faith Is Necessary and Sufficient]]). The D&C "keep my commandments" texts describe faithfulness *within* the covenant and the path to full sanctification, not a wage that earns entry. LDS scripture's own grace-primacy (2 Nephi; 1 Nephi 12) shows the tradition is not works-righteousness. Compare the symmetry argument in [[The Prooftext Double Standard on Grace and Works]] and [[Grace Is Unconditional Love Not an Earned Wage]].

**Where it turns:** Ryan's strongest point is lexical — Kimball's sentence targets "belief... alone," not "cheap grace," so the harmonization changes Kimball's words; and the D&C conditions entry on continual obedience. Hansen's strongest point is that a single word ("alone") can carry two meanings — grace as *sufficient saving power* (which LDS affirm) versus grace as *license without discipleship* (which Kimball denies) — so two apostles can both be right about different targets. The exchange is a clean test case of whether "saved by grace" and "keep my commandments... or you cannot come" describe one coherent soteriology or two.
