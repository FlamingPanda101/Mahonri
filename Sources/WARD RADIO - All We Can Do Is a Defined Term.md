# Ward Radio — We've Been Wrong about "After All We Can Do" This WHOLE TIME?!?! (Was Bruce R. McConkie Wrong?)

A Ward Radio episode in which host **[[Cardon Ellis]]** and **[[Brad Whitbeck]]** host **[[Jeff Harmon]]**, co-founder of Angel Studios, to unpack Harmon's Substack article proposing a fresh reading of the most-contested Latter-day Saint grace verse, [[2 Nephi 25.23|2 Nephi 25:23]] ("by grace that we are saved, after all we can do").

## Summary
- **"All we can do" is a *defined term*.** Borrowing from contract law (a capitalized term is fixed by its own in-document definition), Harmon argues the Book of Mormon defines "all we can do" at [[Alma 24.11|Alma 24:11]] — where the Anti-Nephi-Lehies use the identical phrase to mean *repentance* — so 2 Nephi 25:23 reads "saved by grace after we repent." See [[All We Can Do Is Defined as Repentance in Alma 24]].[^1]
- **A new key alongside the old ones.** The panel contrasts this intratextual word-study with the traditional defenses: the 1830s-English idiom "in spite of / despite all we can do," [[Steven Robinson|Stephen Robinson]]'s bicycle parable, and [[Brad Wilcox]]'s "Jesus doesn't make up the difference, but he makes all the difference." See [[After All We Can Do Means In Spite of All We Can Do]].[^2]
- **Pastoral half: don't conflate salvation with exaltation.** Latter-day Saints often "jump over" faith and repentance to the covenant path and perfectionism, burning out ("snap and turn to Jesus, or snap and leave"); repentance is *metanoia* (turning), sin is *hamartia* (missing the mark), and [[Terryl Givens]]'s *All Things New* reframes "damned/saved" as "wounded/healed." See [[Conflating Salvation with Exaltation Breeds Works Burnout]].[^3]
- **Framing / rhetoric.** The provocative title ("Was Bruce R. McConkie wrong?") lands as a closing quip — "an official petition to update the footnotes... [[Bruce R. McConkie]] done messed up, according to Angel Studios" — not a documented critique of McConkie's writings. Sponsor reads for ScriptureNotes; a closing tangent on the Word of Wisdom as covenant vs. commandment (the Samson/Nazarite analogy).[^4]
