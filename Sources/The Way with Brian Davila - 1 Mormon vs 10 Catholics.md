# 1 Mormon vs. 10 Catholics: Jesus, Mary & the Pope

On his channel *The Way*, [[Brian Davila]] hosts a ~131-minute debate pitting Latter-day Saint apologist [[Jacob Hansen]] (Thoughtful Faith) against ten Catholic interlocutors. Hansen advances six sequential claims, arguing throughout "as a historian" who brackets his own LDS commitments; the Catholics respond as a rotating panel. It is a genuine two-sided exchange on Marian dogma, the papacy, the problem of evil, the Book of Mormon, Joseph Smith's theology, and the incarnation.[^1]

## Summary

- **Mary (Claims 1).** Hansen argues the Catholic dogmas of Mary's **sinlessness / Immaculate Conception** (and the Assumption) are *not apostolic* — the NT excepts only Jesus from sin, Origen/Basil/Augustine attest Mary sinned, and the dogma was only defined in 1854. The Catholics answer with the *positive* case — "full of grace" ([[Luke 1.28|Luke 1:28]]), the New Eve typology, [[Genesis 3.15|Genesis 3:15]], and [[Revelation 12|Revelation 12]] — and read "all have sinned" ([[Romans 3.23|Romans 3:23]]) as general, not absolute. See [[Marian Sinlessness Is a Later Development Not Apostolic]] and [[Mary as the New Eve and Full of Grace - The Catholic Marian Case]].[^2]
- **The papacy (Claim 2).** Hansen argues there was **no pope** (no immediate universal jurisdiction + infallible office) in the first three centuries — Cyprian's "no bishop of bishops," Victor I's unenforceable excommunication, the Chieti Document — while the Catholics counter with Sardica, Stephen I's prevailing ruling, Clement, Ignatius, and Irenaeus. See [[No Bishop Held Universal Jurisdiction in the First Three Centuries]] and the Irenaeus translation sub-debate [[Irenaeus's Preeminent Authority Means Prominence Not Jurisdiction]] (cf. the Protestant treatments [[The Rock of Matthew 16 Is Peters Confession Not a Papal Office]], [[The Apostles Held Equal Power With Peter]]).[^3]
- **"The Catholic God is evil" (Claim 3).** Hansen runs [[Hayden Carroll]]'s **creation dilemma**: God foreknows the damned yet creates them. The Catholics give the deepest reply in the vault's corpus — being is convertible with good, God is pure act, hell is freely chosen — captured in the new [[Existence Even in Hell Is Better Than Nonexistence]]. Background: [[The Creation Dilemma]], [[The Judas Argument - Better Never to Have Been Born]], [[The Mary Problem for Catholic Theism]].[^4]
- **Book of Mormon & Joseph Smith (Claims 4–5).** Hansen argues the Book of Mormon **brings people closer to Christ** (the "fruits" data; unlike the Quran) and that Joseph Smith's teachings about God (embodiment, the King Follett "God was once a man," spirit birth) **do not contradict** the Bible, using [[Philippians 2.5-8|Philippians 2:5-8]] kenosis as precedent. These restate claims the vault already covers: [[The Book of Mormon Passes the Matthew 7 By Their Fruits Test]], [[Different Jesus Both Uncharitable and Precisely True]], [[He Came Down From Heaven Reframed as the Condescension and Exaltation Pattern]], [[King Follett Discourse]], [[God Was Once a Man]], [[Spirit Birth and Intelligences]]. The Great-Apostasy tangent restates [[The Institutional Church and the Body of Believers Are Two Senses of One Word]].[^5]
- **The incarnation (Claim 6).** Hansen argues **Catholics cannot claim God became man** — one person cannot bear two mutually exclusive natures. The Catholics deploy the full Chalcedonian machinery (person vs. nature, communication of idioms, suffering "proper to the humanity," death as separation of soul and flesh), captured in the new [[Christ Suffered in His Humanity Not His Divinity - The Chalcedonian Reply]] (cf. [[The Hypostatic Union Is a Greek Solution to a Greek Problem]], [[Ephesus and Chalcedon Defined the Two Natures of Christ]]).[^6]
