# Let's Talk w/ Hayden Carroll — Does the Bible Teach Sola Scriptura? | Full Live Debate

A ~130-minute formal moderated debate hosted at Agape Fellowship. Resolution: **"The doctrine of *sola scriptura* is taught in the modern Protestant canon of scripture,"** where *sola scriptura* is defined (agreed by both sides, read by the moderator) as "**the traditional Protestant 66 books alone are the only infallible authority and final standard for the church's faith and practice, while all other authorities are subordinate and fallible.**"[^1] **Quinn Fryberg** ([[Quinn Fryberg]], evangelical) argues the **affirmative**; **Hayden Carroll** ([[Hayden Carroll]], Latter-day Saint, host) argues the **negative**. Mason moderates. Format: 15-min openings, 10-min rebuttals, two 15-/10-min cross-examinations, 5-min closings. This is a distinct debate from Carroll's earlier [[Lets Talk w Hayden Carroll - Is the Bible the Final Authority Debate|"Is the Bible the Final Authority?"]] (opponent Kelly).

## Summary
- **Fryberg's affirmative** is a four-proposition cumulative case: (1) scripture is God-breathed revelation and so carries divine authority ([[2 Timothy 3.16-17|2 Tim 3:16-17]], [[2 Peter 1.20-21|2 Pet 1:20-21]], Jesus' "have you not read"); (2) scripture is *sufficient* to equip the church ([[Isaiah 55.10-11|Isa 55:10-11]]); (3) scripture *tests* competing authorities (Corban, [[Matthew 15.6|Matt 15:3-7]]; the Bereans, [[Acts 17.10-11|Acts 17:11]]); (4) the canon establishes no continuing co-equal infallible authority ([[Jude 1.3|Jude 3]] "once for all delivered"; [[Ephesians 2.19-22|Eph 2:19-20]]; the New Jerusalem's 12 gates + 12 foundations of Revelation 21 with "no future revelator").[^2] See [[Sola Scriptura - Scripture Self-Attests Its Authority]] and [[Scripture as the Test of Prophets and Revelation]].
- Fryberg's epistemology for the canon: **God** (not any church or council) determines what is scripture; the church only *recognizes* it, and the **Holy Spirit infallibly leads** that recognition — so the "human side" of canonization is fallible but the "divine side" is not.[^3] His internal *criterion* for excluding the Apocrypha is the **silent years**: God ceased revealing scripture for ~400 years between the Old Testament and the New, so intertestamental books written in that gap are disqualified. See [[The Silent Years Argument Excludes the Apocrypha From the Canon]].
- **Carroll's negative** presses one pivot: the resolution names *these 66 books*, so their **identity and exclusivity are part of the doctrine** — yet the canon neither lists itself nor supplies "a sufficient internal criterion by which the exact Protestant canon can be deduced." A verse can call *scripture* inspired without identifying *which books* are scripture; the **category** of scripture and this **particular collection** are not the same thing.[^4] See [[The Canon Question Is Logically Prior to Canon Authority]] and [[The Canon Premise Is an Infallible Rule Outside Scripture]].
- Carroll's supporting moves: the **Lincoln/Biden analogy** (a first-century author writing "scripture" can no more mean the modern 66-book canon than Lincoln naming "the worst president" could mean Joe Biden);[^5] the **θεόπνευστος gloss** ("God-breathed" more plausibly "life-giving," since in the earliest Greek the word is applied to springs, water, living creatures, burial ointments, perfumes, and clothing — not uniquely to scripture);[^6] the **circularity** charge (if the Roman Church cannot teach infallibly, then its teaching that scripture is infallible is itself fallible);[^7] the **unfalsifiability / Book-of-Mormon parity** charge ("God led the church to recognize the canon" is an unfalsifiable assertion an LDS could equally make for the Book of Mormon);[^8] and the **no-infallible-Bible-in-hand** problem (Fryberg concedes his English Bible is fallible; zero first-century manuscripts survive; the oldest fragment, P52, is "like a credit card").[^9] See [[An Infallible Autograph We No Longer Possess Cannot Be the Rule of Faith]] and [[Moroni-Calvin Parallel - The Self-Authentication Gambit]].
- Both sides marshal the **church fathers**: Fryberg reads Irenaeus and Cyril of Jerusalem as exalting scripture as the final standard; Carroll answers with Augustine ("I should not believe the gospel except as moved by the authority of the Catholic Church"), Basil (unwritten apostolic tradition and scripture "have the same force"), and Vincent of Lérins (interpret the canon "according to the traditions of the universal church") — arguing the patristic witness is *diverse*, not sola-scriptura.[^10] See [[The Early Church Did Not Operate Under Sola Scriptura]].
- Fryberg's headline rejoinder to the unfalsifiability charge: **unfalsifiable ≠ false** — the one-way speed of light can never be measured directly and is accepted as a "convention," yet is treated as true; so a non-falsifiable claim (God guides the church to the canon) can still be true and internally consistent.[^11]
