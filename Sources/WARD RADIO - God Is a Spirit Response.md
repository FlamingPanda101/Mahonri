# Ward Radio — "God Is a Spirit"? Here's the Best Response to Anti-Mormons

A Ward Radio panel answers the standard evangelical / anti-Mormon soundbite that [[John 4.24|John 4:24]] ("God is a Spirit") refutes the Latter-day Saint teaching that God the Father has a body of flesh and bone. They lay out three complementary responses — context, Greek, and a consistency (tu-quoque) argument — and candidly weigh how well each works as a *soundbite*.

## Summary
- **The objection, fairly stated.** The critics' soundbite: "the Bible says God is a spirit; you say he has a body of flesh and bone; your view is incompatible with Scripture" — "in two sentences I just took down Mormonism for some 15-year-old." The panel grants this is a genuinely potent one-liner.[^1]
- **Answer 1 — context.** John 4 is Jesus and the Samaritan woman debating *where/how* to worship; verse 23 ("worship the Father in spirit and in truth") shows the point is inward, new-covenant worship over outward law-of-Moses ritual, not a metaphysical denial of God's body. Critics "hyper-focus on one phrase and ignore the rest."[^2]
- **Answer 2 — the Greek is qualitative.** *Pneuma* in John 4:24 can be qualitative (like "God is love," "God is light"); a non-LDS lexicon (Blue Letter Bible / Strong's) glosses it broadly ("breath, breeze… the rational soul… divine"). So "God is spirit = formless, bodiless" is not a fair rendering of the original. Greek also has no indefinite article, so the "a" in "a spirit" is a translation artifact.[^3]
- **Answer 3 — the consistency test (their favourite).** Scripture calls the risen, embodied Christ a "spirit" — "the last Adam was made a quickening spirit" ([[1 Corinthians 15.45-46|1 Corinthians 15:45]]). Trinitarians insist Christ is embodied, yet the Bible calls him a spirit; therefore spirit and body are "not mutually exclusive," and "God is spirit" cannot by itself mean "God has no body."[^4]
- **Candour.** The hosts concede the defense needs more words than the attack — "any jackass can kick down a door, but it takes a builder to build a barn" — and flag soundbite-refinement as an open project.[^5]
