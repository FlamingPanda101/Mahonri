# Ward Radio — The Gospel of Thomas and the P.Oxy 5575 Fragment

A [[Ward Radio]] segment in which host [[Cardon Ellis]] and guest [[Jonah Barnes]] (with [[Austin Falter]], Marcus, and Brock) present the newly published papyrus **P.Oxy 5575** — a Greek fragment from Oxyrhynchus that interweaves the Gospel of Thomas with sayings paralleled in Matthew and Luke — and argue that its early (2nd-century) paleographic dating "blows the doors off" biblical manuscript dating, undermines sola scriptura, biblical infallibility, and univocality, and shows the Restoration's point that "we need a prophet, not a book." Barnes then walks six sayings of Thomas he reads as echoing restored doctrine (self-knowledge, divine sonship, deification). The tone is comedic and the panel repeatedly disclaims rigor.

## Summary

- **A new Greek Thomas fragment (P.Oxy 5575) is paleographically dated to the 2nd century.** Barnes reports that the fragment — Koine Greek, from Oxyrhynchus rather than the later Coptic Nag Hammadi codex — was dated by comparanda (handwriting samples dated 157, 161, 178, 185) to the mid-to-late 100s AD, "as early as any New Testament manuscript in existence," placing it in a "club of three texts" that would predate Luke and the letters of Paul, Peter, and John. See [[P.Oxy 5575 Dates a Thomas-Synoptic Text to the Second Century]]. [^1]
- **Thomas is a "sayings gospel" whose core may predate its Gnostic framing.** Unlike the four narrative gospels, Thomas is 114 bare sayings ("Jesus said…"); only the first and last lines are narrative "sandwich bread," and scholars note the middle reads far older than the 3rd-century framing — which, Barnes says, biblical scholars resist because dating the core early "shakes the infallibility of two institutions," the Bible and the academy. See [[The Gospel of Thomas Has an Early Sayings Core]]. [^2]
- **The fragment weaves Thomas together with Matthew and Luke — upstream or downstream, either way it dents the canon narrative.** The reconstructed text runs Thomas → Luke 12 / Matthew 6 → Thomas, like a mini-Diatessaron. The "upstream" theory makes a lost source birth Matthew, Luke, and Thomas alike (Thomas older than all); the "downstream" theory makes it a mid-2nd-century harmony (Thomas of equal standing to the Synoptics). Barnes argues both readings undercut a univocal, infallible, sufficient Bible and support continuing revelation. See [[P.Oxy 5575 Interweaves Thomas With Matthew and Luke]] and, on the payoff, [[God Himself Not a Book Is the Ultimate Authority]] and [[A Living Canon Is a Feature Not a Bug]]. [^3]
- **The one embarrassing saying (logion 114, "I will make her a man") is a late scribal insertion.** Barnes grants the notorious closing line is "off the wall" but argues it is a single, late attestation added a century or two after the core — "just like there was an apostasy that affected the plain and precious truths of the books in our canon, there was an apostasy that affected the non-canonized ones, too" — and that critics who bin the whole gospel over it (naming [[Wes Huff]]) apply a double standard, keeping corruptions in Mark and John. See [[The Make Her a Man Saying Is a Late Insertion Into Thomas]]. [^4]
- **Six Thomas sayings echo restored doctrine — self-knowledge, divine sonship, and deification.** Barnes reads logion 3 ("the kingdom is inside of you… when you come to know yourselves… you are the sons of the living father") as pre-mortal existence, self-knowledge as salvation, and "living" as *resurrected*; and logion 30 ("where there are gods, they are gods; where there are two or one, I am with them") as Jesus calling believers *gods*. See [[Gospel of Thomas Teaches Self-Knowledge as Divine Sonship]] and [[Gospel of Thomas Calls Believers Gods]]. [^5]

## Concepts Discussed

Also touched but not given their own atomic notes: logion 32 (a fortified city on a hill "cannot fall, nor can it be hidden" — Barnes reads Matthew 5:14 through it, that to be a light is to be attacked); logion 63, the rich fool who dies the night he fills his storehouse (paralleling [[Luke 12.16-21|Luke 12:16-21]], played as an "anti-boomer" joke); and logion 6, "do not tell lies and do not do what you hate," read as *fast from sin, not food* against Deuteronomic dietary law. The opening formula ("the secret words which the living Jesus spoke, written by Didymus Judas Thomas") is likened to [[Moroni 2]], and a folk etymology links the Coptic word for "hidden" to the Jaredite prophet Ether hidden in a cave.
