# Dan McClellan — What If the Bible Isn't the Word of God?

A short-form YouTube commentary in which [[Dan McClellan]] responds line-by-line to a Christian pastor's video defending biblical inerrancy and the Bible as the final authority. McClellan's thesis is twofold: (1) the phrase "word of God" in the Bible never denotes the *text itself* — only the (usually oral) message or Christ — so calling the Bible "the word of God" is a doctrine imposed from outside the text; and (2) because a text cannot interpret itself, an interpreting community or **tradition is always the de facto final authority**, never the Bible. He argues the pastor's own closing advice (that new believers must let other Christians tell them what scripture means) proves the point.

## Summary
- **"Word of God" never means the text.** The phrase "is always used either to refer to Jesus or to the message that God shares independent of the medium," most often *orally* communicated; you can find it applied to a message transmitted textually "but never specifically to the textual transmission itself."[^1]
- **The Bible is never the final authority.** "It's always a community or a tradition that is the final authority... the Bible does not speak. It is a text. It must be interpreted in order to have any meaning."[^2] Someone outside the text must assert "that's still God's word."[^3]
- **2 Timothy 3:16 reread.** The verse "does not identify itself as scripture"; *theopneustos* "meant life-giving, not inspired as we know it today. It didn't come to mean that until [[Origen]] in the 3rd century CE."[^4]
- **Authoritative ≠ "word of God."** Jesus treated scripture as true and authoritative, "which is distinct from treating it as God's word, which Jesus never identifies the scriptures as"; "Your word is truth" (John 17:17) and "scripture cannot be broken" (John 10:35) do not identify the scriptures as God's word.[^5]
- **Everyone already reads from above the text.** Believers have already discarded the Bible's endorsements of slavery, genocide, and misogyny — "that renegotiated package... is the word of God as we dictate it, not as we find it in the text"[^6] — and reject its plain statements (fetus as property, Exodus 22 firstborn sacrifice, Matthew 5's law-in-force, internal contradictions) because "the tradition is the real authority."[^7]
