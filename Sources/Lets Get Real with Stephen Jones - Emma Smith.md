# Lets Get Real with Stephen Jones - Emma Smith

**Show:** Let's Get Real (Episode E0059) | **Host:** [[Stephen Jones]] | **Guest:** [[Don Bradley]] (historian, *The Lost 116 Pages*) | **Title:** "Emma Smith: Untold Stories That Will Change How You See Joseph Smith | Don Bradley E0059"

A long-form interview treating [[Emma Smith]] "not as a premise for an argument, but as a person." Bradley's throughline: because Emma "knew Joseph Smith better than anyone else," understanding her illuminates Joseph — and taking her seriously dismantles the critical habit of enlisting her against him. The episode ranges from Emma's education and marriage, through her role in the Book of Mormon translation, to polygamy, her denial of it, why she never went west, and her legacy in the Relief Society and temple.

- Bradley reconstructs that Emma was Joseph's **first scribe**, at the very beginning of the lost book of Lehi — inferred from her memories that Joseph could not spell "Sariah," did not know Jerusalem had walls, and misspelled "February" in a letter she then took over. He frames the translation as a husband-and-wife labor.[^1] See [[Emma Smith Was Joseph's First Book of Mormon Scribe]].
- He holds that the **angel required Joseph to marry Emma** to obtain the plates ("Emma's involvement was not optional"), making her divinely necessary to bringing forth the Book of Mormon.[^2] See [[The Angel Required Joseph to Marry Emma to Obtain the Plates]].
- Emma is presented as a **model of faith without visionary sight** — she handled but never saw the plates, was told in [[Doctrine and Covenants 25|D&C 25]] to "murmur not," and represents the ordinary believer ("Emma is us").[^3] See [[Emma Smith Models Faith Without Seeing]].
- On polygamy, Bradley (who researched Joseph Smith and polygamy full-time for two years for [[Brian Hales]]) affirms the historical consensus that Joseph practiced it, contextualizes **why Emma resisted** it, and offers a motive reconstruction for **why she later denied** it — following Joseph's own public denial, believing he had burned/rejected [[Doctrine and Covenants 132|D&C 132]], protecting her sons from the fate polygamy brought Joseph, and guarding the church's respectability.[^4] See [[Why Emma Smith Denied Joseph's Polygamy]].
- He answers the "did Emma apostatize?" question: she **never left the church** — never excommunicated, never rebaptized elsewhere; the church left her location, and grave, debt, and stewardship kept her in Nauvoo, where her family preserved the Smith properties the church now owns.[^5] See [[Emma Smith Never Left the Church]].
- He confronts the rhetorical move of **turning Emma into an argument** rather than a person: the "duped wife" frame offers her only pity, not respect, and contradicts her uncompromising testimony that Joseph was a prophet.[^6] See [[Critics Turn Emma Smith Into an Argument Not a Person]].
- Bradley closes on Emma's **legacy** (devotional, not treated as a separate concept here): she gave the Relief Society its charitable direction and its motto "charity never faileth" ([[Moroni 7]]), chose hymns still sung today, and as the first woman to receive the endowment stands at the head of the chain of temple ordinances for women; her lifelong care for the needy embodies the sheep of [[Matthew 25.31-46|Matthew 25:31-46]].[^7]
