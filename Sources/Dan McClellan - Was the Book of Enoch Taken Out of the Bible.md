# Dan McClellan — Was the Book of Enoch Taken Out of the Bible?

[[Dan McClellan]] reacts to a viral table debate over whether the [[1 Enoch|Book of Enoch]] was "taken out of the Bible." His verdict: the popular claim commits a **category error**. 1 Enoch fell out of favor with all Jewish and most Christian authorities *before* a bound biblical canon existed, so there was no "Bible" from which to remove it — even though the book was widely regarded as inspired scripture in early Judaism and early Christianity. He grants that the pro-Enoch speaker "has a point" about its early authority while correcting both parties' history.

- **You cannot remove a book from a collection that did not yet exist.** Before the 4th century CE, "inspired scripture" was an *unbound* category far broader than the later *bound* Bible; 1 Enoch dropped out of favor while the boundary was still open, so "taken out of the Bible" flattens the distinction between inspired-scripture-as-category and the-Bible-as-canon.[^1][^2]
- **1 Enoch was genuinely treated as inspired scripture** by many early Jews and Christians: [[Jude 1.14-15|Jude]] quotes the Book of the Watchers as "the prophecy of Enoch, the seventh from Adam"; [[Tertullian]] defended it (late 2nd c., *On the Apparel of Women* ch. 3); and it survives in more Dead Sea Scrolls copies than all but ~5–6 books of the Hebrew Bible.[^3][^4][^5]
- **The only Bible that ever included 1 Enoch still includes it** — the [[Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church|Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo]] canon, whose Aksumite isolation preserved the whole Septuagintal corpus (1 Enoch, [[Jubilees]], and more).[^6]
- **The dating objection collapses the timeline.** 1 Enoch was composed c. 300 BCE–1st c. CE — *before* the Jewish canon closed — not "after the Bible was set," so it was neither "taken out" nor (as the mirror confusion runs) "added."[^7][^8] The "it warns against adding" verses ([[Deuteronomy 4.2|Deut 4:2]] / [[Revelation 22.18-19|Revelation 22]]) address *their own books*, not a whole Bible that did not yet exist.[^9]
