# FAIR - Internally Consistent References in the Book of Mormon Part 15

The concluding installment of [[Jeff Markham]]'s 15-part FAIR series cataloguing roughly 120 "internally consistent references" in the [[Book of Mormon]] — subtle matches of a word, name, office, geographic detail, timeline, or event that had to line up, sometimes across whole books, to keep the text self-consistent. Part 15 works the short books at the close of the small plates (Jacob, Omni, Words of Mormon) and then closes the series. The same catalogue was presented on the [[The Stick of Joseph - Internal Consistencies of the Book of Mormon|Stick of Joseph podcast]]; the broader argument lives in [[The Book of Mormon's Undesigned Internal Consistencies Argue Against Fabrication]].

## Summary

- **Small-plates offices and prophecies interlock.** Jacob's statement that he and Joseph were consecrated priests "by the hand of Nephi" (Jacob 1:18) matches Nephi's own record of consecrating them (2 Nephi 5:26); Jacob's "righteous branch from the fruit of the loins of Joseph" (Jacob 2:25) picks up Lehi's citation of Joseph of Egypt's prophecy (2 Nephi 3:5) [examples 116–117].
- **A planted prophecy of lost lands is fulfilled centuries later.** Jacob's obscure warning that the people would "possess the land of your inheritance" only if they repent (Jacob 3:4) is redeemed in Omni (Mosiah I flees the land of Nephi) and confirmed in Mosiah 9:1, where the Lamanites hold "our fathers' first inheritance" [example 118].
- **A one-verse aside resolves a Jaredite cliffhanger.** Coriantumr, left "as if he had no life" at the end of Ether (15:32), turns out to have survived and lived nine moons among the people of Zarahemla (Omni 1:21) [example 119].
- **Words of Mormon's wars-then-peace sets up Mosiah's opening line.** Mormon's account of King Benjamin's battles and hard-won peace (Words of Mormon 1:12–18) is presupposed by "no more contention in all the land" that opens Mosiah 1:1 [example 120].
- **The cumulative point.** Markham concedes the final arc diagram of all ~120 references is "large to the point of being unwieldy" — which is the point: whoever authored the book tracked "hundreds of names, events, timelines, visions, sermons, physical items, geography," presenting them consistently, and that should inform any judgment of who wrote it and how.
