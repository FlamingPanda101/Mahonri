# Let's Talk w/ Hayden Carroll — Ex-Mormon Baptist Says My Theology Is WRONG!

A roughly 83-minute cordial-but-pointed dialogue in which **[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)|Mark]]**, a former Latter-day Saint turned Baptist "believer on Jesus Christ," presses **[[Hayden Carroll]]** on a single thesis: that eternal life is a **present possession** received now by belief ([[John 6.47|John 6:47]], "he that believeth on me *hath* everlasting life"), whereas — Mark argues — LDS theology defers it to a post-resurrection exaltation, quoting [[Dallin H. Oaks]]'s 1998 statement that the gift "can only be obtained after the final resurrection."[^1] Carroll answers that "eternal life" bears more than one sense and that the Restoration's own [[D&C 45.3-5|D&C 45:3-5]] promises present, believing possession of it.[^2] The exchange then widens into assurance, the nature of faith, regeneration, biblical authority, the unevangelized, and finally Carroll's own offensive — the "Creation Dilemma" against the creedal God.

## Summary
- **Eternal life: now or later?** The spine of the debate. Mark hammers the present-tense "hath" of John 6:47 and Oaks's "only after the final resurrection"; Carroll replies that eternal life is used in Scripture and by prophets in multiple senses (knowing God, quality of life, and glorified exaltation), and that in the John 6 / [[1 John 5.11-12|1 John 5:11-12]] sense a believer has it now.[^2] Both men, strikingly, end up affirming they *have* eternal life today.
- **What saves — belief or faith?** Mark distinguishes **belief** (an easy, one-time heart-choice) from **faith** (works/evidence, Hebrews 11) and says belief alone saves; Carroll reads *pistis* as allegiance and Ephesians 2 as grace-through-faith *manifested* in works. Mark also charges LDS soteriology is works-based and unable to ground assurance; Carroll marshals grace statements from [[Bruce R. McConkie]], Oaks, and others that salvation "cannot be earned."[^3]
- **Regeneration and the sinless soul.** Mark's reading of [[1 John 3.9|1 John 3:9]] ("cannot sin") splits a sinless regenerate **soul** from a still-sinful **body**; Carroll calls this an extra-biblical addition to the text.[^4] Mark also argues **once saved, always saved** is irreversible — even apostasy could not undo it.
- **Authority and the unevangelized.** Mark judges LDS doctrine by an infallible King James Bible he grounds in admitted "personal conviction"; Carroll presses that this is the same heart-level choice Mark rejects in [[Moroni 10.4-5|Moroni 10:3-5]], and that Mark cannot justify the standard by which he judges.[^5] On the unreached, Mark reads [[Titus 2.11|Titus 2:11]] to mean everyone gets one chance in mortality — a position Carroll finds unusually inclusivist. The video closes with Carroll's **Creation Dilemma** (why would an unbound, foreknowing God who creates *ex nihilo* make souls He knows will be damned?), to which Mark answers with the free-will "robots" reply and finally an appeal to mystery.[^6]
