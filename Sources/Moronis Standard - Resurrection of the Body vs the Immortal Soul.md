# Resurrection of the Body vs the Immortal Soul

[[Alex Arnold]] of *Moroni's Standard* argues that Creedal Christianity has, for nearly 2,000 years, recited "the resurrection of the body" in the [[Apostles Creed|Apostles' Creed]] and [[Nicene Creed]] while its working pastoral theology — the funeral sermon about a loved one "in a better place, watching over" the family — actually teaches **Platonic soul-immortality**, a different and incompatible claim about what death is.[^1] Drawing on [[N.T. Wright]], [[Jon Levenson]], [[John Collins|John J. Collins]], [[George Nickelsburg]], [[Caroline Walker Bynum]], [[Oscar Cullmann]] and [[Philip Johnston]] — none of them LDS — he traces how the gap opened: the Hebrew Bible's earliest layers knew only [[Sheol Was the Undifferentiated Realm of All the Dead|Sheol]], bodily resurrection entered the canon late under the Maccabean crisis, Second Temple Judaism exploded with differentiated afterlife theology, and then a second-century [[The Immortal Soul Is a Platonic Import Not a Biblical Doctrine|Platonic import]] displaced the Hebrew whole-person anthropology from operative Christian practice. The LDS framework, he concludes, recovers that displaced Hebrew anthropology and extends it by revelation.

## Summary

- **The creed-vs-funeral gap is real and named by mainstream scholars.** Wright says most contemporary Christians actually believe "Platonic soul immortality dressed in Christian language," not what the creed says; [[Oscar Cullmann]]'s 1955 Harvard lecture (*Immortality of the Soul or Resurrection of the Dead*) argued the two are "fundamentally different and incompatible doctrines" and that Christianity adopted "Plato rather than Paul" on what death is.[^2][^3] See [[Soul Immortality and Bodily Resurrection Are Incompatible Traditions]].
- **The Hebrew afterlife developed; it was not handed down whole.** The pre-exilic texts know only Sheol, an undifferentiated underworld for everyone ([[Psalm 115.17|Psalm 115:17]]; Job 14; [[Ecclesiastes]] 9; the shade of Samuel in [[1 Samuel 28|1 Samuel 28]]); explicit resurrection language arrives at [[Daniel 12|Daniel 12:2]] (~165 BC) as an answer to the Maccabean theodicy crisis.[^4][^5] See [[Bodily Resurrection Emerged as the Answer to the Maccabean Theodicy Crisis]].
- **The Hebrew person is a psychophysical unity** — *nephesh*, the whole living being ([[Genesis 2.7|Genesis 2:7]]: the human *became* a living soul, did not *receive* one) — so death is a rupture and resurrection is the only completion, not a bonus.[^6] See [[The Hebrew Person Is a Psychophysical Unity Not a Soul in a Body]].
- **The immortal soul is a Platonic import** (the *Phaedo*; the second-century apologists' "acute Hellenization," [[Adolf von Harnack]]), and the medieval church's tangled resurrection puzzles ([[Caroline Walker Bynum]]) are the fingerprints of an anthropology that could not value the body as constitutive.[^7][^8] See [[The Immortal Soul Is a Platonic Import Not a Biblical Doctrine]].
- **LDS doctrine recovers the whole-person framework.** [[D&C 88]]:15–16 ("the spirit and the body are the soul of man"), [[D&C 93]], and [[Alma 40.11|Alma 40–41]] make the resurrection the permanent reuniting of spirit and body; the [[Three Degrees of Glory in the Bible|degrees of glory]] ([[D&C 76]]) are a revealed *expansion* with structural precedent in Second Temple texts Joseph Smith could not have accessed.[^9][^10] See [[LDS Resurrection Doctrine Recovers the Hebrew Whole-Person Framework]].
