# Hello Saints — Could He Have Been Lying? Investigating Joseph Smith's Claims

An irenic interview in which Jeff, an Evangelical pastor in Utah (the *Hello Saints* channel), re-watches the travel video he filmed five years earlier retracing Joseph Smith's steps in Palmyra, and pauses throughout to ask BYU historian [[Casey Griffiths]] the blunt "could he have been lying?" questions — about the gold plates, the witnesses, the treasure-seeking, the Book of Mormon's production, and the doctrinal gulf on the nature of God. Griffiths answers by walking through what the friendly *and* hostile sources actually say, conceding the genuinely weak or uncertain points and laying out a cumulative case that sincerity — not a con — best explains Joseph Smith.

## Summary

- **The con-man reading fails on means, motive, and the witnesses.** Griffiths' cumulative answer: the 531-page Book of Mormon was produced in three months (April–June 1829) with no qualified collaborator (Oliver Cowdery, the only educated helper, was present only those months; a Sidney Rigdon ghostwriter is "mental gymnastics"); the interlocking testimony of family, the Whitmers, Emma's family, and even hostile Palmyra neighbors would require "Academy Awards all around"; and — decisively — nobody got rich (Martin Harris *mortgaged his farm* to print it), the principals were hunted for life, and Joseph died a prisoner still bound to the book ([[D&C 135]]). See [[Joseph Smith Gained No Wealth and Died Affirming the Book of Mormon]], and the production/witness cases in [[Dictation Speed and Method Argue Against Fabrication]], [[No Naturalistic Source Accounts for the Whole Book of Mormon]], and [[Witnesses Never Recanted Despite Estrangement]]. [^1] [^2]
- **The First Vision reads as a sincere spiritual crisis, and the multiple accounts are a strength.** Putting the accounts together, Joseph's two concerns were forgiveness of his own sins and which church to join — "deeply spiritual considerations," not a plot to "con the world." Griffiths treats the four (or five) firsthand and eight-to-nine secondhand accounts as complementary, comparing the variation to the multiple New Testament accounts of Paul on the Damascus road, and corrects the myth that the Smith family disbelieved Joseph — they were "incredibly supportive" (Alvin's deathbed charge; Hyrum dying beside him). See [[First Vision Accounts Are No More Contradictory Than the Gospels]], [[The Four Firsthand First Vision Accounts]], and [[The Smith Family Religious Division Framed the First Vision]]. [^3] [^4] [^5]
- **The stock origin-story objections are defused one by one.** The "second Muhammad" / Galatians 1 "another gospel" charge: angelic ministration and new revelation are biblical (Paul, Samuel, Moses), so "if you've got a problem with angels, you've got a problem with Christianity," and the Book of Mormon is relentlessly Christ-centered — see [[Muhammad Had No Corroborating Witnesses or Physical Artifacts]] and [[Galatians 1-8 Latter-day Saints Preach Pauls Gospel]]. Treasure-seeking and the seer stone: "it seems like it's true," but they were not unique, refused to use the gifts for gain, and hostile and friendly sources "line up" — see [[Joseph Smith Treasure Hunting Extent]]. Plagiarism: the Spalding-manuscript theory "fell apart in 1888" when the manuscript was found to be about Romans, not Israelites — see [[Spalding Theory of Book of Mormon Origin]]. The [[Charles Anthon]] episode and the Hill Cumorah / two-Cumorahs dispute are handled candidly as *disputed among Latter-day Saints* — see [[Charles Anthon Transcript Episode]] and [[The Two-Cumorahs Theory]]. [^6] [^7] [^8] [^9]
- **The one genuine, non-negotiable difference is the nature of God — including a fresh reading of "abomination."** Griffiths grants (with Jeffrey R. Holland) that the two real LDS–Protestant divides are the open canon and the doctrine of the Godhead; he notes the Book of Mormon itself "reads more trinitarian than the Bible," and offers a *process* reading of the 1838 "creeds are an abomination" line — the offense is a committee of well-meaning but power-invested men "closing the door" and voting on God's nature, versus God revealing it by appearing. See [[The Creeds Abomination Targets Teachings Not Christians]] and [[The Godhead Model vs the Trinity Model]]. [^10] [^11]
