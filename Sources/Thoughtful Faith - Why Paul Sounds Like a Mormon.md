# Thoughtful Faith — Why Paul Sounds Like a Mormon

A short, narrated Thoughtful Faith explainer arguing that the entire Protestant *faith-versus-works* reading of [[Paul the Apostle|Paul]] rests on an **anachronism**. Following the New Perspective on Paul ([[E.P. Sanders]], [[James Dunn]], [[N.T. Wright]]), the video contends that the Reformers recruited Paul into their sixteenth-century fight with Catholic merit theology, whereas Paul was answering a first-century covenant question: now that Israel's Messiah has come, died, and risen, **who belongs to God's covenant people — and must Gentiles become Jews to belong?** Read back in his own frame, Paul's "justification," "grace," "faith," and "works of the law" all shift meaning — and the resulting picture (covenant membership by loyalty to the risen King, judged at last by works, inside a cosmic story of new creation) "looks a lot like" Latter-day Saint soteriology. This is the shorter, narrator-only companion to the channel's [[Thoughtful Faith - Why Christians Misread Paul|Blake Ostler interview]]; the two overlap heavily and are cross-linked rather than duplicated.

- **The faith/works debate asks a question Paul was not answering.** "The reformers were using Paul to try and justify their break from the Catholic Church when Paul himself was trying to deal with an entirely different situation" — like "reading Abraham Lincoln's speeches through the lens of the Cold War." Paul "wasn't debating medieval merit theology. He wasn't debating whether a guilty sinner could earn heaven." His crisis was Israel's covenant after the resurrection: "Who are God's people now? Has circumcision ended? What about... Gentiles?" See [[Pauls Question Was Gentile Inclusion Not Individual Merit]] *(new)*.[^1][^2][^3]
- **"Works of the law" = Jewish covenant boundary markers, not good deeds.** In first-century Judaism the phrase "referred specifically to the practices that marked Jews out as God's covenant people — circumcision, food laws, Sabbath, purity regulations." The question "was not, can you earn heaven by doing good works?" but "do Gentiles have to become Jews before they belong to God's covenant family?" Corroborates [[Works of the Law Means Mosaic Boundary Markers Not Obedience as Such]].[^4][^5]
- **Justification is God's declaration of covenant membership; grace and faith are covenant terms.** *Justification* is "legal language" — a judge declaring "this person is officially a citizen... God declaring someone to be a member of his covenant family." *Grace* is "God holding up his end of the covenant through Jesus," a gift offered to all "simply because God loved us." *Faith* (*pistis*) "carries ideas of trust, loyalty, covenant faithfulness, and allegiance" — "pledging one's loyalty to the new king." Corroborates [[Justification Is Covenant Entry Not a Guaranteed Final Verdict]], [[Faith Is Allegiance to Christ Not Mere Mental Assent]], and [[Paul Redefined Charis as Unmerited Gift Against Greco-Roman Reciprocity]].[^6][^7][^8]
- **Paul rejects boundary-marker works but never obedience; the gospel is cosmic, not a personal salvation formula.** "Paul never rejects obedience itself... the obedience of faith... judged of their works... inheriting the kingdom... new creation" — "covenant faithfulness in Christ's kingdom." Wright and others "restore the scale of Paul's vision": the gospel "isn't merely Jesus died so I can go to heaven" but the announcement that "Jesus is Lord of the new kingdom," "the *euangelion*, the victory of the new king." Corroborates [[The Gospel Is the Good News of God's Kingdom Not a Doctrinal Formula]].[^9][^10][^11]
