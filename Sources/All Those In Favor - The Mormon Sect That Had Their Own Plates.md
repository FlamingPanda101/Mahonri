# All Those In Favor — The Mormon Sect That Had Their Own Plates

Baylor, Cade, and "Voltron" react to a Cabinet of Curiosities documentary on the **Strangites** — the schismatic Latter Day Saint movement founded by [[James Strang]], who produced his own buried plates (the Voree plates and later the "plates of Laban") and his own scripture, the Book of the Law of the Lord. The hosts' apologetic focus is a single critic's argument: that Strang's *witnessed* plates make the Book of Mormon witnesses evidentially unremarkable — "just as much evidence Strang wasn't lying as there is that Joseph Smith wasn't lying."[^1]

## Summary
- **The witness-parity objection and its answer.** Critics say Strang's plates had witnesses "just like" Joseph Smith's, so the two cases carry equal weight. The hosts answer that Strang's witnesses attest only to physical metal plates — a natural fact — whereas Joseph's decisive Three Witnesses attest to an **angel**, a categorically different, naturalistically unexplained claim ("Show me the testimony of someone who saw the angel Laban").[^2] See [[Strang's Witnesses Saw Plates Not an Angel]] and [[The Strang Plates Have Witnesses Just Like the Book of Mormon]].
- **The argument backfires.** To wield the Strang plates against the Book of Mormon, a critic must concede both sets of plates physically existed; by the same evidential standard Joseph's better-documented gold plates existed too — "you'd be stuck saying that Joseph's plates existed" — while the miracle (the angel, the 65-day translation) stays unexplained.[^3] See [[The Strangite Plates Argument Concedes Joseph Smith's Plates Existed]].
- **Martin Harris among the Strangites.** The one Book of Mormon witness who briefly joined Strang never testified to anything miraculous for him, was not an official Strangite witness, kept preaching the Book of Mormon until expelled, and was buried with the Book of Mormon and Doctrine and Covenants — not the Book of the Law of the Lord.[^4] See [[Martin Harris Among the Strangites]].
- **Documentary corrections.** The hosts also fix several details: the New York hill where Joseph found the plates is not the Book of Mormon's Hill Cumorah; Joseph Smith ordained Black men to the priesthood (the [[Priesthood Ban Origins|later ban]] was Brigham Young's); and the water-not-wine sacrament revelation began as a precaution against poisoning.
