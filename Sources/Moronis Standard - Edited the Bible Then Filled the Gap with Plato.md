# Early Christians Edited the Bible. Then Filled the Gap with Plato.

[[Alex Arnold]] of *Moroni's Standard* argues that two events usually studied separately are really **one event seen from two angles**: the 7th-century-BC **Deuteronomistic** scribes *removed* specific theological content from the Hebrew record, and the 4th-century-AD **creedal councils** *installed* Greek philosophical categories to fill the interpretive space that exact removal had opened. "What got cut from the text is almost exactly what got replaced by the creeds."[^1] His pivot is that the Book of Mormon's [[1 Nephi 13.26-29|1 Nephi 13]] describes *both* sides of that event — a deliberate removal, by an identifiable institution, producing specific doctrinal consequences — and did so in 1830, before either half was confirmed by scholarship that was assembled independently, in different countries and centuries, by scholars who were mostly non-LDS and often non-Christian.[^2]

## Summary
- **The textual edit (7th c. BC).** [[Michael Heiser]] (*The Unseen Realm*) and [[Emanuel Tov]] show the older text carried a **divine council** ([[Deuteronomy 32.8-9|Deut 32:8]] "sons of God," Dead Sea Scrolls, vs. Masoretic "sons of Israel") that the standardized text muted; [[Julius Wellhausen]] (1878), [[Martin Noth]] (1943), and [[Frank Moore Cross]] establish a Josianic scribal school that reshaped Deuteronomy–Kings toward centralized worship and Yahweh-only monotheism. The New Testament shows the *same mechanism*: the [[1 John 5.7|Comma Johanneum]] entered the Greek text only via a manuscript ([[Erasmus]]; Codex Montfortianus) widely judged fabricated. See [[What the Deuteronomists Cut Is What the Creeds Replaced]].
- **The philosophical backfill (4th c. AD).** [[Jaroslav Pelikan]] and [[Adolf von Harnack]] ("the Hellenization of Christianity") trace how incorporeality, impassibility, and divine simplicity — from [[Plato]]'s *Timaeus*, the Stoics, and the middle Platonists, applied by [[Justin Martyr]], [[Clement of Alexandria]], and [[Origen]] — were read back onto the Hebrew God, and codified at [[Council of Nicaea|Nicaea]] (325) under the unbaptized [[Constantine]] using the non-biblical term *homoousios*. [[Gerhard May]] dates *creatio ex nihilo* to the 2nd century, not to Genesis.
- **The correspondence is specific.** [[Margaret Barker]] (first-temple theology), [[Mark Smith]], and [[William Dever]] (Kuntillet Ajrud, "Yahweh and his Asherah," ~800 BC) reconstruct a pre-reform religion with an embodied God, a Father–Son distinction, deification, and the divine feminine — the very list LDS theology affirms and the creeds negate. [[Yonatan Adler]] (Yale UP, 2023) independently finds no distinctively Jewish practice before ~167 BC, cutting against "recovery of an ancient orthodoxy."
- **Steelman + the standing objections.** Arnold concedes the historical case does not *prove* the Restoration ("that requires faith"), grants Barker's strong conclusions are non-mainstream, and answers three objections — cherry-picking, Barker-as-fringe, and Spirit-guided-councils (the [[The Robber Council Refutes Holy-Spirit-Guided Councils|Ephesus 449 / Chalcedon 451 "robber council"]] case). His honest core: *the loss and the replacement are historical fact; only the recovery is a faith claim.*
