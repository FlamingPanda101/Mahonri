# Thoughtful Faith - Tucker Carlson Misled on Tithing

A [[Jacob Hansen]] (Thoughtful Faith) response to a clip from the Tucker Carlson Show in which documentary filmmaker [[Nathan Apffel]] argues that (a) tithing "is not scriptural" for a modern-day Christian and (b) the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints "hoards" money — reportedly making "25 billion last year in interest alone" and sitting on a fund large enough to run the roughly $7-billion-a-year global church "in perpetuity." Hansen answers each claim from scripture and from the financial history compiled by excommunicated historian [[D. Michael Quinn]]. This is a companion piece to the [[Latter Daily Saints - The Mormon Church Profits from War Claim Ep 109|Latter Daily Saints]] response to the same interview.

## Summary

- **Tithing is scriptural, and the New Testament raised the bar, not lowered it.** Tithe-paying is commanded across the Bible ([[Abraham]] to [[Melchizedek]], [[Genesis 28.20-22|Jacob's tenth]], [[Moses]], [[Malachi 3.8-10|Malachi 3]]); the first Christians did not tithe because they consecrated 100% ([[Acts 4.32-35|Acts 4]], [[Acts 5.1-11|Ananias]]) — so to say God did not expect financial giving is "demonstrably false." Grounds [[Tithing Is Biblically Commanded and the New Testament Raised the Standard]].
- **Tithing is a voluntary command for spiritual formation, not a celestial toll.** Against "you don't get into the celestial kingdom unless you pay your 10%," Hansen (quoting [[Russell M. Nelson]]) frames tithing as "not a tax... a voluntary offering"; the celestial kingdom is "the kind of person that you are transformed into," not a gate you pay to pass. Grounds [[Tithing Is a Command for Spiritual Formation Not a Celestial Toll]].
- **The reserve fund is prudence, not hoarding.** The "$25B interest / fund-it-forever" figures are the critic's own aggressive estimates; markets change, $100B is only ~$6,000/member, and the reserve insures a fast-growing developing-world membership that consumes far more than it contributes. Grounds [[A Large Reserve Fund Is Prudent Stewardship Not Hoarding]].
- **Even a hostile witness clears the leaders.** [[D. Michael Quinn]] — excommunicated, at times harshly critical — was given unfettered access to Church financial records and judged the finances "faith-promoting": general-authority living allowances (~$120K) are set by the leaders themselves and dwarfed by nonprofit-CEO pay and even by what the Church pays its own employees. Grounds [[An Excommunicated Historian Found LDS Finances Faith-Promoting]].
