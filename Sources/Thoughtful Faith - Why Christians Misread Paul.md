# Thoughtful Faith — Why Christians Misread Paul

[[Jacob Hansen]] interviews Latter-day Saint philosopher [[Blake Ostler]] as debate prep for an upcoming Las Vegas conversation with a Reformed (Presbyterian) Protestant on "How are we saved?" Anticipating the Protestant's opening move to [[Ephesians 2.8-9|Ephesians 2:8-9]] and [[Romans 4.4-5|Romans 4:4-5]], Ostler argues that the entire grace/works impasse rests on **mistranslating and de-contextualizing [[Paul the Apostle|Paul]]**. Read through the New Perspective on Paul and Second-Temple *covenantal nomism*, Paul's "justified" means *entry into right covenant relationship*, not "saved once-for-all with eternal security"; his real problem is Gentile inclusion, not how an individual earns heaven; and his honor-shame world makes both Protestant *imputed* righteousness and Catholic *infused* righteousness category mistakes. The throughline: once Paul is put back in his first-century Jewish frame, he "looks a lot like Joseph Smith was teaching."[^1]

- **Justification is covenant entry, and "saved," "justified," and "exalted" are three different words.** "What we have here is a failure to communicate." Paul's *justified* means "put into a right relationship with God" (covenantal nomism), not the Protestant "saved once and for all with eternal security."[^2] In LDS thought (D&C 76 / [[Philippians 2.9-11|Philippians 2:11]]) *all* are saved — every knee bends and confesses Christ, and only sons of perdition are lost — while *exaltation* (theosis, deifying union with the Godhead) is the covenant path's end product. The Article of Faith 3 phrase "saved by obedience to the laws and ordinances" is therefore an **equivocation** on "saved." See [[Saved Justified and Exalted Are Three Distinct Terms]] and [[Justification Is Covenant Entry Not a Guaranteed Final Verdict]].[^3][^4][^5]
- **Righteousness (*dikaiosynē*) is honor-language, not Latin forensic righteousness.** The biblical texts operate in a Mediterranean culture of honor and shame; *dikaiosynē* means being accounted honorable / in correct relationship, not the Latin *iustitia* the Reformers argued over. Ostler's *A Knight's Tale* illustration: Prince Edward can knight a commoner because the king's honor is unimpeachable — so Christ, whose honor is unimpeachable, stands before the Father and declares the believer worthy of a kingdom of glory. This dissolves the Protestant "legal fiction" of imputed/alien righteousness (grounded, Ostler says, in ethical voluntarism / divine command theory) and the Catholic notion of infused righteousness alike. See [[Dikaiosune Is Honor Language Not Latin Righteousness]].[^6][^7][^8]
- **Faith (*pistis*) is trust/fidelity, not mental assent.** *Pistis* is primarily an interpersonal term — trust — not an epistemological one; its Latin bridge (*fides, fiducia, fidelity, fiduciary*) shares that semantic range. The best gloss: "faithful in the sense that he would be faithful to a wife" — fiduciary duty on Christ's side, simple trust on ours. See [[Faith Is Allegiance to Christ Not Mere Mental Assent]].[^9][^10]
- **Saved by grace, judged by works — universally.** "Every single book in the Bible maintains that we are judged and receive according to our works." Paul uses the judgment-by-works phrase "no less than eight times." Grace gets you into the covenant; the terms of the covenant (now subsumed into the single law of love) still obligate you, and Galatians expressly says one can "fall from grace." James corrects not Paul but *pseudo*-Paulinism — the "sin so grace abounds" distortion Paul himself fought ("God forbid"). See [[Works of the Law Means Mosaic Boundary Markers Not Obedience as Such]], [[Can a True Believer Fall Away]].[^11][^12][^13]
- **First-century Jewish hope was resurrection and the renewed earth, not "going to heaven" — and Joseph Smith rebuilt that soteriology blind.** Paul was "obsessed with who's in the covenant," not the heaven/hell dichotomy Catholics and Protestants fixate on; Israel's hope was the day of the Lord and bodily resurrection into a new creation (Martha's hope for Lazarus). D&C 76 reconciles universal salvation, graded rewards, and judgment by works — the exact first-century synthesis — though Joseph Smith "had no idea about the soteriology of first century Jews" and "was neither a theologian nor a philosopher." Ostler reads this as evidence he "bumbled and stumbled his way into the thought world of ancient Christianity to restore it." See [[Joseph Smith Reconstructed First-Century Soteriology Without Knowing It]].[^14][^15][^16]
