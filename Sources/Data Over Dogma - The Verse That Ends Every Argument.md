# Data Over Dogma — The Verse That Ends Every Argument

A "twisted scripture" episode on **[[Proverbs 3.5-6|Proverbs 3:5-6]]** ("Trust in the LORD... lean not unto thine own understanding"), which the hosts argue is weaponized as a conversation-ending "trump card." McClellan (himself a Latter-day Saint) and Beecher (a non-believer) use the verse as a springboard to attack the broader epistemology of "reading with the Holy Spirit."

- **The verse as a thought-stopping cliché.** A listener wrote in that his pastor would "whip this one out" to end any question — "I don't get to have an opinion about it." The hosts label this a "thought-stopping cliché" deployed as "I'm right and you're wrong, the end." They note (correctly, citing the Hermeneia Proverbs commentary) that the verse is *not* "a general disparagement of intellect."[^1][^2][^3] See [[Proverbs 3.5-6 as a Thought-Stopping Cliche]].
- **"Reading with the Spirit" is special pleading.** McClellan calls the claim that only Spirit-filled readers can understand scripture "the definition of special pleading": two sincere readers "reading with the Holy Spirit" reach opposite conclusions, so the felt witness reduces to intuition — "the same thing that is responsible for optical illusions." He extends the parity charge across Muslim, Buddhist, and LDS ("burning in the bosom") conversion experience.[^4][^5][^6][^7] See [[Reading With the Holy Spirit Is Special Pleading]].
- **The Bible "has no independent inherent meaning of its own."** Meaning is "something we create in negotiation with it and we frequently overrule" — slavery is the "parade example": on a wooden "lean not on your own understanding" reading, "slavery should be a-okay," and abolitionists were told exactly that.[^8][^9] See [[The Bible Has No Inherent Meaning Apart From Interpretation]].
- **"Choose to believe, then you'll get the evidence" is unfalsifiable.** The hosts dissect John Lennox's "red Ferrari" analogy (from the Diary of a CEO interview) and pair it with Carl Sagan's "invisible dragon in the garage": an apologetic that withholds evidence until you commit, then tells every disappointed seeker "you didn't really look," is unverifiable and unfalsifiable.[^10][^11][^12] See [[Choose to Believe Then Get the Evidence Is Unfalsifiable]].
