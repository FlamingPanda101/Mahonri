# Data Over Dogma — Is Faith Without Works Dead?

A "chapter and verse" walkthrough of the **Epistle of James**, focused on chapter 2's faith-and-works diatribe ([[James 2.14-26|James 2:14-26]]). The critical significance of this source for the vault is its provenance: a **non-apologist, historical-critical B-tier scholar** ([[Dan McClellan]], himself a Latter-day Saint) independently affirms the anti-*sola fide* reading of James that the vault's Catholic and LDS apologists defend — a hostile-to-neutral witness conceding the point — while diverging sharply on authorship and the harmony of scripture.

- **"James" is really "Jacob," and the letter is probably pseudepigraphic.** The Greek title is *Iakōbou Epistolē* — the epistle of *Jacob*, which "became James through its various iterations."[^1] No one clearly quotes James until [[Origen]] in the second quarter of the 3rd century CE, despite James being one of the three most prominent early Christians; this silence leads "the majority of scholars" to think it was *not* written by James the brother of Jesus but is pseudepigraphic, written into the early 2nd century.[^2][^3] (McClellan reads Dale Allison's 2013 International Critical Commentary on James.) See [[James 2 Was Written to Rebut Paul on Faith and Works]].
- **James 2 deliberately rebuts Paul, quoting the same Abraham proof-text to the opposite conclusion.** "It sounds an awful lot like James is disagreeing with Paul... when it comes to salvation by faith,"[^4] responding to Romans (or a reading of Romans then in circulation).[^5] James 2:24 — "justified by works and not by faith alone" — is "explicitly, directly calling out Paul in Romans," who "quotes the same verse" (Genesis 15:6) "and arriving at a different conclusion."[^6][^7] See [[James 2 Was Written to Rebut Paul on Faith and Works]] and [[James 2-24 - Justified by Works Not Faith Alone]].
- **The "works only demonstrate saving faith" harmonization is presuppositional eisegesis.** McClellan describes the common Protestant reading (works are the "downstream outcome of a saving faith," proving faith to others) and calls it "pretty presuppositional... renegotiating what's going on in James so that you can make James align with Paul."[^8][^9] For James, by contrast, "the faith was contingent on the works... the works are what put in the reps for the faith,"[^10] and "faith is not an ends in and of itself" but "a component that contributes to creating a better world."[^11] See [[Works Constitute Saving Faith Rather Than Merely Displaying It]].
- **"Mercy triumphs over judgment," and Luther's "epistle of straw."** On James 2:13, McClellan argues justice and mercy are *not* the "standing tension" the Latter-day Saint and broader Christian traditions imagine; Hebrew *mishpat*/*tsedeqah* and *chesed* concern right relationship, not prescribed punishment.[^12][^13] And James's discomfiting anti-faith-alone thrust is why [[Martin Luther]] branded it "an epistle of straw" and relegated it (with Hebrews, Revelation, and the Apocrypha) in his first edition.[^14][^15] See [[Justice and Mercy Are Not in Standing Tension]] and [[Luther and the Epistle of Straw]].
