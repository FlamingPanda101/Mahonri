# Informed Saints — Has Egyptology Disproven the Book of Abraham?

The [[Informed Saints]] hosts interview Egyptologist [[John Gee]] (M.A. Berkeley, Ph.D. Yale; published in mainstream Egyptology on the **hypocephalus**) about **[[Facsimile 2]]** of the [[Book of Abraham]]. The framing is the popular claim that "Egyptology has disproven the Book of Abraham" because the hieroglyphs on the facsimiles "can just be translated and it's game over."[^1] Gee's answer is not that the critics mistranslate, but that the whole "just translate it" move rests on category errors about what a hypocephalus is, what state Facsimile 2 was in, and how confidently anyone can read it.

## Summary
- **The "game over" translation is an illusion on both sides.** Critics substitute a *modern Egyptologist's* interpretation for the *ancient Egyptian* one — "and those are not the same thing." Gee published an article in an Egyptological venue showing that almost none of the figure-identifications Egyptologists had proposed actually matched what the figures were *labeled* as on documents that carry labels; most Egyptologists are not trained in Greco-Roman Egypt (~1,000 years off their pyramids/New Kingdom specialties), so the confident verdicts are overstated.[^2][^3][^4]
- **A hypocephalus is a funerary amulet, not part of the Abraham roll.** Facsimile 2 belongs to a class of ~100–120 known objects placed at the head of a mummy; its function (per Book of the Dead spell 162) is to put flame/light "at the head" so a pharaoh destroys enemies or a deceased person passes the gates of the afterlife — "almost like your yarmulke serves as your temple recommend." Its owner (Sheshonq) differs from the owner of Facsimiles 1 and 3, so it is a *separate* artifact the 19th-century dealers happened to sell together.[^5][^6][^7]
- **Joseph Smith's damaged-facsimile restorations and readable captions fall inside the attested range — some are "bullseyes."** Facsimile 2 was incomplete in Joseph's day; Reuben Hedlock's engraved reconstructions (the solar boat, a multi-headed central figure, the upright orientation) all have parallels in other hypocephali. The one readable text-block, which Joseph said pertains to "the holy temple of God," has genuine parallels on Ptolemaic *temple gates* at Karnak — fitting a document whose job is to pass you *through gates* — and the whole thing is astronomical, matching the Book of Abraham's cosmology (Kolob, [[Abraham 3.2-3|Abraham 3]]).[^8][^9][^10]
- **The facsimiles are not the keystone.** Gee stresses they play "a very very very small role" in the Church — cited in general conference perhaps once, rarely in manuals — so nothing salvific hinges on getting them "just right": "If the tomb wasn't empty on the third day, we have a big problem. If the facsimiles aren't interpreted just right, we don't have a big problem." Study deeply, believe boldly.[^11][^12]
