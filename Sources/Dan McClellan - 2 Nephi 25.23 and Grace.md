# Dan McClellan — Why Mormonism Is a False Religion (2 Nephi 25:23 and Grace)

A short-form YouTube reply in which [[Dan McClellan]] answers an Evangelical apologist — addressed only as "David" — whose video argued that **Mormonism is a false religion** because it teaches a works-based salvation, prooftexted from [[2 Nephi 25.23|2 Nephi 25:23]] ("it is by grace that we are saved, after all we can do"). McClellan *defends* the Book of Mormon verse: read in its early-19th-century idiom, it is "an explicit endorsement of grace-alone salvation" — "saved by grace **despite** all we can do." He grounds this in his peer-reviewed 2020 *Journal of Book of Mormon Studies* paper, then argues the New Testament itself nowhere teaches Protestant sola fide, and closes with his signature thesis that scripture functions as a "rubber stamp" for whatever a tradition currently needs.

Note the double edge for LDS apologetics: McClellan **undercuts the Evangelical critique** (the grace reading, the New Perspective on Paul), but from a **naturalistic frame** — he treats the Book of Mormon as a 19th-century text steeped in Protestant soteriology, reads James as a pseudepigraphon contradicting Paul, and holds an anti-realist view of textual meaning. He is an ally on the verse and a foil on the framework.

## Summary
- **"After all we can do" = "despite all we can do."** In his 2020 paper *"2 Nephi 25:23 in Literary and Rhetorical Context,"* McClellan surveyed every late-18th–mid-19th-century occurrence of the phrase he could find; in "absolutely every single occurrence" the best (often only) reading is *despite* all we can do — never "only once we have done everything."[^1] Examples: an 1829 French grammar,[^2] and grace paragraphs of 1710/1761/1777 on the believer who "after all he can do, is still an unprofitable servant," riffing on [[Luke 17.10|Luke 17:10]].[^3] The Book of Mormon makes the *same* riff in [[Mosiah 2.21|Mosiah 2:21]].[^4]
- **The works-merit reading is a recent tradition.** The "my works + God's grace" gloss "David" assumes "is really an interpretive tradition that arose in the early 20th century… and has been waning since the beginning of the 21st," corrected by "even leaders in the church."[^5]
- **No New Testament text teaches Protestant grace-alone salvation, and several contradict it.** The Synoptics and John demand "trusting and obeying God"; Paul's "works of the law" targets the law of Moses; and James "says the exact opposite of what Paul is saying… pseudographically written decades later."[^6][^7]
- **Scripture as "rubber stamp."** "The Bible and the Book of Mormon are just conversation partners… the texts are primarily there as a rubber stamp to authorize whatever those other conversation partners decide is needed" — which is "why things change frequently."[^8]
