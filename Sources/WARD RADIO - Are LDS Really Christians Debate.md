# Ward Radio — Are LDS Really Christians? (Devin vs Daniel)

A [[Ward Radio]] Lincoln–Douglas–style debate hosted by [[Cardon Ellis]]. **Affirmative:** [[Devin Barnes|Devin]] of Latter-day Logic — Latter-day Saints *are* Christians. **Negative:** **Daniel**, the [[Daniel (The Free Fidian)|Free Fidian]] (a Baptist commentator) — the *LDS organization* is not Christian. Structure: 18-min openings, 10-min rebuttals, two 12-min cross-examinations, super-chats, 5-min closings. The debate is almost entirely a clash over **which definition of "Christian" governs** (descriptive/biblical vs. creedal/regenerate) plus whether **Joseph Smith's testable claims survive scrutiny**. See the sibling "are Mormons Christian" cluster: [[Are Latter-day Saints Christians]], [[Latter-day Saints Are Christians Just Not Creedal Christians]], [[The Creeds Cannot Be the Neutral Baseline - The Circularity of Creedal Disqualification]].

## Summary

- **Devin: "Christian" splits into four questions — descriptive, historical, confessional, salvific — and the critic equivocates between them.** LDS meet the descriptive (worship Christ as divine Lord and Savior) and historical (emerged from and claims continuity with Christianity) senses; he *concedes* they are not creedal/confessional Christians and says the salvific sense is between each person and God. **NEW:** [[Four Senses of Christian and the Equivocation Charge]].[^1]
- **Devin: the Restoration repeats Christianity's own break from Judaism, so the same rule that legitimizes one legitimizes the other.** First-century Christians claimed to be true heirs of a corrupted Judaism exactly as Joseph Smith claimed to recover a corrupted Christianity; he demands a *non-circular* rule that vindicates the New Testament rupture while rejecting the Restoration. **NEW:** [[The Restoration Repeats Christianitys Own Break From Judaism]]; cf. [[Catholic Doctrinal Development Is the Same Double Standard]], [[Christianity and Judaism Were Meant to Be One Religion]].[^2]
- **Devin: the creeds cannot be the neutral baseline.** Constantine (an emperor, not an apostle) convened Nicaea; "false doctrines such as the trinity, the hypostatic union" use Greek terms (unmoved mover, "without parts or passions") foreign to the New Testament — so disqualifying the LDS by the creeds is circular. Reinforces [[The Creeds Cannot Be the Neutral Baseline - The Circularity of Creedal Disqualification]].[^3]
- **Devin: define "Christian" by the dictionary or by Christ, not by a denomination.** The dictionary ("believes in and follows the teachings of Jesus Christ") and Jesus's own mark of discipleship — [[John 13.35|John 13:35]], "by this shall all men know that ye are my disciples, if ye have love one to another" — put the test in orthopraxy, not creeds. Cf. [[Christian Means Belonging to Christ by Doing the Fathers Will]].[^4]
- **Daniel: "Christian" means biblically *regenerate*, and he targets the organization, not individuals.** A Christian is one whose old heart God replaces with a new heart, saved by grace not works (he chains Mark 7:20-23, Revelation 21:8, John 6:28-29, [[Isaiah 53|Isaiah 53:5]], 2 Corinthians 5:17, [[Titus 3.5|Titus 3:4-7]]). An individual in an LDS pew may be Christian; the *LDS organization*, he argues, is not.[^5]
- **Daniel: everything rests on Joseph Smith, so test him — and his testable claims fail.** If Joseph's claims are false "the entire LDS faith collapses." This is the negative case's spine; it already has a home at [[Every Testable Joseph Smith Claim Fails Only Subjective Evidence Survives]]. His closing hook is [[John 3.12|John 3:12]]: "if I have told you earthly things and you do not believe, how will you believe if I tell you heavenly things?"[^6]
- **Daniel, exhibit 1 — polygamy promised exaltation.** The [[Joseph Smith and Helen Mar Kimball Sealing|Helen Mar Kimball]] autobiography (marriage would "ensure your eternal salvation and exaltation"), [[Brigham Young|Brigham Young]] in Journal of Discourses 11 ("the only men who become gods... are those who enter into polygamy"), D&C 132 (Emma "destroyed" if she refuses), [[Joseph F. Smith]] and [[John Taylor]] — all tie the highest heaven to a practice that now brings excommunication, a switch that tracked the *Mormon v. United States* ruling. See [[Polygamy Doctrine Did Not Change Only the Exaltation-Requires-Polygamy Idea Did]], [[Rejecting Polygamy Unravels Sealing and the Plan of Salvation]], [[The Polygamy Objection Is Really a Debate About the Nature of God]].[^7]
- **Daniel, exhibit 2 — the Bible-corruption / JST problem.** Article of Faith 8 and 1 Nephi 13 place the loss of "plain and precious" truths *after* the apostles; the Joseph Smith Translation *inserts* prophecies of Joseph Smith into Genesis 50 and Isaiah 29 — additions absent from the Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaᵃ, a century pre-Jesus). Cf. [[Every Testable Joseph Smith Claim Fails Only Subjective Evidence Survives]].[^8]
- **Daniel, exhibit 3 — the Book of Abraham is a falsifiable test Joseph fails.** LDS and non-LDS Egyptologists agree the papyri (dated ~1,000+ years after Abraham) do not say what the translation claims; on Facsimile 3 he identified Osiris as "Abraham" and the goddess Isis ("the great Isis, mother of God") as "King Pharaoh." This exchange is thoroughly mapped at [[The Rediscovered Papyri Are Evidence Against the Book of Abraham]], [[Joseph Was Not Translating the Surviving Papyrus]], [[The Book of Abraham Debate Plays a Provenance vs Content Double Standard]].[^9]
- **Daniel, exhibit 4 — the degrees-of-glory afterlife removes the gospel's urgency.** If almost everyone reaches a kingdom of glory (he expects at least the terrestrial), the biblical "fear of the Lord" (2 Corinthians 5:11) and the two destinations of [[Matthew 25.31-46|Matthew 25]] lose their force. See [[LDS Afterlife - Degrees of Glory]], [[God Will Not Force You Into Heaven Against Your Will]].[^10]
- **Devin's rebuttals.** Polygamy: Abraham, Jacob, and David (2 Samuel 12:7-8, "I gave you your master's wives") practiced it — "polygamy and murder... both sinful except for when God commanded it." The JST is *inspired commentary*, not a claim to restore the autograph ("Moses added himself to the Bible" too). The Book of Abraham facsimiles are *explanations*, not translations — Jesus likewise repurposed an Egyptian Osiris motif into a new story ([[Luke 16.22|Luke 16]], Lazarus and the rich man) — and the surviving fragment is inches of a 40–45-foot scroll whose image and text need not correspond. See [[Translating a Text Versus Interpreting a Drawing Are Different Claims]].[^11]
- **Devin: the true test of a prophet is fruits, not a failed prediction.** The Bible never says a false prophecy makes a false prophet; Jesus's test is [[Matthew 7.16-20|Matthew 7]] "by their fruits" — and the fruits are the Book of Mormon ("the most Christian book on the planet") and a church without yachts or scandal. Cf. [[Judge the Book of Abraham by Its Fruits]], [[Testing Interpretation by the Prophet's Actions]], [[Deuteronomy 18.20-22|Deuteronomy 18]].[^12]
- **Devin on the afterlife:** the parable of the laborers in the vineyard and baptism for the dead mean the last-hour convert still reaches the celestial kingdom; "Protestant heaven" matches the LDS terrestrial (second) heaven — the incentive to accept the restored gospel is simply *more* grace, "like going on a cruise" with all the food already paid for.[^13]
- **Cross-examination, Devin → Daniel: the Protestant interpretive-authority problem.** Two sincere Protestants can read one verse oppositely with no pope to adjudicate; so (Devin presses) Daniel has no authority to declare the LDS "not Christian." Daniel: Scripture judges itself, perseverance marks the regenerate ("they went out from us because they were not of us," 1 John 2:19), and Deuteronomy supplies a prophet-test.[^14]
- **Cross-examination, Daniel → Devin: the prophetic-authority problem.** How to know which prophet to follow? Devin: a living prophet outranks a dead one, and doctrine requires all apostles to ratify (D&C 1, "whether by mine own voice or the voice of my servants"). Would he re-practice polygamy if commanded? "Yes, if it's the voice of the Lord... but not willingly" — while conceding he would *not* ransack California or cheat, which Daniel presses as a real limit on the obedience he professes. Devin can produce no pre-1890 quote calling the JST "commentary," and no list of what in the Bible is "translated correctly." Cf. [[Brigham Young Failed Prophet Argument]].[^15]
- **Super-chats.** Acts 19 (disciples who had not heard of the Holy Spirit — "non-trinitarians but still Christians"; Daniel: likely disciples of John); the Book of Mormon and race ("a very racist book" — Daniel; Devin: Protestantism used the Bible for slavery, "I wouldn't touch that with a 10-foot pole"); the Ancient of Days as Adam (Adam–God); and the Good Samaritan — "at what point do we become Samaritans rather than infidels?"[^16]
- **Closings.** Devin: Daniel "followed the script perfectly" of a first-century Jew rejecting a first-century Christian; the Book of Mormon fulfills Isaiah 29's sealed book (Lebanon reforested by 1901) and is "the most Christian book on the planet." Daniel: three tested claims fail (polygamy-for-exaltation, manuscript-less prophecy, the Book of Abraham), so — by [[John 3.12|John 3:12]] — the untestable heavenly claims cannot be trusted; faith must rest "on the rock of truth," and other religions have emotional witnesses and good works too.[^17]
