# Apostles Creed

The Apostles' Creed is the baptismal creed of the Western church, its wording settling into its received form by the early medieval period (developing from the older Old Roman Symbol). Recited every Sunday across Catholic and Protestant congregations, its closing article confesses (traditional English text):

> ...I believe in the Holy Ghost; the holy catholic Church; the communion of saints; the forgiveness of sins; **the resurrection of the body**; and the life everlasting. Amen.

## Significance

The phrase "the resurrection of the body" (Latin *carnis resurrectionem*, literally "the resurrection of the flesh") is the whole hinge of the *Moroni's Standard* argument that [[Alex Arnold]] builds: the creed the Western church recites weekly **formally affirms bodily resurrection**, while its popular pastoral theology — the funeral sermon about a soul now "in a better place" — actually teaches the [[The Immortal Soul Is a Platonic Import Not a Biblical Doctrine|Platonic immortal soul]], an incompatible claim about what death is. The creedal affirmation "never went away, it just stopped being operative." The [[Nicene Creed]] of 325 (with its 381 expansion) likewise confesses "the resurrection of the dead" / "the life of the world to come."
