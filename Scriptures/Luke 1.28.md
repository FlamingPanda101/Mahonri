# Luke 1:28

> "And the angel came in unto her, and said, Hail, thou that art highly favoured, the Lord is with thee: blessed art thou among women." *(KJV)*

The Greek phrase behind "highly favoured" is **κεχαριτωμένη (kecharitōmenē)** — a perfect passive participle of *charitoō*, "to grace / to favor." The perfect tense denotes a completed action with an abiding present state ("having been graced, and remaining so"). Catholics read it as a quasi-title ("full of grace" in the Vulgate's *gratia plena*); many Protestants and Latter-day Saints render it "favored one."

## How traditions read it

- **Catholic:** the perfect participle marks a settled, prior, comprehensive endowment of grace — a scriptural seed of the **Immaculate Conception** (Mary graced from her conception). The angel *replaces* her name with the title, signaling a unique fullness of grace. Contrasted with [[Acts 6.7|Acts 6:7]], where Stephen is "full" using a different Greek word (*plērēs*).
- **LDS / Protestant:** "highly favored" states God's favor toward Mary without implying sinlessness; being graced or favored is true of any believer who receives Christ, and the verse says nothing about conception or a lifelong absence of sin.
