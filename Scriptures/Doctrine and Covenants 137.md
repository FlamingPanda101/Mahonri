# Doctrine and Covenants 137

> "Thus came the voice of the Lord unto me, saying: All who have died without a knowledge of this gospel, who would have received it if they had been permitted to tarry, shall be heirs of the celestial kingdom of God;" (D&C 137:7)

> "For I, the Lord, will judge all men according to their works, according to the desire of their hearts." (D&C 137:9)

> "And I also beheld that all children who die before they arrive at the years of accountability are saved in the celestial kingdom of heaven." (D&C 137:10)

## Significance
An 1836 vision of Joseph Smith in the Kirtland Temple in which he saw his brother [[Alvin Smith]] — who died as an adult without LDS baptism — in the celestial kingdom. Verse 7 grounds that on a **counterfactual**: those who *would have received* the gospel had they lived. In the conversation, [[Tim Stratton]] and [[Hayden Carroll]] agree this is functionally **middle knowledge**: "That's God's counterfactual knowledge. And if he had that prior to the formation of the universe, that would count as middle knowledge."[^1] Verse 10 is the LDS answer on infant salvation — children who die before the age of accountability are saved in the celestial kingdom, a "hard doctrine" the LDS "wave our flags" over rather than leaving open.[^2] See [[Doctrine and Covenants 137 Anticipates Middle Knowledge]].

## How traditions read it
- **LDS:** Definitive scriptural assurance of celestial salvation for those who die in infancy and for the honest-hearted unevangelized. Read with [[Every Person Gets a Fair Opportunity Through Proxy Ordinances|proxy ordinance work]].
- **Protestant (Stratton):** Not authoritative for him, but he grants that its logic — God saving on the basis of what a person *would freely have done* — is precisely the Molinist structure he defends, and better than the rival Christian answers he surveys in his article "What About Babies Who Die."[^3]
