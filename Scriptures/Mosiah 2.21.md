# Mosiah 2:21

> I say unto you that if ye should serve him who has created you from the beginning, and is preserving you from day to day, by lending you breath, that ye may live and move and do according to your own will, and even supporting you from one moment to another—I say, if ye should serve him with all your whole souls yet ye would be unprofitable servants.

## Significance
King Benjamin's address makes the Book of Mormon's own "unprofitable servants" argument: even total, whole-souled service leaves us in God's debt, not his. [[Dan McClellan]] cites this as the Book of Mormon riffing on [[Luke 17.10|Luke 17:10]] — evidence that Restoration scripture sits inside the same late-18th/early-19th-century grace idiom as [[2 Nephi 25.23|2 Nephi 25:23]], where "after all we can do" marks the *insufficiency* of human effort rather than a checklist that must be completed before grace applies.
