# Titus 2:11

> For the grace of God that bringeth salvation hath appeared to all men,

**[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** makes this the anchor of his answer to the classic "what about those who never heard of Christ?" problem. Reading "hath appeared to **all** men" universally, he concludes: "during mortality each one of us will have at least one opportunity to... encounter the grace of God that brings salvation. How God does that I don't know... that's God's problem."[^1] He pairs it with Psalm 19:1 ("the heavens declare the glory of God") to argue even the unreached — "those who live in the deepest jungles" — get a genuine chance in this life.[^2]

[[Hayden Carroll]] flags this as an unusually inclusivist position for an evangelical — "I've never heard somebody say this before" — and notes the irony that it approaches the Latter-day Saint conviction that every person receives a fair opportunity, though Mark locates the single opportunity strictly *in mortality* rather than partly beyond death.[^3]
