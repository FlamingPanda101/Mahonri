# Romans 5:1-9

> Therefore being justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ... Much more then, being now justified by his blood, we shall be saved from wrath through him. For if, when we were enemies, we were reconciled to God by the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, we shall be saved by his life. *(KJV, vv. 1, 9-10)*

## Used in
- [[Justification Is Covenant Entry Not a Guaranteed Final Verdict]] — the centerpiece dispute: Ryan reads "much more... we shall be saved from wrath" as a *guarantee* (if X justified now, then definitely Y saved later); Hansen reads it as being well-*positioned* to be justified at the final judgment if one remains faithful to the covenant.
- [[Can a True Believer Fall Away]] — Ryan cites the "much more" logic as grounds for eternal security.

## How traditions read it
- **Reformed / Protestant (Ryan):** The *a fortiori* ("much more") is an unconditional guarantee — those justified now *will* be saved from the coming wrath; adding conditions "adds to the text." Peace with God (v.1) is a present, settled status.
- **Latter-day Saint (Hansen):** V.1 is *entry* into the covenant relationship (the beginning of the journey, not the end). The final judgment "by works" (Romans 2:6-13) still lies ahead; "much more" means the reconciled are better positioned to be justified then *if* they abide in the relationship.
