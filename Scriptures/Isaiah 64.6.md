# Isaiah 64:6

> "But we are all as an unclean thing, and all our righteousnesses are as filthy rags; and we all do fade as a leaf; and our iniquities, like the wind, have taken us away." (KJV)

## Significance
The **S** ("Sins cannot be removed by our good deeds") of [[Tim Stratton]]'s GOSPEL acronym. Stratton cites it for the point that human good works cannot cleanse: "there's nothing we as infected people can do about it... Isaiah 64:6 says, 'our good works are like filthy rags.'"[^1] He is careful to add this does not mean unbelievers "can't do good things — it's just your good things don't work; they're not going to clean you." The claim is about the *insufficiency* of works for justification, not their nonexistence — the miracle of Christ's atonement is what cleanses. See [[Calvinism Is a Different Gospel - GOSPEL vs ED]].

## How traditions read it
- **Protestant (sola fide):** a classic proof-text that even our righteous acts are worthless for justification, requiring imputed righteousness.
- **Catholic / LDS:** the "filthy rags" are Israel's self-reliant or hypocritical works in Isaiah's immediate context (national lament), not a blanket verdict that Spirit-empowered good works are worthless; grace enables works that genuinely please God. Compare [[Works Are the Fruit of Faith Not the Price of Salvation]].
