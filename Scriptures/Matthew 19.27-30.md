# Matthew 19:27-30

> 27 Then answered Peter and said unto him, Behold, we have forsaken all, and followed thee; what shall we have therefore?
> 28 And Jesus said unto them, Verily I say unto you, That ye which have followed me, in the regeneration when the Son of man shall sit in the throne of his glory, ye also shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel.
> 29 And every one that hath forsaken houses, or brethren, or sisters, or father, or mother, or wife, or children, or lands, for my name's sake, shall receive an hundredfold, and shall inherit everlasting life.
> 30 But many that are first shall be last; and the last shall be first.

[[Peter]] voices the disciples' anxiety at how much Jesus asks — "we have forsaken all" — and Jesus answers not by softening the demand but by promising a hundredfold return and everlasting life to all who forsake houses or lands "for my name's sake." The passage grounds the argument that Jesus *expected* radical consecration, so requiring a mere tithe today is, if anything, the lesser ask; and that consecration is rewarded, not extracted as a toll.
