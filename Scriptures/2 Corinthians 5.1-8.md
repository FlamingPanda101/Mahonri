# 2 Corinthians 5.1-8

> For in this we groan, earnestly desiring to be clothed upon with our house which is from heaven... For we that are in this tabernacle do groan, being burdened: not for that we would be unclothed, but clothed upon, that mortality might be swallowed up of life... We are confident, I say, and willing rather to be absent from the body, and to be present with the Lord. *(2 Corinthians 5:2, 4, 8, KJV)*

## Significance

Verse 8 ("absent from the body... present with the Lord") is routinely quoted to prove the soul departs at once to its final heavenly home. [[Alex Arnold]] of *Moroni's Standard* insists on reading it in its context (vv. 1–4): Paul does **not** celebrate disembodiment. He calls it **nakedness**, says he would rather be *clothed* than *unclothed*, and longs for "mortality [to] be swallowed up of life." Paul's actual destination is the **resurrection**; the disembodied "at home with the Lord" state is only the best available condition *before* that destination is reached. The verse describes a genuine closeness to God in the interim while making the body's redemption — not its shedding — the thing Paul awaits.
