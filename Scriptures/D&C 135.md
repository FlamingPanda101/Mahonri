# D&C 135

> **135:1** — "To seal the testimony of this book and the Book of Mormon, we announce the martyrdom of Joseph Smith the Prophet, and Hyrum Smith the Patriarch. They were shot in Carthage jail, on the 27th of June, 1844, about five o'clock p.m., by an armed mob... Hyrum was shot first and fell calmly, exclaiming: I am a dead man! Joseph leaped from the window, and was shot dead in the attempt, exclaiming: O Lord my God!"

> **135:3** — "Joseph Smith, the Prophet and Seer of the Lord, has done more, save Jesus only, for the salvation of men in this world, than any other man that ever lived in it."

> **135:4** — "When Joseph went to Carthage to deliver himself up... he said: 'I am going like a lamb to the slaughter; but I am calm as a summer's morning; I have a conscience void of offense towards God, and towards all men.'"

## Why It Matters Here

Section 135 is John Taylor's announcement of the murders of [[Joseph Smith]] and [[Hyrum Smith]] at Carthage Jail on 27 June 1844. It is cited in the sincerity-vs-fraud debate as evidence against the *profit-motive* reading of Joseph Smith: the men died pursued and impoverished, still bound to the Book of Mormon, not enriched by it. [[Casey Griffiths]] invokes "section 135" to make exactly this point — that the martyrs went to their deaths devoted to the record, which "sounds like a little bit more than a" con.[^1]

## Used In

- [[Joseph Smith Gained No Wealth and Died Affirming the Book of Mormon]] — the martyrdom as the final rebuttal to the "he did it for money" objection.

## Interpretive Note

Latter-day Saints read verse 3 as a canonized tribute to Joseph's world-historical role in the Restoration; critics read the same verse as hagiography. The undisputed historical core — that Joseph and Hyrum were shot by a mob while jailed, and that Joseph had no wealth to show for the movement — is what carries weight in the fraud debate, independent of the section's evaluative language.
