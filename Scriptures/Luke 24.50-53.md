# Luke 24:50-53

> And he led them out as far as to Bethany, and he lifted up his hands, and blessed them. And it came to pass, while he blessed them, he was parted from them, and carried up into heaven. And they worshipped him, and returned to Jerusalem with great joy: and were continually in the temple, praising and blessing God. Amen. *(KJV)*

## Significance

The closing verses of Luke — the seam of "[[Luke's Lacuna - The Forty-Day Ministry Is a Measurable Hole Between Luke and Acts|Luke's Lacuna]]." Immediately after telling the disciples to "tarry… until ye be endued with power from on high" ([[Luke 24.49|Luke 24:49]]), Jesus **lifts up his hands, blesses them**, is parted from them, and the disciples respond by returning to be "**continually in the temple**." Latter-day Saint commentators on [[Ward Radio]] read the sequence as pointed: told to wait for an endowment of power, the disciples go to the temple and stay there — so the physical temple is where the "power from on high" is expected. The uplifted-hands blessing is also read as a priestly/temple gesture. See [[The Lost Forty-Day Teaching Points to a Suppressed Temple Endowment]].

Mainstream reading: Luke frames the Gospel's end at the temple to bracket the whole book (which opened in the temple with Zacharias) and to mark the disciples' continuing Jewish piety and joyful worship pending Pentecost — a literary *inclusio*, not a temple-ordinance cue.
