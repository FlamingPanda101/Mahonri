# Isaiah 29:11-12 — The words of a sealed book, the learned and the unlearned

> "And the vision of all is become unto you as the words of a book that is sealed, which men deliver to one that is learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I cannot; for it is sealed: And the book is delivered to him that is not learned, saying, Read this, I pray thee: and he saith, I am not learned." (Isaiah 29:11-12, KJV)

The same chapter promises "a marvellous work and a wonder" among a people whose "wise men shall perish" (Isaiah 29:14).

## Latter-day Saint reading
Latter-day Saints read Isaiah 29 as a prophecy of the coming forth of the [[Book of Mormon]]: the "sealed book" is the plates, and the exchange between a *learned* man who cannot read it and an *unlearned* man who brings it forth is fulfilled in the Anthon-transcript episode. Joseph Smith copied characters off the plates, [[Martin Harris]] carried them to learned scholars who could not translate them, and it was the unlearned Joseph who then translated the record — "not because of his learning... but because of his inspiration."[^1]

Historian [[Don Bradley]] adds a personal-witness dimension: because [[Emma Smith]] was Joseph's first scribe and an eyewitness to how uneducated he was (he could not spell "Sariah," did not know Jerusalem had walls, and butchered the word "February"), *Emma herself was a witness to the fulfillment of Isaiah 29*. Her own last testimony echoes verse 14, calling the translation "marvelous to me, a marvel, and a wonder as much so as anyone else."[^1]
