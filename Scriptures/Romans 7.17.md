# Romans 7:17

> Now then it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me.

**[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** enlists this verse to support his soul/body anthropology: Paul's "it is no more I that do it, but sin that dwelleth in me" is read as evidence of two agents — an "old man" (the sinful flesh) and a "new man" (the regenerated, sinless self) — so that the born-again person's true self "cannot sin" even while the body does.[^1] He ties it to [[1 John 3.9|1 John 3:9]] and Matthew 5:48.

**[[Hayden Carroll]]'s objection:** Paul's "old man / new man" language does not license splitting a believer into two persons; conversion makes one *a* new man, and reading Romans 7 as "your soul has nothing to do with your sin" is an interpretation the text does not state — "that's still me though."[^2] Romans 7 is itself a contested passage (regenerate vs. pre-regenerate "I"), so it under-determines Mark's specific soul-sinlessness claim.
