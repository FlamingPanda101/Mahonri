# John 19:26-27

> "When Jesus therefore saw his mother, and the disciple standing by, whom he loved, he saith unto his mother, Woman, behold thy son! Then saith he to the disciple, Behold thy mother! And from that hour that disciple took her unto his own home." *(KJV)*

From the cross Jesus addresses his mother as **"Woman"** (Greek *gynai*) — the same address he uses at Cana (John 2:4).

## How traditions read it

- **Catholic:** Jesus deliberately calls Mary "Woman," identifying her with **the woman of Genesis 3:15** and Revelation 12 — the New Eve. Committing her to the beloved disciple ("Behold thy mother") is read as entrusting Mary's spiritual motherhood to the whole Church. Part of the typological case that Mary is the sinless second Eve. See [[Genesis 3.15|Genesis 3:15]] and [[Revelation 12|Revelation 12]].
- **LDS / Protestant:** "Woman" is a respectful (or at least ordinary) form of address, and the passage's plain sense is Jesus providing for his mother's care after his death — not a doctrinal statement about her sinlessness or a universal motherhood.
