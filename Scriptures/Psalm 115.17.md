# Psalm 115.17

> The dead praise not the LORD, neither any that go down into silence. *(Psalm 115:17, KJV)*

## Significance

A flagship prooftext for the early-Hebrew picture of death as descent into [[Sheol Was the Undifferentiated Realm of All the Dead|Sheol]] — a silent, diminished, non-worshipping state, not conscious bliss in heaven. [[Alex Arnold]] of *Moroni's Standard* pairs it with Job 14:10–12 (a man lies down and does not rise "until the heavens are no more") and [[Ecclesiastes]] 9 (the dead "know nothing, have no further reward") to show that the pre-exilic texts locate *everyone* — righteous and wicked alike — in the same shadowy realm, with no immediate flight of the soul to God's presence. On this framework, being cut off from "the worship of God" is precisely what makes death a rupture of the whole person that only resurrection can heal.

## Interpretation across traditions
- **Note:** [[Psalm 115.4-7|Psalm 115:4–7]] (idols "have mouths, but they speak not") is a *different* unit of the same psalm, on the impotence of idols; verse 17 is the afterlife datum.
