# Isaiah 55:10-11

> For as the rain cometh down, and the snow from heaven, and returneth not thither, but watereth the earth, and maketh it bring forth and bud, that it may give seed to the sower, and bread to the eater: so shall my word be that goeth forth out of my mouth: it shall not return unto me void, but it shall accomplish that which I please, and it shall prosper in the thing whereto I sent it. *(KJV)*

## Significance
Quinn Fryberg uses this passage for the **sufficiency** limb of his *sola scriptura* case: just as rain accomplishes the purpose for which it is sent, God's word "shall not return void" but accomplishes its purpose — which, joined to [[2 Timothy 3.16-17|2 Timothy 3:16-17]], he takes to be equipping the church for every good work.

**Counter-reading:** the passage describes the efficacy of God's *spoken/revealed word* generally; it does not define the boundaries of a written canon or claim that the written word alone is the sole infallible rule — the point Carroll presses throughout (a verse about "God's word" need not denote the 66-book collection). Cf. [[The Word of God Is Not Confined to the Written Word]].
