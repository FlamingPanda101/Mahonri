# Alma 36:6-13

Alma the Younger recounts his own conversion in the first person to his son Helaman:

> **6** For I went about with the sons of Mosiah, seeking to destroy the church of God; but behold, God sent his holy angel to stop us by the way.
>
> **7** And behold, he spake unto us, as it were the voice of thunder, and the whole earth did tremble beneath our feet; and we all fell to the earth, for the fear of the Lord came upon us …
>
> **10** And it came to pass that I fell to the earth; and it was for the space of three days and three nights that I could not open my mouth, neither had I the use of my limbs.

## The internal-consistency detail
This is the **third** appearance of the same event: Mormon's narration in [[Mosiah 27.11-13|Mosiah 27:11-13]], then Alma's own recital here (~38 chapters later). About ten details recur *in the same order* — going about to destroy the church, the angel's voice like thunder, the trembling earth, falling to the earth, three days speechless and limbless, and later the "gall of bitterness" (v.18) — while the two accounts differ exactly as real re-tellings do. Alma, speaking privately to his son, omits the public detail of the priests fasting and praying for him and adds intimate ones ("encircled about by the everlasting chains of death"). Markham compares this audience-shaped variation to the multiply-attested [[First Vision Accounts Differ by Audience Not Contradiction|First Vision accounts]].

## Interpretation
Alma 36 is also the Book of Mormon's most-cited chiasmus, centered on Christ (see [[Chiasmus as Evidence of Ancient Authorship in the Book of Mormon]]) — so the same passage carries both a poetic macro-structure and this undesigned cross-consistency with Mosiah 27, two independent layers Markham keeps distinct.
