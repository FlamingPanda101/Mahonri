# 1 Corinthians 15.14-19

> And if Christ be not risen, then is our preaching vain, and your faith is also vain... And if Christ be not raised, your faith is vain; ye are yet in your sins. Then they also which are fallen asleep in Christ are perished. If in this life only we have hope in Christ, we are of all men most miserable. *(1 Corinthians 15:14, 17–19, KJV)*

## Significance

The hinge of Paul's great resurrection chapter. Modern translations render v. 17 "if Christ has not been raised, your faith is **futile**." [[Alex Arnold]] of *Moroni's Standard* argues this sentence "only makes theological sense if the resurrection of the body is the central claim, not... a supplement to something that the soul has already achieved on its own." If the soul's immediate flight to heaven at death were the real payoff, Paul's insistence that the *whole faith collapses* without a future bodily resurrection would be unintelligible. Paul does not treat resurrection as a bonus added to a soul already safe; he makes it the load-bearing center of the gospel — the New Testament endpoint of the Hebrew whole-person framework.
