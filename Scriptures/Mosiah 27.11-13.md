# Mosiah 27:11-13

> **11** And as I said unto you, as they were going about rebelling against God, behold, the angel of the Lord appeared unto them; and he descended as it were in a cloud; and he spake as it were with a voice of thunder, which caused the earth to shake upon which they stood;
>
> **12** And so great was their astonishment, that they fell to the earth, and understood not the words which he spake unto them.
>
> **13** Nevertheless he cried again, saying: Alma, arise and stand forth, for why persecutest thou the church of God? …

## The internal-consistency detail
This is the **first** falling to the earth — Alma the Younger and the sons of Mosiah, struck down in terror by the rebuking angel. It is the antecedent that makes sense of Ammon's joyful "he fell **again** to the earth" in [[Alma 27.17|Alma 27:17]], ~20 chapters later. Mormon narrates the event here; Alma re-tells it in the first person to his son in [[Alma 36.6-13|Alma 36:6-13]], with roughly ten details preserved in the same order.

## Interpretation
The scene is the Book of Mormon's paradigmatic dramatic conversion (compared with Saul/Paul on the Damascus road). Its later re-tellings — joyful inversion in Alma 27, private paternal recital in Alma 36 — are Markham's exhibit that the text tracks its own history across weeks of dictation.
