# Isaiah 5.20

**Reference:** Isaiah 5:20 (KJV)

> Woe unto them that call evil good, and good evil; that put darkness for light, and light
> for darkness; that put bitter for sweet, and sweet for bitter!

## Context
One of a series of "woes" in Isaiah's song of the vineyard chapter, pronouncing judgment on
Judah for moral inversion — the deliberate reversal of moral categories and the confusion of
good with evil. It is a stock proof-text, across traditions, for the charge that a culture
has redefined its moral vocabulary.

## Used in
- [[Adopting the Gay Label Hinders Discipleship]] — [[Andrew Robinson]] cites Isaiah 5:20 to
  argue that "Satan's playground is definitions and words and labels," reversing sweet and
  bitter by relabeling moral categories (and, he adds, dissolving the definitions of gender
  and family until they "become meaningless").
- [[Pride Is Not the Antidote to Shame]] — invoked for the same "war over definitions"
  theme: relief built on redefining oneself into a fixed identity is a counterfeit of the
  relief found in the primary covenant labels.

## Interpretive notes
Latter-day Saint, Protestant, and Catholic readers alike read the verse as condemning
moral inversion; the panel's specific *application* to same-sex-attraction labeling is a
contemporary rhetorical use, not a claim that the verse is *about* that topic. The
underlying principle the panel draws on — that naming things rightly matters morally — is
broadly shared; the application is contested by those who would say the panel is itself
mislabeling (see the steelman sections of the two citing notes).
