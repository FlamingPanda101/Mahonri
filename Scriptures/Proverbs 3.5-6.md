# Proverbs 3:5-6

> Trust in the LORD with all thine heart; and lean not unto thine own understanding. In all thy ways acknowledge him, and he shall direct thy paths. *(Proverbs 3:5-6, KJV)*

## In context
A couplet of instruction from the wisdom collection of Proverbs (chs. 1–9), most likely composed in the **Persian or early Hellenistic period**. The unit contrasts two *sources* of guidance — human self-sufficiency vs. trust in Adonai — and belongs to the same critical-wisdom family as [[Job]] and Ecclesiastes (Kohelet), which press the limits of autonomous human insight.

The Hermeneia commentary on Proverbs, quoted by Dan McClellan, insists the verse is misread as anti-intellectualism: "one should not interpret Proverbs 3:5 as a general disparagement of intellect and understanding. Rather this verse has to do with a fundamental question. Is human insight the point of departure for sapiential reflection or does wisdom ultimately come from Adonai?"[^1]

## How the verse is used and contested
- **Devotional / apologetic use:** frequently deployed to counsel epistemic humility — trust God's wisdom above one's own reasoning when the two seem to conflict.
- **Weaponized use (the "twisted scripture" the hosts critique):** the same words are pulled out as a **thought-stopping cliché** to forbid questioning altogether — "I don't get to have an opinion about it." See [[Proverbs 3.5-6 as a Thought-Stopping Cliche]].
- **Latter-day Saint balance:** LDS scripture pairs trust in the Lord with the mandate to *study* — "seek learning, even by study and also by faith" (D&C 88:118) and "study it out in your mind" before seeking confirmation (D&C 9:8) — so the verse is read as trust-plus-inquiry, not a ban on thought.
