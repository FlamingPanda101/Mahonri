# Matthew 11:25-26

> At that time Jesus answered and said, I thank thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them unto babes. Even so, Father: for so it seemed good in thy sight. *(Matthew 11:25-26, KJV)*

## In context
A saying of Jesus with a close synoptic parallel in **Luke 10:21** (both drawn from the "Q" tradition): divine truth is "hid... from the wise and prudent" and "revealed... unto babes." In its setting it contrasts the religious experts who rejected Jesus with the ordinary people who received him.

The verse is enlisted in the "reading with the Spirit" debate as a proof-text for privileged spiritual access: knowledge of God is said to be granted to the humble/childlike rather than the learned. Dan McClellan and Dan Beecher raise it as an apparent scriptural warrant for the very "epistemological leveling up" they critique — the claim that spiritual insight bypasses the trained intellect.[^1]

## Interpretation
- **As deployed against scholarship:** "the wise" are dismissed and the untrained believer is exalted, so critical study is beside the point.
- **In context:** the "wise and prudent" are specifically those who, in their self-assurance, *rejected* Jesus; "babes" are the receptive, not the uninformed. The verse commends humility of reception, not ignorance of content — it does not counsel abandoning study, and Jesus elsewhere reasons from the scriptures with the experts on their own terms.
