# Matthew 20:1-16 — Laborers in the Vineyard

> And when they came that were hired about the eleventh hour, they received every man a penny. But when the first came, they supposed that they should have received more; and they likewise received every man a penny... Is it not lawful for me to do what I will with mine own? Is thine eye evil, because I am good? So the last shall be first, and the first last: for many be called, but few chosen. *(KJV, vv. 9-10, 15-16)*

## Used in
- [[Being With Heavenly Father vs a Reward in Glory]] — Ryan's key parable: every laborer receives the same denarius regardless of hour hired, so the deathbed convert (like the thief on the cross) and the lifelong Christian "enjoy the same heaven being with God, because it's based on the merits of Jesus." Hansen replies the workers still *did the work they were asked*, and the point is God's generosity, not identical reward.

## How traditions read it
- **Reformed / Protestant (Ryan):** Heaven (being with God) is one and the same for all who trust Christ, received on His merit, not proportioned to length or quantity of service.
- **Latter-day Saint (Hansen):** Distinguishes *entry* into glory (by faith, the shared "penny") from *degrees* of reward within glory; those hired still labored, and other texts (parable of the minas, Luke 19) show differentiated rewards for faithfulness.
