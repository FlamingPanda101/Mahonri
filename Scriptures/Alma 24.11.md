# Alma 24:11 (Book of Mormon)

> "And now behold, my brethren, since it has been all that we could do, (as we were the most lost of all mankind) to repent of all our sins and the many murders which we have committed, and to get God to take them away from our hearts, for it was all we could do to repent sufficiently before God that he would take away our stain—"

The king of the [[Alma 24.7|Anti-Nephi-Lehies]] explains why the converted Lamanites buried their weapons of war: their entire spiritual labor — "**all that we could do**" — was **to repent**. The phrase appears **twice in this one verse**, and both times names repentance, not an open-ended list of works. The speakers are self-described as the "most lost of all mankind" (murderers), for whom repentance was the whole of what lay in their power.

**Why it matters for [[2 Nephi 25.23|2 Nephi 25:23]].** [[Jeff Harmon]] argues this is the Book of Mormon internally *defining* the identical phrase used in 2 Nephi 25:23 ("by grace that we are saved, after all we can do"). If "all we can do" = "repent," then 2 Nephi 25:23 reads "saved by grace after we repent," dissolving the works-salvation charge. See [[All We Can Do Is Defined as Repentance in Alma 24]].
