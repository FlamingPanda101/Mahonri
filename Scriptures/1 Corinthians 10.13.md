# 1 Corinthians 10:13

> "There hath no temptation taken you but such as is common to man: but God is faithful, who will not suffer you to be tempted above that ye are able; but will with the temptation also make a way to escape, that ye may be able to bear it." (KJV)

## Significance
[[Tim Stratton]] turns this verse into a two-minute test for **libertarian free will** and **middle knowledge**. If God provides "a way of escape" every time a Christian is tempted, yet the Christian still sins, then **he could have done otherwise** — which is libertarian freedom. And since an omniscient God was not "surprised" by that choice and "knew before he created the world," God has middle knowledge. "At that point, you are a mere Molinist."[^1] The argument brackets the salvation question (both sides agree a saved person still sins) to isolate the free-will point. See [[TRUMP - The Molinist Alternative to TULIP]].

## How traditions read it
- **Molinist / non-Calvinist:** The promised "way to escape" presupposes a real, unactualized alternative — genuine ability to do otherwise.
- **Calvinist (ED):** The way of escape is real but the person, left to his own most-desired inclination, will not take it unless God so determines; the verse describes God's provision, not a libertarian power. This is the [[Coerced Love Is Not Love - Agency Against Irresistible Grace|compatibilist rejoinder]].
