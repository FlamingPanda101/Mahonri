# Luke 23.43

> And Jesus said unto him, Verily I say unto thee, To day shalt thou be with me in paradise. *(Luke 23:43, KJV)*

## Significance

The single most-cited proof that the soul goes immediately to its final heavenly reward at death — Jesus' word to the penitent thief on the cross. [[Alex Arnold]] of *Moroni's Standard* argues the Greek *paradeisos* does **not** mean the final destination of the redeemed: in the Septuagint it renders the **Garden of Eden**, and in Jewish apocalyptic literature (e.g. [[1 Enoch]]) it names "a region *within* the intermediate state." When Paul uses the word in [[2 Corinthians 12.2|2 Corinthians 12]] he places it beside "the third heaven" as parallel descriptions of one vision — a level within a differentiated cosmology, not final glory. On the LDS reading the thief enters the spirit world, "which includes a state of paradise," exactly as promised — without requiring that paradise equal the final resurrection destination.

## Interpretation across traditions
- **Popular Protestant/Catholic:** the soul goes at once to conscious blessedness ("today").
- **Intermediate-state reading (LDS; N.T. Wright):** paradise is the blessed part of the spirit world before resurrection, distinct from the Father's final presence — consistent with [[John 20.17|John 20:17]], where the risen Jesus says he has "not yet ascended to my Father."
