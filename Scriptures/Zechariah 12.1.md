# Zechariah 12:1

> The burden of the word of the LORD for Israel, saith the LORD, which stretcheth forth the heavens, and layeth the foundation of the earth, and **formeth the spirit of man within him**. *(KJV)*

**Key phrase:** "formeth the spirit of man within him" — Hebrew **וְיֹצֵר** (*wə-yōṣēr*), from **יָצַר** (*yāṣar*), "to form, shape, fashion" (as a potter shapes clay).

## Significance

This is the verse anti-Mormon polemicist [[Sam Shamoun]] cited in the Las Vegas debate to argue that God *creates* human spirits — implying creation *ex nihilo*.[^1] The Latter-day Saint reply turns the verb against the argument: *yāṣar* ("form") denotes **shaping pre-existing material**, not calling something out of nothing. It is the same verb used in [[Genesis 2.7|Genesis 2:7]] — "the LORD God **formed** (*yāṣar*) man of the dust of the ground" — where God demonstrably works with an existing substrate (dust). So a verse offered *for* ex nihilo instead fits the LDS view that spirits are **organized from eternal intelligence**, not created from nothing.[^2] Contrast the counter-idiom "made the world of matter without form" in [[Wisdom 11.17|Wisdom 11:17]].

## Interpretations
- **Trinitarian / creedal:** God "forms" the spirit as an act of creation; taken with the ex-nihilo tradition, the spirit is a fresh creation infused into the body.
- **Latter-day Saint:** "Form" (*yāṣar*) presupposes material acted upon; paired with the doctrine of uncreated intelligence, God organizes rather than creates the spirit from nothing. Cf. "create" (*bārā'*) read as *organize* in [[Bara Means Organize and Ex Nihilo Is a Second-Century Invention]].
