# Ephesians 2:1-5

> And you hath he quickened, who were dead in trespasses and sins... Even when we were dead in sins, [he] hath quickened us together with Christ, (by grace ye are saved;) *(KJV, vv. 1, 5)*

Paul's image of spiritual death and resurrection: the unregenerate are "dead in trespasses," and God "quickens" (makes alive). Reformed theology reads this as teaching that God's regenerating act **precedes and causes** the sinner's response of faith.

## In the salvation debate
[[Ryan (Protestant Debater)|Ryan]] (Reformed) pairs Ephesians 2 with [[Ezekiel 36]] to argue monergism: "You got Ephesians 2 where it says we're dead in our trespasses and sins and he makes us alive... He takes out the heart of stone, gives us a new heart." A corpse cannot raise itself, so faith is God's gift to the elect, not a human choice that precedes grace. [[Jacob Hansen]] accepts total human helplessness apart from Christ but argues the death-metaphor is over-read when it is used to deny real agency altogether (see [[Regeneration Precedes Faith So We Only Seem to Choose God]]).
