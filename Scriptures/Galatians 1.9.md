# Galatians 1:9

> "But though we, or an angel from heaven, preach any other gospel unto you than that which we have preached unto you, let him be accursed. As we said before, so say I now again, If any man preach any other gospel unto you than that ye have received, let him be accursed." (Galatians 1:8-9, KJV)

## Significance
Stratton's closing punchline. Having argued that exhaustive-divine-determinist Calvinism inverts the gospel — God creating most people "damned to hell... from the womb" — he applies Paul's anathema: "if... even if an angel from heaven comes and gives a different gospel, then they are to be accursed."[^1] The move is deliberately even-handed: the same "different gospel" test Protestants aim at Mormonism, Stratton turns on Calvinism ("if it's good for the goose, it's good for the gander"). See [[Calvinism Is a Different Gospel - GOSPEL vs ED]].

## How traditions read it
The verse is a standard Protestant boundary-marker against "another gospel." Stratton's controversial application is that **Calvinism itself** falls under it — a charge Calvinists reject by insisting TULIP *is* the biblical gospel, "the entire Bible, our final authority, teaches Calvinism."[^2]
