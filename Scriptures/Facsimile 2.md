# Facsimile 2

The second of three illustrations printed with the [[Book of Abraham]] in the LDS [[Pearl of Great Price]]. It is a **hypocephalus** — a round Egyptian funerary disk placed under the head of a mummy — reproduced from the Joseph Smith papyri (the hypocephalus of a man named Sheshonq) with Joseph Smith's own numbered explanations of its figures.

## What it is
A hypocephalus is one of a class of ~100–120 known objects whose function (per Book of the Dead spell 162) was to place flame or light "at the head" of the deceased so they could pass the gates of the afterlife. Because Facsimile 2 was damaged when Joseph published it, the engraver Reuben Hedlock's reconstructions filled the missing portions; those restorations (the solar boat, a multi-headed central figure, the upright orientation) each have parallels among other attested hypocephali.

## Joseph Smith's reading
Joseph filed Facsimile 2 under **astronomy** — his journals call it "the astronomical cut." His captions tie its figures to the sun, the four quarters of the earth, the firmament, and the governing star **Kolob**, matching the cosmology of [[Abraham 3.2-3|Abraham 3]]. The one readable text-block, which he said pertains to "the holy temple of God," has genuine parallels on the Ptolemaic temple gates at Karnak — fitting for a document whose job is to pass its owner *through gates*.
