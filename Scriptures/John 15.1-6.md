# John 15:1-6

> I am the true vine, and my Father is the husbandman. Every branch in me that beareth not fruit he taketh away... I am the vine, ye are the branches: He that abideth in me, and I in him, the same bringeth forth much fruit: for without me ye can do nothing. If a man abide not in me, he is cast forth as a branch, and is withered; and men gather them, and cast them into the fire, and they are burned. *(KJV, vv. 1-2, 5-6)*

*Related: v.10 — "If ye keep my commandments, ye shall abide in my love; even as I have kept my Father's commandments, and abide in his love."*

## Used in
- [[Can a True Believer Fall Away]] — the exchange's central text. Hansen: a branch that is truly "in me" can fail to "abide" and be "cast forth... and burned," so a believer can fall away. Ryan: the unfruitful branch was never savingly in the vine — a false believer near Christ, not attached to Him.

## How traditions read it
- **Latter-day Saint (Hansen):** "Abide" means *remain* — the branch really is in the vine and must stay; keeping His commandments (v.10) is how one abides, so perseverance is genuinely contingent on the agent.
- **Reformed / Protestant (Ryan):** A branch that bears no fruit reveals it never truly belonged (cf. 1 John 2:19); true union with the vine *inevitably* produces fruit (v.5), so falling away exposes a false profession rather than a lost salvation.
