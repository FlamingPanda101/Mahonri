# 3 Nephi 27:17

> And he that endureth not unto the end, the same is he that is also hewn down and cast into the fire, from whence they can no more return, because of the justice of the Father.

**[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** cites this as a Book of Mormon proof that Latter-day Saint scripture makes salvation depend on works: those "who have repented and been baptized but are not able to keep the commandments" are, he says, "cast into fire forever," and "enduring to the end means keeping the commandments, and we don't do that."[^1] He uses it both to argue LDS soteriology is works-based and to challenge any assurance of a present, secure eternal life.

**[[Hayden Carroll]]'s reply:** "enduring to the end" is not sinless perfection but continuing in faith and love toward Christ ("If you love me, keep my commandments"); the verse names those who *finally* reject Him, not the imperfect disciple who relies on grace.[^2] He reads it alongside the Book of Mormon's insistence that we "merit nothing" and are saved through Christ's merits — see [[LDS Salvation Is by Grace Not Earned by Works]].
