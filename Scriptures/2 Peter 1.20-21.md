# 2 Peter 1:20-21

> Knowing this first, that no prophecy of the scripture is of any private interpretation. For the prophecy came not in old time by the will of man: but holy men of God spake as they were moved by the Holy Ghost. *(KJV)*

## Significance
A standard prooftext for the divine origin of scripture: prophecy "came not by the will of man" but as men "were moved by the Holy Ghost." Quinn Fryberg pairs it with [[2 Timothy 3.16-17|2 Timothy 3:16-17]] to establish that scripture originates from God and therefore carries divine authority.

**Counter-reading in the debate:** Hayden Carroll notes the verse speaks of **prophecy**, not "scripture" as a delineated collection — and "the entire New Testament" is not prophecy (much of it is historical narrative) — so it supports divine *inspiration* without identifying *which books* are canon or establishing an infallible 66-book list. Carroll also flags the mainstream-critical view that 2 Peter is pseudonymous (its reference to Paul's collected letters suggesting a date decades after Peter's death), which Fryberg disputes on the ground that a theistic worldview has no reason to reject Petrine authorship.
