# 1 John 3:9

> Whosoever is born of God doth not commit sin; for his seed remaineth in him: and he cannot sin, because he is born of God.

The battleground verse for the debate's second half. **[[Mark (Ex-Mormon Baptist)]]** takes it strictly literally: one born of God "**cannot sin**," and since believers plainly still sin in the body, the verse must be distinguishing the **regenerate soul** (made sinless and "sealed up until the day of redemption") from the still-sinful **body**.[^1] He supports the soul/body split with [[Romans 7.17|Romans 7:17]] (old man / new man).

**[[Hayden Carroll]]'s rebuttal** is hermeneutical: the text says nothing about a soul-versus-body partition — "you're adding to the text." He also notes the reading fractures the person into two agents, whereas conversion makes one "a new man," not "an old man and a new man," which he calls a contradiction.[^2]

Traditions read the "cannot sin" tension variously: as habitual/continuous sinning (the Greek present tense), as the ideal of the divine seed, or (Mark's view) as a regenerated inner self. The dispute is a live crux in Johannine exegesis, not a settled proof for either side.
