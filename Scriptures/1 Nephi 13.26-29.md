# 1 Nephi 13:26-29

> [26] And after they go forth by the hand of the twelve apostles of the Lamb, from the Jews unto the Gentiles, thou seest the formation of that great and abominable church, which is most abominable above all other churches; for behold, they have taken away from the gospel of the Lamb many parts which are plain and most precious; and also many covenants of the Lord have they taken away. [27] And all this have they done that they might pervert the right ways of the Lord, that they might blind the eyes and harden the hearts of the children of men. [28] Wherefore, thou seest that after the book hath gone forth through the hands of the great and abominable church, that there are many plain and precious things taken away from the book, which is the book of the Lamb of God. [29] And after these plain and precious things were taken away ... an exceedingly great many do stumble, yea, insomuch that Satan hath great power over them. *(Book of Mormon, current edition)*

## The Load-Bearing Claim
This is the passage behind the Latter-day Saint "plain and precious things removed" thesis. On [[Alex Arnold]]'s reading it is *not* the generic charge that the Bible contains errors, but a **specific, falsifiable, three-part claim**: a **mechanism** (deliberate removal — "taken away"), an **actor** (an identifiable institution), and a **consequence** ("an exceedingly great many do stumble"). It is that specificity — published in 1830 — that he argues later scholarship confirmed from independent directions.

## Interpretive Note — "great and abominable church"
Within the Book of Mormon the phrase is read symbolically (the wider [[1 Nephi 14.10|1 Nephi 14]] "two churches" — the church of the Lamb vs. the church of the devil) rather than as any single named denomination; the Church repudiated the older denomination-specific reading. Applied to *textual* history, apologists locate the "removal" chiefly upstream of the extant New Testament manuscript tradition — in the Old Testament's pre-canonical formation (the [[Deuteronomistic Corruption of the Old Testament|Deuteronomistic reform]]) and in doctrine/covenant — rather than in the copying of surviving NT books.
